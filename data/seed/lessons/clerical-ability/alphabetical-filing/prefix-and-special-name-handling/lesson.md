# Prefix and Special Name Handling

## Explanations

### Introduction

Prefix and special name handling refers to the clerical filing rules used for names that contain prefixes, particles, symbols, abbreviations, apostrophes, spaces, and unusual formatting. In government filing systems, names are not always simple single-word surnames. Many records contain surnames such as “Dela Cruz,” “McArthur,” “O’Brien,” or “St. Claire.” Clerical workers must therefore understand how these names are arranged consistently within alphabetical filing systems.

In the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE) Sub-Professional level, prefix and special-name handling questions test an examinee’s ability to apply standardized filing rules accurately under time pressure. These questions require careful letter-by-letter comparison and close attention to spaces, punctuation marks, prefixes, and compound forms.

Government offices such as the CSC, COA, DBM, and DILG handle thousands of personnel records, legal documents, and public applications. Standardized handling of special names ensures efficient retrieval and prevents filing confusion caused by inconsistent name interpretation.

### Why Prefix and Special Name Handling Is Tested in the CSE

- Government records contain many compound and prefixed surnames.
- Clerical workers must follow standardized filing rules consistently.
- Accurate filing prevents retrieval errors.
- Special names frequently appear in personnel and legal records.
- Filing consistency improves office efficiency.
- Proper handling avoids duplication and misplacement.
- Attention to detail is essential in clerical work.

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. Ignoring surname prefixes such as “De,” “Dela,” or “Mc.”
2. Treating spaces as irrelevant during comparison.
3. Filing apostrophized names incorrectly.
4. Separating compound surnames improperly.
5. Treating abbreviations as symbols instead of words.
6. Comparing names based on pronunciation rather than spelling.
7. Ignoring punctuation rules.
8. Rushing through similar-looking names.

### 4.1 Understanding Prefix and Special Name Handling

Special names contain additional filing elements such as:
- Prefixes
- Spaces
- Hyphens
- Apostrophes
- Abbreviations
- Compound forms

Examples:
- De Leon
- Dela Cruz
- O’Brien
- St. Claire
- McArthur

These names are treated systematically during filing.

Example:

- Dela Cruz, Ana
- Diaz, Ben
- Domingo, Carla

Correct order:
1. Dela Cruz, Ana
2. Diaz, Ben
3. Domingo, Carla

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Standardized special-name handling prevents confusion caused by inconsistent interpretation. When every clerk follows identical rules for prefixes and punctuation, records remain easy to retrieve and organize.

### 4.2 Prefix Handling

Prefixes are treated as part of the surname. When comparing, ignore spaces and compare letter by letter as a continuous string.

Common prefixes:
- De
- Del
- Dela
- Delos
- Mc
- Mac
- St.

Example:

- De Leon, Marco → filed as "DeLeon"
- Dela Cruz, Ana → filed as "DelaCruz"
- Del Rosario, Ben → filed as "DelRosario"

Compare as continuous strings:
- D-e-l-a-C... (Dela Cruz)
- D-e-l-e-o... (De Leon)
- D-e-l-R-o... (Del Rosario)

Position 4: a (Dela Cruz) vs e (De Leon) vs R (Del Rosario)

Since a < e < r:

Correct order:
1. Dela Cruz, Ana
2. De Leon, Marco
3. Del Rosario, Ben

Example:

- Macaraig, Leo
- McArthur, Ben
- Mendoza, Carla

Correct order:
1. Macaraig, Leo
2. McArthur, Ben
3. Mendoza, Carla

Compare letter by letter exactly as written.

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Prefixes are legally and historically part of surnames. Ignoring them would separate related family records and create inconsistent filing systems.

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** “Prefixes should be ignored during filing.”
>
> **Why it fails:** “Dela Cruz” cannot be filed under “Cruz” because the official surname includes “Dela.”
>
> **Correct model:** Prefixes are treated as part of the complete surname.

### 4.3 Compound Surnames

Compound surnames contain multiple words functioning as one surname.

Examples:
- Dela Cruz
- Del Mundo
- Del Rosario
- Van Buren

These surnames are filed as complete units.

Example:

- Del Mundo, Ana
- Dela Cruz, Ben
- Del Rosario, Carla

Correct order:
1. Dela Cruz, Ben
2. Del Mundo, Ana
3. Del Rosario, Carla

Example:

- Van Buren, Leo
- Vargas, Ben
- Velasco, Ana

Correct order:
1. Van Buren, Leo
2. Vargas, Ben
3. Velasco, Ana

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Compound surnames preserve family identity and cultural naming conventions. Filing them as single units prevents accidental separation of related records.

### 4.4 Apostrophized Names

Names containing apostrophes are compared letter by letter.

Examples:
- O’Brien
- D’Angelo

The apostrophe itself is ignored during comparison.

Example:

- O’Brien, Ana
- Ocampo, Ben
- Ong, Carla

Correct order:
1. O’Brien, Ana
2. Ocampo, Ben
3. Ong, Carla

Example:

- D’Angelo, Leo
- Dela Cruz, Ben
- Diaz, Ana

Correct order:
1. D’Angelo, Leo
2. Dela Cruz, Ben
3. Diaz, Ana

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** “Apostrophes create separate filing sections.”
>
> **Why it fails:** “O’Brien” is compared as ordinary letters within the surname sequence.
>
> **Correct model:** Apostrophes are ignored during alphabetical comparison.

### 4.5 Abbreviated Prefixes

Abbreviated prefixes such as “St.” are treated exactly as written.

Example:

- St. Claire, Ana
- Santos, Ben
- Santiago, Carla

Correct order:
1. Santiago, Carla
2. Santos, Ben
3. St. Claire, Ana

Example:

- St. John, Leo
- Suarez, Ana
- Sy, Ben

Correct order:
1. St. John, Leo
2. Suarez, Ana
3. Sy, Ben

### 4.6 Spacing Rules

When filing compound surnames, spaces are IGNORED during comparison. Compare letter by letter as if the name were one continuous word.

Example:

- De Leon → filed as "DeLeon"
- Dela Cruz → filed as "DelaCruz"

Compare as continuous strings:
- D-e-l-a-C... (Dela Cruz)
- D-e-l-e-o... (De Leon)

Position 4: a vs e → a comes first

Correct order:
1. Dela Cruz
2. De Leon

Example:

- San Juan → filed as "SanJuan"
- Sanchez → "Sanchez"

Compare:
- S-a-n-J... (San Juan)
- S-a-n-c... (Sanchez)

Position 4: c (Sanchez) vs J (San Juan) → lowercase comparison: c < j

Correct order:
1. Sanchez
2. San Juan

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Ignoring spaces during comparison treats compound surnames as single filing units, which is consistent with how they function as complete family names. This prevents the arbitrary separation of names that happen to contain spaces from those that don't (e.g., "Dela Cruz" vs "Delacruz" would file identically).

### 4.7 Special Character Handling

Special characters such as periods and apostrophes are generally ignored during filing comparisons.

Examples:
- St. Claire
- O’Brien

Comparison focuses on letters rather than punctuation.

Example:

- O’Brien
- Ong

Compare:
O = O

Next:
b vs n

Since b comes before n:

Correct order:
1. O’Brien
2. Ong

### Check Your Understanding

**1.** Which comes first: “Dela Cruz” or “De Leon”? → **Dela Cruz** (ignoring spaces: DelaCruz position 4 = “a” vs DeLeon position 4 = “e”; a < e)

**2.** Which comes first: “Macaraig” or “McArthur”? → **Macaraig** (a comes before c)

**3.** Which comes first: “O’Brien” or “Ocampo”? → **O’Brien** (b comes before c)

**4.** Which comes first: “San Juan” or “Sanchez”? → **Sanchez** (ignoring spaces: SanJuan position 4 = “J” vs Sanchez position 4 = “c”; c < j)

### 4.8 Speed Techniques for Special Names

#### Technique 1: Compare Exactly as Written

Do not guess pronunciation.

#### Technique 2: Ignore Punctuation

Periods and apostrophes are generally ignored.

#### Technique 3: Ignore Spaces in Compound Surnames

Compare compound surnames as continuous strings.

#### Technique 4: Treat Prefixes as Part of the Surname

Never separate prefixes from surnames.

### Exam Strategies

- Compare names letter by letter carefully.
- Treat prefixes as part of surnames.
- Ignore pronunciation completely.
- Ignore spaces in compound surnames — compare as one word.
- Ignore apostrophes and periods during comparison.
- Watch for compound surnames.
- Compare only until a difference appears.
- Practice recognizing special-name patterns quickly.

### Guided Practice

Complete the missing steps. Answers are provided below each problem.

**1.** Arrange alphabetically: De Leon, Marco — Dela Cruz, Ana — Del Rosario, Ben

- Step 1: Identify that all three are compound surnames with prefixes. Ignore spaces to form continuous strings: DeLeon, DelaCruz, DelRosario
- Step 2: Compare letter by letter — D-e-l matches for all three. At position 4: a (DelaCruz) vs e (DeLeon) vs R (DelRosario)
- Step 3: Apply alphabetical order (case-insensitive): a < e < r, so the order is: _____

**Answer:** 1. Dela Cruz, Ana — 2. De Leon, Marco — 3. Del Rosario, Ben

**2.** Arrange alphabetically: O’Brien, Ana — Ocampo, Ben — Ong, Carla

- Step 1: Identify special character — O’Brien contains an apostrophe. Rule: ignore the apostrophe and compare letters only: OBrien, Ocampo, Ong
- Step 2: Compare letter by letter — O matches for all. Position 2: B (OBrien) vs c (Ocampo) vs n (Ong): _____
- Step 3: Determine order: _____

**Answer:** 1. O’Brien, Ana — 2. Ocampo, Ben — 3. Ong, Carla (b < c < n)

**3.** Arrange alphabetically: Macaraig, Leo — McArthur, Ben — Mendoza, Carla

- Step 1: Identify prefix — “Mc” in McArthur is treated as part of the surname. Compare all as written: Macaraig, McArthur, Mendoza
- Step 2: Position 1: M = M = M. Position 2: a (Macaraig) vs c (McArthur) vs e (Mendoza): _____
- Step 3: _____

**Answer:** 1. Macaraig, Leo — 2. McArthur, Ben — 3. Mendoza, Carla (a < c < e)

**4.** Arrange alphabetically: San Juan, Ana — Sanchez, Ben — Santos, Carla

- Step 1: Identify compound surname “San Juan.” Ignore spaces: SanJuan. Compare with Sanchez and Santos
- Step 2: S-a-n matches for all three. Position 4: _____
- Step 3: _____

**Answer:** 1. Sanchez, Ben — 2. San Juan, Ana — 3. Santos, Carla (position 4: c (Sanchez) < J (SanJuan) < t (Santos); c < j < t)

**5.** Arrange alphabetically: St. Claire, Ana — Santiago, Ben — Santos, Carla

- Step 1: _____
- Step 2: _____
- Step 3: _____

**Answer:** 1. Santiago, Ben — 2. Santos, Carla — 3. St. Claire, Ana (ignore period: StClaire. Position 1–2: Sa vs Sa vs St; position 2: a (Santiago, Santos) < t (StClaire). Between Santiago and Santos: position 4 = t vs t, position 5 = i < o)
