# Indexing Basics

## Explanations

### Introduction

Indexing is the process of determining the name, subject, or number under which a record will be filed and creating a reference point that allows retrieval. While filing is the physical act of placing a document in its correct location, indexing is the intellectual step that comes before it — deciding *how* to label and classify a record so it can be found again. Without proper indexing, even a perfectly organized filing cabinet becomes useless because no one can locate what they need.

In the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE) Sub-Professional level, indexing questions test whether an examinee can break a name or title into its correct filing units, assign the proper order to those units, and apply standard indexing rules consistently. These skills are fundamental to every government office that maintains personnel records, correspondence files, and transaction documents.

Government clerks at agencies like the CSC, DILG, DBM, and COA handle thousands of records daily. Each record must be indexed correctly before it is filed — whether in a physical cabinet or a digital records management system. A single indexing error means a lost document, a delayed transaction, or a failed audit. Mastering indexing basics is the first step toward reliable records management.

### Why Indexing Basics Is Tested in the CSE

- Government employees must retrieve records quickly and accurately from large filing systems.
- Proper indexing prevents misfiled documents that cause delays in public service delivery.
- Indexing is the foundation of all filing systems — alphabetical, numerical, and subject-based.
- Clerks must distinguish between filing units, key units, and cross-references.
- Accurate indexing demonstrates attention to detail and systematic thinking.
- Records management standards (ISO 15489) require consistent indexing procedures.
- Indexing errors in government transactions can result in audit findings and accountability issues.
- The skill transfers directly to digital document management systems used in modern government offices.

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. Confusing indexing with filing — indexing determines the label; filing places the document.
2. Failing to identify the key unit (the first unit used for filing comparison).
3. Treating all parts of a name as a single unit instead of breaking them into separate filing units.
4. Ignoring punctuation rules — not knowing when to disregard commas, hyphens, and apostrophes.
5. Indexing titles and suffixes as part of the name instead of placing them at the end.
6. Not recognizing when a cross-reference is needed for alternative name forms.
7. Applying personal name indexing rules to business names or vice versa.
8. Skipping the mental step of unit separation and jumping directly to alphabetical comparison.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define indexing and explain how it differs from filing.
- Break personal names into correct filing units (key unit, second unit, third unit).
- Apply the "surname first" rule for personal name indexing.
- Handle titles, suffixes, and professional designations during indexing.
- Identify when a cross-reference entry is needed.
- Index names with prefixes, particles, and compound elements.
- Apply indexing rules quickly and accurately under exam time pressure.
- Distinguish between indexing rules for personal names vs. business names.

### 4.1 What Is Indexing?

Indexing is the mental process of deciding how a record will be identified in a filing system. It involves three steps:

1. **Inspecting** — reading the document to determine what name or subject it should be filed under.
2. **Determining the units** — breaking the name or subject into individual filing units.
3. **Coding** — marking or noting the units in their correct order for filing.

**Key terminology:**

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Filing unit | Each word, abbreviation, or initial in a name that is treated as a separate element for comparison |
| Key unit | The first filing unit — the one used for primary alphabetical placement |
| Second unit | The second element used when key units are identical |
| Third unit | The third element, used when both key and second units match |
| Cross-reference | An alternate entry that directs the searcher to the primary filing location |
| Coding | The physical act of marking units on a document (underlining, numbering) |

**Example:**

The name "Maria Clara Santos" is indexed as:

| Key Unit | Second Unit | Third Unit |
|----------|-------------|------------|
| Santos | Maria | Clara |

The surname "Santos" becomes the key unit because personal names are indexed surname-first. "Maria" is the second unit (given name), and "Clara" is the third unit (middle name).

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Indexing breaks names into discrete, comparable units because alphabetical comparison requires a defined starting point. If you compared "Maria Clara Santos" as a single string, it would file under "M" — but in a government office with hundreds of employees named Maria, that creates an unmanageable cluster. By extracting the surname as the key unit, the system distributes records across the entire alphabet and matches how people naturally search: "I need the Santos file."

### 4.2 Indexing Personal Names

The fundamental rule for personal names: **surname first, then given name, then middle name or initial.**

#### Standard Format

| Original Name | Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 |
|---------------|----------|--------|--------|
| Juan dela Cruz | DelaCruz | Juan | — |
| Maria Santos Reyes | Reyes | Maria | Santos |
| Jose P. Garcia | Garcia | Jose | P |
| Ana Marie B. Villanueva | Villanueva | Ana | Marie |

**Rules:**
- The surname is always the key unit.
- Given names follow in the order they appear.
- Middle initials are treated as separate units.
- Each unit is compared letter by letter during filing.

#### Filipino Name Conventions

In Philippine practice, the standard name format is: **Given Name + Middle Name (mother's maiden surname) + Surname (father's surname)**. For indexing:

| Full Name | Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 |
|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Roberto Santos Cruz | Cruz | Roberto | Santos |
| Elena Reyes Garcia | Garcia | Elena | Reyes |
| Pedro Aquino Mendoza | Mendoza | Pedro | Aquino |

The last name (surname/father's surname) is the key unit. The middle name (mother's maiden surname) becomes the third unit.

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "The middle name should be indexed before the given name because it comes after the surname alphabetically."
>
> **Why it fails:** Consider "Elena Reyes Garcia." If you indexed it as Garcia → Reyes → Elena, you would file it differently from the standard Garcia → Elena → Reyes. When two people share the surname Garcia, you need the given name (Elena) as the second comparison point — that is what people search by after the surname.
>
> **Correct model:** Always index as Surname → Given Name → Middle Name. The given name is Unit 2 because it is the most commonly known identifier after the surname.

#### Initials and Abbreviations

Single initials are indexed as written — they are separate filing units consisting of one letter.

| Original | Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 | Unit 4 |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|--------|
| J. P. Rizal | Rizal | J | P | — |
| Ma. Teresa L. Santos | Santos | Ma | Teresa | L |
| R. A. Gonzales | Gonzales | R | A | — |

**Rules for initials:**
- A single initial is one filing unit.
- "Ma." (abbreviation for Maria) is treated as one unit: "Ma"
- Periods after initials are disregarded — they do not affect filing order.
- A single initial files BEFORE a full name beginning with the same letter (nothing before something).

**Example:** "J. Santos" files before "Jose Santos" because the unit "J" (one letter) comes before "Jose" (four letters) — a single letter with nothing after it precedes a full word starting with the same letter.

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** The "nothing before something" rule exists because a single initial represents an unknown or abbreviated name. Filing it first ensures that when you search for "J. Santos," you find it at the beginning of all the J-names under Santos — a predictable, consistent location regardless of what the J actually stands for.

### 4.3 Handling Titles, Suffixes, and Professional Designations

Titles and suffixes are NOT filing units for primary comparison. They are placed at the end of the indexed name and used only as distinguishing elements when all other units are identical.

#### Titles (Before the Name)

| Title Type | Examples |
|-----------|----------|
| Courtesy titles | Mr., Mrs., Ms., Miss |
| Professional titles | Dr., Atty., Engr., Arch. |
| Religious titles | Fr., Sr., Rev., Msgr. |
| Military/rank titles | Gen., Col., Sgt., Cpl. |
| Political titles | Hon., Sen., Rep., Gov. |

**Indexing rule:** Ignore the title for filing purposes. Place it in parentheses at the end.

| Original | Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 | Title |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|-------|
| Dr. Jose Rizal | Rizal | Jose | — | (Dr.) |
| Atty. Maria Santos | Santos | Maria | — | (Atty.) |
| Hon. Juan dela Cruz | DelaCruz | Juan | — | (Hon.) |
| Gen. Antonio Luna | Luna | Antonio | — | (Gen.) |

#### Suffixes (After the Name)

| Suffix Type | Examples |
|------------|----------|
| Generational | Jr., Sr., III, IV |
| Academic | Ph.D., M.D., Ed.D. |
| Professional | CPA, RN, LPT |

**Indexing rule:** Suffixes are placed after all name units. They are used to distinguish between identical names.

| Original | Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 | Suffix |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|--------|
| Jose Santos Jr. | Santos | Jose | — | (Jr.) |
| Jose Santos Sr. | Santos | Jose | — | (Sr.) |
| Maria Cruz, CPA | Cruz | Maria | — | (CPA) |
| Roberto Garcia III | Garcia | Roberto | — | (III) |

**When suffixes matter:** If two records have identical key units, second units, and third units, the suffix determines filing order:

- Santos, Jose (Jr.) files BEFORE Santos, Jose (Sr.)
- Garcia, Roberto (II) files BEFORE Garcia, Roberto (III)

Numeric suffixes file in numeric order. "Jr." files before "Sr." (Junior before Senior).

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Titles and suffixes are excluded from primary indexing because they describe a person's status, not their identity. "Dr. Jose Santos" and "Mr. Jose Santos" might be the same person at different career stages. By indexing both under "Santos, Jose," the system keeps related records together and uses the title only as a last-resort distinguisher — preventing the same person's files from being scattered across D (for Dr.) and M (for Mr.).

### 4.4 Punctuation and Special Characters in Indexing

When indexing, most punctuation marks are disregarded. The letters remain; the punctuation disappears.

| Punctuation | Rule | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| Period (.) | Disregard | J.P. → J P (two units) |
| Comma (,) | Disregard | Santos, Juan → Santos Juan |
| Apostrophe (') | Disregard, close up | O'Brien → OBrien |
| Hyphen (-) | Disregard, close up | Dela Cruz-Santos → DelaCruzSantos |
| Space in prefix | Close up | De Leon → DeLeon (one unit) |

**Critical rule for hyphens:** A hyphenated name is treated as ONE filing unit. Remove the hyphen and combine the parts.

| Original Name | Key Unit | Unit 2 |
|---------------|----------|--------|
| Anna Reyes-Santos | ReyesSantos | Anna |
| Marie Claire Sotto-Padilla | SottoPadilla | Marie Claire |

**Critical rule for apostrophes:** Remove the apostrophe and treat the result as one unit.

| Original Name | Key Unit | Unit 2 |
|---------------|----------|--------|
| Patrick O'Brien | OBrien | Patrick |
| Corazon D'Angelo | DAngelo | Corazon |

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "Hyphenated surnames should be split into two separate filing units."
>
> **Why it fails:** If "Reyes-Santos" were split into "Reyes" and "Santos" as separate units, the name would file identically to someone whose surname is just "Reyes" with a middle name "Santos." This creates ambiguity — is it a compound surname or a surname + middle name? The system cannot distinguish them.
>
> **Correct model:** Hyphenated surnames are ONE unit. Remove the hyphen, close up the letters: ReyesSantos. This preserves the compound surname as a distinct, unique filing identity.

### 4.5 Cross-References

A cross-reference is a secondary index entry that points to the primary filing location. It is used when a record might reasonably be searched under more than one name.

**When to create a cross-reference:**

1. **Hyphenated surnames** — file under the combined form, cross-reference under each part.
2. **Name changes** — file under the current name, cross-reference under the former name.
3. **Nicknames or aliases** — file under the legal name, cross-reference under the known alias.
4. **Compound business names** — file under the first word, cross-reference under other distinctive words.

**Cross-reference format:**

```
[Alternative name] — SEE [Primary filing name]
```

**Examples:**

| Situation | Primary Entry | Cross-Reference |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------|
| Hyphenated name: Anna Reyes-Santos | ReyesSantos, Anna | Santos, Anna Reyes — SEE ReyesSantos, Anna |
| Name change: Maria Cruz (now Maria Garcia) | Garcia, Maria | Cruz, Maria — SEE Garcia, Maria |
| Nickname: "Bongbong" Marcos | Marcos, Ferdinand Jr. | Marcos, Bongbong — SEE Marcos, Ferdinand Jr. |

Cross-references ensure that no matter which name a searcher uses, they can find the record. In government offices, this is critical for personnel files where employees may be known by maiden names, married names, or common nicknames.

### 4.6 The Indexing Process: Step by Step

Here is the complete indexing procedure applied to any name:

**Step 1: Inspect** — Read the full name and identify its type (personal, business, government).

**Step 2: Identify the key unit** — For personal names, this is the surname. For business names, this is the first word as written.

**Step 3: Separate into units** — Break the remaining name into individual filing units.

**Step 4: Handle special elements:**
- Remove punctuation (periods, commas, apostrophes)
- Close up hyphens and prefixes (De La → DeLa, O'Brien → OBrien)
- Move titles and suffixes to the end

**Step 5: Code the document** — Mark the units on the document (underline key unit, number subsequent units).

**Step 6: Determine if a cross-reference is needed** — If the name has alternative forms, create a cross-reference card.

### Check Your Understanding

**1.** What is the key unit when indexing "Dr. Elena Reyes Garcia"? → **Garcia** (surname is always the key unit; title "Dr." is placed at the end)

**2.** How many filing units does "J. P. Santos" have? → **3 units** (Santos = key unit, J = unit 2, P = unit 3)

**3.** How is the hyphenated name "Cruz-Aquino" treated? → **As one unit: CruzAquino** (remove hyphen, close up)

**4.** Where does "Jr." go when indexing "Roberto Santos Jr."? → **At the end, after all name units** (Santos, Roberto, Jr.)

**5.** When is a cross-reference needed? → **When a name has alternative forms** (hyphenated names, name changes, nicknames)

### 4.7 Indexing Order vs. Filing Order

A common source of confusion: indexing and filing are related but distinct operations.

| Step | What Happens | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Indexing | Determine the units and their order | "Dr. Maria Santos Cruz" → Cruz, Maria, Santos (Dr.) |
| Filing | Place the indexed record in alphabetical position among other records | Cruz, Maria Santos files between Cruz, Jose and Cruz, Pedro |

**Indexing produces the label. Filing uses the label to find the correct position.**

On the CSE, questions may ask you to:
- "Index the following name" — break it into units in correct order.
- "Arrange in correct filing order" — index all names first, then compare alphabetically.
- "Identify the key unit" — name only the first filing unit.

Understanding this distinction prevents errors where examinees try to alphabetize before properly indexing.

### 4.8 Indexing Rules Summary Table

| Rule | Application | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| Surname first | Personal names | Juan Santos → Santos, Juan |
| As written | Business names | Globe Telecom → Globe, Telecom |
| Disregard punctuation | All names | O'Brien → OBrien |
| Hyphen = one unit | Compound names | Cruz-Santos → CruzSantos |
| Titles at end | Personal names | Dr. Cruz → Cruz (Dr.) |
| Suffixes at end | Personal names | Cruz Jr. → Cruz (Jr.) |
| Nothing before something | Initials vs. full names | J. Cruz before Jose Cruz |
| Prefixes = one unit | Names with particles | De Leon → DeLeon |
| Cross-reference | Alternative name forms | SEE primary entry |

### Exam Strategies

- Always index BEFORE comparing. Do not try to alphabetize raw names — convert them to indexed form first.
- Underline or mentally mark the key unit immediately when you read a name.
- Watch for titles disguised as names: "Dr. Santos" — the key unit is Santos, not Dr.
- Count your units. If a question asks "how many filing units," remember: each word, initial, or closed-up prefix is one unit.
- For hyphenated names, mentally remove the hyphen and close up — then treat as one unit.
- On timed exams, practice the three-second rule: you should be able to identify the key unit of any name within three seconds.
- Eliminate answer choices that put titles or suffixes in the wrong position.
- If two names appear identical after indexing, check for suffixes (Jr./Sr.) or titles that distinguish them.

### Memory Aids

#### The STICK Method for Personal Name Indexing

**S** — Surname first (always the key unit)
**T** — Titles go to the end (Dr., Atty., Hon.)
**I** — Initials are separate units (J. = one unit)
**C** — Close up hyphens and prefixes (O'Brien → OBrien, De Leon → DeLeon)
**K** — Keep suffixes at the end (Jr., Sr., III)

#### Unit-Counting Finger Method

Hold up one finger for each filing unit as you read through the indexed name:

- "Santos" → 1 finger (key unit)
- "Maria" → 2 fingers (unit 2)
- "Clara" → 3 fingers (unit 3)
- "(Dr.)" → don't count (title, not a filing unit for comparison)

This physical counting prevents the common error of miscounting units on the exam.

#### The "Nothing Before Something" Reminder

Think of it as an empty shelf slot: an empty space always comes before a filled space.

- "J" is like a shelf with one book.
- "Jose" is like a shelf with four books.
- The shelf with fewer books (less content) comes first.

So: J. Santos files BEFORE Jose Santos, always.

2. Santos, Roberto (no unit 3)
3. Santos, Roberto, A (has unit 3)
4. Santos, Roberto (Jr.) — suffix distinguishes from #2; files after plain name

#### 19.

Does "Ma. Teresa" count as one unit or two?

Answer: Two units — "Ma" is unit 1 (abbreviation for Maria), "Teresa" is unit 2. Each word/abbreviation separated by a space is a separate unit.

#### 20.

Index: "Dra. Corazon C. Aquino-Cojuangco"

Answer:
| Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 | Title |
|----------|--------|--------|-------|
| AquinoCojuangco | Corazon | C | (Dra.) |

#### 21.

Index and arrange:
- Fr. Pedro Santos
- Pedro A. Santos
- P. Santos
- Pedro Santos III

Answer:
1. Santos, P (initial — nothing before something)
2. Santos, Pedro (no unit 3, no suffix)
3. Santos, Pedro, A (has unit 3)
4. Santos, Pedro (III) — suffix distinguishes; files after plain name but before unit-3 entries? No — suffix entries with no unit 3 file after the plain name but before entries with a unit 3 because "nothing before something" applies at unit 3 level.

Corrected order:
1. Santos, P
2. Santos, Pedro (Fr.) — title only, no unit 3
3. Santos, Pedro (III) — suffix only, no unit 3
4. Santos, Pedro, A — has unit 3

Note: When two entries have identical filing units (Santos, Pedro) and differ only in title/suffix, the entry with a title files before the entry with a suffix (alphabetical: titles are parenthetical notes, compared only when needed). In practice, both "Santos, Pedro (Fr.)" and "Santos, Pedro (III)" have the same filing units — the distinguishing elements are compared last.

#### 23.

A clerk receives documents for "Roberto Garcia" and "Roberto Garcia, CPA." Are these filed together or separately?

Answer: Filed together. "CPA" is a professional designation treated like a suffix — placed at the end as a distinguishing element. Both index as "Garcia, Roberto" with the second entry having "(CPA)" appended. They file in this order:
1. Garcia, Roberto
2. Garcia, Roberto (CPA)

### Worked Example 1

**Question:** Index the following name and identify all filing units: "Atty. Jose Maria P. De La Cruz Jr."

**Step 1: Inspect.** This is a personal name with a title (Atty.), a prefix (De La), and a suffix (Jr.).

**Step 2: Identify the key unit.** The surname is "De La Cruz." Since prefixes are closed up, the key unit is "DeLaCruz."

**Step 3: Separate remaining units.**
- Jose → Unit 2
- Maria → Unit 3
- P → Unit 4

**Step 4: Handle special elements.**
- "Atty." → title, placed at end: (Atty.)
- "De La Cruz" → close up prefix: DeLaCruz
- "Jr." → suffix, placed at end: (Jr.)
- "P." → remove period, keep as unit: P

**Final indexed form:**

| Key Unit | Unit 2 | Unit 3 | Unit 4 | Suffix | Title |
|----------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| DeLaCruz | Jose | Maria | P | (Jr.) | (Atty.) |

**Filing units for comparison:** 4 (DeLaCruz, Jose, Maria, P)

### Worked Example 2

**Question:** Arrange these names in correct filing order:
- Dr. Ana Santos
- Ana B. Santos
- A. Santos
- Ana Santos-Cruz

**Step 1: Index each name.**
- Santos, Ana (Dr.)
- Santos, Ana, B
- Santos, A
- SantosCruz, Ana

**Step 2: Group by key unit.**
- Key unit "Santos": three entries
- Key unit "SantosCruz": one entry

**Step 3: Compare within "Santos" group.**
- Santos, A → Unit 2 is "A" (single initial)
- Santos, Ana → Unit 2 is "Ana" (full name)
- Santos, Ana, B → Unit 2 is "Ana," Unit 3 is "B"

Apply "nothing before something": A (initial) comes before Ana (full name).
Then: Santos, Ana (no unit 3) before Santos, Ana, B (has unit 3).

**Step 4: Place "SantosCruz" relative to "Santos."**
Compare: Santos vs. SantosCruz
- S-a-n-t-o-s vs. S-a-n-t-o-s-C-r-u-z
- "Santos" is shorter, all letters match → Santos comes first (nothing before something at the key unit level).

**Final filing order:**
1. A. Santos → Santos, A
2. Dr. Ana Santos → Santos, Ana (Dr.)
3. Ana B. Santos → Santos, Ana, B
4. Ana Santos-Cruz → SantosCruz, Ana

### Worked Example 4

**Question:** How many filing units does "Gen. Fernando R. De Los Reyes III" have?

**Step 1: Index the name.**
- Key unit: DeLosReyes (prefix closed up)
- Unit 2: Fernando
- Unit 3: R
- Suffix: (III) — not a filing unit for comparison
- Title: (Gen.) — not a filing unit for comparison

**Step 2: Count filing units.**
Only units used for alphabetical comparison count:
1. DeLosReyes

### Worked Example 5

**Question:** A personnel office has two employees: "Jose Santos Jr." and "Jose Santos Sr." How are they indexed and which files first?

**Step 1: Index both names.**
- Santos, Jose (Jr.)
- Santos, Jose (Sr.)

**Step 2: Compare filing units.**
- Key unit: Santos = Santos ✓
- Unit 2: Jose = Jose ✓
- No Unit 3 for either.

**Step 3: All filing units are identical — use suffix to distinguish.**
- Jr. (Junior) files before Sr. (Senior)

**Answer:**
1. Santos, Jose (Jr.) — files first
2. Santos, Jose (Sr.) — files second

**Rationale:** When all filing units match, suffixes are compared. Junior precedes Senior because "Jr." indicates the younger/later person, and the convention is Jr. before Sr. (alphabetical: J before S).

## Key Takeaways

- Indexing is the intellectual process of determining filing units; filing is the physical placement of the record.
- The key unit for personal names is ALWAYS the surname — never the title, given name, or suffix.
- Each word, initial, or abbreviation separated by a space is one filing unit.
- Titles (Dr., Atty., Hon., Gen.) and suffixes (Jr., Sr., III) are NOT filing units — they are placed at the end as distinguishing elements.
- Hyphens and apostrophes are removed; the remaining letters are closed up into one unit (O'Brien → OBrien, Cruz-Santos → CruzSantos).
- Prefixes (De, Dela, De Los, Van, Mac) are closed up with the surname into one key unit (De La Cruz → DeLaCruz).
- "Nothing before something" — a single initial files before a full name starting with the same letter (J. before Jose).
- Cross-references are created for hyphenated names, name changes, and nicknames to ensure records are findable under alternative forms.
- Count only filing units used for comparison — titles and suffixes are not included in the count.
- Always index before comparing — never try to alphabetize raw, unindexed names.

## Summary

Indexing is the foundational clerical skill that determines how records are labeled, classified, and made retrievable in any filing system. It is the mental step that precedes filing: before a document can be placed in its correct alphabetical position, a clerk must first break the name into filing units, identify the key unit (surname for personal names), handle special elements like titles, suffixes, hyphens, and prefixes, and determine whether cross-references are needed for alternative name forms.

The core rules are consistent and systematic. Personal names are always indexed surname-first. Titles and suffixes are excluded from primary comparison and placed at the end as distinguishing elements. Punctuation — periods, commas, apostrophes, and hyphens — is disregarded, with the remaining letters closed up. Prefixes like "De," "Dela," and "De Los" are combined with the surname into a single key unit. The "nothing before something" principle ensures that initials always file before full names beginning with the same letter.

For the CSE Sub-Professional exam, indexing questions test precision and speed. Examinees must be able to identify key units instantly, count filing units accurately, and apply punctuation and prefix rules without hesitation. The STICK mnemonic (Surname first, Titles at end, Initials as separate units, Close up hyphens/prefixes, Keep suffixes at end) provides a reliable mental checklist. Mastering these basics prepares examinees for more advanced topics including business name indexing, government office filing, and complete records management systems.
