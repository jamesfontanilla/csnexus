# Name Comparison

## Explanations

### Introduction

Name comparison is a clerical checking skill that requires examinees to determine whether two or more written names are exactly identical or contain differences. In the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE) Sub-Professional level, this skill falls under clerical operations — testing the examinee's ability to detect subtle discrepancies in names as they would appear on government forms, personnel records, and official correspondence.

Government clerks process hundreds of names daily: verifying payroll entries against employee records, cross-checking applicant names on CS Form 212 against identification documents, and confirming that names on disbursement vouchers match those in the registry. A single mismatched letter — "Gonzales" versus "Gonzalez," or "Ma." versus "Maria" — can cause delayed transactions, misfiled records, or even fraudulent claims slipping through undetected.

Name comparison questions on the CSE present two versions of a name (or a list of names) and ask whether they are identical. The challenge is not intellectual complexity — it is perceptual accuracy under time pressure. The exam rewards examinees who can systematically scan character by character rather than relying on a quick visual impression.

### Why Name Comparison Is Tested in the CSE

- Government employees verify names across multiple documents daily (IDs, forms, payroll, vouchers).
- Errors in name transcription can delay benefits, pensions, and salary disbursements.
- Clerks must catch discrepancies between handwritten and typed versions of names.
- Name verification prevents identity fraud in government transactions.
- Filipino names contain particles, suffixes, and abbreviations that create many opportunities for error.
- Accuracy in name checking is a foundational skill for records management and data entry.
- The skill directly measures attention to detail — a core competency for civil service positions.

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. Glancing at names holistically instead of comparing character by character.
2. Confusing similar-looking letters: "l" (lowercase L) vs. "I" (uppercase i), "rn" vs. "m."
3. Missing transposed letters: "Reyse" vs. "Reyes," "Crzu" vs. "Cruz."
4. Overlooking differences in name particles: "Dela Cruz" vs. "De La Cruz" vs. "dela Cruz."
5. Ignoring punctuation differences: "Ma." vs. "Ma" vs. "Maria."
6. Assuming names with the same pronunciation are identical: "Gonzales" vs. "Gonzalez."
7. Skipping middle names or initials when comparing: "Juan P. Santos" vs. "Juan Santos."
8. Rushing through familiar-looking Filipino surnames without checking each letter.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Compare two written names character by character to determine if they are identical.
- Identify common types of name discrepancies: substitution, transposition, omission, and addition.
- Recognize Filipino name patterns that create comparison traps (particles, suffixes, abbreviations).
- Apply systematic scanning techniques that prevent perceptual errors.
- Distinguish between names that look similar but differ in one or more characters.
- Maintain accuracy while working under time pressure.
- Detect spacing and punctuation differences in names.
- Handle compound surnames, hyphenated names, and generational suffixes correctly.

### 4.1 What Name Comparison Tests

Name comparison is not about filing order, spelling rules, or alphabetizing. It tests one thing: **can you determine whether two representations of a name are exactly the same?**

The task is deceptively simple. Your brain is wired to recognize patterns and fill in expected information — which means it actively works against you in this task. When you see "Santos" and "Santso," your brain reads both as "Santos" because it recognizes the pattern. Beating this requires a deliberate, mechanical comparison strategy.

**The four types of discrepancies:**

| Type | Example | What Happened |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Substitution | Gonzale**s** vs. Gonzale**z** | One letter replaced by another |
| Transposition | Re**ye**s vs. Re**ey**s | Two adjacent letters swapped |
| Omission | Vil**l**anueva vs. Vi**l**anueva | A letter is missing |
| Addition | Cruz vs. Cru**i**z | An extra letter is inserted |

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Character-by-character comparison works because it overrides the brain's pattern-recognition shortcut. When you read a word holistically, your visual cortex matches it against stored patterns and "autocorrects" minor differences — which is useful for reading speed but catastrophic for error detection. By forcing yourself to compare position 1 with position 1, position 2 with position 2, and so on, you bypass this autocorrection and catch discrepancies that holistic reading would miss.

### 4.2 Filipino Name Patterns That Create Traps

Filipino names have structural features that make comparison particularly challenging. Understanding these patterns helps you know where to look more carefully.

#### Particles and Prefixes

| Pattern | Variations You May See |
|---------|----------------------|
| De / de | De Leon, de Leon, DeLeon, De leon |
| Dela / De La / dela | Dela Cruz, De La Cruz, dela Cruz, DeLaCruz |
| Delos / De Los | Delos Reyes, De Los Reyes, delos Reyes |
| Del | Del Rosario, del Rosario, DelRosario |
| San / Santa / Santo | San Juan, SanJuan, Santa Maria, Sta. Maria |

**Key point:** In name comparison, "Dela Cruz" and "De La Cruz" are NOT the same — they differ in spacing. The question is not whether they refer to the same person, but whether the written forms are identical.

#### Common Abbreviations

| Full Form | Abbreviation | Trap |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Maria | Ma. | "Ma." vs. "Ma" (period matters) |
| Santa | Sta. | "Sta." vs. "Santa" (not identical) |
| Santo | Sto. | "Sto." vs. "Santo" |
| Junior | Jr. | "Jr." vs. "Jr" vs. "JR" |
| Senior | Sr. | "Sr." vs. "Sr" |

#### Generational Suffixes

Names may include: Jr., Sr., II, III, IV. Watch for:
- Presence vs. absence: "Juan Santos Jr." vs. "Juan Santos"
- Punctuation: "Jr." vs. "Jr"
- Spacing: "Santos,Jr." vs. "Santos, Jr."

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "If two names refer to the same person, they are 'the same' for comparison purposes."
>
> **Why it fails:** "Ma. Teresa Dela Cruz" and "Maria Teresa De La Cruz" clearly refer to the same person, but they are NOT identical strings. Name comparison tests exact character matching — not identity verification. Every space, period, and letter must match exactly.
>
> **Correct model:** Two names are "the same" ONLY if every character — including spaces, periods, capitalization, and punctuation — is identical in both versions.

### 4.3 Systematic Comparison Techniques

#### The Left-to-Right Sweep

The most reliable method: start at the first character of both names and move right, comparing each position.

```
Name A:  R - E - Y - E - S
Name B:  R - E - Y - S - E
         ✓   ✓   ✓   ✗ (mismatch at position 4)
```

Stop as soon as you find a difference. You do not need to identify all differences — one is enough to mark the pair as "not identical."

#### The Chunk Method

For longer names, break them into chunks of 3-4 characters and compare chunk by chunk:

```
Name A:  VIL | LAN | UEV | A
Name B:  VIL | LAN | UEV | A
          ✓     ✓     ✓    ✓  → Identical
```

```
Name A:  VIL | LAN | UEV | A
Name B:  VIL | ANE | UVA | —
          ✓     ✗  (stop here)
```

#### The Anchor Method

For names with multiple parts (first name, middle name, surname), compare each part separately:

```
Name A:  [Juan] [Pedro] [Santos]
Name B:  [Juan] [Pedro] [Santso]
          ✓       ✓       ✗ (transposition in surname)
```

This prevents your eye from "sliding" across word boundaries and missing errors.

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Breaking names into segments exploits how working memory functions. Humans can reliably hold and compare 3-4 characters at a time. By chunking a 15-character surname into groups of 3-4, you keep each comparison within working memory capacity — reducing the chance that a discrepancy slips past while your attention is split across too many characters simultaneously.

### 4.4 Visually Confusing Letter Pairs

Certain letter combinations are designed to trick the eye. Memorize these high-risk pairs:

| Pair | Why It's Confusing | Example |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| rn / m | "rn" looks like "m" in many fonts | Hernandez vs. Hemandez |
| l / I | Lowercase L vs. uppercase I | Ilagan vs. llagan |
| cl / d | "cl" can resemble "d" | Clemente vs. demente |
| vv / w | Double-v resembles w | — |
| O / 0 | Letter O vs. digit zero | — |
| S / 5 | Letter S vs. digit five | — |
| b / d | Mirror images | Bautista vs. Dautista |
| p / q | Mirror images | — |
| n / u | Inverted shapes | Nunez vs. Nuuez |
| c / e | Similar curves | Pacete vs. Pacece |

**Strategy:** When you encounter these letters in a name, slow down and verify each one individually.

### 4.5 Spacing and Punctuation Rules

In name comparison, spaces and punctuation are characters. They must match exactly.

**Spacing differences that make names NOT identical:**

| Name A | Name B | Identical? |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Dela Cruz | De La Cruz | No — different spacing |
| De Leon | DeLeon | No — space vs. no space |
| San Juan | SanJuan | No — space vs. no space |
| Ma. Teresa | Ma.Teresa | No — space after period |

**Punctuation differences:**

| Name A | Name B | Identical? |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Santos, Jr. | Santos Jr. | No — comma present vs. absent |
| Ma. Cruz | Ma Cruz | No — period present vs. absent |
| O'Brien | OBrien | No — apostrophe present vs. absent |
| Santos-Reyes | Santos Reyes | No — hyphen vs. space |

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Treating spaces and punctuation as characters works because in official records, these marks carry legal significance. "Dela Cruz" and "De La Cruz" may appear on different government documents for the same person — and a clerk must flag this discrepancy for correction rather than assuming they are equivalent. The comparison task trains exactly this skill: detecting ANY difference, no matter how minor it appears.

### 4.6 Capitalization in Name Comparison

Unless a question explicitly states "ignore capitalization," treat uppercase and lowercase as different characters.

| Name A | Name B | Identical? |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| SANTOS | Santos | No |
| dela Cruz | Dela Cruz | No |
| MacArthur | Macarthur | No |
| DE LEON | De Leon | No |

**CSE convention:** Most name comparison questions on the CSE present names in the same case format (both in title case, or both in all caps). If both names are in the same format, capitalization is not the trap — look for other differences. But if the question presents mixed cases, capitalization differences count.

### 4.7 Common Filipino Surname Traps

These surnames are frequently confused on the CSE because they look or sound similar:

| Surname A | Surname B | Difference |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Gonzales | Gonzalez | s vs. z |
| Reyes | Reyes | (identical — control pair) |
| Villanueva | Vilanueva | ll vs. l |
| Hernandez | Hernandes | z vs. s |
| Rodriguez | Rodrigues | z vs. s |
| Bautista | Bautsita | ti vs. it (transposition) |
| Aquino | Aqunio | in vs. ni (transposition) |
| Mendoza | Mendosa | z vs. s |
| Enriquez | Enriques | z vs. s |
| Manalo | Manallo | l vs. ll |
| Pangilinan | Pangilnan | ili vs. il (omission) |
| Tolentino | Tolenteno | i vs. e |

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "If the names sound the same when spoken aloud, they must be identical."
>
> **Why it fails:** "Gonzales" and "Gonzalez" are pronounced identically in Filipino English, but they differ at the final letter. Similarly, "Villanueva" and "Vilanueva" sound the same but differ in the number of L's. Phonetic equivalence does not equal character-level identity.
>
> **Correct model:** Ignore pronunciation entirely. Compare only what is written — letter by letter, character by character. Two names are identical only if every written character matches.

### 4.8 Multi-Part Name Comparison

Full Filipino names on government forms typically follow this structure:

**Surname, First Name Middle Name/Initial Suffix**

Example: `DELA CRUZ, MARIA TERESA SANTOS JR.`

When comparing full names, check each component:

1. **Surname** — including particles (De, Dela, Delos, Del, San, etc.)
2. **First name** — including abbreviations (Ma., Jose, etc.)
3. **Middle name or initial** — presence/absence and spelling
4. **Suffix** — Jr., Sr., II, III (presence, punctuation, spacing)

**Example comparison:**

```
Name A: DELA CRUZ, JUAN PEDRO REYES JR.
Name B: DELA CRUZ, JUAN PEDRO REYES JR.
→ Identical ✓
```

```
Name A: DELA CRUZ, JUAN PEDRO REYES JR.
Name B: DE LA CRUZ, JUAN PEDRO REYES JR.
→ NOT identical (spacing in surname differs)
```

```
Name A: SANTOS, MA. TERESA VILLANUEVA
Name B: SANTOS, MA. TERESA VILANUEVA
→ NOT identical (double-L vs. single-L in middle name)
```

### Check Your Understanding

**1.** Are "Gonzales" and "Gonzalez" identical? → **No** (final letter: s vs. z)

**2.** Are "Dela Cruz" and "De La Cruz" identical? → **No** (spacing differs)

**3.** Are "Ma. Santos" and "Ma Santos" identical? → **No** (period present vs. absent)

**4.** What type of error is "Reyes" → "Reyse"? → **Transposition** (e and s are swapped)

**5.** Are "VILLANUEVA" and "VILLANUEVA" identical? → **Yes** (all characters match)

### 4.9 Government Form Context

In actual civil service work, name comparison occurs when verifying data across official documents. Understanding where these comparisons happen helps you appreciate why the CSE tests this skill:

**Common verification scenarios:**

| Source Document | Comparison Document | What to Check |
|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| CS Form 212 (Personal Data Sheet) | Government-issued ID | Full name spelling |
| Payroll register | DTR (Daily Time Record) | Employee name |
| Disbursement voucher | Official receipt | Payee name |
| SALN (Statement of Assets) | Employee 201 file | Declarant name |
| Leave application | Service record | Applicant name |

**Philippine government form conventions:**

- Names on CS Form 212 follow: SURNAME, FIRST NAME MIDDLE NAME (all caps)
- Extension names (Jr., Sr., III) appear after the surname: SANTOS JR., JUAN PEDRO
- Married women may use maiden or married surname — both must be verified
- "Ñ" (enye) is a distinct character: MUÑOZ ≠ MUNOZ
- Hyphenated surnames indicate compound names: SANTOS-REYES is one surname unit

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Government forms use standardized ALL-CAPS formatting precisely because it eliminates one variable (capitalization) from the comparison task. When both documents use the same format, clerks can focus entirely on character-level accuracy without being distracted by case differences. This is why CSE questions typically present both names in the same case — it mirrors real workplace conditions.

### 4.10 Speed vs. Accuracy Tradeoff

The CSE is time-pressured: 165 items in 2 hours 40 minutes (approximately 58 seconds per item). Name comparison items should take 20-40 seconds each. Here's how to manage the tradeoff:

**Speed techniques that maintain accuracy:**

1. **First-letter scan:** If the first letters differ, you're done immediately.
2. **Length check:** Quickly estimate if both names are the same length. Different lengths = not identical.
3. **Ending check:** After scanning left-to-right, do a quick right-to-left check of the last 2-3 characters (catches s/z substitutions).
4. **Particle shortcut:** If you see "Dela" in one and "De La" in the other, stop — they're different.
5. **Double-letter flag:** When you see ll, rr, nn, ss — immediately verify the other name has the same double.

**When to guess and move on:**

- If you've spent 45+ seconds and cannot find a difference, mark "identical" and move on.
- If you've found one difference, mark "not identical" immediately — do not look for additional differences.
- Never re-read a pair you've already compared. Trust your systematic method.

### 4.11 CSE Question Formats for Name Comparison

The CSE presents name comparison in several formats. Knowing what to expect eliminates surprise on exam day:

**Format 1: Simple Pair Comparison**
```
Directions: Compare the two names. Mark A if they are EXACTLY alike,
mark B if they are NOT exactly alike.

1. VILLANUEVA, ROBERTO C.    VILLANUEVA, ROBERTO C.
2. GONZALES, MARIA TERESA    GONZALEZ, MARIA TERESA
```

**Format 2: Find the Identical Pair**
```
Directions: Which pair of names is EXACTLY alike?

A. HERNANDEZ, JOSE P.    HEMANDEZ, JOSE P.
B. BAUTISTA, ANNA MARIE  BAUTISTA, ANNA MARIE
C. DELA CRUZ, PEDRO      DE LA CRUZ, PEDRO
D. SANTOS, MA. LOURDES   SANTOS, MA LOURDES
```

**Format 3: Count Identical/Different Pairs**
```
Directions: How many of the following pairs are EXACTLY alike?

1. REYES, JUAN CARLOS     REYES, JUAN CARLOS
2. MENDOZA, ELENA P.      MENDOZA, ELENA P
3. AQUINO, ROBERTO JR.    AQUINO, ROBERTO JR.
4. PANGILINAN, SOFIA      PANGILNAN, SOFIA
5. TORRES, ANTONIO M.     TORRES, ANTONIO M.
```

**Format 4: Identify the Error Type**
```
Directions: What type of difference exists between the two names?

BAUTISTA, FRANCISCO    BAUTSITA, FRANCISCO

A. One letter is substituted
B. Two letters are transposed
C. One letter is omitted
D. One letter is added
```

**Key insight:** Regardless of format, the underlying skill is the same — character-by-character comparison. The format only changes what you report, not how you compare.

### Exam Strategies

- Use your finger or pencil tip to track position as you compare — physical anchoring prevents your eye from jumping ahead.
- Start with the surname (last name) since it is usually the longest component and most likely to contain the trap.
- If both names look identical at first glance, slow down — the question would not be asked if there were no trap. Look harder.
- For "which pair is identical" questions, use elimination: find the difference in each pair until you find the one with no difference.
- Count the total characters in each name — if the counts differ, one has an omission or addition error.
- Pay special attention to double letters (ll, rr, ss, nn) — one may be single.
- Check particles and suffixes last — they are common trap locations.
- On the CSE, you have roughly 1 minute per item. Spend no more than 30-40 seconds per name comparison — if you cannot find a difference in that time, mark them as identical and move on.
- Trust your systematic method over your "feeling" about whether names match.

### Memory Aids

#### The STOP Method for Name Comparison

**S** — Spacing: Check all spaces match exactly
**T** — Transpositions: Look for swapped adjacent letters
**O** — Omissions/Additions: Count characters if unsure
**P** — Punctuation: Verify periods, commas, hyphens, apostrophes

#### High-Risk Letter Pairs Mnemonic: "RN Makes M"

Remember that "rn" visually resembles "m" — this is the single most common visual trap in name comparison. When you see an "m" in a name, verify it is not actually "rn," and vice versa.

#### The "Double Letter" Check

Whenever you see a double letter (ll, rr, ss, nn, tt, cc), immediately verify the other name also has the double. This catches:
- Villanueva vs. Vilanueva
- Guerrero vs. Guerero
- Tolentino vs. Tollentino

#### Filipino Surname Ending Pairs: "S vs. Z"

Many Filipino-Spanish surnames have variants ending in either -es or -ez:
- Gonzales / Gonzalez
- Hernandez / Hernandes
- Rodriguez / Rodrigues
- Enriquez / Enriques
- Mendoza / Mendosa (a vs. z)

Always check the final two characters of surnames ending in a sibilant sound.

### Guided Practice

#### Problem 1

Compare the following pair. Are they identical?

Name A: `VILLANUEVA, ROBERTO CRUZ`
Name B: `VILLANUEVA, ROBERTO CRUZ`

**Step 1:** Compare surnames.
- V-I-L-L-A-N-U-E-V-A = V-I-L-L-A-N-U-E-V-A ✓

**Step 2:** Compare first names.
- R-O-B-E-R-T-O = R-O-B-E-R-T-O ✓

**Step 3:** Compare middle names.
- C-R-U-Z = C-R-U-Z ✓

**Answer:** Identical ✓

#### Problem 2

Compare the following pair. Are they identical?

Name A: `GONZALES, MARIA TERESA REYES`
Name B: `GONZALEZ, MARIA TERESA REYES`

**Step 1:** Compare surnames.
- G-O-N-Z-A-L-E-**S** vs. G-O-N-Z-A-L-E-**Z**
- Position 8: S vs. Z ✗

**Answer:** NOT identical — substitution error in surname (s → z)

#### Problem 3

Compare the following pair. Are they identical?

Name A: `DELA CRUZ, JUAN PEDRO`
Name B: `DE LA CRUZ, JUAN PEDRO`

**Step 1:** Compare surnames character by character.
- D-E-L-A-[space]-C vs. D-E-[space]-L-A-[space]-C
- Position 3: L vs. space ✗

**Answer:** NOT identical — spacing difference in surname particle

#### Problem 4

Compare the following pair. Are they identical?

Name A: `HERNANDEZ, MA. LOURDES SANTOS`
Name B: `HERNANDEZ, MA. LOURDES SANTSO`

**Step 1:** Compare surnames.
- H-E-R-N-A-N-D-E-Z = H-E-R-N-A-N-D-E-Z ✓

**Step 2:** Compare first names.
- MA. LOURDES = MA. LOURDES ✓

**Step 3:** Compare middle names.
- S-A-N-T-**O-S** vs. S-A-N-T-**S-O**
- Position 5-6: OS vs. SO ✗

**Answer:** NOT identical — transposition error in middle name (os → so)

#### Problem 5

Which pair below is identical?

A. AQUINO, BENIGNO COJUANGCO / AQUINO, BENIGNO COJUANGCO
B. PANGILINAN, FRANCIS TOLENTINO / PANGILNAN, FRANCIS TOLENTINO
C. VILLANUEVA, ANTONIO GARCIA / VILANUEVA, ANTONIO GARCIA

**Step 1:** Check pair A.
- AQUINO = AQUINO ✓, BENIGNO = BENIGNO ✓, COJUANGCO = COJUANGCO ✓ → Identical

**Step 2:** Verify by checking others for differences.
- Pair B: PANGILINAN vs. PANGILNAN (missing "I" — omission) ✗
- Pair C: VILLANUEVA vs. VILANUEVA (double-L vs. single-L) ✗
- Pair D: MENDOZA vs. MENDOSA (z vs. s) ✗

**Answer:** A is the identical pair.

### Which Method?

Identify the type of discrepancy in each pair.

#### 1.

Name A: `BAUTISTA` / Name B: `BAUTSITA`

Type: **Transposition** — "TI" swapped to "SI" at positions 5-6. Wait — actually positions 5-6: T-I vs. T-S? Let's recheck: B-A-U-T-I-S-T-A vs. B-A-U-T-S-I-T-A. Positions 5-6: I-S vs. S-I. **Transposition** of "IS" to "SI."

#### 2.

Name A: `VILLANUEVA` / Name B: `VILANUEVA`

Type: **Omission** — one "L" is missing from the second name.

#### 3.

Name A: `GONZALES` / Name B: `GONZALEZ`

Type: **Substitution** — final letter "S" replaced by "Z."

#### 4.

Name A: `DELA CRUZ` / Name B: `DE LA CRUZ`

Type: **Addition/Spacing** — a space is inserted between "DE" and "LA" in the second version.

#### 5.

Name A: `MA. TERESA` / Name B: `MA TERESA`

Type: **Omission** — the period after "MA" is missing in the second version.

#### 6.

Name A: `HERNANDEZ` / Name B: `HEMANDEZ`

Type: **Substitution** — "RN" replaced by "M" (visual confusion: rn ≈ m).

## Key Takeaways

- Name comparison tests exact character matching — not whether two names refer to the same person.
- The four error types are substitution, transposition, omission, and addition.
- Filipino name particles (Dela, De La, Delos, Del, San) create spacing traps — treat spaces as characters.
- Punctuation matters: "Ma." ≠ "Ma", "Jr." ≠ "Jr", "Santos-Reyes" ≠ "Santos Reyes."
- Visually confusing pairs (rn/m, l/I, n/u) require deliberate character-level verification.
- Use the STOP method: Spacing, Transpositions, Omissions, Punctuation.
- Compare systematically (left-to-right, chunk, or anchor method) — never rely on visual impression.
- Filipino-Spanish surnames often have s/z variants (Gonzales/Gonzalez) — always check final letters.
- Double letters (ll, rr, ss) are common omission trap locations.
- Under time pressure, stop as soon as you find ONE difference — you do not need to find all differences.
- The "rn" vs. "m" visual trap is the single most exploited confusion in name comparison items.
- Generational suffixes (Jr., Sr., II, III) and their punctuation are frequent trap locations.
- Length differences between two names guarantee they are not identical — use this as a quick first check.
- On the CSE, approximately 50% of name comparison pairs will be identical — do not assume every pair has an error.

## Summary

Name comparison is a perceptual accuracy skill tested in the Clerical Ability section of the Philippine Civil Service Examination (Sub-Professional level). The task requires examinees to determine whether two written versions of a name are character-for-character identical, detecting discrepancies as subtle as a single letter substitution, a transposed pair of characters, a missing period, or a spacing difference in a surname particle.

The challenge lies not in intellectual complexity but in overcoming the brain's natural tendency to read holistically and autocorrect minor differences. Filipino names present unique challenges: particles like "Dela" vs. "De La," abbreviations like "Ma." vs. "Ma," surname variants like "Gonzales" vs. "Gonzalez," and visually confusing letter combinations like "rn" vs. "m." Systematic comparison techniques — the left-to-right sweep, chunk method, and anchor method — provide reliable frameworks for catching these discrepancies.

Success in name comparison requires disciplined, mechanical scanning rather than speed-reading. The STOP mnemonic (Spacing, Transpositions, Omissions, Punctuation) provides a quick mental checklist for the most common error types. With practice, examinees develop the perceptual precision needed to process name verification tasks accurately within the CSE's time constraints. Remember: in clerical work, a single mismatched character can mean the difference between a processed transaction and a rejected one — accuracy is not optional, it is the job.
