# Proportion Word Problems

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Proportion word problems** are mathematical exercises where two equivalent ratios are described in everyday language, and you must translate those verbal descriptions into a proportion equation to find a missing value. Unlike basic ratio problems that simply ask you to express a comparison, proportion word problems require you to *recognize* that two ratios are equal, *set up* the proportion correctly, and *solve* for an unknown quantity using cross multiplication or equivalent-ratio reasoning.

These problems appear extensively in the Philippine Civil Service Examination because they model real-world tasks that government employees encounter daily:

- **Budgeting:** "If ₱15,000 covers supplies for 3 months, how much is needed for 8 months?"
- **Workforce planning:** "If 6 workers finish a project in 10 days, how many workers are needed to finish it in 4 days?"
- **Transportation:** "A vehicle travels 180 km in 3 hours. At the same speed, how far will it travel in 5 hours?"
- **Procurement:** "If 12 reams of paper cost ₱3,600, how much do 20 reams cost?"
- **Recipe scaling:** "A recipe for 4 servings uses 3 cups of flour. How much flour is needed for 10 servings?"
- **Map reading:** "On a map where 1 cm = 25 km, two cities are 7.2 cm apart. What is the actual distance?"
- **Construction:** "If 8 bags of cement cover 24 square meters, how many bags are needed for 60 square meters?"
- **Population studies:** "If 3 out of every 50 applicants pass the exam, how many pass out of 800 applicants?"

#### Why Proportion Word Problems Commonly Appear in the CSE

The Civil Service Exam tests your ability to:
1. **Interpret written information accurately** — translating verbal statements into mathematical relationships
2. **Identify proportional relationships** — recognizing when two quantities change together (directly or inversely)
3. **Solve systematically** — applying cross multiplication and algebraic reasoning under time pressure
4. **Distinguish direct from inverse relationships** — a critical reasoning skill for resource allocation and planning
5. **Apply mathematics to practical scenarios** — budgeting, scheduling, procurement, and logistics

#### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Confusing direct and inverse proportion** — using direct proportion setup when the relationship is inverse (or vice versa)
2. **Reversing the proportion** — placing values in wrong positions so cross multiplication yields an incorrect answer
3. **Arithmetic errors in cross multiplication** — especially with large numbers under time pressure
4. **Ignoring units** — mixing hours with minutes, or meters with kilometers, leading to nonsensical answers
5. **Setting up only one ratio** — writing a single fraction instead of an equation with two equal ratios
6. **Forgetting to simplify** — arriving at a correct but unsimplified answer that doesn't match any choice
7. **Misidentifying what is being asked** — solving for the wrong unknown variable
8. **Skipping verification** — not checking whether the answer is logically reasonable

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Translate word statements into proportions correctly
- Identify direct and inverse proportional relationships
- Solve missing values using cross multiplication
- Apply cross multiplication accurately and efficiently
- Solve multi-step proportion problems systematically
- Interpret real-life proportional situations in workplace, finance, and transportation contexts
- Solve CSE-style proportion word problems confidently under time pressure

### 4.1 Understanding Proportion Word Problems
> 🤔 **Why does this work?** The principle behind this operation follows from the fundamental properties of arithmetic. Understanding the "why" — not just the "how" — lets you recognize when to apply this method in unfamiliar problem contexts on the CSE.


#### What Are Proportion Word Problems?

A **proportion** is a statement that two ratios are equal:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$$

A **proportion word problem** presents a real-world scenario where:
1. A known relationship exists between two quantities (expressed as a ratio)
2. You must find an unknown value that maintains the same relationship

The key insight: if two situations share the same rate, ratio, or relationship, you can set up a proportion and solve for the missing piece.

#### Converting Verbal Information into Mathematical Relationships

Every proportion word problem contains clues that signal a proportional relationship:

| Signal Phrase | What It Tells You | Example |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| "at the same rate" | Direct proportion | "At the same rate, how many...?" |
| "if...then how much/many" | Proportional relationship exists | "If 5 cost ₱200, how much do 12 cost?" |
| "for every" | Constant ratio | "For every 3 defective items, there are 97 good ones" |
| "per" | Unit rate | "₱45 per kilogram" |
| "in proportion to" | Direct proportion | "Pay is in proportion to hours worked" |
| "varies directly" | Direct proportion | "Distance varies directly with time" |
| "varies inversely" | Inverse proportion | "Speed varies inversely with time" |
| "at this pace/speed" | Constant rate | "At this pace, how long to finish?" |

#### Identifying Known and Unknown Values

Before writing any proportion, organize the information:

**Step 1:** Identify the two pairs of related quantities.
**Step 2:** Determine which value is unknown (assign it as *x*).
**Step 3:** Arrange the ratios so corresponding quantities are in the same position.

**Example:** "5 notebooks cost ₱100. How much do 8 notebooks cost?"

- Pair 1: 5 notebooks → ₱100
- Pair 2: 8 notebooks → ₱x
- Proportion: 5/100 = 8/x (notebooks over cost, both sides)


> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "The formula always works the same way regardless of the problem context."

> **Why it fails:** CSE problems often present variations where the standard formula must be adapted. Blindly applying a memorized formula without checking the context leads to systematic errors.

> **Correct model:** Always read the problem to identify what type of relationship exists (direct, inverse, part-whole, etc.), then apply the appropriate formula. Verify your answer makes sense in the problem's context before selecting it.

### 4.2 Translating Word Statements into Proportions

#### Setting Up Equivalent Ratios

The most critical skill in proportion word problems is correctly translating words into two equal fractions. The golden rule:

> **Keep the same type of quantity in the same position on both sides of the equation.**

**Correct setup:**
$$\frac{\text{notebooks}_1}{\text{cost}_1} = \frac{\text{notebooks}_2}{\text{cost}_2}$$

**Incorrect setup (reversed):**
$$\frac{\text{notebooks}_1}{\text{cost}_1} = \frac{\text{cost}_2}{\text{notebooks}_2}$$

#### Identifying Corresponding Quantities

When you read a word problem, group the information into two scenarios:

| | Scenario 1 (Known) | Scenario 2 (Has Unknown) |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity A | value given | value given or unknown |
| Quantity B | value given | value given or unknown |

**Example:** "A car travels 120 km in 2 hours. At the same speed, how far will it travel in 5 hours?"

| | Scenario 1 | Scenario 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | 120 km | x km |
| Time | 2 hours | 5 hours |

Proportion: 120/2 = x/5

#### Importance of Ratio Order

The order matters. Consider this problem: "If 3 painters can paint a room in 12 hours, how long will it take 6 painters?"

If you set up: 3/12 = 6/x → Cross multiply: 3x = 72 → x = 24 hours

But wait — more painters should mean *less* time. The answer of 24 hours is wrong because this is an **inverse proportion**, not a direct one. You need: 3 × 12 = 6 × x → x = 6 hours.

**Key lesson:** Before setting up the proportion, ask yourself: "When one quantity increases, does the other increase (direct) or decrease (inverse)?"

#### Practice: Translating Statements

| Word Statement | Proportion Setup |
|---|---|
| "5 kg of rice costs ₱250. How much do 8 kg cost?" | 5/250 = 8/x |
| "A machine produces 200 items in 4 hours. How many in 10 hours?" | 200/4 = x/10 |
| "If 15 workers finish in 6 days, how many days for 10 workers?" | 15 × 6 = 10 × x (inverse) |
| "On a map, 2 cm = 50 km. What distance does 7 cm represent?" | 2/50 = 7/x |
| "A recipe for 6 people uses 4 cups of flour. How much for 15 people?" | 6/4 = 15/x |


> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "If my computed answer is close to one of the choices, it must be right."

> **Why it fails:** The CSE deliberately includes distractors that result from common errors — using the wrong operation, misidentifying the proportion type, or reversing the ratio. A "close" answer could be the result of a systematic mistake that the test writers anticipated.

> **Correct model:** Verify your setup before computing. Check that you've identified the correct proportion type, set up the equation properly, and solved accurately. A wrong setup with correct arithmetic still produces a wrong answer — and the CSE will include that wrong answer among the choices.

### 4.3 Direct Proportion Word Problems
> 🤔 **Why does this work?** When you follow this procedure, you're exploiting a mathematical invariant — something that stays constant regardless of how you manipulate the numbers. Identifying that invariant is the key to solving problems efficiently rather than memorizing steps.


#### Recognizing Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when:
- As one increases, the other increases at the same rate
- As one decreases, the other decreases at the same rate
- Their ratio remains constant

**Mathematical form:**
$$\frac{x_1}{y_1} = \frac{x_2}{y_2}$$

#### Keywords That Signal Direct Proportion

- "at the same rate"
- "at the same price per unit"
- "at the same speed"
- "proportional to"
- "for every" (in most contexts)
- "per unit" (constant rate)

#### Real-Life Direct Proportion Scenarios

| Scenario | Relationship |
|----------|-------------|
| More items purchased → higher total cost | Items ∝ Cost |
| More hours worked → more distance traveled | Time ∝ Distance (constant speed) |
| More ingredients → more servings | Ingredients ∝ Servings |
| More hours worked → more salary earned | Hours ∝ Salary (hourly rate) |
| More fuel consumed → more distance covered | Fuel ∝ Distance (constant efficiency) |

#### Solving Direct Proportion Problems: Step-by-Step

**Problem:** "If 8 meters of cloth cost ₱1,200, how much will 14 meters cost at the same rate?"

**Step 1:** Identify the relationship. More cloth → more cost. This is direct proportion.

**Step 2:** Set up the proportion.
$$\frac{8}{1200} = \frac{14}{x}$$

**Step 3:** Cross multiply.
$$8x = 14 \times 1200$$
$$8x = 16{,}800$$

**Step 4:** Solve for x.
$$x = \frac{16{,}800}{8} = 2{,}100$$

**Step 5:** Verify. 14 meters is more than 8 meters, so the cost (₱2,100) should be more than ₱1,200. ✓

**Answer:** 14 meters of cloth cost **₱2,100**.

---

**Problem:** "A government office processes 45 applications in 3 days. At the same rate, how many applications can it process in 7 days?"

**Step 1:** More days → more applications processed. Direct proportion.

**Step 2:** Set up:
$$\frac{45}{3} = \frac{x}{7}$$

**Step 3:** Cross multiply:
$$3x = 45 \times 7 = 315$$

**Step 4:** Solve:
$$x = \frac{315}{3} = 105$$

**Step 5:** Verify. 7 days is more than 3 days, so more applications (105 > 45). ✓

**Answer:** The office can process **105 applications** in 7 days.

---

**Problem:** "Maria earns ₱4,500 for 5 days of work. How much will she earn in 22 working days?"

**Step 1:** More days → more earnings. Direct proportion.

**Step 2:** 
$$\frac{4500}{5} = \frac{x}{22}$$

**Step 3:** 
$$5x = 4500 \times 22 = 99{,}000$$

**Step 4:** 
$$x = \frac{99{,}000}{5} = 19{,}800$$

**Answer:** Maria earns **₱19,800** in 22 working days.

### 4.4 Inverse Proportion Word Problems

#### Recognizing Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when:
- As one increases, the other decreases
- As one decreases, the other increases
- Their **product** remains constant

**Mathematical form:**
$$x_1 \times y_1 = x_2 \times y_2$$

Or equivalently:
$$\frac{x_1}{x_2} = \frac{y_2}{y_1}$$

Notice the "flip" — in inverse proportion, the second ratio is inverted compared to direct proportion.

#### Keywords That Signal Inverse Proportion

- "how many workers to finish faster"
- "if speed increases, time decreases"
- "fewer machines, more days"
- "shared among more people" (each gets less)
- "working together"
- "at a faster/slower rate"

#### Real-Life Inverse Proportion Scenarios

| Scenario | Relationship |
|----------|-------------|
| More workers → fewer days to finish | Workers × Days = constant |
| Higher speed → less travel time | Speed × Time = constant distance |
| More pipes filling a tank → less time to fill | Pipes × Time = constant |
| More people sharing food → less per person | People × Share = constant total |
| Wider pipe → lower water pressure needed | Width × Pressure = constant flow |

#### Solving Inverse Proportion Problems: Step-by-Step

**Problem:** "If 6 workers can complete a project in 12 days, how many days will it take 9 workers to complete the same project?"

**Step 1:** Identify the relationship. More workers → fewer days. This is inverse proportion.

**Step 2:** Use the constant product formula:
$$6 \times 12 = 9 \times x$$

**Step 3:** Solve:
$$72 = 9x$$
$$x = \frac{72}{9} = 8$$

**Step 4:** Verify. 9 workers is more than 6 workers, so fewer days (8 < 12). ✓

**Answer:** 9 workers will complete the project in **8 days**.

---

**Problem:** "A car traveling at 60 km/h takes 4 hours to reach a destination. How long will it take at 80 km/h?"

**Step 1:** Higher speed → less time. Inverse proportion.

**Step 2:**
$$60 \times 4 = 80 \times x$$

**Step 3:**
$$240 = 80x$$
$$x = \frac{240}{80} = 3$$

**Step 4:** Verify. 80 km/h is faster than 60 km/h, so less time (3 < 4). ✓

**Answer:** At 80 km/h, the trip takes **3 hours**.

---

**Problem:** "A supply of food is enough for 15 people for 8 days. If 5 more people join, how many days will the food last?"

**Step 1:** More people → food lasts fewer days. Inverse proportion.

**Step 2:** Total people = 15 + 5 = 20
$$15 \times 8 = 20 \times x$$

**Step 3:**
$$120 = 20x$$
$$x = \frac{120}{20} = 6$$

**Answer:** The food will last **6 days** for 20 people.

### 4.5 Solving Missing Values in Proportions
> 🤔 **Why does this work?** This shortcut works because it's a special case of the more general rule. By understanding the underlying principle, you can verify your answer logically even if you forget the exact formula under exam pressure.


#### The Cross Multiplication Method

For any proportion $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, cross multiplication gives:
$$a \times d = b \times c$$

This is the primary tool for solving proportion equations.

#### Finding Missing Numerators

**Problem:** $\frac{x}{15} = \frac{12}{20}$

Cross multiply: $20x = 15 \times 12 = 180$

Solve: $x = \frac{180}{20} = 9$

#### Finding Missing Denominators

**Problem:** $\frac{7}{x} = \frac{21}{36}$

Cross multiply: $7 \times 36 = 21x$

$252 = 21x$

$x = \frac{252}{21} = 12$

#### Multi-Variable Setups

Sometimes you need to find an intermediate value first:

**Problem:** "If 4 machines produce 200 units in 5 hours, how many units will 6 machines produce in 8 hours?"

**Step 1:** Find production rate per machine per hour:
- 4 machines × 5 hours = 200 units
- 1 machine in 1 hour = 200 ÷ (4 × 5) = 10 units

**Step 2:** Calculate for new scenario:
- 6 machines × 8 hours × 10 units = 480 units

**Answer:** 6 machines produce **480 units** in 8 hours.

### 4.6 Multi-Step Proportion Word Problems

#### What Makes a Problem "Multi-Step"?

Multi-step proportion problems require:
- More than one proportion calculation, OR
- Combining proportion with another operation (addition, subtraction, percentage), OR
- Extracting intermediate values before reaching the final answer

#### Solving Strategy for Multi-Step Problems

1. **Read the entire problem** before starting calculations
2. **Identify all given information** and what is being asked
3. **Determine intermediate steps** — what do you need to find first?
4. **Solve each step sequentially** — use the result of one step as input for the next
5. **Verify the final answer** — does it make logical sense?

#### Example: Combined Ratios

**Problem:** "A construction project requires cement, sand, and gravel in the ratio 1:3:5. If 90 bags of sand are needed, how many total bags of material are required?"

**Step 1:** Find the value of one ratio unit.
- Sand = 3 parts = 90 bags
- 1 part = 90 ÷ 3 = 30 bags

**Step 2:** Find each material.
- Cement = 1 × 30 = 30 bags
- Sand = 3 × 30 = 90 bags
- Gravel = 5 × 30 = 150 bags

**Step 3:** Find total.
- Total = 30 + 90 + 150 = 270 bags

**Answer:** **270 total bags** of material are required.

---

#### Example: Scaling with Constraints

**Problem:** "A government office has a budget of ₱500,000. Personnel costs and operational costs are in the ratio 3:2. If personnel costs increase by 20%, what is the new total budget needed to maintain the same operational costs?"

**Step 1:** Find original allocation.
- Total parts = 3 + 2 = 5
- Personnel = (3/5) × 500,000 = ₱300,000
- Operational = (2/5) × 500,000 = ₱200,000

**Step 2:** Calculate increased personnel cost.
- New personnel = 300,000 × 1.20 = ₱360,000

**Step 3:** New total budget.
- New total = 360,000 + 200,000 = ₱560,000

**Answer:** The new total budget needed is **₱560,000**.

---

#### Example: Map Application

**Problem:** "On a map, 3 cm represents 75 km. Two cities are 8.4 cm apart on the map. A bus travels at 60 km/h. How long will the trip take?"

**Step 1:** Find actual distance using proportion.
$$\frac{3}{75} = \frac{8.4}{x}$$
$$3x = 75 \times 8.4 = 630$$
$$x = 210 \text{ km}$$

**Step 2:** Find travel time.
$$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} = \frac{210}{60} = 3.5 \text{ hours}$$

**Answer:** The trip takes **3.5 hours** (3 hours and 30 minutes).

### 4.8 Real-Life Applications of Proportions

#### Recipes and Cooking

"A recipe for 4 servings uses 2.5 cups of flour. How much flour for 10 servings?"
$$\frac{4}{2.5} = \frac{10}{x} \Rightarrow 4x = 25 \Rightarrow x = 6.25 \text{ cups}$$

#### Salary Computations

"An employee earns ₱18,000 for 15 days. How much for 22 days?"
$$\frac{18000}{15} = \frac{x}{22} \Rightarrow 15x = 396{,}000 \Rightarrow x = ₱26{,}400$$

#### Transportation Systems

"A bus covers 240 km in 4 hours. At the same speed, how far in 7 hours?"
$$\frac{240}{4} = \frac{x}{7} \Rightarrow 4x = 1680 \Rightarrow x = 420 \text{ km}$$

#### Fuel Consumption

"A car uses 12 liters of fuel for 180 km. How much fuel for 300 km?"
$$\frac{12}{180} = \frac{x}{300} \Rightarrow 180x = 3600 \Rightarrow x = 20 \text{ liters}$$

#### Map Scales

"Scale: 1 cm = 40 km. Distance on map: 5.5 cm. Actual distance?"
$$\frac{1}{40} = \frac{5.5}{x} \Rightarrow x = 220 \text{ km}$$

#### Engineering Measurements

"A blueprint uses a scale of 1:200. A wall measures 4.5 cm on the blueprint. Actual length?"
$$4.5 \times 200 = 900 \text{ cm} = 9 \text{ meters}$$

#### Construction Planning

"If 5 workers build a wall in 8 days, how many days for 4 workers?" (Inverse)
$$5 \times 8 = 4 \times x \Rightarrow x = 10 \text{ days}$$

#### Budgeting

"A department spends ₱45,000 for 3 months of supplies. Budget needed for 12 months?"
$$\frac{45000}{3} = \frac{x}{12} \Rightarrow 3x = 540{,}000 \Rightarrow x = ₱180{,}000$$

#### Inventory Management

"A warehouse ships 350 units in 2 weeks. At this rate, how many in 5 weeks?"
$$\frac{350}{2} = \frac{x}{5} \Rightarrow 2x = 1750 \Rightarrow x = 875 \text{ units}$$

#### Population Studies

"In a survey, 7 out of 50 residents prefer public transport. In a barangay of 3,000, how many prefer it?"
$$\frac{7}{50} = \frac{x}{3000} \Rightarrow 50x = 21{,}000 \Rightarrow x = 420 \text{ residents}$$

### 4.9 Problem-Solving Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Identify the Relationship First

Before any calculation, determine:
- **Direct proportion?** Both quantities move in the same direction.
- **Inverse proportion?** Quantities move in opposite directions.

Ask: "If I increase the first quantity, does the second quantity go UP or DOWN?"

#### Strategy 2: Organize Information in a Table

| | Known | Unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity A | value | value or x |
| Quantity B | value | value or x |

#### Strategy 3: Set Up the Proportion Carefully

For direct proportion:
$$\frac{A_1}{B_1} = \frac{A_2}{B_2}$$

For inverse proportion:
$$A_1 \times B_1 = A_2 \times B_2$$

#### Strategy 4: Check Unit Consistency

Make sure both sides use the same units. Convert if necessary:
- Hours to minutes (×60)
- Kilometers to meters (×1000)
- Days to hours (×24)

#### Strategy 5: Verify Your Answer

After solving, ask:
- Does the answer make logical sense?
- If more workers, is the time less? (inverse)
- If more items, is the cost more? (direct)
- Is the magnitude reasonable?

### 4.10 Estimation and Mental Math Techniques

#### Recognizing Proportional Patterns Mentally

**Doubling/Halving:** If the multiplier is 2 or 0.5, just double or halve.
- "6 items cost ₱300. How much do 12 cost?" → Just double: ₱600.

**Simple multiples:** If the relationship is a clean multiple, skip cross multiplication.
- "3 hours → 150 km. How far in 9 hours?" → 9 is 3× of 3, so 3 × 150 = 450 km.

**Unit rate method:** Find the value of 1 unit first, then multiply.
- "8 kg costs ₱400. How much for 5 kg?" → 1 kg = ₱50, so 5 kg = ₱250.

#### Estimation Before Solving

Before calculating, estimate the answer range:
- "If 4 workers take 20 days, how many days for 10 workers?"
- 10 workers is 2.5× more than 4 workers
- So days should be about 20 ÷ 2.5 = 8 days
- If your calculated answer is far from 8, recheck your setup.

#### Simplification Shortcuts

**Simplify before cross multiplying** to avoid large numbers:

$$\frac{12}{36} = \frac{x}{15}$$

Simplify left side first: 12/36 = 1/3

Now: 1/3 = x/15 → x = 5

Much easier than: 12 × 15 = 36x → 180 = 36x → x = 5

### 4.11 Common Errors in Proportion Word Problems

#### Error 1: Reversing Ratios

**Wrong:** "5 workers in 10 days. How many days for 8 workers?"
Setting up as 5/10 = 8/x (direct) → x = 16 days ✗

**Correct:** This is inverse. 5 × 10 = 8 × x → x = 6.25 days ✓

#### Error 2: Confusing Direct and Inverse Relationships

**Test:** "If I increase workers, do days increase or decrease?"
- Increase → Direct proportion
- Decrease → Inverse proportion

More workers = less time = INVERSE.

#### Error 3: Incorrect Cross Multiplication

$$\frac{3}{x} = \frac{9}{24}$$

**Wrong:** 3 × 9 = 24x → 27 = 24x → x = 1.125 ✗

**Correct:** 3 × 24 = 9x → 72 = 9x → x = 8 ✓

Remember: Cross multiplication means numerator of one side × denominator of the other.

#### Error 4: Inconsistent Units

"A car travels 90 km in 1.5 hours. How far in 45 minutes?"

**Wrong:** 90/1.5 = x/45 → x = 2,700 km (absurd!)

**Correct:** Convert 45 minutes to 0.75 hours first.
90/1.5 = x/0.75 → 1.5x = 67.5 → x = 45 km ✓

#### Error 5: Arithmetic Mistakes

Always double-check multiplication and division, especially with:
- Decimals (move decimal points carefully)
- Large numbers (break into smaller multiplications)
- Fractions (find common denominators)

#### Error 6: Misunderstanding Wording

"3 out of every 5 students passed" means 3/5, NOT 3/2 or 5/3.

"For every 2 boys there are 3 girls" means boys:girls = 2:3, NOT 3:2.

#### Error 7: Skipping Simplification

If your answer is 150/6, simplify to 25. Exam choices will show the simplified form.

### Step-by-Step Rules

#### Verbal-to-Proportion Conversion Procedure

1. Read the problem completely
2. Identify the two quantities being related
3. Determine if the relationship is direct or inverse
4. Assign the unknown as x
5. For direct: set up a/b = c/d with matching positions
6. For inverse: set up a₁ × b₁ = a₂ × b₂
7. Solve using cross multiplication (direct) or product equality (inverse)
8. Verify the answer makes logical sense

#### Cross Multiplication Procedure

Given: a/b = c/d
1. Multiply diagonally: a × d and b × c
2. Set them equal: ad = bc
3. Isolate the unknown variable
4. Simplify the result

#### Verification Method

After solving:
1. Substitute your answer back into the original proportion
2. Check that both sides are equal (or products are equal for inverse)
3. Confirm the answer is logically reasonable given the context

### Exam Strategies

#### Identifying Proportional Clues Instantly

Scan for these keywords in the problem:
- "at the same rate" → direct proportion
- "how many workers/days" → likely inverse proportion
- "how much will ___ cost" → likely direct proportion
- "if speed increases" → inverse (speed vs. time)
- "shared among" → inverse (people vs. share)

#### Simplifying Mentally

- Look for common factors before cross multiplying
- Use the unit rate when one value is easy to divide
- Round to estimate, then pick the closest answer choice

#### Estimating Before Solving

- Determine if the answer should be bigger or smaller than the given values
- Eliminate answer choices that violate this logic
- This alone can eliminate 1-2 wrong choices immediately

#### Eliminating Unreasonable Choices

If the problem says "more workers, fewer days" and one choice shows MORE days, eliminate it immediately.

#### Solving Efficiently Under Time Pressure

1. Identify direct vs. inverse (5 seconds)
2. Set up the equation (10 seconds)
3. Cross multiply and solve (15-20 seconds)
4. Quick verification (5 seconds)

Target: 30-45 seconds per proportion problem.

### Real CSE-Like Examples

#### Easy Examples

**Example 1:** "If 6 pencils cost ₱42, how much do 10 pencils cost?"

- Direct proportion: more pencils → more cost
- 6/42 = 10/x → 6x = 420 → x = ₱70
- **Answer: ₱70**

**Example 2:** "A typist types 240 words in 4 minutes. How many words in 15 minutes?"

- Direct proportion: more time → more words
- 240/4 = x/15 → 4x = 3,600 → x = 900
- **Answer: 900 words**

**Example 3:** "If 3 kg of mangoes cost ₱255, how much do 7 kg cost?"

- Direct proportion: 3/255 = 7/x → 3x = 1,785 → x = ₱595
- **Answer: ₱595**

#### Medium Examples

**Example 4:** "If 12 workers can build a fence in 8 days, how many days will 16 workers take?"

- Inverse proportion: more workers → fewer days
- 12 × 8 = 16 × x → 96 = 16x → x = 6
- **Answer: 6 days**

**Example 5:** "On a map, 4 cm represents 100 km. If two towns are 7 cm apart on the map, what is the actual distance?"

- Direct proportion: 4/100 = 7/x → 4x = 700 → x = 175 km
- **Answer: 175 km**

**Example 6:** "A car uses 15 liters of fuel for 225 km. How much fuel is needed for 375 km?"

- Direct proportion: 15/225 = x/375 → 225x = 5,625 → x = 25 liters
- **Answer: 25 liters**

#### Hard Examples

**Example 7:** "A tank is filled by Pipe A in 6 hours and Pipe B in 9 hours. If both pipes work together, how long to fill the tank?"

- Pipe A fills 1/6 per hour; Pipe B fills 1/9 per hour
- Combined rate: 1/6 + 1/9 = 3/18 + 2/18 = 5/18 per hour
- Time = 18/5 = 3.6 hours = 3 hours 36 minutes
- **Answer: 3 hours and 36 minutes**

**Example 8:** "If 5 machines produce 300 items in 4 hours, how many items will 8 machines produce in 6 hours?"

- Rate per machine per hour: 300 ÷ (5 × 4) = 15 items
- New production: 8 × 6 × 15 = 720 items
- **Answer: 720 items**

**Example 9:** "A project requires 10 workers for 24 days. After 6 days, 4 workers leave. How many more days to finish?"

- Work done in 6 days: 10 × 6 = 60 worker-days
- Total work: 10 × 24 = 240 worker-days
- Remaining work: 240 - 60 = 180 worker-days
- Remaining workers: 10 - 4 = 6
- Days needed: 180 ÷ 6 = 30 days
- **Answer: 30 more days**

### Quick Recap

| Concept | Key Formula | When to Use |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Direct Proportion | a₁/b₁ = a₂/b₂ | Both quantities change in the same direction |
| Inverse Proportion | a₁ × b₁ = a₂ × b₂ | Quantities change in opposite directions |
| Cross Multiplication | ad = bc | Solving any proportion equation |
| Unit Rate | Total ÷ Quantity = Rate per unit | Quick mental math |
| Multi-Step | Break into sub-problems | Complex scenarios with multiple variables |

**Direct proportion signals:** same rate, per unit, more → more, less → less

**Inverse proportion signals:** more workers → less time, faster → shorter duration, shared among more → less each

### Memory Aids

#### Direct vs. Inverse Memory Trick

**"SAME-SAME = Direct"** — both go the SAME direction (both up or both down)

**"SEE-SAW = Inverse"** — like a seesaw, one goes up while the other goes down

#### Cross Multiplication Reminder

**"Cross and Multiply, then Divide to Simplify"**
- Draw an X through the proportion
- Multiply the connected numbers
- Divide by the number next to x

#### Ratio Order Mnemonic

**"First mentioned, first written"**
- "cost of 5 items" → items/cost = 5/cost
- Keep the same order on both sides

#### Estimation Shortcut

**"Double-check direction before you calculate"**
- More items → answer should be MORE (cost, distance, etc.)
- More workers → answer should be LESS (time, days)
- If your answer violates this, your setup is wrong

#### Exam-Time Hacks

1. **Scan choices first** — if three choices are close and one is wildly different, the outlier is likely wrong
2. **Use the unit rate** — divide to find "per 1" then multiply; often faster than cross multiplication
3. **Simplify the proportion** — reduce fractions before multiplying to keep numbers small
4. **Plug your answer back** — takes 5 seconds and catches arithmetic errors
