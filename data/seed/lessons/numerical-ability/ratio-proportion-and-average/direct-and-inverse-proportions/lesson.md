# Direct and Inverse Proportions

## Explanations

### Introduction

A **proportion** describes a relationship between two quantities where a change in one produces a predictable change in the other. In the Philippine Civil Service Examination, proportion problems are among the most frequently tested items in the Numerical Ability subtest because they model real-world situations that government employees encounter daily — budgeting, workforce planning, procurement, transportation scheduling, and resource allocation.

There are two fundamental types of proportional relationships:

- **Direct Proportion** — both quantities move in the same direction. When one increases, the other increases at a constant rate. When one decreases, the other decreases at the same rate.
- **Inverse Proportion** — the quantities move in opposite directions. When one increases, the other decreases, and their product remains constant.

Understanding the difference between these two relationships is critical. A single misidentification — treating a direct proportion as inverse or vice versa — guarantees a wrong answer, regardless of how well you execute the arithmetic.

**Why proportions appear in the CSE:**
- Budget officers scale allocations proportionally across departments
- HR planners calculate how many workers are needed to complete projects on schedule
- Procurement staff compute unit costs and total costs for bulk purchases
- Transportation planners determine travel times based on speed and distance
- Engineers scale measurements from blueprints to actual construction
- Administrative officers distribute workloads across teams

**Common mistakes examinees make:**
1. Confusing direct and inverse relationships — the single most common error
2. Setting up the proportion equation backwards
3. Forgetting to verify whether the answer makes logical sense
4. Applying cross multiplication to inverse proportion setups without adjusting
5. Mixing up units (hours vs. days, meters vs. kilometers)
6. Rushing through word problems without identifying which type of proportion applies

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Define direct proportion correctly and identify its constant-ratio property
- Define inverse proportion correctly and identify its constant-product property
- Distinguish direct from inverse relationships using contextual clues in word problems
- Solve direct proportion problems systematically using cross multiplication
- Solve inverse proportion problems systematically using the constant-product method
- Interpret real-life proportional relationships in workplace, finance, and transportation contexts
- Solve CSE-style proportion problems efficiently under time pressure
- Verify answers using logical reasoning and estimation

## Core Concepts

### 4.1 What Is a Direct Proportion?

A **direct proportion** (also called **direct variation**) exists between two quantities when they increase or decrease together at a constant rate. The ratio between the two quantities always remains the same.

**Mathematical definition:**

Two quantities *x* and *y* are directly proportional if:

$$y = kx$$

where *k* is the **constant of proportionality** (a fixed number that never changes for that relationship).

**What this means in plain language:**
- If you double *x*, then *y* also doubles.
- If you triple *x*, then *y* also triples.
- If you cut *x* in half, then *y* is also cut in half.
- The ratio y/x always equals the same constant *k*.

**Everyday examples of direct proportion:**

| Situation | Quantity 1 | Quantity 2 | Constant (k) |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Buying notebooks | Number of notebooks | Total cost | Price per notebook |
| Hourly wages | Hours worked | Salary earned | Hourly rate |
| Constant speed travel | Time traveled | Distance covered | Speed |
| Printing documents | Number of pages | Ink consumed | Ink per page |
| Fuel consumption (constant speed) | Distance driven | Fuel used | Fuel per km |

**Key indicator:** In a direct proportion, if you divide any pair of corresponding values, you always get the same number.

**Example:**
If 3 notebooks cost ₱75, then:
- 6 notebooks cost ₱150 (ratio: 75/3 = 150/6 = 25)
- 9 notebooks cost ₱225 (ratio: 225/9 = 25)
- The constant *k* = ₱25 per notebook

### 4.2 Identifying Direct Proportions

To determine whether a relationship is a direct proportion, apply these tests:

**Test 1 — Same-direction movement:**
Ask: "When one quantity increases, does the other also increase?" If yes, it *might* be direct proportion.

**Test 2 — Constant ratio:**
Divide corresponding values. If the ratio is always the same, it IS a direct proportion.

| Workers | Output (units) | Ratio (Output ÷ Workers) |
|---------|---------------|--------------------------|
| 2 | 40 | 20 |
| 5 | 100 | 20 |
| 8 | 160 | 20 |
| 10 | 200 | 20 |

Since the ratio is always 20, this is a direct proportion with k = 20 units per worker.

**Test 3 — Keyword recognition in word problems:**

Keywords that signal DIRECT proportion:
- "at the same rate"
- "per unit" (per hour, per kilogram, per piece)
- "for every"
- "proportional to"
- "costs ₱X each"
- "earns ₱X per hour"
- "travels at a constant speed"
- "if more... then more..."
- "if fewer... then fewer..."

**Graphical interpretation:**
A direct proportion produces a straight line through the origin (0,0) when graphed. The steeper the line, the larger the constant *k*.

**Ratio table drill:**

If 4 workers produce 120 items per day:

| Workers | Items |
|---------|-------|
| 4 | 120 |
| 8 | ? |
| 12 | ? |
| 1 | ? |

Solution: k = 120/4 = 30 items per worker
- 8 workers → 8 × 30 = 240 items
- 12 workers → 12 × 30 = 360 items
- 1 worker → 1 × 30 = 30 items

### 4.3 Solving Direct Proportion Problems

**The Standard Method — Cross Multiplication:**

When two quantities are directly proportional:

$$\frac{x_1}{y_1} = \frac{x_2}{y_2}$$

To find a missing value, cross multiply and solve.

**Step-by-step procedure:**
1. Identify the two quantities involved.
2. Confirm the relationship is direct (both increase or both decrease together).
3. Set up the proportion with known values on one side and the unknown on the other.
4. Cross multiply.
5. Solve for the unknown.
6. Verify: Does the answer make logical sense?

---

**Easy Example 1: Notebook Cost**

*If 5 notebooks cost ₱125, how much do 8 notebooks cost?*

Step 1: Quantities — notebooks and cost.
Step 2: More notebooks → more cost. Direct proportion. ✓
Step 3: Set up: 5/125 = 8/x
Step 4: Cross multiply: 5x = 125 × 8 = 1,000
Step 5: Solve: x = 1,000 ÷ 5 = ₱200
Step 6: Verify: 8 notebooks > 5 notebooks, so cost should be > ₱125. ₱200 > ₱125. ✓

**Answer: ₱200**

---

**Easy Example 2: Hourly Wage**

*Maria earns ₱4,500 for 10 hours of work. How much will she earn for 16 hours at the same rate?*

Step 1: Quantities — hours and earnings.
Step 2: More hours → more earnings. Direct proportion. ✓
Step 3: 10/4,500 = 16/x
Step 4: 10x = 4,500 × 16 = 72,000
Step 5: x = 72,000 ÷ 10 = ₱7,200
Step 6: 16 hours > 10 hours, so earnings > ₱4,500. ₱7,200 > ₱4,500. ✓

**Answer: ₱7,200**

---

**Medium Example 1: Recipe Scaling**

*A recipe requires 3 cups of flour to make 24 cookies. How many cups of flour are needed to make 60 cookies?*

Step 1: Quantities — flour and cookies.
Step 2: More cookies → more flour. Direct proportion. ✓
Step 3: 3/24 = x/60
Step 4: 24x = 3 × 60 = 180
Step 5: x = 180 ÷ 24 = 7.5 cups
Step 6: 60 cookies > 24 cookies, so flour > 3 cups. 7.5 > 3. ✓

**Answer: 7.5 cups**

---

**Medium Example 2: Map Scale**

*On a map, 2 cm represents 15 km. What actual distance does 7 cm represent?*

Step 1: Quantities — map distance and actual distance.
Step 2: More cm on map → more km in reality. Direct proportion. ✓
Step 3: 2/15 = 7/x
Step 4: 2x = 15 × 7 = 105
Step 5: x = 105 ÷ 2 = 52.5 km
Step 6: 7 cm > 2 cm, so distance > 15 km. 52.5 > 15. ✓

**Answer: 52.5 km**

---

**Hard Example 1: Multi-step Salary**

*A government employee earns ₱18,750 for 15 working days. If she works 22 days in a month (including overtime at the same daily rate), what is her total pay?*

Step 1: Quantities — days and pay.
Step 2: More days → more pay. Direct proportion. ✓
Step 3: 15/18,750 = 22/x
Step 4: 15x = 18,750 × 22 = 412,500
Step 5: x = 412,500 ÷ 15 = ₱27,500
Step 6: 22 days > 15 days, so pay > ₱18,750. ₱27,500 > ₱18,750. ✓

**Answer: ₱27,500**

### 4.4 What Is an Inverse Proportion?

An **inverse proportion** (also called **indirect proportion** or **inverse variation**) exists between two quantities when one increases while the other decreases, such that their product remains constant.

**Mathematical definition:**

Two quantities *x* and *y* are inversely proportional if:

$$xy = k$$

or equivalently:

$$y = \frac{k}{x}$$

where *k* is the **constant of proportionality** (the fixed product).

**What this means in plain language:**
- If you double *x*, then *y* is cut in half.
- If you triple *x*, then *y* becomes one-third.
- If you cut *x* in half, then *y* doubles.
- The product x × y always equals the same constant *k*.

**Everyday examples of inverse proportion:**

| Situation | Quantity 1 | Quantity 2 | Constant (k = product) |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|------------------------|
| Construction project | Number of workers | Days to finish | Total worker-days |
| Travel | Speed | Time to arrive | Distance |
| Filling a tank | Number of pipes | Time to fill | Total pipe-hours |
| Sharing equally | Number of people | Share per person | Total amount |
| Printing | Number of printers | Time to finish | Total printer-hours |

**Key indicator:** In an inverse proportion, if you multiply any pair of corresponding values, you always get the same number.

**Example:**
If 4 workers can finish a job in 12 days:
- Product: 4 × 12 = 48 worker-days
- 6 workers → 48 ÷ 6 = 8 days
- 8 workers → 48 ÷ 8 = 6 days
- 2 workers → 48 ÷ 2 = 24 days

Notice: More workers → fewer days. The product is always 48.

### 4.5 Identifying Inverse Proportions

To determine whether a relationship is an inverse proportion, apply these tests:

**Test 1 — Opposite-direction movement:**
Ask: "When one quantity increases, does the other decrease?" If yes, it *might* be inverse proportion.

**Test 2 — Constant product:**
Multiply corresponding values. If the product is always the same, it IS an inverse proportion.

| Speed (km/h) | Time (hours) | Product (Speed × Time) |
|--------------|-------------|------------------------|
| 30 | 4 | 120 |
| 40 | 3 | 120 |
| 60 | 2 | 120 |
| 120 | 1 | 120 |

Since the product is always 120 (the distance), this is an inverse proportion.

**Test 3 — Keyword recognition in word problems:**

Keywords that signal INVERSE proportion:
- "fewer days" when more workers are added
- "faster" when more machines are used
- "less time" with increased speed
- "shared equally among more people"
- "if more... then less..."
- "if fewer... then more..."
- "working together"
- "at the same total workload"
- "can finish the job in"

**Graphical interpretation:**
An inverse proportion produces a hyperbola (a curve that approaches but never touches the axes). As one value gets very large, the other approaches zero.

**Product table drill:**

If 6 machines can complete a job in 10 hours:

| Machines | Hours | Product |
|----------|-------|---------|
| 6 | 10 | 60 |
| 10 | ? | 60 |
| 3 | ? | 60 |
| 15 | ? | 60 |

Solution: k = 6 × 10 = 60 machine-hours
- 10 machines → 60 ÷ 10 = 6 hours
- 3 machines → 60 ÷ 3 = 20 hours
- 15 machines → 60 ÷ 15 = 4 hours

### 4.6 Solving Inverse Proportion Problems

**The Standard Method — Constant Product:**

When two quantities are inversely proportional:

$$x_1 \times y_1 = x_2 \times y_2$$

To find a missing value, use the fact that both products equal the same constant.

**Step-by-step procedure:**
1. Identify the two quantities involved.
2. Confirm the relationship is inverse (one increases while the other decreases).
3. Write the constant-product equation: x₁ × y₁ = x₂ × y₂
4. Substitute known values.
5. Solve for the unknown.
6. Verify: Does the answer make logical sense? (More of one → less of the other)

---

**Easy Example 1: Workers and Days**

*If 8 workers can paint a building in 6 days, how many days will it take 12 workers?*

Step 1: Quantities — workers and days.
Step 2: More workers → fewer days. Inverse proportion. ✓
Step 3: 8 × 6 = 12 × y₂
Step 4: 48 = 12y₂
Step 5: y₂ = 48 ÷ 12 = 4 days
Step 6: 12 workers > 8 workers, so days < 6. 4 < 6. ✓

**Answer: 4 days**

---

**Easy Example 2: Speed and Time**

*A car traveling at 60 km/h takes 3 hours to reach a destination. How long will it take at 90 km/h?*

Step 1: Quantities — speed and time.
Step 2: More speed → less time. Inverse proportion. ✓
Step 3: 60 × 3 = 90 × y₂
Step 4: 180 = 90y₂
Step 5: y₂ = 180 ÷ 90 = 2 hours
Step 6: 90 km/h > 60 km/h, so time < 3 hours. 2 < 3. ✓

**Answer: 2 hours**

---

**Medium Example 1: Pipes Filling a Tank**

*5 pipes can fill a tank in 8 hours. How long will it take 4 pipes to fill the same tank?*

Step 1: Quantities — pipes and hours.
Step 2: Fewer pipes → more time. Inverse proportion. ✓
Step 3: 5 × 8 = 4 × y₂
Step 4: 40 = 4y₂
Step 5: y₂ = 40 ÷ 4 = 10 hours
Step 6: 4 pipes < 5 pipes, so time > 8 hours. 10 > 8. ✓

**Answer: 10 hours**

---

**Medium Example 2: Food Supply**

*A camp has enough food for 120 soldiers for 15 days. If 40 more soldiers arrive, how long will the food last?*

Step 1: Quantities — soldiers and days.
Step 2: More soldiers → fewer days (same food supply). Inverse proportion. ✓
Step 3: 120 × 15 = 160 × y₂ (120 + 40 = 160 soldiers)
Step 4: 1,800 = 160y₂
Step 5: y₂ = 1,800 ÷ 160 = 11.25 days
Step 6: 160 soldiers > 120 soldiers, so days < 15. 11.25 < 15. ✓

**Answer: 11.25 days**

---

**Hard Example 1: Construction Workforce**

*A contractor needs to build a road. With 24 workers, the project takes 35 days. The client wants it done in 20 days. How many additional workers are needed?*

Step 1: Quantities — workers and days.
Step 2: Fewer days → more workers. Inverse proportion. ✓
Step 3: 24 × 35 = x₂ × 20
Step 4: 840 = 20x₂
Step 5: x₂ = 840 ÷ 20 = 42 workers needed
Step 6: Additional workers = 42 − 24 = 18
Verify: 20 days < 35 days, so workers > 24. 42 > 24. ✓

**Answer: 18 additional workers**

### 4.7 Comparing Direct and Inverse Proportions

This is the most critical skill for the CSE. Many examinees can solve proportions correctly once they identify the type — but they misidentify the type and get the wrong answer.

**Side-by-side comparison:**

| Feature | Direct Proportion | Inverse Proportion |
|---------|------------------|-------------------|
| Direction | Same direction | Opposite directions |
| When x increases | y increases | y decreases |
| When x decreases | y decreases | y increases |
| Constant | Ratio (y/x = k) | Product (xy = k) |
| Formula | y = kx | xy = k |
| Graph | Straight line through origin | Hyperbola |
| Equation setup | x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂ | x₁y₁ = x₂y₂ |

**Decision flowchart:**

1. Read the problem carefully.
2. Identify the two quantities.
3. Ask: "If I increase Quantity 1, what happens to Quantity 2?"
   - If Quantity 2 ALSO increases → **Direct Proportion**
   - If Quantity 2 DECREASES → **Inverse Proportion**
4. Apply the correct formula.

**Practice identifying:**

| Scenario | Type | Reasoning |
|----------|------|-----------|
| More items purchased → higher total cost | Direct | Both increase |
| More workers → fewer days to finish | Inverse | Opposite directions |
| Higher speed → less travel time | Inverse | Opposite directions |
| More hours worked → more salary | Direct | Both increase |
| More people sharing → smaller share each | Inverse | Opposite directions |
| Longer fabric → higher price | Direct | Both increase |
| More pipes → less time to fill | Inverse | Opposite directions |
| More students → more chairs needed | Direct | Both increase |
| Wider road → fewer lanes of traffic jam | Inverse | Opposite directions |
| More fuel → greater distance traveled | Direct | Both increase |

**Tricky scenarios to watch for:**

- "A car uses 5 liters per 100 km. How much fuel for 350 km?" → DIRECT (more distance, more fuel)
- "A car traveling at 80 km/h takes 3 hours. How long at 60 km/h?" → INVERSE (less speed, more time)
- "12 workers finish in 8 days. How many days for 16 workers?" → INVERSE (more workers, fewer days)
- "12 workers produce 240 items. How many items do 16 workers produce?" → DIRECT (more workers, more output — assuming same rate per worker)

The last two examples show why context matters. Workers and days (fixed job) = inverse. Workers and output (ongoing production) = direct.

### 4.8 Real-Life Applications of Proportions

**Wages and Working Hours (Direct)**
A government clerk earns ₱650 per hour. Working 8 hours = ₱5,200. Working 10 hours = ₱6,500. The ratio of pay to hours is always 650.

**Transportation — Speed and Time (Inverse)**
A bus covers Manila to Baguio (250 km). At 50 km/h → 5 hours. At 100 km/h → 2.5 hours. The product (speed × time) is always 250.

**Fuel Consumption (Direct)**
A vehicle uses 8 liters per 100 km. For 250 km → 20 liters. For 400 km → 32 liters. More distance, more fuel at a constant rate.

**Construction Projects (Inverse)**
Building a school: 10 workers take 30 days. 15 workers take 20 days. 30 workers take 10 days. Product is always 300 worker-days.

**Recipe Adjustments (Direct)**
A recipe for 4 servings needs 2 cups of rice. For 10 servings → 5 cups. For 20 servings → 10 cups. Constant ratio of 0.5 cups per serving.

**Engineering — Map Scaling (Direct)**
Scale 1:50,000 means 1 cm = 500 m. A 6 cm line = 3,000 m = 3 km. More cm, more actual distance.

**Budgeting — Equal Sharing (Inverse)**
₱12,000 shared among 4 people = ₱3,000 each. Among 6 people = ₱2,000 each. Among 12 people = ₱1,000 each. More people, less per person.

**Population Density (Direct)**
If a barangay has 200 people per hectare, then 5 hectares → 1,000 people. 10 hectares → 2,000 people.

**Workforce Scheduling (Inverse)**
A government office needs to process 480 applications. With 8 clerks → 60 each. With 12 clerks → 40 each. With 16 clerks → 30 each.

**Machine Operations (Inverse)**
A factory has printing machines. 4 machines finish a batch in 6 hours. 8 machines finish in 3 hours. 12 machines finish in 2 hours.

### 4.10 Problem-Solving Strategies

**The 5-Step System for Proportion Problems:**

1. **READ** — Identify the two quantities and their given values.
2. **CLASSIFY** — Determine if the relationship is direct or inverse.
3. **SET UP** — Write the correct equation (ratio or product).
4. **SOLVE** — Perform the arithmetic.
5. **CHECK** — Verify the answer makes logical sense.

**Classification shortcuts:**

Ask yourself: "If I increase the first quantity, should the answer be BIGGER or SMALLER than the given value?"
- If BIGGER → Direct proportion → use x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂
- If SMALLER → Inverse proportion → use x₁y₁ = x₂y₂

**Avoiding setup mistakes:**

Common error: Writing 5/100 = x/8 when it should be 5/100 = 8/x (or equivalently 5/8 = 100/x).

Rule: Keep corresponding quantities in the same position.
- Correct: notebooks₁/cost₁ = notebooks₂/cost₂
- Correct: workers₁ × days₁ = workers₂ × days₂

**Solving efficiently under time pressure:**

- Simplify before cross-multiplying. If 6/150 = 10/x, notice that 6/150 = 1/25, so 1/25 = 10/x → x = 250.
- For inverse proportion, find the constant first (multiply the given pair), then divide by the new value.
- Use estimation to eliminate obviously wrong choices before computing.

### 4.11 Estimation and Mental Math Techniques

**For Direct Proportion:**

*If 4 items cost ₱180, how much do 7 items cost?*

Mental approach:
- Unit cost: ₱180 ÷ 4 = ₱45 per item
- 7 items: 7 × ₱45 = ₱315

This "find the unit value" approach is often faster than cross multiplication.

**For Inverse Proportion:**

*If 6 workers take 10 days, how many days for 15 workers?*

Mental approach:
- Total work: 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days
- With 15 workers: 60 ÷ 15 = 4 days

**Estimation for answer elimination:**

*If 3 kg costs ₱240, how much does 8 kg cost?*

Quick estimate: 8 is a bit less than 3× of 3. So the answer should be a bit less than 3 × ₱240 = ₱720. Actually, 8/3 ≈ 2.67, so answer ≈ 2.67 × 240 ≈ ₱640.

Exact: 240 × 8 ÷ 3 = 1,920 ÷ 3 = ₱640. ✓

**Doubling/halving shortcuts:**
- If the quantity doubles → direct: answer doubles; inverse: answer halves
- If the quantity triples → direct: answer triples; inverse: answer becomes 1/3
- If the quantity is halved → direct: answer halves; inverse: answer doubles

### 4.12 Common Errors in Direct and Inverse Proportions

**Error 1: Confusing direct and inverse**
- Problem: "8 workers finish in 6 days. How many days for 12 workers?"
- Wrong thinking: "More workers, more days" → sets up as direct → gets 9 days
- Correct: More workers means FEWER days (inverse) → 8×6 = 12×y → y = 4 days

**Error 2: Incorrect proportion setup**
- Problem: "5 books cost ₱200. How much do 12 books cost?"
- Wrong: 5/200 = x/12 (mixing up which is unknown)
- Correct: 5/200 = 12/x → x = ₱480

**Error 3: Arithmetic mistakes in cross multiplication**
- 5x = 200 × 12 = 2,400 → x = 2,400 ÷ 5 = 480 ✓
- Common slip: forgetting to multiply both sides, or dividing by the wrong number

**Error 4: Reversing values**
- Problem: "A car at 80 km/h takes 3 hours. Time at 60 km/h?"
- Wrong: 80 × 3 = 60 × y → 240 = 60y → y = 4 hours ✓ (this one is actually correct)
- But some students write: 60 × 3 = 80 × y → y = 2.25 (WRONG — reversed the speeds)

**Error 5: Inconsistent units**
- Problem mixes hours and minutes, or km and meters
- Always convert to the same unit before setting up the proportion

**Error 6: Not reading the question carefully**
- "How many ADDITIONAL workers?" means subtract the original number from your answer
- "How much MORE?" means subtract the original amount
- "How much LESS time?" means subtract from the original time

## Step-by-Step Rules

### Direct Proportion Solving Procedure
1. Read the problem and identify the two related quantities.
2. Confirm: "More of A means more of B" → Direct.
3. Write: A₁/B₁ = A₂/B₂
4. Substitute known values (three values known, one unknown).
5. Cross multiply: A₁ × B₂ = B₁ × A₂
6. Solve for the unknown.
7. Check: Is the answer larger/smaller in the expected direction?

### Inverse Proportion Solving Procedure
1. Read the problem and identify the two related quantities.
2. Confirm: "More of A means less of B" → Inverse.
3. Write: A₁ × B₁ = A₂ × B₂
4. Substitute known values.
5. Compute the constant: k = A₁ × B₁
6. Solve: unknown = k ÷ (known value)
7. Check: Is the answer larger/smaller in the expected direction?

### Cross Multiplication System (Direct Only)
Given a/b = c/d:
- a × d = b × c
- Solve for whichever variable is unknown

### Constant-Product System (Inverse Only)
Given x₁y₁ = x₂y₂:
- Compute k = x₁ × y₁
- Unknown = k ÷ (the other known value)

### Verification System
After solving, always ask:
- Did I identify the correct type (direct vs. inverse)?
- Does the direction of my answer make sense?
- Is the magnitude reasonable?
- Did I answer what was actually asked (total vs. additional)?

## Exam Strategies

### Identifying Proportional Relationships Instantly
- Train yourself to recognize the "direction test" in under 5 seconds
- If both quantities move the same way → direct
- If they move opposite ways → inverse
- Don't overthink — trust the direction test

### Spotting Keywords Quickly
- "at the same rate" → direct
- "how many days/hours to finish" with changing workers → inverse
- "per unit" language → direct
- "shared among" → inverse
- "working together" → inverse

### Estimating Before Solving
- Before computing, predict whether the answer should be larger or smaller than the given value
- This immediately eliminates 1-2 wrong choices
- If your computed answer contradicts your prediction, you likely misidentified the proportion type

### Eliminating Unreasonable Choices
- If 5 workers take 10 days, and you're asked about 8 workers, the answer MUST be less than 10 days
- Any choice ≥ 10 is automatically wrong
- Use this to narrow down even before solving

### Solving Efficiently Under Time Pressure
- Find the unit value (for direct) or the constant product (for inverse) first
- Simplify fractions before multiplying
- Use mental math for simple multiplications
- Don't show work on scratch paper unless the numbers are complex
- Average time target: 30-45 seconds per proportion problem

## Real CSE-Like Examples

### Easy Examples

**Example 1:**
If 6 pens cost ₱90, how much do 10 pens cost?

Type: Direct (more pens → more cost)
Solution: 6/90 = 10/x → 6x = 900 → x = ₱150
Answer: **₱150**

**Example 2:**
A typist can type 40 pages in 5 hours. How many pages in 8 hours?

Type: Direct (more hours → more pages)
Solution: 40/5 = x/8 → 5x = 320 → x = 64 pages
Answer: **64 pages**

**Example 3:**
If 10 workers can finish a task in 6 days, how many days for 15 workers?

Type: Inverse (more workers → fewer days)
Solution: 10 × 6 = 15 × y → 60 = 15y → y = 4 days
Answer: **4 days**

### Medium Examples

**Example 4:**
A car travels 240 km in 4 hours. At the same speed, how far will it travel in 7 hours?

Type: Direct (more time → more distance at constant speed)
Solution: 240/4 = x/7 → 4x = 1,680 → x = 420 km
Answer: **420 km**

**Example 5:**
A garrison of 500 soldiers has provisions for 28 days. If 200 more soldiers join, how long will the provisions last?

Type: Inverse (more soldiers → fewer days)
Solution: 500 × 28 = 700 × y → 14,000 = 700y → y = 20 days
Answer: **20 days**

**Example 6:**
A factory produces 1,200 units with 8 machines running for 6 hours. How many hours would 12 machines need to produce the same 1,200 units?

Type: Inverse (more machines → fewer hours)
Solution: 8 × 6 = 12 × y → 48 = 12y → y = 4 hours
Answer: **4 hours**

### Hard Examples

**Example 7:**
A contractor employs 36 workers to build a wall in 15 days. After 6 days, 12 workers leave. How many more days will it take to finish the remaining work?

Work done in 6 days: 36 × 6 = 216 worker-days
Total work: 36 × 15 = 540 worker-days
Remaining work: 540 − 216 = 324 worker-days
Remaining workers: 36 − 12 = 24
Days needed: 324 ÷ 24 = 13.5 days
Answer: **13.5 more days**

**Example 8:**
A government project requires 20 employees working 8 hours per day to finish in 12 days. If the deadline is moved up to 8 days, and employees can only work 6 hours per day, how many employees are needed?

Total work: 20 × 8 × 12 = 1,920 person-hours
New constraint: x × 6 × 8 = 1,920
48x = 1,920
x = 40 employees
Answer: **40 employees**

## Quick Recap

| Concept | Key Point |
|---------|-----------|
| Direct Proportion | Both quantities move in the same direction; ratio is constant (y/x = k) |
| Inverse Proportion | Quantities move in opposite directions; product is constant (xy = k) |
| Identifying type | Ask: "If I increase one, does the other increase or decrease?" |
| Direct formula | x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂ → cross multiply |
| Inverse formula | x₁ × y₁ = x₂ × y₂ → find constant, then divide |
| Verification | Check that the answer's direction matches the proportion type |
| Common trap | Workers and days (fixed job) = inverse; workers and output (ongoing) = direct |
| Exam speed | Find unit value (direct) or constant product (inverse) first |

## Memory Aids

**"SAME = DIVIDE, OPPOSITE = MULTIPLY"**
- Same direction (direct) → set up as a ratio (division)
- Opposite direction (inverse) → set up as a product (multiplication)

**"More-More or Less-Less = Direct"**
- More notebooks, more cost ✓
- Less time working, less salary ✓

**"More-Less or Less-More = Inverse"**
- More workers, less days ✓
- Less speed, more time ✓

**The Worker Test:**
When you see "workers" in a problem, ask: "Is the total job FIXED or is production ONGOING?"
- Fixed job (build a wall, paint a house) → Inverse
- Ongoing production (make items per day) → Direct

**Cross Multiplication Reminder:**
For a/b = c/d: "Cross means X — multiply diagonally"
a × d = b × c

**Constant Product Reminder:**
"The job doesn't change — only who does it and how long it takes"
Workers × Days = SAME before and after

**Estimation Hack:**
Before solving, ask: "Should my answer be BIGGER or SMALLER?"
If your calculation gives the opposite direction, you set up the wrong type.
