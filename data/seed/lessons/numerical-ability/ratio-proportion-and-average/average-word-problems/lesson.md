## Explanations

### Introduction

**Average word problems** are questions that embed the concept of arithmetic mean inside real-world scenarios — ages of family members, speeds of vehicles, monthly salaries, classroom scores, or combined group statistics. Instead of simply asking "find the mean of 5, 10, 15," these problems require you to extract numerical relationships from a narrative, identify what is known and unknown, and apply the average formula strategically.

Averages are everywhere in professional and civic life:
- **Age analysis:** Government demographic surveys report average ages of populations, employees, or retirees.
- **Transportation:** Traffic engineers compute average speeds across road segments to plan infrastructure.
- **Finance:** Budget officers calculate average monthly expenditures; HR computes average salary grades.
- **Workplace statistics:** Supervisors track average daily output, average processing time, average error rates.
- **Engineering reports:** Quality control uses average measurements to detect deviations.
- **Public-service planning:** Per-capita spending, average class sizes, average wait times at government offices.

In the Philippine Civil Service Examination, average word problems appear frequently because they simultaneously test:
- Reading comprehension (extracting numbers from text)
- Arithmetic fluency (addition, multiplication, division)
- Algebraic reasoning (working backward from averages to find unknowns)
- Logical interpretation (understanding what the average means in context)

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Averaging speeds directly** — Adding two speeds and dividing by 2 only works when distances are equal AND you're told to average the speeds (not compute average speed over a trip). The correct approach uses Total Distance ÷ Total Time.
2. **Forgetting to convert averages to totals** — Many problems require you to find the total first (Average × Count), then manipulate it. Skipping this step leads to wrong answers.
3. **Miscounting group members** — When a person joins or leaves a group, the count changes. Using the old count with the new average (or vice versa) is a common trap.
4. **Confusing "average age now" with "average age in the future"** — Every person ages by the same number of years, so the average also increases by that same number. But if someone joins or leaves, the calculation changes.
5. **Arithmetic errors under pressure** — Large sums, multi-step problems, and time pressure combine to produce careless mistakes.
6. **Misreading what the question asks** — Some questions ask for the new average; others ask for the value of a missing member. Read carefully.
7. **Ignoring units** — Mixing km/h with m/s, or monthly with annual figures.

### 4.1 Review of the Average Formula
> 🤔 **Why does this work?** The principle behind this operation follows from the fundamental properties of arithmetic. Understanding the "why" — not just the "how" — lets you recognize when to apply this method in unfamiliar problem contexts on the CSE.


The **arithmetic mean** (average) connects three quantities in a single relationship:

$$
\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum of All Values}}{\text{Number of Values}}
$$

This formula can be rearranged into three forms depending on what you need:

| Find | Formula |
|------|---------|
| Average | Sum ÷ Count |
| Total Sum | Average × Count |
| Count | Sum ÷ Average |

#### Converting Averages into Totals

This is the single most important skill for average word problems. When a problem says "the average of 5 numbers is 40," your first move should be:

$$
\text{Total Sum} = 40 \times 5 = 200
$$

Once you have the total, you can add members, remove members, find missing values, or combine groups.

#### The "Equal Sharing" Interpretation

Think of the average as what each item would be if everything were distributed equally. If 6 employees earn an average of ₱25,000/month, the total payroll is ₱150,000 — as if each person earned exactly ₱25,000.

#### Balancing Interpretation

The average is the "balance point." Values above the average are offset by values below it. If the average of a group is 50, and one member has value 60 (which is +10 above average), there must be a combined deficit of −10 among the other members.

This balancing view is powerful for mental estimation:
- If 4 values average 20, and three of them are 18, 22, and 25, the fourth must be 15 (because 18 + 22 + 25 + 15 = 80 = 20 × 4).


> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "The formula always works the same way regardless of the problem context."

> **Why it fails:** CSE problems often present variations where the standard formula must be adapted. Blindly applying a memorized formula without checking the context leads to systematic errors.

> **Correct model:** Always read the problem to identify what type of relationship exists (direct, inverse, part-whole, etc.), then apply the appropriate formula. Verify your answer makes sense in the problem's context before selecting it.

### 4.2 Age Average Problems

Age-average problems are among the most common in the CSE. They involve computing average ages of groups, finding a missing person's age, or determining how averages change over time.

#### Key Principle: Total Age = Average Age × Number of Persons

**Example 1 — Finding Average Age:**
A family has members aged 45, 42, 18, 15, and 10. What is the average age?

$$
\text{Average} = \frac{45 + 42 + 18 + 15 + 10}{5} = \frac{130}{5} = 26 \text{ years}
$$

**Example 2 — Finding a Missing Age:**
The average age of 4 friends is 22 years. Three of them are 20, 24, and 19. What is the age of the fourth friend?

$$
\text{Total} = 22 \times 4 = 88
$$
$$
\text{Fourth friend} = 88 - (20 + 24 + 19) = 88 - 63 = 25 \text{ years}
$$

#### How Averages Change When People Join or Leave

**When a new person joins:**
$$
\text{New Average} = \frac{\text{Old Total} + \text{New Person's Age}}{\text{Old Count} + 1}
$$

**When a person leaves:**
$$
\text{New Average} = \frac{\text{Old Total} - \text{Departing Person's Age}}{\text{Old Count} - 1}
$$

**Example 3 — Person Joins:**
The average age of 5 employees is 30. A new employee aged 24 joins. What is the new average?

$$
\text{Old Total} = 30 \times 5 = 150
$$
$$
\text{New Total} = 150 + 24 = 174
$$
$$
\text{New Average} = \frac{174}{6} = 29 \text{ years}
$$

#### Future and Past Age Averages

**Critical insight:** If every person in a group ages by *k* years, the average also increases by exactly *k* years.

**Example 4 — Future Average:**
The average age of 4 siblings is 15 years now. What will their average age be in 6 years?

$$
\text{New Average} = 15 + 6 = 21 \text{ years}
$$

Why? Each sibling gains 6 years, so the total increases by 4 × 6 = 24, and dividing by 4 gives +6 to the average.

**Example 5 — Past Average:**
The average age of 3 colleagues is 35 now. What was their average age 4 years ago?

$$
\text{Past Average} = 35 - 4 = 31 \text{ years}
$$

#### Combined-Group Age Problems

When two groups merge, the combined average is NOT simply the average of the two averages (unless both groups have the same size).

$$
\text{Combined Average} = \frac{\text{Total of Group A} + \text{Total of Group B}}{\text{Count A} + \text{Count B}}
$$

**Example 6:**
Group A has 4 people with average age 25. Group B has 6 people with average age 35. What is the combined average?

$$
\text{Total A} = 25 \times 4 = 100
$$
$$
\text{Total B} = 35 \times 6 = 210
$$
$$
\text{Combined Average} = \frac{100 + 210}{4 + 6} = \frac{310}{10} = 31 \text{ years}
$$

Notice: 31 is NOT the midpoint of 25 and 35 (which would be 30). The larger group "pulls" the average toward its own mean.

#### Replacement Problems

**Example 7:**
The average age of 5 workers is 32. One worker aged 40 is replaced by a new worker. The new average becomes 30. How old is the new worker?

$$
\text{Old Total} = 32 \times 5 = 160
$$
$$
\text{New Total} = 30 \times 5 = 150
$$
$$
\text{Difference} = 160 - 150 = 10
$$
$$
\text{New worker's age} = 40 - 10 = 30 \text{ years}
$$


> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "If my computed answer is close to one of the choices, it must be right."

> **Why it fails:** The CSE deliberately includes distractors that result from common errors — using the wrong operation, misidentifying the proportion type, or reversing the ratio. A "close" answer could be the result of a systematic mistake that the test writers anticipated.

> **Correct model:** Verify your setup before computing. Check that you've identified the correct proportion type, set up the equation properly, and solved accurately. A wrong setup with correct arithmetic still produces a wrong answer — and the CSE will include that wrong answer among the choices.

### 4.3 Speed Average Problems
> 🤔 **Why does this work?** When you follow this procedure, you're exploiting a mathematical invariant — something that stays constant regardless of how you manipulate the numbers. Identifying that invariant is the key to solving problems efficiently rather than memorizing steps.


Speed-average problems are notorious for tripping up examinees because the intuitive approach (averaging the speeds) is usually wrong.

#### The Correct Formula

$$
\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance Traveled}}{\text{Total Time Taken}}
$$

This is NOT the same as (Speed₁ + Speed₂) ÷ 2 unless the time spent at each speed is equal.

#### Why You Cannot Simply Average Speeds

Consider a car that travels 60 km at 30 km/h, then 60 km at 60 km/h.

- Time for first leg: 60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours
- Time for second leg: 60 ÷ 60 = 1 hour
- Total distance: 120 km
- Total time: 3 hours
- **Average speed: 120 ÷ 3 = 40 km/h**

The naive average would be (30 + 60) ÷ 2 = 45 km/h — which is WRONG. The car spent more time at the slower speed, pulling the average down.

#### Equal-Distance Trips (Harmonic Mean)

When the distance is the same for each leg, the average speed simplifies to the **harmonic mean**:

$$
\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2 \times v_1 \times v_2}{v_1 + v_2}
$$

For the example above: (2 × 30 × 60) ÷ (30 + 60) = 3600 ÷ 90 = 40 km/h ✓

#### Equal-Time Trips (Arithmetic Mean)

When the TIME is the same for each leg, the average speed IS the simple average:

$$
\text{Average Speed} = \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}
$$

**Example 8:**
A delivery rider travels at 40 km/h for 2 hours, then at 60 km/h for 2 hours. What is the average speed?

- Distance₁ = 40 × 2 = 80 km
- Distance₂ = 60 × 2 = 120 km
- Total distance = 200 km, Total time = 4 hours
- Average speed = 200 ÷ 4 = 50 km/h = (40 + 60) ÷ 2 ✓ (equal time)

#### Multi-Stage Travel Problems

**Example 9:**
A bus travels 120 km at 60 km/h, then 80 km at 40 km/h, then 60 km at 30 km/h. What is the average speed for the entire trip?

- Time₁ = 120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours
- Time₂ = 80 ÷ 40 = 2 hours
- Time₃ = 60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours
- Total distance = 120 + 80 + 60 = 260 km
- Total time = 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 hours
- **Average speed = 260 ÷ 6 ≈ 43.33 km/h**

#### Commuting Scenarios

**Example 10:**
Maria drives to work (30 km) at 60 km/h and returns home at 40 km/h. What is her average speed for the round trip?

- Same distance each way → use harmonic mean
- Average speed = (2 × 60 × 40) ÷ (60 + 40) = 4800 ÷ 100 = **48 km/h**

Note: NOT 50 km/h. She spends more time at the slower speed (return trip takes 45 minutes vs. 30 minutes going).

#### Finding Missing Speed or Distance

**Example 11:**
A jeepney covers 100 km. For the first 60 km, it travels at 30 km/h. If the average speed for the entire trip is 25 km/h, what was the speed for the remaining 40 km?

- Total time = Total distance ÷ Average speed = 100 ÷ 25 = 4 hours
- Time for first part = 60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours
- Time for second part = 4 − 2 = 2 hours
- Speed for second part = 40 ÷ 2 = **20 km/h**

### 4.4 Financial Average Problems

Financial averages appear in budgeting, salary analysis, expense tracking, and business reporting — all relevant to government work.

#### Average Expenses

**Example 12:**
A government employee's daily expenses for a week are: ₱350, ₱420, ₱380, ₱500, ₱290, ₱450, ₱310. What is the average daily expense?

$$
\text{Total} = 350 + 420 + 380 + 500 + 290 + 450 + 310 = 2{,}700
$$
$$
\text{Average} = \frac{2{,}700}{7} ≈ ₱385.71
$$

#### Average Salary Problems

**Example 13:**
In an office of 8 employees, the average monthly salary is ₱28,000. If one employee earning ₱35,000 resigns, what is the new average salary?

$$
\text{Old Total} = 28{,}000 \times 8 = 224{,}000
$$
$$
\text{New Total} = 224{,}000 - 35{,}000 = 189{,}000
$$
$$
\text{New Average} = \frac{189{,}000}{7} = ₱27{,}000
$$

#### Finding a Missing Financial Value

**Example 14:**
A store's average daily sales for 6 days is ₱12,500. If the sales for 5 of those days total ₱58,000, what were the sales on the sixth day?

$$
\text{Total for 6 days} = 12{,}500 \times 6 = 75{,}000
$$
$$
\text{Sixth day} = 75{,}000 - 58{,}000 = ₱17{,}000
$$

#### Budget Planning with Averages

**Example 15:**
A family wants their average monthly savings to be ₱5,000 over 6 months. After 5 months, they have saved ₱4,200, ₱5,500, ₱3,800, ₱6,000, and ₱4,500. How much must they save in the sixth month?

$$
\text{Target Total} = 5{,}000 \times 6 = 30{,}000
$$
$$
\text{Saved so far} = 4{,}200 + 5{,}500 + 3{,}800 + 6{,}000 + 4{,}500 = 24{,}000
$$
$$
\text{Sixth month} = 30{,}000 - 24{,}000 = ₱6{,}000
$$

#### Average Income and Sales Performance

**Example 16:**
A salesperson earns commission based on sales. Her average monthly commission for the first quarter (3 months) was ₱18,000. In April, she earned ₱24,000. What is her new average over 4 months?

$$
\text{Total for 3 months} = 18{,}000 \times 3 = 54{,}000
$$
$$
\text{New Total} = 54{,}000 + 24{,}000 = 78{,}000
$$
$$
\text{New Average} = \frac{78{,}000}{4} = ₱19{,}500
$$

### 4.5 Group Average Problems
> 🤔 **Why does this work?** This shortcut works because it's a special case of the more general rule. By understanding the underlying principle, you can verify your answer logically even if you forget the exact formula under exam pressure.


Group-average problems involve combining, separating, or comparing averages across different groups. The key insight: **you cannot simply average the averages unless all groups are the same size.**

#### Combining Group Averages

$$
\text{Combined Average} = \frac{n_1 \times \bar{x}_1 + n_2 \times \bar{x}_2}{n_1 + n_2}
$$

**Example 17:**
Section A has 30 students with an average score of 78. Section B has 20 students with an average score of 85. What is the combined average?

$$
\text{Combined} = \frac{30 \times 78 + 20 \times 85}{30 + 20} = \frac{2{,}340 + 1{,}700}{50} = \frac{4{,}040}{50} = 80.8
$$

Notice: The combined average (80.8) is closer to Section A's average (78) because Section A has more students.

#### Separating Group Averages

**Example 18:**
A class of 40 students has an average score of 72. If the 15 boys have an average of 68, what is the girls' average?

$$
\text{Total (all)} = 72 \times 40 = 2{,}880
$$
$$
\text{Total (boys)} = 68 \times 15 = 1{,}020
$$
$$
\text{Total (girls)} = 2{,}880 - 1{,}020 = 1{,}860
$$
$$
\text{Girls' average} = \frac{1{,}860}{25} = 74.4
$$

#### Weighted Interpretation

Larger groups have more influence on the combined average. This is essentially a **weighted average** where the weights are the group sizes.

**Example 19:**
Department A (50 employees) has average performance rating 4.2. Department B (10 employees) has average rating 4.8. The combined average is:

$$
\frac{50 \times 4.2 + 10 \times 4.8}{60} = \frac{210 + 48}{60} = \frac{258}{60} = 4.3
$$

Department B's higher rating barely moves the needle because it has far fewer people.

#### Changing-Average Problems (Member Added/Removed)

**Example 20:**
The average weight of 9 players is 70 kg. A new player joins and the average becomes 71 kg. What is the new player's weight?

$$
\text{Old Total} = 70 \times 9 = 630 \text{ kg}
$$
$$
\text{New Total} = 71 \times 10 = 710 \text{ kg}
$$
$$
\text{New player} = 710 - 630 = 80 \text{ kg}
$$

#### Comparison of Group Averages

**Example 21:**
Team X (8 members) averages 45 points. Team Y (12 members) averages 55 points. Which team contributed more total points, and what is the overall average?

- Total X = 8 × 45 = 360
- Total Y = 12 × 55 = 660
- Team Y contributed more.
- Overall average = (360 + 660) ÷ 20 = 1,020 ÷ 20 = **51 points**

### 4.6 Multi-Step Average Word Problems

These problems require organizing information across multiple steps — finding intermediate totals, combining groups, or tracking changes sequentially.

**Example 22 — Sequential Changes:**
The average score of 5 students is 80. Two more students join with scores 90 and 95. Then one student with score 70 leaves. What is the final average?

Step 1: Original total = 80 × 5 = 400
Step 2: After two join: total = 400 + 90 + 95 = 585, count = 7
Step 3: After one leaves: total = 585 − 70 = 515, count = 6
Final average = 515 ÷ 6 ≈ **85.83**

**Example 23 — Business Application:**
A company's average revenue for Q1 (Jan–Mar) was ₱500,000/month. For Q2 (Apr–Jun), the average was ₱650,000/month. For the first half of the year, what is the average monthly revenue?

$$
\text{Total Q1} = 500{,}000 \times 3 = 1{,}500{,}000
$$
$$
\text{Total Q2} = 650{,}000 \times 3 = 1{,}950{,}000
$$
$$
\text{Average (6 months)} = \frac{1{,}500{,}000 + 1{,}950{,}000}{6} = \frac{3{,}450{,}000}{6} = ₱575{,}000
$$

**Example 24 — Transportation with Changing Conditions:**
A truck travels 150 km at 50 km/h (loaded), delivers its cargo, then returns the same 150 km at 75 km/h (empty). What is the average speed for the round trip?

- Time going = 150 ÷ 50 = 3 hours
- Time returning = 150 ÷ 75 = 2 hours
- Total distance = 300 km, Total time = 5 hours
- Average speed = 300 ÷ 5 = **60 km/h**

Or using harmonic mean: (2 × 50 × 75) ÷ (50 + 75) = 7,500 ÷ 125 = 60 km/h ✓

### 4.9 Problem-Solving Strategies

#### Step-by-Step System for Any Average Word Problem

1. **Identify** what is given: averages, counts, individual values
2. **Convert** every average into a total: Total = Average × Count
3. **Manipulate** the totals: add new members, subtract departing ones, combine groups
4. **Compute** the new average: New Total ÷ New Count
5. **Verify** — does the answer make sense? (It should fall between the minimum and maximum values)

#### Elimination Technique for Multiple Choice

If the average of a group is 50 and a new member with value 70 joins, the new average must be:
- Greater than 50 (new member is above average)
- Less than 70 (can't exceed the maximum)
- Closer to 50 than to 70 if the group is large

This eliminates impossible choices immediately.

#### Working Backward

When given the final average and asked for a missing value:
1. Compute the required total from the final average
2. Subtract all known values
3. The remainder is your answer

### 4.10 Estimation and Mental Math Techniques

#### Grouping Compatible Values

To mentally add 78 + 85 + 72 + 65:
- Group: (78 + 72) + (85 + 65) = 150 + 150 = 300
- Average = 300 ÷ 4 = 75

#### Deviation Method

Instead of adding large numbers, work with deviations from an estimated average:
- Estimate average ≈ 75
- Deviations: +3, +10, −3, −10 → sum of deviations = 0
- Actual average = 75 + 0/4 = 75 ✓

#### Reasonableness Check

The average MUST lie between the smallest and largest values. If your answer falls outside this range, you made an error.

For speed averages: the average speed must be less than or equal to the highest speed and greater than or equal to the lowest speed.

### 4.11 Common Errors in Average Word Problems

| Error | Why It Happens | How to Avoid |
|-------|---------------|--------------|
| Averaging speeds directly | Intuition says "add and divide by 2" | Always use Total Distance ÷ Total Time |
| Forgetting total sums | Jumping to division without computing the sum | First step: convert every average to a total |
| Dividing by wrong count | Miscounting after additions/removals | Write down the count explicitly at each step |
| Arithmetic mistakes | Rushing under time pressure | Use estimation to verify reasonableness |
| Wrong interpretation | Confusing "new average" with "new member's value" | Re-read what the question asks |
| Averaging averages | Treating group averages as equal-weight values | Use weighted formula: Σ(nᵢ × x̄ᵢ) ÷ Σnᵢ |
| Incomplete simplification | Leaving answer as fraction when choices are whole numbers | Simplify fully before selecting |

### Step-by-Step Procedures Summary

#### Age-Average Procedure
1. Total Age = Average Age × Number of People
2. Apply changes (add/remove person, time passes)
3. New Average = New Total ÷ New Count

#### Speed-Average Procedure
1. Find distance for each leg
2. Find time for each leg: Time = Distance ÷ Speed
3. Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time

#### Financial-Average Procedure
1. Total = Average × Number of Periods
2. Apply changes (new month, removed expense)
3. New Average = New Total ÷ New Count

#### Group-Average Procedure
1. Total for each group = Group Average × Group Size
2. Combined Total = Sum of all group totals
3. Combined Average = Combined Total ÷ Total Members

#### Answer Verification
- Is the answer between the min and max values?
- Does the direction make sense? (Adding a high value should raise the average)
- Do the units match?

### Exam Strategies

1. **Estimate before solving** — Quickly gauge where the answer should fall. This eliminates 1–2 choices immediately.
2. **Convert averages to totals immediately** — This is your most powerful move. Don't try to work with averages directly.
3. **Identify hidden totals** — "The average of 5 numbers is 40" means the total is 200. Extract this instantly.
4. **Simplify mentally** — Use the deviation method for close-together values.
5. **Watch for the speed trap** — Never average speeds unless times are equal.
6. **Check units** — Ensure distances are in the same unit, times are in the same unit.
7. **Use the answer choices** — If you're stuck, plug each choice back into the problem to see which one produces the stated average.

### CSE-Style Practice Examples

#### Easy Examples

**E1.** The ages of 5 children are 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16. What is their average age?

Solution: (8 + 10 + 12 + 14 + 16) ÷ 5 = 60 ÷ 5 = **12 years**

**E2.** A worker earns ₱450, ₱500, ₱480, and ₱570 over 4 days. What is the average daily earning?

Solution: (450 + 500 + 480 + 570) ÷ 4 = 2,000 ÷ 4 = **₱500**

**E3.** A car travels 200 km in 4 hours. What is the average speed?

Solution: 200 ÷ 4 = **50 km/h**

#### Medium Examples

**M1.** The average of 6 numbers is 45. If one number (30) is removed, what is the new average?

Solution: Total = 45 × 6 = 270. New total = 270 − 30 = 240. New average = 240 ÷ 5 = **48**

**M2.** A cyclist rides 40 km at 20 km/h and returns at 10 km/h. What is the average speed?

Solution: Time₁ = 40 ÷ 20 = 2h. Time₂ = 40 ÷ 10 = 4h. Average = 80 ÷ 6 ≈ **13.33 km/h**
Or: (2 × 20 × 10) ÷ (20 + 10) = 400 ÷ 30 ≈ 13.33 km/h

**M3.** Class A (25 students) averages 82. Class B (35 students) averages 76. Combined average?

Solution: (25 × 82 + 35 × 76) ÷ 60 = (2,050 + 2,660) ÷ 60 = 4,710 ÷ 60 = **78.5**

#### Hard Examples

**H1.** The average salary of 20 employees is ₱30,000. When a manager (₱55,000) and an intern (₱15,000) join, what is the new average?

Solution: Old total = 30,000 × 20 = 600,000. New total = 600,000 + 55,000 + 15,000 = 670,000. New average = 670,000 ÷ 22 ≈ **₱30,454.55**

**H2.** The average of 8 numbers is 50. The average of the first 3 is 45 and the average of the last 3 is 55. What is the average of the middle 2 numbers?

Solution: Total = 50 × 8 = 400. First 3 total = 135. Last 3 total = 165. Middle 2 total = 400 − 135 − 165 = 100. Average = 100 ÷ 2 = **50**

**H3.** A train covers the first 1/3 of a journey at 40 km/h, the next 1/3 at 60 km/h, and the last 1/3 at 80 km/h. What is the average speed?

Let total distance = 240 km (divisible by 3). Each segment = 80 km.
- Time₁ = 80 ÷ 40 = 2h
- Time₂ = 80 ÷ 60 = 4/3 h
- Time₃ = 80 ÷ 80 = 1h
- Total time = 2 + 4/3 + 1 = 13/3 h
- Average speed = 240 ÷ (13/3) = 240 × 3/13 = 720/13 ≈ **55.38 km/h**

### Mini Practice Set (20 Questions)

**1.** The average of 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 is:
Answer: (10+20+30+40+50) ÷ 5 = 150 ÷ 5 = **30**

**2.** The average age of 3 siblings is 12. Their total age is:
Answer: 12 × 3 = **36 years**

**3.** A car travels 180 km in 3 hours. Average speed:
Answer: 180 ÷ 3 = **60 km/h**

**4.** Average of 4 numbers is 25. If a fifth number (35) is added, new average:
Answer: Total = 100 + 35 = 135. Average = 135 ÷ 5 = **27**

**5.** Monthly expenses: ₱8,000, ₱9,500, ₱7,200, ₱10,300. Average:
Answer: 35,000 ÷ 4 = **₱8,750**

**6.** Average of 6 students is 75. One student scoring 60 leaves. New average:
Answer: Total = 450. New = 450 − 60 = 390. Average = 390 ÷ 5 = **78**

**7.** A bus goes 100 km at 50 km/h and returns at 25 km/h. Average speed:
Answer: (2 × 50 × 25) ÷ (50 + 25) = 2500 ÷ 75 = **33.33 km/h**

**8.** Group A (10 people, avg 40) and Group B (15 people, avg 50). Combined average:
Answer: (400 + 750) ÷ 25 = 1150 ÷ 25 = **46**

**9.** Average salary of 5 workers is ₱20,000. A new worker earning ₱32,000 joins. New average:
Answer: (100,000 + 32,000) ÷ 6 = 132,000 ÷ 6 = **₱22,000**

**10.** Average age of 4 friends is 28. In 5 years, their average age will be:
Answer: 28 + 5 = **33 years**

**11.** The average of 7 numbers is 14. Sum of the numbers:
Answer: 14 × 7 = **98**

**12.** A student scores 85, 90, 78, 92, and needs average of 88. Score needed on 5th test:
Answer: Target total = 88 × 5 = 440. Current = 345. Needed = 440 − 345 = **95**

**13.** Average weight of 12 boxes is 8 kg. One box (14 kg) is removed. New average:
Answer: Total = 96. New = 96 − 14 = 82. Average = 82 ÷ 11 ≈ **7.45 kg**

**14.** A delivery van travels 60 km at 30 km/h, then 90 km at 45 km/h. Average speed:
Answer: Time₁ = 2h, Time₂ = 2h. Total = 150 km ÷ 4h = **37.5 km/h**

**15.** Average of 20 values is 50. Average of first 12 is 45. Average of last 8:
Answer: Total = 1000. First 12 = 540. Last 8 = 460. Average = 460 ÷ 8 = **57.5**

**16.** 5 employees average ₱25,000. One earning ₱30,000 is replaced by one earning ₱20,000. New average:
Answer: Old total = 125,000. Change = −10,000. New total = 115,000. Average = 115,000 ÷ 5 = **₱23,000**

**17.** Average of 3 numbers is 60. Two of them are 55 and 70. Third number:
Answer: Total = 180. Third = 180 − 55 − 70 = **55**

**18.** A train covers 300 km. First 100 km at 50 km/h, next 200 km at 100 km/h. Average speed:
Answer: Time₁ = 2h, Time₂ = 2h. Average = 300 ÷ 4 = **75 km/h**

**19.** Class of 40 students averages 70. Boys (15) average 65. Girls' average:
Answer: Total = 2800. Boys = 975. Girls = 1825. Average = 1825 ÷ 25 = **73**

**20.** Average daily sales for Mon–Fri is ₱15,000. Sat sales are ₱25,000. Average for 6 days:
Answer: Total = 75,000 + 25,000 = 100,000. Average = 100,000 ÷ 6 ≈ **₱16,666.67**

### Quick Recap

| Concept | Key Formula |
|---------|-------------|
| Average (Mean) | Sum ÷ Count |
| Total from Average | Average × Count |
| Age: future average | Current average + years elapsed |
| Age: person joins | (Old Total + New Age) ÷ (Old Count + 1) |
| Speed average | Total Distance ÷ Total Time |
| Speed (equal distance) | 2v₁v₂ ÷ (v₁ + v₂) |
| Financial average | Total amount ÷ Number of periods |
| Combined groups | (n₁×x̄₁ + n₂×x̄₂) ÷ (n₁ + n₂) |

### Memory Aids

- **"TAC" — Total, Average, Count** — These three are always connected. Know any two, find the third.
- **"Total First"** — In every word problem, your first move is converting averages to totals.
- **"Speed Trap"** — Never average speeds directly. Always go back to Distance ÷ Time.
- **"Big Group Wins"** — In combined averages, the larger group pulls the result toward its average.
- **"Everyone Ages Together"** — If k years pass, the average increases by exactly k (no calculation needed).
- **"Balance Point"** — The average is where positive and negative deviations cancel out.
