# Ratio Word Problems

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Ratio word problems** are mathematical exercises where relationships between quantities are described in everyday language, and you must translate those verbal descriptions into numerical ratios to find a solution. Unlike straightforward computation problems (e.g., "simplify 12:8"), ratio word problems require you to *interpret* a situation, *identify* the quantities being compared, *set up* the correct ratio, and *solve* for an unknown value.

These problems appear extensively in the Philippine Civil Service Examination because they mirror real-world tasks that government employees perform daily:

- **Budgeting:** "The department allocates ₱3 for personnel services for every ₱2 for maintenance and other operating expenses."
- **Business and procurement:** "A supplier delivers 5 boxes of bond paper for every 2 boxes of folder supplies."
- **Transportation:** "For every 3 buses assigned to Route A, 7 buses serve Route B."
- **Inventory management:** "The warehouse stocks 4 units of Item X for every 1 unit of Item Y."
- **Engineering and construction:** "The concrete mix uses 1 part cement to 2 parts sand to 4 parts gravel."
- **Classroom statistics:** "There are 2 teachers for every 45 students in the public school system."
- **Recipes and food service:** "The cafeteria uses 3 cups of rice for every 5 cups of water."
- **Government reports:** "Out of every 100 applicants, 35 pass the Civil Service Exam."

#### Why Ratio Word Problems Commonly Appear in the CSE

The Civil Service Exam tests your ability to:
1. **Interpret written information accurately** — a core competency for processing memos, reports, and directives
2. **Identify relationships between data points** — essential for budget analysis and statistical reporting
3. **Solve problems systematically** — required for planning, resource allocation, and decision-making
4. **Work under time pressure** — ratio word problems reward efficient, organized thinking

#### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Reversing the ratio order** — writing girls:boys when the problem asks for boys:girls
2. **Confusing part-to-part with part-to-whole** — using 3:5 when the total is needed (3:8)
3. **Ignoring context clues** — missing keywords like "for every," "out of," or "to"
4. **Incorrect scaling** — multiplying only one term of the ratio
5. **Arithmetic carelessness** — simple multiplication or division errors under time pressure
6. **Using the wrong total** — adding ratio parts incorrectly before distributing
7. **Misidentifying what is being asked** — solving for the wrong quantity
8. **Skipping unit verification** — not checking whether the answer makes logical sense

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Translate word statements into ratios correctly and in the proper order
- Identify the quantities being compared in any verbal ratio statement
- Distinguish part-to-part from part-to-whole relationships
- Solve ratio word problems systematically using scaling and equivalent ratios
- Solve missing-value and multi-step ratio problems
- Apply ratios to practical situations involving budgets, recipes, populations, and workplace scenarios
- Solve CSE-style ratio word problems efficiently under time pressure

### 4.1 Understanding Ratio Word Problems

#### What Are Ratio Word Problems?

A ratio word problem presents a real-world scenario in natural language and asks you to:
1. Identify the quantities being compared
2. Express their relationship as a ratio
3. Use that ratio to find an unknown value

The challenge is not the arithmetic — it's the *interpretation*. The same numbers can mean different things depending on how the sentence is structured.

#### Converting Words into Mathematical Relationships

Every ratio word problem contains **signal words** that tell you a comparison is being made:

| Signal Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| "for every" | Direct ratio relationship | "2 teachers *for every* 35 students" → 2:35 |
| "to" | Comparison between quantities | "The ratio of boys *to* girls is 3:5" → 3:5 |
| "out of" | Part-to-whole relationship | "10 *out of* 40 employees are supervisors" → 10:40 or 1:4 |
| "per" | Rate or unit ratio | "₱50 *per* kilogram" → 50:1 |
| "in every" | Frequency within a group | "3 defective items *in every* 100" → 3:100 |
| "as many as" | Multiplicative comparison | "twice *as many* boys as girls" → 2:1 |
| "compared to" | Direct comparison | "5 *compared to* 8" → 5:8 |

#### Identifying Compared Quantities

Before writing any ratio, ask yourself:
- **What two (or more) things are being compared?**
- **In what order does the problem state them?**
- **Is it part-to-part or part-to-whole?**

**Example 1:** "There are 2 teachers for every 35 students."
- Compared quantities: teachers and students
- Order: teachers first, students second
- Ratio: 2:35 (part-to-part)

**Example 2:** "The ratio of boys to girls is 3:5."
- Compared quantities: boys and girls
- Order: boys first, girls second
- Ratio: 3:5 (part-to-part)

**Example 3:** "Out of 40 employees, 10 are supervisors."
- Compared quantities: supervisors and total employees
- Order: supervisors to total
- Ratio: 10:40 = 1:4 (part-to-whole)

#### Determining Ratio Order

**The order in which quantities appear in the ratio MUST match the order stated in the problem.** This is the single most common source of errors.

- "boys to girls" → boys:girls (NOT girls:boys)
- "cement to sand" → cement:sand
- "wins to losses" → wins:losses

**Trap:** Some problems state the second quantity first in the sentence:
- "There are 20 girls. The ratio of boys to girls is 3:4."
- Even though "girls" appears first in the sentence, the ratio is boys:girls = 3:4.

**Rule:** Always follow the ratio label, not the sentence order.

#### Understanding Context Clues

Context clues help you determine:
- Whether the problem asks for a part or the whole
- Whether you need to find one quantity or a total
- Whether the ratio needs to be simplified or scaled

**Practice Breakdown:**

*"A school has 480 students. The ratio of boys to girls is 5:7. How many boys are there?"*

Step-by-step interpretation:
1. Total students = 480
2. Ratio of boys to girls = 5:7
3. Total parts = 5 + 7 = 12
4. Boys = (5/12) × 480 = 200
5. The question asks for boys → Answer: 200

### 4.2 Translating Word Statements into Ratios

#### The Conversion Process

Translating a verbal statement into a ratio follows a consistent procedure:

**Step 1:** Identify the keyword or signal phrase
**Step 2:** Identify the two quantities being compared
**Step 3:** Determine the correct order
**Step 4:** Write the ratio in proper notation

#### Sentence-to-Ratio Drills

| Verbal Statement | Ratio |
|-----------------|-------|
| "5 apples for every 2 oranges" | 5:2 |
| "7 employees to 1 manager" | 7:1 |
| "12 liters out of 20 liters" | 12:20 = 3:5 |
| "For every 3 wins, there are 2 losses" | 3:2 |
| "The mixture contains 4 parts water and 1 part bleach" | 4:1 |
| "Twice as many women as men" | 2:1 (women:men) |
| "₱150 for every 3 kilograms" | 150:3 = 50:1 |
| "1 supervisor for every 8 staff members" | 1:8 |

#### Importance of Ratio Order

Consider this statement: "The ratio of red balls to blue balls is 3:7."

- **Correct:** red:blue = 3:7
- **Wrong:** blue:red = 3:7

If the problem later asks "how many blue balls are there?" you must use the 7 part, not the 3 part. Reversing the order gives you the wrong answer every time.

#### Avoiding Reversed Ratios

**Strategy:** Underline or circle the first quantity mentioned in the ratio label. That quantity always gets the first number.

"The ratio of **cats** to **dogs** is 2:5" → cats = 2 parts, dogs = 5 parts

Even if the problem says "There are 25 dogs in the shelter," you still assign dogs = 5 parts.

#### Classroom Examples

1. "In a class of 50, 30 are girls." → girls:total = 30:50 = 3:5; girls:boys = 30:20 = 3:2
2. "A teacher assigns 4 homework problems for every 1 quiz problem." → homework:quiz = 4:1
3. "The passing rate is 7 out of 10." → passing:total = 7:10

#### Workplace Examples

1. "The office has 3 clerks for every 1 senior officer." → clerks:officers = 3:1
2. "Budget allocation: ₱5 million for salaries, ₱2 million for operations." → salaries:operations = 5:2
3. "For every 10 applications received, 4 are approved." → approved:received = 4:10 = 2:5

#### Transportation Examples

1. "Route A has 6 buses for every 4 buses on Route B." → A:B = 6:4 = 3:2
2. "A jeepney carries 20 passengers per trip; a bus carries 60." → jeepney:bus = 20:60 = 1:3
3. "For every 5 km of highway, there are 2 km of local roads." → highway:local = 5:2

### 4.3 Part-to-Part and Part-to-Whole Applications

#### Identifying Relationship Types

Every ratio word problem involves one of two relationship types:

**Part-to-Part:** Compares one group to another group within the same whole.
- "boys to girls" — both are parts of the total students
- "red to blue" — both are parts of the total balls
- "wins to losses" — both are parts of total games

**Part-to-Whole:** Compares one group to the entire collection.
- "boys out of all students" — one part compared to the total
- "defective items out of total production" — one part compared to the total
- "passing students out of all examinees" — one part compared to the total

#### How Wording Changes Ratio Meaning

The same situation can produce different ratios depending on what is asked:

**Situation:** A bag contains 3 red balls and 5 blue balls.

| Question | Type | Ratio |
|----------|------|-------|
| "What is the ratio of red to blue?" | Part-to-part | 3:5 |
| "What is the ratio of red to total?" | Part-to-whole | 3:8 |
| "What is the ratio of blue to total?" | Part-to-whole | 5:8 |
| "What is the ratio of blue to red?" | Part-to-part | 5:3 |

#### Identifying the "Whole"

The whole (total) is the sum of all parts in the ratio:
- If boys:girls = 3:5, then total parts = 3 + 5 = 8
- If cement:sand:gravel = 1:2:4, then total parts = 1 + 2 + 4 = 7
- If A:B:C = 2:3:5, then total parts = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10

**Critical rule:** Only add ratio parts when you need the whole. If the problem gives you the whole directly, use it.

#### Grouping Diagrams

Visualize ratios as groups:

**Boys:Girls = 3:5, Total = 40 students**

```
Boys:  [■][■][■]         → 3 parts
Girls: [■][■][■][■][■]   → 5 parts
Total: 8 parts = 40 students
Each part = 40 ÷ 8 = 5 students
Boys = 3 × 5 = 15
Girls = 5 × 5 = 25
```

#### Budgeting Examples

"A barangay allocates its ₱100,000 fund in the ratio 2:3:5 for health, education, and infrastructure."

- Total parts = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10
- Health = (2/10) × ₱100,000 = ₱20,000
- Education = (3/10) × ₱100,000 = ₱30,000
- Infrastructure = (5/10) × ₱100,000 = ₱50,000

#### Inventory Examples

"A warehouse has items in the ratio 4:3:1 for Category A, B, and C. Total items: 640."

- Total parts = 4 + 3 + 1 = 8
- Category A = (4/8) × 640 = 320
- Category B = (3/8) × 640 = 240
- Category C = (1/8) × 640 = 80

### 4.4 Solving Missing-Value Ratio Problems

#### What Are Missing-Value Problems?

These problems give you a ratio and one actual quantity, then ask you to find the other quantity. They are the most common ratio word problem type on the CSE.

**General form:** "The ratio of A to B is x:y. If A = n, find B."

#### The Scaling Method

The simplest approach: determine the **scale factor** (how many times the ratio has been multiplied to get the actual value), then apply it to find the unknown.

**Example:** The ratio of boys to girls is 2:3. If there are 14 boys, how many girls are there?

1. Ratio: boys:girls = 2:3
2. Actual boys = 14
3. Scale factor = 14 ÷ 2 = 7
4. Girls = 3 × 7 = **21**

#### The Proportion Method

Set up equivalent fractions and cross-multiply:

**Example:** If 2:3 = 14:x, find x.

$$\frac{2}{3} = \frac{14}{x}$$
$$2x = 3 \times 14$$
$$2x = 42$$
$$x = 21$$

#### Step-by-Step System for Missing-Value Problems

1. **Identify the ratio** — write it in x:y form
2. **Identify the known actual value** — which part of the ratio does it correspond to?
3. **Calculate the scale factor** — divide the actual value by its ratio part
4. **Apply the scale factor** — multiply the unknown ratio part by the scale factor
5. **Verify** — check that your answer maintains the original ratio

#### Ratio Tables

A ratio table organizes the scaling visually:

| | Boys | Girls |
|---|---|---|
| Ratio | 2 | 3 |
| ×7 | 14 | 21 |

Another example:

**"If 5 notebooks cost ₱100, how much do 15 notebooks cost?"**

| | Notebooks | Cost (₱) |
|---|---|---|
| Given | 5 | 100 |
| ×3 | 15 | 300 |

Scale factor = 15 ÷ 5 = 3, so cost = 100 × 3 = ₱300.

#### More Examples

**Example 1:** "The ratio of teachers to students is 1:25. If there are 8 teachers, how many students are there?"
- Scale factor = 8 ÷ 1 = 8
- Students = 25 × 8 = **200**

**Example 2:** "Flour and sugar are mixed in the ratio 3:1. If you use 12 cups of flour, how many cups of sugar do you need?"
- Scale factor = 12 ÷ 3 = 4
- Sugar = 1 × 4 = **4 cups**

**Example 3:** "The ratio of passed to failed is 7:3. If 210 students passed, how many failed?"
- Scale factor = 210 ÷ 7 = 30
- Failed = 3 × 30 = **90**

**Example 4:** "A map scale is 1:50,000. If two cities are 3 cm apart on the map, what is the actual distance?"
- Scale factor = 3 ÷ 1 = 3
- Actual distance = 50,000 × 3 = 150,000 cm = **1.5 km**

### 4.5 Multi-Step Ratio Word Problems

#### What Makes a Problem "Multi-Step"?

Multi-step ratio problems require more than one operation to solve. They may involve:
- Finding a total before distributing
- Combining two different ratios
- Working backwards from a result
- Comparing multiple groups
- Adding or removing quantities from a ratio

#### Solving Problems with Multiple Operations

**Example 1: Splitting a Total**

"Three friends share ₱4,800 in the ratio 2:3:7. How much does the one with the largest share receive?"

1. Total parts = 2 + 3 + 7 = 12
2. Value per part = ₱4,800 ÷ 12 = ₱400
3. Largest share (7 parts) = 7 × ₱400 = **₱2,800**

**Example 2: Finding the Difference**

"The ratio of men to women in an office is 5:3. If there are 64 employees total, how many more men than women are there?"

1. Total parts = 5 + 3 = 8
2. Value per part = 64 ÷ 8 = 8
3. Men = 5 × 8 = 40
4. Women = 3 × 8 = 24
5. Difference = 40 − 24 = **16**

**Example 3: Working Backwards**

"After 10 new girls joined a class, the ratio of boys to girls became 2:3. If there are 18 boys, how many girls were there originally?"

1. New ratio: boys:girls = 2:3
2. Boys = 18, so scale factor = 18 ÷ 2 = 9
3. Current girls = 3 × 9 = 27
4. Original girls = 27 − 10 = **17**

**Example 4: Combining Ratios**

"The ratio of A to B is 2:3, and the ratio of B to C is 3:5. What is the ratio of A to C?"

1. A:B = 2:3
2. B:C = 3:5
3. Since B = 3 in both ratios, they can be combined directly
4. A:B:C = 2:3:5
5. A:C = **2:5**

**Example 5: Changing Ratios**

"The ratio of red to blue marbles is 4:5. If 6 red marbles are added, the ratio becomes 2:1. How many blue marbles are there?"

1. Let red = 4x, blue = 5x
2. After adding 6 red: (4x + 6):5x = 2:1
3. 4x + 6 = 2(5x)
4. 4x + 6 = 10x
5. 6 = 6x → x = 1
6. Blue = 5(1) = **5**

#### Workplace Scenarios

"A government office has 120 employees. The ratio of permanent to contractual to job-order workers is 5:3:2. If 12 contractual workers are converted to permanent, what is the new ratio?"

1. Total parts = 5 + 3 + 2 = 10
2. Each part = 120 ÷ 10 = 12
3. Original: Permanent = 60, Contractual = 36, Job-order = 24
4. After conversion: Permanent = 72, Contractual = 24, Job-order = 24
5. New ratio = 72:24:24 = **3:1:1**

#### Budgeting Problems

"A department's ₱500,000 budget is split between salaries and operations in the ratio 7:3. If operations receives an additional ₱50,000 from a supplemental fund, what is the new ratio of salaries to operations?"

1. Salaries = (7/10) × 500,000 = ₱350,000
2. Operations = (3/10) × 500,000 = ₱150,000
3. New operations = ₱150,000 + ₱50,000 = ₱200,000
4. New ratio = 350,000:200,000 = **7:4**

### 4.7 Real-Life Applications of Ratio Word Problems

#### Recipes

"A recipe calls for flour and butter in the ratio 3:1. If you want to use 9 cups of flour, how many cups of butter do you need?"
- Scale factor = 9 ÷ 3 = 3
- Butter = 1 × 3 = **3 cups**

#### Salary Allocation

"An employee allocates her monthly salary in the ratio 5:3:2 for needs, savings, and wants. If her salary is ₱30,000, how much goes to savings?"
- Total parts = 10
- Savings = (3/10) × 30,000 = **₱9,000**

#### Classroom Population

"In a school, the student-to-teacher ratio is 35:1. If there are 1,050 students, how many teachers are needed?"
- Scale factor = 1,050 ÷ 35 = 30
- Teachers = 1 × 30 = **30**

#### Engineering Measurements

"A concrete mix requires cement, sand, and gravel in the ratio 1:2:4. If 3 bags of cement are used, how many bags of gravel are needed?"
- Scale factor = 3 ÷ 1 = 3
- Gravel = 4 × 3 = **12 bags**

#### Transportation Systems

"A bus company assigns vehicles to Route A and Route B in the ratio 3:5. If 24 buses serve Route A, how many serve Route B?"
- Scale factor = 24 ÷ 3 = 8
- Route B = 5 × 8 = **40 buses**

#### Inventory Management

"A store stocks shirts and pants in the ratio 7:4. If there are 280 shirts, how many pants are in stock?"
- Scale factor = 280 ÷ 7 = 40
- Pants = 4 × 40 = **160**

#### Business Statistics

"A company's revenue to expenses ratio is 5:3. If revenue is ₱2,500,000, what are the expenses?"
- Scale factor = 2,500,000 ÷ 5 = 500,000
- Expenses = 3 × 500,000 = **₱1,500,000**

#### Map Scales

"A map has a scale of 1:200,000. If two towns are 4.5 cm apart on the map, what is the actual distance in kilometers?"
- Actual distance = 4.5 × 200,000 = 900,000 cm = **9 km**

#### Sports Statistics

"A basketball player's free throw ratio is 7:3 (made:missed). If she attempted 80 free throws, how many did she make?"
- Total parts = 7 + 3 = 10
- Made = (7/10) × 80 = **56**

#### Government Budgeting

"A municipality divides its ₱12,000,000 annual budget among education, health, and infrastructure in the ratio 4:3:5. How much does health receive?"
- Total parts = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12
- Health = (3/12) × 12,000,000 = **₱3,000,000**

### 4.8 Problem-Solving Strategies

#### The 5-Step System

**Step 1: Read Carefully**
Read the entire problem before writing anything. Identify what is given and what is asked.

**Step 2: Identify What Is Asked**
Circle or underline the question. Is it asking for a quantity, a ratio, a difference, or a total?

**Step 3: Organize Known Information**
Write down:
- The ratio (in correct order)
- Any actual values given
- The total (if provided)

**Step 4: Check Units and Ratio Order**
Verify that your ratio matches the order stated in the problem. Confirm units are consistent.

**Step 5: Solve and Verify**
Calculate the answer, then check:
- Does the answer maintain the original ratio?
- Is the answer reasonable given the context?
- Did you answer what was actually asked?

#### Quick Checking Methods

1. **Ratio verification:** If boys:girls = 3:5 and you found boys = 21, girls = 35, check: 21/35 = 3/5 ✓
2. **Total check:** If parts sum to 8 and total is 40, each part = 5. Verify: 3(5) + 5(5) = 15 + 25 = 40 ✓
3. **Reasonableness:** If the ratio is 1:9 and total is 100, the smaller group should be about 10. If you got 50, something is wrong.

#### Elimination Strategies for Multiple Choice

1. **Eliminate answers that don't maintain the ratio** — if ratio is 2:3 and total is 50, the answer must be divisible appropriately
2. **Eliminate unreasonable magnitudes** — if the total is 100, an answer of 500 is impossible
3. **Use estimation** — if ratio is 3:7 and total is 200, the larger part is roughly 140 (7/10 × 200)

### 4.9 Estimation and Mental Math Techniques

#### Quick Scaling Recognition

Train yourself to recognize common scale factors instantly:

| Ratio Part | Actual Value | Scale Factor |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 2 | 14 | ×7 |
| 3 | 21 | ×7 |
| 5 | 35 | ×7 |
| 4 | 28 | ×7 |
| 3 | 45 | ×15 |
| 7 | 63 | ×9 |

#### Estimating Equivalent Ratios Mentally

**Technique 1: Doubling/Halving**
- 3:5 → 6:10 → 12:20 → 24:40 (keep doubling both sides)
- 8:12 → 4:6 → 2:3 (keep halving both sides)

**Technique 2: Benchmark Fractions**
- 1:4 means "one-quarter" — if total is 100, the smaller part is about 20
- 2:3 means "two-fifths vs three-fifths" — if total is 50, parts are 20 and 30
- 3:7 means "three-tenths vs seven-tenths" — if total is 200, parts are 60 and 140

**Technique 3: Percentage Equivalents**
- 1:1 = 50% each
- 1:3 = 25% and 75%
- 1:4 = 20% and 80%
- 2:3 = 40% and 60%
- 3:7 = 30% and 70%

#### Identifying Proportional Patterns Quickly

When you see a ratio problem in multiple choice, quickly estimate:
- If ratio is 2:5 and total is 70, larger part ≈ 50 (actually 50). Scan choices for 50.
- If ratio is 3:4 and one part is 21, other part ≈ 28 (since 21/3 = 7, 4×7 = 28).

#### Mental Drills

Practice these mentally (answers below):
1. Ratio 2:3, total = 50. Larger part = ?
2. Ratio 1:5, smaller part = 8. Larger part = ?
3. Ratio 4:7, total = 110. Smaller part = ?
4. Ratio 5:3, larger part = 45. Total = ?

**Answers:** 1) 30  2) 40  3) 40  4) 72

### 4.10 Common Errors in Ratio Word Problems

#### Error 1: Reversing Ratio Order

**Wrong:** "The ratio of cats to dogs is 3:5" → writing dogs:cats = 3:5
**Right:** cats:dogs = 3:5

**Prevention:** Always label your ratio. Write "cats:dogs = 3:5" not just "3:5."

#### Error 2: Confusing Part-to-Part and Part-to-Whole

**Problem:** "Boys and girls are in the ratio 2:3. What fraction of the class are boys?"
**Wrong:** 2/3 (this is boys:girls, not boys:total)
**Right:** 2/5 (boys:total = 2:(2+3) = 2:5)

**Prevention:** Ask yourself: "Am I comparing to another part, or to the whole?"

#### Error 3: Ignoring Units

**Problem:** "A rope is cut in the ratio 2:5. The shorter piece is 40 cm. Find the total length."
**Wrong:** Total = 40 × 5 = 200 cm (this finds only the longer piece)
**Right:** Scale factor = 40 ÷ 2 = 20. Total = (2+5) × 20 = 140 cm

#### Error 4: Incorrect Scaling

**Wrong:** Multiplying only one side of the ratio
- 3:5, scale by 4 → 12:5 (WRONG — must scale both sides)
**Right:** 3:5, scale by 4 → 12:20

#### Error 5: Arithmetic Carelessness

Common arithmetic mistakes:
- 7 × 8 = 54 (should be 56)
- 144 ÷ 12 = 14 (should be 12)
- 3 + 5 + 7 = 14 (should be 15)

**Prevention:** Double-check arithmetic, especially under time pressure.

#### Error 6: Misunderstanding Wording

**Problem:** "For every 3 boys, there are 5 girls" vs. "There are 3 more boys than girls"
- First statement: ratio is 3:5
- Second statement: this is NOT a ratio problem — it's a difference problem

**Prevention:** Look for ratio keywords ("for every," "to," "out of"). If the problem uses "more than" or "less than" without ratio language, it may not be a ratio problem.

#### Error 7: Using the Wrong Total

**Problem:** "The ratio of A to B to C is 2:3:5. If A = 20, find the total."
**Wrong:** Total = 20 × 10 = 200 (multiplied A by total parts)
**Right:** Scale factor = 20 ÷ 2 = 10. Total = (2+3+5) × 10 = 100

### Step-by-Step Rules Summary

#### Verbal-to-Ratio Conversion Procedure
1. Read the statement completely
2. Identify signal words ("for every," "to," "out of," "per")
3. Identify the two quantities being compared
4. Write them in the order stated
5. Simplify if required

#### Missing-Value Solving System
1. Write the ratio in x:y form
2. Match the known value to its ratio part
3. Divide to find the scale factor
4. Multiply the unknown ratio part by the scale factor
5. Verify the answer maintains the ratio

#### Ratio Scaling Procedure
1. Identify the original ratio
2. Determine what factor is needed (from given information)
3. Multiply ALL parts of the ratio by that factor
4. Sum the scaled parts if total is needed

#### Answer Verification Method
1. Divide your answers by their respective ratio parts
2. All divisions should yield the same scale factor
3. If they don't match, recheck your work

### Common Mistakes Summary

| Mistake | Why It Happens | How to Avoid |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| Reversed ratio order | Reading sentence order instead of ratio label | Always label: "A:B = x:y" |
| Part-to-part vs part-to-whole confusion | Not identifying whether total is involved | Ask: "Compared to what?" |
| Incomplete simplification | Dividing by a common factor that isn't the GCF | Check if further simplification is possible |
| Skipping steps | Rushing under time pressure | Write each step, even briefly |
| Arithmetic errors | Mental math mistakes | Verify with reverse operation |
| Unit inconsistency | Mixing cm with m, or ₱ with thousands | Convert all to same unit first |

#### Simplifying Mentally
- Both numbers even? Halve both: 8:12 → 4:6 → 2:3
- Both divisible by 5? Divide: 15:25 → 3:5
- One number is a multiple of the other? Divide: 6:18 → 1:3

#### Estimating Before Solving
Before calculating, estimate the answer:
- If ratio is 1:4 and total is 500, smaller part ≈ 100
- If ratio is 3:2 and larger part is 60, smaller part ≈ 40

#### Eliminating Unreasonable Choices
- Answer cannot exceed the total
- Answer must be positive
- Answer should be a whole number if the context requires it (people, items)
- Answer must maintain the ratio when checked

#### Solving Efficiently Under Time Pressure
1. Skip the problem if it requires more than 3 steps and you're running low on time
2. Use estimation to narrow choices to 2, then calculate precisely
3. For "splitting total" problems: divide total by sum of parts, then multiply
4. For "find the other" problems: divide known by its ratio part, multiply by unknown's ratio part

### CSE-Like Examples

#### Easy Examples

**Example 1:**
"The ratio of pens to pencils in a box is 2:5. If there are 10 pens, how many pencils are there?"
- Scale factor = 10 ÷ 2 = 5
- Pencils = 5 × 5 = **25**
- Ratio analysis: 10:25 = 2:5 ✓

**Example 2:**
"A recipe uses sugar and flour in the ratio 1:4. If 3 cups of sugar are used, how many cups of flour are needed?"
- Scale factor = 3 ÷ 1 = 3
- Flour = 4 × 3 = **12 cups**
- Ratio analysis: 3:12 = 1:4 ✓

**Example 3:**
"In a class of 45 students, the ratio of boys to girls is 4:5. How many boys are there?"
- Total parts = 4 + 5 = 9
- Each part = 45 ÷ 9 = 5
- Boys = 4 × 5 = **20**
- Ratio analysis: 20:25 = 4:5 ✓

#### Medium Examples

**Example 4:**
"Three siblings share an inheritance of ₱900,000 in the ratio 2:3:4. How much more does the eldest (largest share) receive than the youngest (smallest share)?"
- Total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9
- Each part = 900,000 ÷ 9 = 100,000
- Eldest = 4 × 100,000 = 400,000
- Youngest = 2 × 100,000 = 200,000
- Difference = **₱200,000**

**Example 5:**
"The ratio of men to women in a company is 5:7. If there are 84 women, what is the total number of employees?"
- Scale factor = 84 ÷ 7 = 12
- Men = 5 × 12 = 60
- Total = 60 + 84 = **144**

#### Hard Examples

**Example 6:**
"The ratio of A's income to B's income is 5:3. The ratio of A's expenditure to B's expenditure is 3:1. If each saves ₱2,000, find A's income."
- Let A's income = 5x, B's income = 3x
- Let A's expenditure = 3y, B's expenditure = y
- Savings: A: 5x − 3y = 2000; B: 3x − y = 2000
- From B: y = 3x − 2000
- Substitute: 5x − 3(3x − 2000) = 2000
- 5x − 9x + 6000 = 2000
- −4x = −4000 → x = 1000
- A's income = 5(1000) = **₱5,000**

**Example 7:**
"A bag contains red, blue, and green marbles in the ratio 3:5:2. If 12 green marbles are removed and 12 red marbles are added, the new ratio becomes 3:2:0. How many blue marbles are there?"
- Original: red = 3x, blue = 5x, green = 2x
- Green removed: 2x = 12 → x = 6
- Blue = 5(6) = **30**

### Quick Recap

| Concept | Key Takeaway |
|---------|-------------|
| Translating words to ratios | Look for signal words; maintain stated order |
| Identifying quantities | Ask "what two things are being compared?" |
| Part-to-part vs part-to-whole | Part-to-part compares groups; part-to-whole compares to total |
| Missing values | Find scale factor = actual ÷ ratio part |
| Multi-step problems | Break into sub-problems; find total parts first |
| Practical applications | Ratios appear in budgets, recipes, maps, populations, and more |

---

### Memory Aids

1. **"Order Matters"** — The first word in "A to B" always gets the first number
2. **"Parts Plus Parts = Whole"** — Add all ratio parts to get the total denominator
3. **"Scale Factor is King"** — Divide any known actual value by its ratio part to unlock everything
4. **"Check with Division"** — Divide each actual value by its ratio part; all should equal the same number
5. **"For Every = Ratio"** — Whenever you see "for every," you're dealing with a ratio
6. **"Out Of = Part-to-Whole"** — "Out of" always means comparing to the total
