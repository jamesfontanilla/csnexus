# Division

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Division** is one of the four fundamental arithmetic operations — the process of splitting a quantity into equal parts or determining how many times one number is contained within another. Division is the inverse of multiplication: if 6 × 4 = 24, then 24 ÷ 4 = 6 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4. In the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE), division appears in direct computation items, word problems involving distribution, rate calculations, and as a critical sub-step in percentage, ratio, and proportion questions.

This subtopic covers four critical areas:
- **Division of Whole Numbers** — single-digit through multi-digit division using long division
- **Division of Integers** — applying sign rules when dividing positive and negative numbers
- **Division of Decimals** — handling decimal points in dividend and divisor correctly
- **Division of Fractions** — using the reciprocal (Keep-Change-Flip) method

Mastering division is essential for any government employee. Budget allocation, per-capita calculations, resource distribution, payroll computation, and statistical averages all require accurate division. The CSE tests division because it demands both procedural fluency and conceptual understanding — you must know *when* to divide and *how* to divide correctly under time pressure.

### Why Division Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Exam tests division because:
- Government employees distribute budgets across departments, offices, and programs — requiring accurate division of totals into parts
- Public servants compute per-unit costs, per-capita figures, and average values in reports
- Payroll processing requires dividing annual salaries into monthly, bi-monthly, or daily rates
- Inventory management involves dividing stock into equal lots for distribution across field offices
- Statistical reports require computing means (sum ÷ count) for population, economic, and performance data
- Division errors in official documents can cause inequitable resource allocation, audit findings, or legal disputes
- The ability to divide quickly and accurately under time pressure indicates the numerical fluency required for administrative work

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Incorrect long division steps** — subtracting incorrectly during the bring-down process or skipping place values
2. **Forgetting to place zero in the quotient** — when a partial dividend is smaller than the divisor, examinees skip the zero placeholder
3. **Sign confusion with integers** — getting the wrong sign when dividing a negative by a negative (result should be positive)
4. **Misplacing the decimal point** — failing to move the decimal correctly when dividing by a decimal divisor
5. **Forgetting to use the reciprocal** — dividing fractions by multiplying straight across instead of flipping the divisor
6. **Not simplifying fraction answers** — leaving answers like 6/8 instead of reducing to 3/4
7. **Division by zero confusion** — believing 0 ÷ 5 = undefined (it equals 0) or that 5 ÷ 0 has an answer
8. **Misidentifying division keywords** in word problems — confusing "shared equally" (division) with "combined" (addition)

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Divide whole numbers of any size accurately using long division, including problems with remainders
- Apply integer division sign rules correctly for all four sign combinations
- Divide decimals with proper decimal-point placement, including divisor-is-decimal cases
- Divide fractions and mixed numbers using the Keep-Change-Flip method
- Solve CSE-style word problems involving division across all number types
- Use estimation and mental math strategies to verify division answers quickly
- Identify and avoid common division errors under exam conditions

### 4.1 What Is Division?

**Division** is the mathematical operation of splitting a number into equal groups or finding how many times one number fits into another. The result is called the **quotient**.

```
Dividend ÷ Divisor = Quotient
   24    ÷    6    =    4
```

The symbol **÷** (division sign) or **/** (slash) indicates division. When you see "24 ÷ 6," you read it as "twenty-four divided by six" or "the quotient of twenty-four and six."

#### Key Terminology

| Term | Definition | In 24 ÷ 6 = 4 |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| **Dividend** | The number being divided (the total) | 24 |
| **Divisor** | The number you divide by (group size or number of groups) | 6 |
| **Quotient** | The result of division | 4 |
| **Remainder** | The amount left over when division is not exact | 0 (exact) |

#### Two Interpretations of Division

Division can mean two different things depending on context:

**1. Sharing (Partitive Division)** — "Split into equal groups"
- 24 candies shared equally among 6 children → each child gets 4 candies
- You know the number of groups; you find the size of each group

**2. Grouping (Measurement Division)** — "How many groups of this size?"
- 24 candies put into bags of 6 → you can fill 4 bags
- You know the group size; you find the number of groups

Both interpretations give the same numerical answer (24 ÷ 6 = 4), but understanding the distinction helps you set up word problems correctly.

#### The Inverse Relationship with Multiplication

Division and multiplication are inverse operations:
```
If  a × b = c,  then  c ÷ b = a  and  c ÷ a = b

If  7 × 8 = 56,  then  56 ÷ 8 = 7  and  56 ÷ 7 = 8
```

This relationship is your primary verification tool: **to check a division answer, multiply the quotient by the divisor — you should get the dividend.**

#### Important Division Rules

**1. Division by 1** — Any number divided by 1 equals itself: a ÷ 1 = a
```
45 ÷ 1 = 45
```

**2. Division of a number by itself** — Any non-zero number divided by itself equals 1: a ÷ a = 1
```
45 ÷ 45 = 1
```

**3. Zero divided by any non-zero number** — Always equals 0: 0 ÷ a = 0
```
0 ÷ 7 = 0  (zero split into any number of groups gives zero per group)
```

**4. Division by zero is UNDEFINED** — You cannot divide by zero: a ÷ 0 = undefined
```
7 ÷ 0 = undefined  (you cannot split something into zero groups)
```

> ⚠️ **Critical Distinction:** 0 ÷ 5 = 0 (this IS defined — zero divided by anything is zero). But 5 ÷ 0 is UNDEFINED. Many examinees confuse these two cases.

#### Division Does NOT Have the Commutative Property

Unlike addition and multiplication, order matters in division:
```
24 ÷ 6 = 4    but    6 ÷ 24 = 0.25
```

#### Real-Life Applications of Division

| Context | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Budgeting | Monthly budget = Annual budget ÷ 12 |
| Payroll | Daily rate = Monthly salary ÷ working days |
| Inventory | Items per office = Total stock ÷ number of offices |
| Statistics | Average score = Total scores ÷ number of examinees |
| Engineering | Load per beam = Total weight ÷ number of beams |
| Transportation | Trips needed = Total passengers ÷ vehicle capacity |

### 4.2 Division of Whole Numbers

Whole numbers are 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ... (non-negative integers). Dividing whole numbers is the most fundamental form of division and the basis for all other types.

#### Basic Division Facts

Just as you memorized multiplication tables, you should know division facts instantly:

```
56 ÷ 7 = 8    because  7 × 8 = 56
72 ÷ 9 = 8    because  9 × 8 = 72
48 ÷ 6 = 8    because  6 × 8 = 48
63 ÷ 7 = 9    because  7 × 9 = 63
81 ÷ 9 = 9    because  9 × 9 = 81
```

> 💡 **Speed Tip:** If you know your multiplication tables cold, division facts are automatic. Every division problem is just a multiplication fact read backwards.

#### Long Division — The Standard Algorithm

Long division is the systematic method for dividing multi-digit numbers. It follows a repeating cycle: **Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring down**.

**Example:** 864 ÷ 12

```
      72
   -------
12 | 864
     84      ← 12 × 7 = 84
     --
      24     ← bring down 4
      24     ← 12 × 2 = 24
      --
       0     ← remainder
```

Step-by-step:
1. **Divide:** How many times does 12 go into 86? → 7 times (12 × 7 = 84)
2. **Multiply:** 12 × 7 = 84
3. **Subtract:** 86 - 84 = 2
4. **Bring down:** Bring down the 4 → 24
5. **Divide:** How many times does 12 go into 24? → 2 times (12 × 2 = 24)
6. **Multiply:** 12 × 2 = 24
7. **Subtract:** 24 - 24 = 0

**Answer: 864 ÷ 12 = 72**

**Verification:** 72 × 12 = 864 ✓

#### Division With Remainders

When the dividend is not evenly divisible by the divisor, there is a **remainder** — the amount left over.

**Example:** 157 ÷ 6

```
      26 R 1
   -------
 6 | 157
     12      ← 6 × 2 = 12
     --
      37     ← bring down 7
      36     ← 6 × 6 = 36
      --
       1     ← remainder
```

**Answer: 157 ÷ 6 = 26 remainder 1** (or 26 R1)

**Verification:** 26 × 6 + 1 = 156 + 1 = 157 ✓

> 💡 **Remainder Rule:** The remainder must ALWAYS be less than the divisor. If your remainder ≥ divisor, your quotient is too small — increase it by 1.

#### The Zero Placeholder in the Quotient

A common error is forgetting to write 0 in the quotient when a partial dividend is smaller than the divisor.

**Example:** 3,024 ÷ 6

```
      504
   -------
 6 | 3024
     30      ← 6 × 5 = 30
     --
      02     ← bring down 2; 2 < 6, so write 0 in quotient
       0     ← 6 × 0 = 0
      --
       24    ← bring down 4
       24    ← 6 × 4 = 24
       --
        0
```

**Answer: 3,024 ÷ 6 = 504** (NOT 54 — the zero matters!)

#### Dividing Larger Numbers

**Example:** 15,625 ÷ 25

```
      625
   --------
25 | 15625
     150     ← 25 × 6 = 150
     ---
       62    ← bring down 2
       50    ← 25 × 2 = 50
       --
       125   ← bring down 5
       125   ← 25 × 5 = 125
       ---
         0
```

**Answer: 15,625 ÷ 25 = 625**

**Verification:** 625 × 25 = 15,625 ✓

#### Divisibility Rules (Shortcuts)

These rules help you quickly determine if a number divides evenly — saving time on the CSE:

| Divisor | Rule | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| 2 | Last digit is even (0, 2, 4, 6, 8) | 4,738 → yes (8 is even) |
| 3 | Sum of digits is divisible by 3 | 729 → 7+2+9 = 18 → yes |
| 4 | Last two digits form a number divisible by 4 | 1,316 → 16 ÷ 4 = 4 → yes |
| 5 | Last digit is 0 or 5 | 2,345 → yes (ends in 5) |
| 6 | Divisible by both 2 AND 3 | 714 → even AND 7+1+4=12 → yes |
| 8 | Last three digits divisible by 8 | 1,024 → 024 ÷ 8 = 3 → yes |
| 9 | Sum of digits is divisible by 9 | 729 → 7+2+9 = 18 → yes |
| 10 | Last digit is 0 | 5,430 → yes |

### 4.3 Word Problems Involving Whole Number Division

Word problems require you to identify that division is needed, set up the equation correctly, and interpret the result in context.

#### Division Keywords

When you see these words/phrases, division is likely the operation needed:

| Keyword/Phrase | Example |
|---------------|---------|
| divided equally | "₱12,000 divided equally among 4 employees" |
| shared among | "shared among 8 offices" |
| split into | "split into 5 equal groups" |
| per | "cost per unit," "rate per hour" |
| each | "how much does each person get?" |
| average | "average score of 30 students" |
| ratio | "in the ratio of 3:2" |
| how many groups | "how many teams of 6 can be formed?" |
| how many times | "how many times does 15 go into 180?" |

#### Easy Examples

**Problem 1:** A government office received 480 reams of paper to be distributed equally among 8 divisions. How many reams does each division receive?

**Solution:** 480 ÷ 8 = 60 reams per division

**Problem 2:** A training session has 156 participants who must be seated in rows of 12. How many rows are needed?

**Solution:** 156 ÷ 12 = 13 rows

#### Medium Examples

**Problem 3:** The Department of Education allocated ₱2,450,000 for textbooks across 35 schools. If each school receives an equal share, how much does each school get?

**Solution:** ₱2,450,000 ÷ 35 = ₱70,000 per school

**Problem 4:** A government vehicle travels 1,575 kilometers on 75 liters of fuel. What is the fuel efficiency in kilometers per liter?

**Solution:** 1,575 ÷ 75 = 21 km/L

#### Hard Examples

**Problem 5:** A municipality has 47,520 registered voters distributed across 132 precincts. If voters are assigned equally, how many voters are in each precinct?

**Solution:** 47,520 ÷ 132 = 360 voters per precinct

**Problem 6:** A government warehouse has 8,750 relief packs. If each barangay receives 125 packs, how many barangays can be served? If there are 73 barangays in the municipality, how many extra packs remain?

**Solution:**
- Barangays that can be served: 8,750 ÷ 125 = 70 barangays
- Packs needed for 73 barangays: 73 × 125 = 9,125
- Since 8,750 < 9,125, only 70 barangays can receive full allocation
- Remaining packs: 8,750 - (70 × 125) = 8,750 - 8,750 = 0 packs remain

### 4.4 Division of Integers

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero: ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...

When dividing integers, you must determine both the **magnitude** (absolute value of the quotient) and the **sign** of the result.

#### The Sign Rules for Division

| Dividend | Divisor | Quotient | Rule |
|----------|---------|----------|------|
| Positive (+) | Positive (+) | **Positive (+)** | Same signs → positive |
| Negative (-) | Negative (-) | **Positive (+)** | Same signs → positive |
| Positive (+) | Negative (-) | **Negative (-)** | Different signs → negative |
| Negative (-) | Positive (+) | **Negative (-)** | Different signs → negative |

> 💡 **Memory Aid:** The sign rules for division are IDENTICAL to multiplication:
> - **Same signs → Positive result**
> - **Different signs → Negative result**

#### Examples by Sign Combination

**Positive ÷ Positive = Positive**
```
+36 ÷ +4 = +9
+120 ÷ +15 = +8
```

**Negative ÷ Negative = Positive**
```
-36 ÷ -4 = +9
-120 ÷ -15 = +8
```

**Positive ÷ Negative = Negative**
```
+36 ÷ -4 = -9
+120 ÷ -15 = -8
```

**Negative ÷ Positive = Negative**
```
-36 ÷ +4 = -9
-120 ÷ +15 = -8
```

#### Step-by-Step Method for Integer Division

1. **Determine the sign:** Same signs → positive; different signs → negative
2. **Divide the absolute values:** Ignore signs and divide normally
3. **Apply the sign:** Attach the sign determined in step 1

**Example:** -144 ÷ (-12)

1. Signs: negative ÷ negative → **positive**
2. Absolute values: 144 ÷ 12 = 12
3. Result: **+12**

**Example:** 225 ÷ (-15)

1. Signs: positive ÷ negative → **negative**
2. Absolute values: 225 ÷ 15 = 15
3. Result: **-15**

#### Special Cases with Zero

```
0 ÷ (-5) = 0      (zero divided by any non-zero integer is zero)
0 ÷ 12 = 0        (same rule)
-7 ÷ 0 = undefined (division by zero is always undefined)
```

### 4.5 Integer Division Strategies

#### Quick Sign-Check System

For multiple-choice questions, determine the sign FIRST — this often eliminates 2 of 4 choices immediately.

**Example:** What is -84 ÷ 7?
- Sign check: negative ÷ positive → **negative**
- If choices are: A) 12  B) -12  C) -14  D) 14
- Eliminate A and D (positive) → answer is B or C
- Compute: 84 ÷ 7 = 12 → Answer: **B) -12**

#### Counting Negative Signs

When dividing a chain of integers:
- **Even number of negative signs → Positive result**
- **Odd number of negative signs → Negative result**

**Example:** (-2) × (-3) ÷ (-6) = ?
- Three negative signs (odd) → result is **negative**
- Magnitude: 2 × 3 ÷ 6 = 6 ÷ 6 = 1
- Result: **-1**

#### Mental Division Shortcuts

**Dividing by 5:** Multiply by 2, then divide by 10
```
135 ÷ 5 = 135 × 2 ÷ 10 = 270 ÷ 10 = 27
```

**Dividing by 25:** Multiply by 4, then divide by 100
```
375 ÷ 25 = 375 × 4 ÷ 100 = 1,500 ÷ 100 = 15
```

**Dividing by 50:** Multiply by 2, then divide by 100
```
450 ÷ 50 = 450 × 2 ÷ 100 = 900 ÷ 100 = 9
```

**Dividing by 4:** Halve, then halve again
```
236 ÷ 4 = 236 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 118 ÷ 2 = 59
```

**Dividing by 8:** Halve three times
```
192 ÷ 8 = 192 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 96 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 48 ÷ 2 = 24
```

### 4.6 Division of Decimals

Decimal division requires careful attention to decimal point placement. There are two main cases: dividing by a whole number and dividing by a decimal.

#### Case 1: Decimal ÷ Whole Number

Place the decimal point in the quotient directly above the decimal point in the dividend, then divide normally.

**Example:** 18.6 ÷ 3

```
      6.2
   ------
 3 | 18.6
     18     ← 3 × 6 = 18
     --
      0.6   ← bring down 6
      0.6   ← 3 × 0.2 = 0.6
      ---
        0
```

**Answer: 18.6 ÷ 3 = 6.2**

**Example:** 45.36 ÷ 4

```
      11.34
   --------
 4 | 45.36
     4       ← 4 × 1 = 4
     -
     05      ← bring down 5
      4      ← 4 × 1 = 4
     --
      13     ← bring down 3
      12     ← 4 × 3 = 12
      --
       16    ← bring down 6
       16    ← 4 × 4 = 16
       --
        0
```

**Answer: 45.36 ÷ 4 = 11.34**

#### Case 2: Dividing by a Decimal (The Key Technique)

When the divisor is a decimal, **move the decimal point in both the divisor and dividend** to the right until the divisor becomes a whole number. Then divide normally.

> 💡 **The Rule:** Moving the decimal point the same number of places in both numbers is equivalent to multiplying both by the same power of 10 — this doesn't change the quotient.

**Example:** 7.2 ÷ 0.6

1. Move decimal 1 place right in both: 72 ÷ 6
2. Divide: 72 ÷ 6 = 12

**Answer: 7.2 ÷ 0.6 = 12**

**Example:** 3.456 ÷ 0.12

1. Move decimal 2 places right in both: 345.6 ÷ 12
2. Divide: 345.6 ÷ 12 = 28.8

**Verification:** 28.8 × 0.12 = 3.456 ✓

**Example:** 0.0525 ÷ 0.005

1. Move decimal 3 places right in both: 52.5 ÷ 5
2. Divide: 52.5 ÷ 5 = 10.5

**Answer: 0.0525 ÷ 0.005 = 10.5**

#### Adding Trailing Zeros

Sometimes you need to add zeros to the dividend to continue dividing.

**Example:** 5 ÷ 8

```
      0.625
   --------
 8 | 5.000
     0       ← 8 × 0 = 0
     -
     50      ← bring down 0
     48      ← 8 × 6 = 48
     --
      20     ← bring down 0
      16     ← 8 × 2 = 16
      --
       40    ← bring down 0
       40    ← 8 × 5 = 40
       --
        0
```

**Answer: 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625**

#### Terminating vs. Repeating Decimals

- **Terminating:** The division ends with remainder 0 (e.g., 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25)
- **Repeating:** The division never ends; digits repeat in a pattern (e.g., 1 ÷ 3 = 0.333...)

For the CSE, most problems use terminating decimals. If you encounter a repeating decimal, round to the number of decimal places specified in the problem.

### 4.7 Decimal Division in Real Life

#### Money Computations

**Problem:** An office supply purchase totaling ₱3,847.50 is split equally among 5 departments. How much does each department pay?

**Solution:** ₱3,847.50 ÷ 5 = ₱769.50

#### Fuel Efficiency

**Problem:** A government vehicle traveled 456.75 km on 32.5 liters of diesel. What is the fuel efficiency?

**Solution:** 456.75 ÷ 32.5 = 14.05 km/L (move decimal 1 place: 4567.5 ÷ 325 = 14.05)

#### Payroll Calculations

**Problem:** An employee's monthly salary is ₱28,350.00. If there are 22 working days in the month, what is the daily rate?

**Solution:** ₱28,350.00 ÷ 22 = ₱1,288.64 (rounded to nearest centavo)

#### Utility Consumption

**Problem:** A government building's electricity bill for the quarter is ₱187,425.60 for 3 months. What is the average monthly cost?

**Solution:** ₱187,425.60 ÷ 3 = ₱62,475.20

#### Engineering Measurements

**Problem:** A 15.75-meter pipe must be cut into pieces that are 1.25 meters each. How many pieces can be cut?

**Solution:** 15.75 ÷ 1.25 = 12.6 → 12 complete pieces (with 0.75 m remaining)

### 4.8 Division of Fractions

Dividing fractions uses the **Keep-Change-Flip** (KCF) method, also called "multiply by the reciprocal."

#### What Is a Reciprocal?

The **reciprocal** of a fraction is obtained by flipping the numerator and denominator:
- Reciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3
- Reciprocal of 2/5 is 5/2
- Reciprocal of 7 (= 7/1) is 1/7

> 💡 **Key Fact:** A number multiplied by its reciprocal always equals 1: (3/4) × (4/3) = 12/12 = 1

#### The Keep-Change-Flip Method

To divide fractions:
1. **Keep** the first fraction as is
2. **Change** the division sign to multiplication
3. **Flip** the second fraction (use its reciprocal)
4. **Multiply** numerators together and denominators together
5. **Simplify** the result

**Example:** 3/4 ÷ 2/5

```
Step 1: Keep    → 3/4
Step 2: Change  → ×
Step 3: Flip    → 5/2

3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8
```

**Example:** 5/6 ÷ 10/3

```
5/6 × 3/10 = 15/60 = 1/4
```

> 💡 **Cross-cancellation shortcut:** Before multiplying, cancel common factors diagonally:
> 5/6 × 3/10 → cancel 5 and 10 (÷5): 1/6 × 3/2 → cancel 6 and 3 (÷3): 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4

#### Dividing Mixed Numbers

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first, then apply KCF.

**Example:** 2 1/3 ÷ 1 1/2

```
Step 1: Convert to improper fractions
  2 1/3 = (2×3 + 1)/3 = 7/3
  1 1/2 = (1×2 + 1)/2 = 3/2

Step 2: Apply KCF
  7/3 ÷ 3/2 = 7/3 × 2/3 = 14/9 = 1 5/9
```

**Example:** 4 2/5 ÷ 1 1/10

```
Step 1: Convert
  4 2/5 = 22/5
  1 1/10 = 11/10

Step 2: KCF
  22/5 × 10/11

Step 3: Cross-cancel (22 and 11 share factor 11; 5 and 10 share factor 5)
  2/1 × 2/1 = 4/1 = 4
```

**Answer: 4 2/5 ÷ 1 1/10 = 4**

#### Dividing a Whole Number by a Fraction

Write the whole number as a fraction over 1, then apply KCF.

**Example:** 6 ÷ 3/4

```
6/1 ÷ 3/4 = 6/1 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8
```

**Interpretation:** How many 3/4-sized pieces fit into 6 wholes? Answer: 8 pieces.

#### Dividing a Fraction by a Whole Number

Write the whole number as a fraction over 1, then apply KCF.

**Example:** 3/5 ÷ 6

```
3/5 ÷ 6/1 = 3/5 × 1/6 = 3/30 = 1/10
```

### 4.9 Fraction Division Strategies

#### Cross-Cancellation Before Multiplying

Always look for common factors between any numerator and any denominator BEFORE multiplying. This keeps numbers small and avoids large products that need simplification later.

**Example:** 8/15 ÷ 4/9

```
8/15 × 9/4

Cross-cancel: 8 and 4 share factor 4 → 2/15 × 9/1
Cross-cancel: 15 and 9 share factor 3 → 2/5 × 3/1 = 6/5 = 1 1/5
```

#### Estimation for Fraction Division

Before computing, estimate whether the answer should be greater than or less than 1:
- If dividend > divisor → quotient > 1
- If dividend < divisor → quotient < 1
- If dividend = divisor → quotient = 1

**Example:** 7/8 ÷ 1/4
- 7/8 is much larger than 1/4, so the answer should be well above 1
- Actual: 7/8 × 4/1 = 28/8 = 3.5 ✓ (greater than 1)

#### Common Fraction Division Patterns

| Problem Type | Quick Method |
|-------------|-------------|
| a/b ÷ a/b | Always = 1 |
| a ÷ 1/n | Always = a × n |
| 1/a ÷ 1/b | Always = b/a |
| a/b ÷ c/d | Always = (a×d)/(b×c) |

#### CSE-Style Fraction Examples

**Easy:** What is 2/3 ÷ 4/9?
```
2/3 × 9/4 = 18/12 = 3/2 = 1 1/2
```

**Medium:** A recipe requires 3/4 cup of sugar. If you have 6 cups, how many batches can you make?
```
6 ÷ 3/4 = 6/1 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8 batches
```

**Hard:** A contractor needs to cut a 5 3/8 meter board into pieces that are 1 3/4 meters long. How many complete pieces can be cut?
```
5 3/8 ÷ 1 3/4 = 43/8 ÷ 7/4 = 43/8 × 4/7 = 172/56 = 43/14 = 3 1/14
→ 3 complete pieces
```

### 4.10 Mixed Operations Involving Division

In expressions with multiple operations, follow the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS). Division and multiplication have equal priority and are performed left to right.

#### Example 1: 48 ÷ 6 × 2

```
= 8 × 2    (divide first, left to right)
= 16
```

> ⚠️ **Common Error:** Some examinees do 6 × 2 = 12 first, then 48 ÷ 12 = 4. This is WRONG. Division and multiplication are performed left to right, not "division before multiplication."

#### Example 2: 100 - 72 ÷ 8 + 5

```
= 100 - 9 + 5    (division first: 72 ÷ 8 = 9)
= 91 + 5          (subtraction left to right)
= 96
```

#### Example 3: (3/4 + 1/2) ÷ 5/8

```
= (3/4 + 2/4) ÷ 5/8    (parentheses first)
= 5/4 ÷ 5/8
= 5/4 × 8/5             (KCF)
= 40/20 = 2
```

#### Example 4: 12.5 ÷ 2.5 + 3 × 1.5

```
= 5 + 4.5    (division and multiplication left to right)
= 9.5
```

### 4.11 Estimation and Mental Division

Under CSE time pressure, estimation helps you eliminate wrong choices quickly and verify your computed answers.

#### Rounding to Compatible Numbers

Round the dividend and divisor to numbers that divide evenly, then compute mentally.

**Example:** 4,789 ÷ 23 ≈ ?
- Round: 4,800 ÷ 24 = 200
- Actual: 4,789 ÷ 23 ≈ 208.2
- The estimate (200) is close enough to identify the correct choice

**Example:** 637 ÷ 9 ≈ ?
- Round: 630 ÷ 9 = 70
- Actual: 637 ÷ 9 ≈ 70.8
- Estimate confirms the answer is approximately 71

#### Front-End Estimation

Use only the leading digits for a quick approximation:

**Example:** 8,347 ÷ 41
- Front-end: 8,000 ÷ 40 = 200
- Actual: 8,347 ÷ 41 ≈ 203.6

#### Halving Strategy

For divisors that are powers of 2, repeatedly halve:

**Example:** 960 ÷ 16
```
960 ÷ 2 = 480
480 ÷ 2 = 240
240 ÷ 2 = 120
120 ÷ 2 = 60
```
**Answer: 60** (divided by 2 four times = divided by 16)

#### Using Multiplication to Check Division

After computing a division answer, quickly multiply to verify:
- If 7,344 ÷ 48 = 153, check: 153 × 48 = 153 × 50 - 153 × 2 = 7,650 - 306 = 7,344 ✓

### 4.12 Common Errors in Division

#### Error 1: Incorrect Long Division Steps

**Wrong:** 432 ÷ 16 = 27 (because 16 × 2 = 32, write 2; bring down... confusion)
**Right:** 432 ÷ 16 = 27 (16 × 27 = 432 ✓ — always verify!)

The most common long division error is subtracting incorrectly or losing track of which digit to bring down. Always verify by multiplying back.

#### Error 2: Sign Confusion in Integer Division

**Wrong:** -48 ÷ -6 = -8 (thinking "negative divided gives negative")
**Right:** -48 ÷ -6 = +8 (same signs → positive)

#### Error 3: Misplaced Decimal Point

**Wrong:** 4.56 ÷ 0.3 = 1.52 (forgetting to move the decimal in the dividend too)
**Right:** 4.56 ÷ 0.3 → 45.6 ÷ 3 = 15.2

#### Error 4: Failure to Use Reciprocals

**Wrong:** 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 8/15 (multiplying straight across)
**Right:** 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6

#### Error 5: Division by Zero Misconceptions

**Wrong:** 0 ÷ 0 = 1 (thinking "anything divided by itself is 1")
**Right:** 0 ÷ 0 is **indeterminate** (undefined). The rule "a ÷ a = 1" only applies when a ≠ 0.

#### Error 6: Forgetting the Zero Placeholder

**Wrong:** 4,032 ÷ 8 = 54 (skipping the zero)
**Right:** 4,032 ÷ 8 = 504

### Exam Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Eliminate by Sign First
For integer division problems, determine the sign immediately. This eliminates half the choices.

#### Strategy 2: Estimate Before Computing
Round to compatible numbers and estimate. If only one choice is near your estimate, select it without full computation.

#### Strategy 3: Use Divisibility Rules
Before doing long division, check if the dividend is divisible by the divisor using divisibility rules. If it divides evenly, eliminate choices with remainders.

#### Strategy 4: Verify by Multiplication
After computing, multiply quotient × divisor. If it equals the dividend, you're correct. This takes seconds and catches arithmetic errors.

#### Strategy 5: Convert to Easier Equivalent Problems
- Dividing by 0.5 = multiplying by 2
- Dividing by 0.25 = multiplying by 4
- Dividing by 0.1 = multiplying by 10

#### Strategy 6: Work Backwards from Choices
For complex division, multiply each choice by the divisor. The one that produces the dividend is correct.

#### Strategy 7: Time Management
- Simple division (single-digit divisor): 15-20 seconds
- Long division (2-digit divisor): 30-45 seconds
- Fraction division: 20-30 seconds
- If a problem takes more than 60 seconds, mark it and return later

**1.** What is 1,296 ÷ 16?
- Answer: 81

**7.** What is 7.84 ÷ 1.4?

**9.** What is 10,080 ÷ 45?

- Explanation: 45 × 224 = 45 × 200 + 45 × 24 = 9,000 + 1,080 = 10,080.

**10.** An office budget of ₱156,000 is divided equally among 12 months. What is the monthly allocation?

**11.** What is 0.036 ÷ 0.004?

**12.** What is 240 ÷ (-16)?

**13.** What is 4/7 ÷ 8/21?

**14.** A 24.6 km road is divided into sections of 4.1 km each. How many sections are there?

### Memory Aids

- **Sign Rules Mnemonic:** "Same Sign Smile (positive), Different Sign Frown (negative)" — same signs give a positive (happy) result; different signs give a negative (sad) result
- **KCF for Fractions:** "Keep the first, Change to multiply, Flip the second" — say it every time until it's automatic
- **Decimal Division:** "Move the dot in BOTH" — never move the decimal in just the divisor; always move it the same number of places in the dividend too
- **Zero Placeholder:** "Does it go in? No? Write ZERO and bring down the next digit" — never skip a position in the quotient
- **Verification:** "Quotient × Divisor + Remainder = Dividend" — your universal check formula
- **Dividing by 5:** "Double it, drop a zero" (multiply by 2, divide by 10)
- **Remainder Check:** "Remainder must be LESS than divisor" — if it's not, your quotient digit is too small
