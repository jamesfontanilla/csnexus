# Word Problems Using Basic Operations

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Word problems** are mathematical questions presented in natural language rather than pure numerical notation. They require you to read a scenario, identify the relevant quantities, determine which operation(s) to apply, and compute the answer. In the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE), word problems dominate the Numerical Ability section because they test both comprehension and computation simultaneously — the exact combination government employees use daily.

This subtopic covers four critical areas:
- **Translating Verbal Statements** — converting English phrases into mathematical expressions
- **Single-Step Problems** — solving scenarios requiring one operation
- **Multi-Step Problems** — solving scenarios requiring two or more operations in sequence
- **Practical Applications** — budgeting, payroll, inventory, transportation, and public-service contexts

Mastering word problems is the ultimate test of numerical literacy. A government employee who can compute 24 × 35 but cannot recognize that "24 folders at ₱35 each" requires multiplication will still fail at the job. The CSE tests word problems because they mirror real administrative tasks: reading a memo, extracting the numbers, choosing the right operation, and producing a correct result under time pressure.

### Why Word Problems Are Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Exam tests word problems because:
- Government employees encounter quantitative information embedded in memos, reports, and policy documents — never as bare equations
- Budget proposals require interpreting verbal descriptions and translating them into computations
- Payroll processing involves reading employment terms and computing correct amounts
- Procurement officers must calculate totals, unit costs, and quantities from purchase requests written in prose
- Statistical reports require extracting data from narrative descriptions and performing calculations
- The ability to translate language into math under time pressure is the core skill that separates competent administrators from those who make costly errors
- Word problems test reading comprehension AND numerical ability simultaneously — two skills inseparable in actual government work

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Choosing the wrong operation** — seeing "each" and always multiplying, when the problem actually requires division
2. **Ignoring key phrases** — skipping words like "remaining," "more than," or "in all" that signal the operation
3. **Misinterpreting comparison statements** — confusing "A is 5 more than B" with "A is 5 times B"
4. **Skipping intermediate steps** — jumping to the final answer in multi-step problems without computing intermediate values
5. **Arithmetic carelessness** — setting up the equation correctly but making computation errors
6. **Ignoring units and labels** — computing a number but not checking whether it makes sense in context (e.g., getting a negative number of employees)
7. **Reading too fast** — missing critical details like "remaining" or "after the deduction"
8. **Confusing total vs. per-unit** — mixing up "total cost" with "cost per item"

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Translate verbal statements into mathematical expressions accurately
- Identify the correct operation needed based on context, not just keywords
- Solve single-step word problems involving addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division
- Solve multi-step problems requiring two or more operations in sequence
- Analyze real-life quantitative situations involving budgets, payroll, inventory, and public service
- Use estimation to verify whether answers are reasonable
- Avoid common traps and misinterpretations in CSE-style word problems

### 4.1 What Are Word Problems?

A **word problem** (also called a **story problem** or **applied problem**) is a mathematical question embedded in a real-world scenario described in words. Unlike "What is 24 × 35?", a word problem might say: "A government office purchased 24 folders at ₱35 each. How much was the total cost?"

Both require the same computation, but the word problem adds three layers of difficulty:
1. **Comprehension** — you must understand what the scenario describes
2. **Translation** — you must convert the verbal description into a mathematical expression
3. **Computation** — you must execute the arithmetic correctly

#### Why Word Problems Are Harder Than Pure Computation

Pure computation tests only layer 3. Word problems test all three layers simultaneously. Most CSE errors happen at layers 1 and 2 — examinees who can multiply perfectly still get word problems wrong because they misidentify the operation or misread the scenario.

#### The Problem-Solving Framework

Use this systematic approach for every word problem:

**Step 1: READ** — Read the entire problem carefully. Do not start computing after the first sentence.

**Step 2: IDENTIFY** — What are the known values? What is the unknown (what the question asks for)?

**Step 3: PLAN** — Which operation(s) will connect the knowns to the unknown?

**Step 4: COMPUTE** — Execute the arithmetic.

**Step 5: CHECK** — Does the answer make sense? Is it reasonable given the context?

#### Real-Life Applications

| Context | Example Problem |
|---------|----------------|
| Budgeting | "A department's annual budget is ₱2,400,000. What is the monthly allocation?" |
| Payroll | "An employee earns ₱750/day for 22 working days. What is the monthly salary?" |
| Inventory | "A warehouse received 500 boxes and distributed 185. How many remain?" |
| Transportation | "A bus carries 45 passengers per trip. How many trips for 360 passengers?" |
| Engineering | "A 150-meter cable is cut into 12.5-meter pieces. How many pieces?" |
| Public Service | "3,600 relief packs distributed to 24 barangays. How many per barangay?" |

### 4.2 Translating Verbal Statements into Mathematical Expressions

The most critical skill in word problems is **translation** — converting English phrases into mathematical operations. This section provides a comprehensive reference for the keywords and phrases that signal each operation.

#### Addition Keywords

| Keyword/Phrase | Example | Translation |
|---------------|---------|-------------|
| plus | "5 plus 3" | 5 + 3 |
| added to | "8 added to 12" | 12 + 8 |
| sum of | "the sum of 15 and 7" | 15 + 7 |
| total | "the total of all expenses" | expense₁ + expense₂ + ... |
| in all | "how many in all?" | add all quantities |
| combined | "combined enrollment" | group₁ + group₂ |
| together | "put together" | add the quantities |
| increased by | "salary increased by ₱2,000" | salary + 2,000 |
| more than | "8 more than 5" | 5 + 8 |
| altogether | "how many altogether?" | add all quantities |

> ⚠️ **Trap:** "8 more than 5" means 5 + 8 = 13, NOT 8 - 5 = 3. The phrase "more than" in this context signals addition, not subtraction.

#### Subtraction Keywords

| Keyword/Phrase | Example | Translation |
|---------------|---------|-------------|
| minus | "20 minus 7" | 20 - 7 |
| less than | "5 less than 12" | 12 - 5 |
| difference | "the difference of 30 and 18" | 30 - 18 |
| decreased by | "budget decreased by ₱5,000" | budget - 5,000 |
| remaining / left | "how many remaining?" | original - used |
| fewer than | "10 fewer than 45" | 45 - 10 |
| reduced by | "reduced by 15%" | original - reduction |
| after deducting | "after deducting taxes" | gross - taxes |
| change (money) | "change from ₱500" | 500 - cost |
| how much more | "how much more does A have?" | A - B |

> ⚠️ **Trap:** "5 less than 12" means 12 - 5 = 7, NOT 5 - 12. The number after "less than" comes first in the equation.

#### Multiplication Keywords

| Keyword/Phrase | Example | Translation |
|---------------|---------|-------------|
| times | "3 times 7" | 3 × 7 |
| product of | "the product of 6 and 9" | 6 × 9 |
| each / per (with quantity) | "₱35 each for 24 items" | 35 × 24 |
| of (with fractions) | "3/4 of 80" | (3/4) × 80 |
| twice / double | "twice the amount" | 2 × amount |
| triple | "triple the rate" | 3 × rate |
| at (rate × quantity) | "₱12 per hour for 8 hours" | 12 × 8 |
| total cost (unit × qty) | "total cost of 15 items at ₱200" | 15 × 200 |
| area | "length × width" | L × W |

#### Division Keywords

| Keyword/Phrase | Example | Translation |
|---------------|---------|-------------|
| divided by | "120 divided by 8" | 120 ÷ 8 |
| quotient of | "the quotient of 56 and 7" | 56 ÷ 7 |
| per (finding rate) | "km per liter" | total km ÷ liters |
| each (finding share) | "how much each?" | total ÷ number of people |
| shared equally | "shared equally among 5" | total ÷ 5 |
| split into | "split into 4 groups" | total ÷ 4 |
| average | "average of 5 scores" | sum ÷ 5 |
| how many groups | "how many teams of 6?" | total ÷ 6 |
| ratio | "in the ratio 3:2" | divide proportionally |

> 💡 **Key Insight:** The word "each" can signal EITHER multiplication or division depending on context:
> - "₱35 **each** for 24 items" → multiplication (finding total)
> - "₱840 shared, how much **each**?" → division (finding per-person share)
> Context determines the operation, not the keyword alone.

#### Phrases Indicating Equality

These phrases tell you what the answer represents:

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| "is" / "was" / "will be" | equals (=) |
| "gives" / "yields" | equals (=) |
| "results in" | equals (=) |
| "the answer is" | equals (=) |

#### Translating Comparison Statements

Comparison statements are the #1 source of translation errors:

| Statement | Correct Translation | Common Error |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| "A is 5 more than B" | A = B + 5 | A = 5 + B (same thing, but some write A - B = 5 and get confused) |
| "A is 3 times B" | A = 3 × B | A = B + 3 |
| "A is 10 less than B" | A = B - 10 | A = 10 - B (WRONG) |
| "A is twice as much as B" | A = 2B | A = B + 2 |
| "A exceeds B by 7" | A - B = 7, so A = B + 7 | B - A = 7 |

#### Practice Translations

**Easy:**
- "The sum of 45 and 23" → 45 + 23 = 68
- "12 less than 50" → 50 - 12 = 38
- "The product of 8 and 15" → 8 × 15 = 120
- "96 divided equally among 8" → 96 ÷ 8 = 12

**Medium:**
- "A salary of ₱25,000 increased by ₱3,500" → 25,000 + 3,500 = 28,500
- "The total cost of 15 items at ₱245 each" → 15 × 245 = 3,675
- "The remaining budget after spending ₱45,000 from ₱120,000" → 120,000 - 45,000 = 75,000
- "The average of 78, 85, 92, and 65" → (78 + 85 + 92 + 65) ÷ 4 = 80

**Hard:**
- "An employee earns ₱750 per day for 22 days, minus ₱2,500 in deductions" → (750 × 22) - 2,500 = 14,000
- "A budget of ₱180,000 split among 3 departments, with each department distributing equally to 5 units" → 180,000 ÷ 3 ÷ 5 = 12,000 per unit
- "Total revenue if 450 tickets are sold at ₱150 each and 280 tickets at ₱250 each" → (450 × 150) + (280 × 250) = 67,500 + 70,000 = 137,500

### 4.3 Identifying the Correct Operation

Keywords are helpful but not sufficient. You must analyze the **situation** to determine the correct operation. Here's how to think about it:

#### The Operation Decision Framework

Ask yourself these questions:

**1. Am I combining quantities?** → ADDITION
- Putting groups together
- Finding totals
- Accumulating over time

**2. Am I finding a difference or what remains?** → SUBTRACTION
- Taking away from a total
- Comparing two quantities
- Finding what's left after removal

**3. Am I finding a total from equal groups?** → MULTIPLICATION
- Repeated addition of the same amount
- Finding area (length × width)
- Scaling up a rate (rate × time)

**4. Am I splitting into equal parts or finding a rate?** → DIVISION
- Distributing equally
- Finding per-unit values
- Determining how many groups fit

#### Why Keywords Alone Fail

Consider: "Maria has 3 times as many books as Juan. If Maria has 45 books, how many does Juan have?"

A keyword-only approach sees "times" and multiplies: 45 × 3 = 135. **WRONG.**

The correct analysis: Maria = 3 × Juan → 45 = 3 × Juan → Juan = 45 ÷ 3 = 15.

The word "times" appears, but the operation needed is division because we're working backwards from the result.

#### Side-by-Side Comparisons

| Scenario | Operation | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| "24 items at ₱35 each. Total cost?" | 24 × 35 | Finding total from unit cost × quantity |
| "₱840 for 24 items. Cost per item?" | 840 ÷ 24 | Finding unit cost from total ÷ quantity |
| "Had 840, spent 35. How much left?" | 840 - 35 | Finding remainder after removal |
| "Had 840, received 35 more. Total?" | 840 + 35 | Combining quantities |

> 💡 **The Relationship Triangle:** For any three related quantities (total, number of groups, size per group):
> - Know groups and size → multiply to find total
> - Know total and groups → divide to find size
> - Know total and size → divide to find groups

### 4.4 Single-Step Word Problems

Single-step problems require exactly one operation. They are the building blocks for more complex problems.

#### Addition Problems

**Problem 1:** A government office has 156 employees in the main building and 89 employees in the annex. How many employees does the office have in total?

```
Known: 156 employees + 89 employees
Unknown: Total employees
Operation: Addition
Solution: 156 + 89 = 245 employees
```

**Problem 2:** A municipal treasurer collected ₱345,000 in January and ₱278,500 in February. What is the total collection for the two months?

```
Known: ₱345,000 + ₱278,500
Unknown: Total collection
Operation: Addition
Solution: 345,000 + 278,500 = ₱623,500
```

#### Subtraction Problems

**Problem 3:** A warehouse had 2,400 relief packs. After distributing 1,750 packs, how many remain?

```
Known: Started with 2,400; distributed 1,750
Unknown: Remaining packs
Operation: Subtraction
Solution: 2,400 - 1,750 = 650 packs
```

**Problem 4:** An employee's gross salary is ₱32,000. If total deductions amount to ₱5,480, what is the net pay?

```
Known: Gross = ₱32,000; Deductions = ₱5,480
Unknown: Net pay
Operation: Subtraction
Solution: 32,000 - 5,480 = ₱26,520
```

#### Multiplication Problems

**Problem 5:** A government office orders 36 reams of paper at ₱185 per ream. What is the total cost?

```
Known: 36 reams × ₱185 per ream
Unknown: Total cost
Operation: Multiplication
Solution: 36 × 185 = ₱6,660
```

**Problem 6:** A clerk processes 45 documents per hour. How many documents can she process in an 8-hour shift?

```
Known: 45 documents/hour × 8 hours
Unknown: Total documents
Operation: Multiplication
Solution: 45 × 8 = 360 documents
```

#### Division Problems

**Problem 7:** A total of 1,440 examination booklets are distributed equally to 48 testing centers. How many booklets does each center receive?

```
Known: 1,440 booklets ÷ 48 centers
Unknown: Booklets per center
Operation: Division
Solution: 1,440 ÷ 48 = 30 booklets per center
```

**Problem 8:** A government vehicle traveled 756 km on 36 liters of fuel. What is the fuel efficiency in km/L?

```
Known: 756 km ÷ 36 liters
Unknown: km per liter
Operation: Division
Solution: 756 ÷ 36 = 21 km/L
```

#### Step-by-Step Procedure for Single-Step Problems

1. Read the problem completely
2. Identify the known values (numbers given)
3. Identify the unknown (what the question asks)
4. Determine the operation based on the relationship between knowns and unknown
5. Set up the equation
6. Compute
7. Label the answer with appropriate units
8. Check: Does the answer make sense?

### 4.5 Multi-Step Problems

Multi-step problems require two or more operations performed in sequence. They are the most common type on the CSE because they test both planning and execution.

#### The Key to Multi-Step Problems: Break Them Down

Never try to solve a multi-step problem in one jump. Identify the intermediate values you need to compute first.

#### Two-Step Problems

**Problem 9:** An employee earns ₱685 per day. If she works 22 days in a month and her total deductions are ₱3,200, what is her net monthly pay?

```
Step 1: Gross pay = 685 × 22 = ₱15,070
Step 2: Net pay = 15,070 - 3,200 = ₱11,870
Operations: Multiplication → Subtraction
```

**Problem 10:** A store sold 45 items at ₱120 each and 30 items at ₱85 each. What is the total revenue?

```
Step 1: Revenue from first group = 45 × 120 = ₱5,400
Step 2: Revenue from second group = 30 × 85 = ₱2,550
Step 3: Total revenue = 5,400 + 2,550 = ₱7,950
Operations: Multiplication → Multiplication → Addition
```

**Problem 11:** A government agency has a budget of ₱960,000 for office supplies. If ₱285,000 has already been spent, and the remaining budget must last 9 months, how much can be spent per month?

```
Step 1: Remaining budget = 960,000 - 285,000 = ₱675,000
Step 2: Monthly allocation = 675,000 ÷ 9 = ₱75,000
Operations: Subtraction → Division
```

#### Three-Step Problems

**Problem 12:** A company has 3 departments. Department A has 25 employees earning ₱18,000 each, Department B has 18 employees earning ₱22,000 each, and Department C has 12 employees earning ₱28,000 each. What is the total monthly payroll?

```
Step 1: Dept A payroll = 25 × 18,000 = ₱450,000
Step 2: Dept B payroll = 18 × 22,000 = ₱396,000
Step 3: Dept C payroll = 12 × 28,000 = ₱336,000
Step 4: Total = 450,000 + 396,000 + 336,000 = ₱1,182,000
Operations: Multiplication × 3 → Addition
```

**Problem 13:** A school canteen buys 50 kg of rice at ₱48/kg and 30 kg of chicken at ₱180/kg. If the total expense is split equally among 5 days, how much is the daily food cost?

```
Step 1: Rice cost = 50 × 48 = ₱2,400
Step 2: Chicken cost = 30 × 180 = ₱5,400
Step 3: Total cost = 2,400 + 5,400 = ₱7,800
Step 4: Daily cost = 7,800 ÷ 5 = ₱1,560
Operations: Multiplication → Multiplication → Addition → Division
```

#### Organizing Multi-Step Solutions

For complex problems, write intermediate results clearly:

```
Given: [list all known values]
Find: [what the question asks]
Plan: [sequence of operations]
Solve:
  Step 1: [first computation] = [result]
  Step 2: [second computation] = [result]
  ...
Answer: [final result with units]
Check: [reasonableness verification]
```

#### Common Multi-Step Patterns on the CSE

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Earn then deduct | Gross pay - deductions = net pay |
| Buy multiple items | (qty₁ × price₁) + (qty₂ × price₂) = total |
| Distribute remainder | (total - used) ÷ groups = per group |
| Rate × time then compare | (rate₁ × time) - (rate₂ × time) = difference |
| Average computation | (sum of all values) ÷ count = average |

### 4.6 Word Problems with Integers

Integer word problems involve positive and negative quantities — gains and losses, rises and falls, credits and debits.

#### Interpreting Positive and Negative in Context

| Positive (+) | Negative (-) |
|-------------|-------------|
| Gain, profit, increase | Loss, deficit, decrease |
| Deposit, credit | Withdrawal, debit |
| Rise in temperature | Drop in temperature |
| Above sea level | Below sea level |
| Surplus | Shortage |

#### Examples

**Problem 14:** A company's profit was ₱45,000 in January but it incurred a loss of ₱12,000 in February. What is the net result for the two months?

```
January: +45,000
February: -12,000
Net: 45,000 + (-12,000) = 45,000 - 12,000 = ₱33,000 profit
```

**Problem 15:** The temperature at 6 AM was -3°C. By noon, it had risen by 11°C. What is the temperature at noon?

```
Start: -3°C
Change: +11°C
Result: -3 + 11 = 8°C
```

**Problem 16:** A government agency's budget surplus was ₱250,000. After an unexpected expense of ₱380,000, what is the new budget position?

```
Start: +250,000
Change: -380,000
Result: 250,000 - 380,000 = -₱130,000 (deficit of ₱130,000)
```

#### Key Principle

When combining gains and losses:
- Same direction (both gains or both losses): ADD the absolute values, keep the common sign
- Opposite directions: SUBTRACT the smaller from the larger, take the sign of the larger

### 4.7 Word Problems with Decimals

Decimal word problems appear in financial contexts (money), measurements, and scientific data. Precision matters — especially with Philippine peso amounts.

#### Money Computations

**Problem 17:** An office supply purchase includes: 12 ballpens at ₱18.75 each, 5 notebooks at ₱45.50 each, and 3 folders at ₱32.25 each. What is the total cost?

```
Ballpens: 12 × 18.75 = ₱225.00
Notebooks: 5 × 45.50 = ₱227.50
Folders: 3 × 32.25 = ₱96.75
Total: 225.00 + 227.50 + 96.75 = ₱549.25
```

**Problem 18:** An employee's daily rate is ₱1,288.64. If she works 4.5 overtime hours at 1.25 times her hourly rate (based on 8 hours/day), how much is her overtime pay?

```
Hourly rate: 1,288.64 ÷ 8 = ₱161.08
Overtime rate: 161.08 × 1.25 = ₱201.35
Overtime pay: 201.35 × 4.5 = ₱906.08 (rounded)
```

#### Measurement Problems

**Problem 19:** A pipe measuring 15.75 meters must be cut into pieces of 1.25 meters each. How many complete pieces can be cut?

```
15.75 ÷ 1.25 = 12.6
Complete pieces: 12 (you cannot have 0.6 of a piece)
```

**Problem 20:** A government vehicle's fuel tank holds 65.5 liters. If it consumes 8.75 liters per 100 km, how far can it travel on a full tank?

```
Distance = (65.5 ÷ 8.75) × 100 = 7.486 × 100 = 748.6 km
```

#### Tips for Decimal Word Problems

- Always align decimal points when adding or subtracting
- When multiplying money, round to 2 decimal places (centavos)
- When dividing for "how many complete items," round DOWN to the nearest whole number
- Double-check decimal placement — a misplaced point turns ₱1,500.00 into ₱150.00 or ₱15,000.00

### 4.8 Word Problems with Fractions

Fraction word problems appear in contexts involving portions, recipes, time allocation, and partial quantities.

#### Common Fraction Scenarios

**Problem 21:** A government employee spends 1/3 of her salary on rent, 1/4 on food, and 1/6 on transportation. What fraction of her salary goes to these three expenses?

```
1/3 + 1/4 + 1/6
LCD = 12
= 4/12 + 3/12 + 2/12
= 9/12 = 3/4

Three-quarters of her salary goes to these expenses.
```

**Problem 22:** A project is 3/5 complete. If the total project cost is ₱2,500,000, how much has been spent so far?

```
Amount spent = 3/5 × 2,500,000
= 3 × 500,000
= ₱1,500,000
```

**Problem 23:** A water tank is 7/8 full. If 2/3 of the water is used for irrigation, what fraction of the full tank remains?

```
Water used: 2/3 × 7/8 = 14/24 = 7/12 of the full tank
Water remaining: 7/8 - 7/12
LCD = 24: 21/24 - 14/24 = 7/24 of the full tank
```

**Problem 24:** A recipe requires 2 1/2 cups of flour. If you want to make 1 1/3 times the recipe, how much flour do you need?

```
2 1/2 × 1 1/3 = 5/2 × 4/3 = 20/6 = 10/3 = 3 1/3 cups
```

#### Choosing Operations with Fractions

- "What fraction OF a quantity?" → Multiplication (fraction × whole)
- "How many portions of size X fit into Y?" → Division (Y ÷ X)
- "What is the total of fractional parts?" → Addition
- "What remains after using a fraction?" → Subtraction (1 - fraction used, or whole - part)

### 4.9 Practical Applications of Basic Operations

This section presents realistic scenarios that mirror actual CSE questions across various government and workplace contexts.

#### Budgeting and Finance

**Problem 25:** A barangay received ₱1,500,000 in Internal Revenue Allotment. The allocation is: 40% for personnel, 30% for MOOE, 20% for development projects, and 10% for contingency. How much is allocated for development projects?

```
Development = 20% of 1,500,000 = 0.20 × 1,500,000 = ₱300,000
```

#### Payroll Computation

**Problem 26:** A casual employee works 8 hours/day at ₱71.25/hour. She worked 20 days in a month with 3 days of overtime (2 extra hours each day at 1.5× rate). What is her total monthly earnings?

```
Regular pay: 71.25 × 8 × 20 = ₱11,400.00
Overtime rate: 71.25 × 1.5 = ₱106.875 ≈ ₱106.88/hour
Overtime pay: 106.88 × 2 × 3 = ₱641.28
Total: 11,400.00 + 641.28 = ₱12,041.28
```

#### Transportation

**Problem 27:** A government shuttle makes 6 trips per day carrying 42 passengers per trip. If the route is 15.5 km one way and fuel costs ₱65.75 per liter with efficiency of 8 km/L, what is the daily fuel cost?

```
Total distance: 6 trips × 2 × 15.5 km = 186 km (round trips)
Fuel needed: 186 ÷ 8 = 23.25 liters
Daily fuel cost: 23.25 × 65.75 = ₱1,528.69
```

#### Inventory Management

**Problem 28:** A government warehouse starts the month with 4,500 units of PPE. During the month: 1,200 units are distributed to hospitals, 850 to health centers, and 600 to barangay health stations. A new shipment of 2,000 units arrives mid-month. How many units are in stock at month-end?

```
Distributed: 1,200 + 850 + 600 = 2,650 units
After distribution: 4,500 - 2,650 = 1,850 units
After new shipment: 1,850 + 2,000 = 3,850 units
```

#### Government Resource Allocation

**Problem 29:** A province has 12 municipalities. Each municipality has an average of 25 barangays. If the provincial government distributes 450,000 seedlings equally to all barangays, how many seedlings does each barangay receive?

```
Total barangays: 12 × 25 = 300
Seedlings per barangay: 450,000 ÷ 300 = 1,500
```

### 4.10 Problem-Solving Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Read the Entire Problem First

Never start computing after reading only part of the problem. The last sentence often contains the actual question, and earlier sentences provide context that changes the interpretation.

#### Strategy 2: Identify What Is Being Asked

Circle or underline the question. Common question forms:
- "How many...?" → a quantity
- "How much...?" → an amount (usually money)
- "What is the total...?" → sum
- "What is the difference...?" → subtraction result
- "What is the average...?" → sum ÷ count

#### Strategy 3: List Known Values

Write down every number mentioned and what it represents:
- 24 folders
- ₱35 per folder
- Question: total cost

#### Strategy 4: Estimate Before Computing

Before doing exact arithmetic, estimate the answer:
- 24 × 35 ≈ 25 × 35 = 875 (actual: 840)
- If your computed answer is 8,400 or 84, you know you made a decimal/place-value error

#### Strategy 5: Check Reasonableness

After computing, ask: "Does this answer make sense?"
- If a daily salary comes out to ₱500,000, something is wrong
- If the number of employees is negative, something is wrong
- If a fraction of a whole is larger than the whole, something is wrong

#### Strategy 6: Work Backwards from Answer Choices

On multiple-choice exams, you can sometimes plug answer choices back into the problem to verify which one works. This is especially useful for complex multi-step problems.

#### Strategy 7: Draw a Simple Diagram

For problems involving distribution, comparison, or sequential events, a quick sketch can clarify the relationships between quantities.

### 4.11 Estimation and Answer Checking

#### Why Estimation Matters on the CSE

The CSE is timed. Estimation serves two purposes:
1. **Elimination** — quickly rule out 2-3 answer choices that are clearly wrong
2. **Verification** — confirm your computed answer is in the right ballpark

#### Estimation Techniques

**Rounding to friendly numbers:**
- 48 × 23 ≈ 50 × 23 = 1,150 (actual: 1,104)
- 7,856 ÷ 39 ≈ 8,000 ÷ 40 = 200 (actual: 201.4)

**Front-end estimation:**
- 4,567 + 3,891 ≈ 4,000 + 3,000 = 7,000 (actual: 8,458) — rough but fast

**Compatible numbers:**
- 1,578 ÷ 23 → use 1,600 ÷ 25 = 64 (actual: 68.6)

#### Identifying Impossible Answers

Before computing, eliminate choices that are obviously wrong:
- A salary cannot be negative
- A quantity of items must be a whole number (usually)
- A percentage cannot exceed 100% (in most contexts)
- A part cannot be larger than the whole
- A daily rate should be less than a monthly salary

#### The "Order of Magnitude" Check

If you're computing 450 × 32, the answer should be in the thousands (roughly 400 × 30 = 12,000). If your answer is 144 or 144,000, you've made a place-value error.

### 4.12 Common Errors in Word Problems

#### Error 1: Misunderstanding the Question

**Problem:** "A store sold 45 items on Monday and 38 items on Tuesday. How many MORE items were sold on Monday?"

**Wrong approach:** 45 + 38 = 83 (found total instead of difference)
**Correct:** 45 - 38 = 7 more items

#### Error 2: Using the Wrong Operation

**Problem:** "Maria has 3 times as many books as Juan. If Maria has 45 books, how many does Juan have?"

**Wrong:** 45 × 3 = 135 (multiplied when should divide)
**Correct:** 45 ÷ 3 = 15 books

#### Error 3: Skipping Important Details

**Problem:** "A worker earns ₱650/day. He worked 20 days but was absent for 3 days (no pay). What is his monthly earnings?"

**Wrong:** 650 × 20 = ₱13,000 (ignored the 3 absent days)
**Correct:** 650 × (20 - 3) = 650 × 17 = ₱11,050

#### Error 4: Arithmetic Carelessness

Setting up the problem correctly but making computation errors. Always verify:
- Carry digits in addition
- Borrow correctly in subtraction
- Count decimal places in multiplication
- Place decimal correctly in division

#### Error 5: Ignoring Units

**Problem:** "A pipe is 2.5 meters long. How many 25-centimeter pieces can be cut?"

**Wrong:** 2.5 ÷ 25 = 0.1 (mixed meters and centimeters)
**Correct:** 250 cm ÷ 25 cm = 10 pieces (or 2.5 ÷ 0.25 = 10)

#### Error 6: Misinterpreting "Remaining" in Multi-Step Problems

**Problem:** "A tank has 500 liters. 120 liters are used Monday, 85 liters Tuesday. The remaining water is shared among 5 containers. How much per container?"

**Wrong:** 500 ÷ 5 = 100 (ignored the usage)
**Correct:** (500 - 120 - 85) ÷ 5 = 295 ÷ 5 = 59 liters per container

### Exam Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Identify the Operation in Under 5 Seconds

Train yourself to recognize operation patterns instantly:
- "Total/sum/combined/altogether" → Addition
- "Remaining/left/difference/change" → Subtraction
- "Each × quantity / total cost / area" → Multiplication
- "Per unit / shared equally / average" → Division

#### Strategy 2: Solve Under Time Pressure

- Allocate 30-45 seconds per single-step problem
- Allocate 60-90 seconds per multi-step problem
- If stuck after 60 seconds on a single-step problem, estimate and move on
- Return to difficult problems after completing easier ones

#### Strategy 3: Estimate Before Solving

Round numbers to the nearest "friendly" value and compute mentally. This gives you a target range for the correct answer and helps eliminate wrong choices immediately.

#### Strategy 4: Eliminate Unreasonable Answer Choices

Before computing, scan the choices:
- If the problem asks for a daily rate and one choice is ₱500,000, eliminate it
- If the problem involves subtraction and one choice is larger than the starting value, eliminate it
- If the problem asks "how many people" and one choice is a decimal, eliminate it

#### Strategy 5: Organize Multi-Step Solutions

For problems with 3+ steps, jot down intermediate results. Do not try to hold everything in your head — working memory errors are the #1 cause of wrong answers on multi-step problems.

#### Strategy 6: Use the Answer Choices as Checkpoints

On multiple choice, if your computed answer doesn't match any choice, you made an error. Re-read the problem — you likely misidentified the operation or missed a detail.

#### Strategy 7: Watch for Trap Answers

Test makers include "trap" choices that result from common errors:
- The result of using the wrong operation
- The intermediate result (not the final answer)
- The result of a common arithmetic mistake (e.g., forgetting to carry)

If you see your intermediate result as a choice, double-check that you've completed all steps.

### Mini Practice Set

**1.** A government office purchased 48 chairs at ₱1,250 each. What is the total cost?
- Answer: ₱60,000
- Explanation: 48 × 1,250 = 60,000.

**2.** An employee's monthly salary is ₱28,000. After deductions of ₱4,350, what is the net pay?
- Answer: ₱23,650
- Explanation: 28,000 - 4,350 = 23,650.

**3.** A warehouse has 3,600 boxes distributed equally to 24 branches. How many boxes per branch?
- Answer: 150
- Explanation: 3,600 ÷ 24 = 150.

**4.** A school collected ₱12,500 from Grade 1, ₱15,800 from Grade 2, and ₱9,700 from Grade 3. What is the total collection?
- Answer: ₱38,000
- Explanation: 12,500 + 15,800 + 9,700 = 38,000.

**5.** An employee earns ₱750 per day for 22 working days. What is the monthly gross pay?
- Answer: ₱16,500
- Explanation: 750 × 22 = 16,500.

**6.** A tank holds 2,400 liters. If 175 liters are consumed daily, how many full days will the water last?
- Answer: 13 days
- Explanation: 2,400 ÷ 175 = 13.71, so 13 full days.

**7.** A project budget is ₱840,000. If ₱315,000 has been spent, and the rest is divided equally among 7 remaining months, how much per month?
- Answer: ₱75,000
- Explanation: (840,000 - 315,000) ÷ 7 = 525,000 ÷ 7 = 75,000.

**8.** A bus carries 55 passengers per trip. How many trips are needed to transport 385 passengers?
- Answer: 7 trips
- Explanation: 385 ÷ 55 = 7.

**9.** An office bought 15 cartridges at ₱2,450 each and 8 reams of paper at ₱185 each. What is the total expense?
- Answer: ₱38,230
- Explanation: (15 × 2,450) + (8 × 185) = 36,750 + 1,480 = 38,230.

**10.** A government lot measures 45 meters by 32 meters. What is the area?
- Answer: 1,440 sq. meters
- Explanation: 45 × 32 = 1,440.

**11.** A company's revenue was ₱2,450,000 and expenses were ₱1,875,000. What is the profit?
- Answer: ₱575,000
- Explanation: 2,450,000 - 1,875,000 = 575,000.

**12.** A clerk processes 38 applications per hour. How many can she process in a 7.5-hour shift?
- Answer: 285
- Explanation: 38 × 7.5 = 285.

**13.** Three departments have 145, 198, and 237 employees respectively. What is the total workforce?
- Answer: 580
- Explanation: 145 + 198 + 237 = 580.

**14.** A donation of ₱1,260,000 is split equally among 84 families. How much per family?
- Answer: ₱15,000
- Explanation: 1,260,000 ÷ 84 = 15,000.

**15.** A worker earns ₱685/day for 20 days but is absent 2 days without pay. What are his earnings?
- Answer: ₱12,330
- Explanation: 685 × (20 - 2) = 685 × 18 = 12,330.

**16.** A store sold 250 items at ₱45 each and 180 items at ₱72 each. What is the total sales?
- Answer: ₱24,210
- Explanation: (250 × 45) + (180 × 72) = 11,250 + 12,960 = 24,210.

**17.** A road project covers 156 km. If 12.5 km are completed per week, how many weeks to finish?
- Answer: 12.48 weeks (approximately 13 weeks)
- Explanation: 156 ÷ 12.5 = 12.48, so approximately 13 weeks.

**18.** A government agency's annual budget is ₱18,600,000. What is the quarterly allocation?
- Answer: ₱4,650,000
- Explanation: 18,600,000 ÷ 4 = 4,650,000.

**19.** An employee's gross pay is ₱32,500. Deductions: tax ₱3,250, SSS ₱1,200, PhilHealth ₱450, Pag-IBIG ₱200. What is the net pay?
- Answer: ₱27,400
- Explanation: 32,500 - (3,250 + 1,200 + 450 + 200) = 32,500 - 5,100 = 27,400.

**20.** A fleet of 8 trucks each carries 125 sacks per trip, making 3 trips daily. How many sacks are delivered daily?
- Answer: 3,000
- Explanation: 8 × 125 × 3 = 3,000.

### Quick Recap

- **Word problems** require three skills: comprehension, translation, and computation
- **Translation** is the hardest part — learn keyword tables but always verify with context
- **Single-step problems** use one operation; identify it by asking "Am I combining, removing, repeating, or splitting?"
- **Multi-step problems** require breaking the problem into smaller sub-problems solved sequentially
- **Estimation** before computing helps eliminate wrong choices and catch errors
- **Common traps** include using the wrong operation, missing details, and confusing intermediate results with final answers
- **Context determines the operation** — the same keyword ("each") can signal multiplication or division depending on what's known and unknown
- **Always check reasonableness** — if the answer doesn't make sense in the real world, re-read the problem

---

### Memory Aids

- **RUPC** — Read, Understand, Plan, Compute (the 4-step problem-solving process)
- **"Total = multiply; Per unit = divide"** — when you know the unit price and quantity, multiply for total; when you know the total and quantity, divide for per-unit
- **"More than = add; Less than = subtract"** — but watch the ORDER: "5 less than 12" = 12 - 5, not 5 - 12
- **"Of means multiply"** — especially with fractions: "3/4 of 80" = 3/4 × 80
- **"Remaining = subtract first, then continue"** — always compute what's left before distributing or dividing
- **The Reasonableness Test** — if your answer for "daily salary" is more than the monthly salary, you divided wrong
- **Intermediate ≠ Final** — if your intermediate result appears in the choices, you probably haven't finished all steps
