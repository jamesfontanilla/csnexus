# Estimation and Mental Math

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Estimation and Mental Math** are the twin engines of numerical fluency — the ability to produce reasonable answers quickly without relying on pen-and-paper algorithms or a calculator. Estimation means finding an approximate value that is close enough to be useful, while mental math means computing exact answers entirely in your head using shortcuts, patterns, and number sense.

In the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE), these skills appear in two ways: as standalone items that ask you to round, estimate, or compute mentally, and as invisible tools that help you eliminate wrong answer choices, verify your computations, and manage time pressure across all Numerical Ability items.

This subtopic covers:
- **Rounding Numbers** — the foundation of all estimation
- **Estimation Techniques** — front-end estimation, compatible numbers, clustering, and benchmarks
- **Mental Computation** — addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division shortcuts
- **Checking Reasonableness** — validating answers under exam conditions
- **Speed Techniques** — strategies for working faster without sacrificing accuracy

### Why Estimation and Mental Math Are Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Exam tests these skills because:
- Government employees must make quick numerical judgments — approving budgets, verifying disbursement vouchers, and reviewing financial reports all require a sense of whether numbers "look right"
- Public servants process large volumes of numerical data where exact computation of every figure is impractical
- Estimation prevents costly errors: catching a ₱10,000 entry that should be ₱1,000 requires number sense, not a calculator
- Time management on the CSE is critical — with roughly 1.5 minutes per item, mental math lets you solve straightforward problems in seconds, saving time for harder questions
- Estimation is the primary tool for eliminating wrong multiple-choice options without full computation
- Administrative work in government requires rapid mental arithmetic: counting inventory, splitting costs across departments, estimating project timelines, and verifying payroll figures

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Rounding the wrong digit** — looking at the digit to be rounded instead of the digit to its right
2. **Over-rounding** — rounding all numbers in the same direction, which compounds the error
3. **Confusing estimation with guessing** — estimation is systematic approximation, not random selection
4. **Ignoring place value** — treating 4,500 and 450 as similar because both start with 4-5
5. **Mental arithmetic sign errors** — losing track of negative signs during mental computation
6. **Skipping the reasonableness check** — computing an answer and selecting it without asking "does this make sense?"
7. **Using complex methods when simple ones work** — over-thinking a problem that can be solved by rounding to the nearest ten
8. **Forgetting to adjust after estimation** — rounding down both numbers in an addition and not recognizing the estimate is too low

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Round whole numbers and decimals to any specified place value accurately
- Apply multiple estimation techniques (front-end, compatible numbers, clustering, benchmarks) and choose the best one for each situation
- Perform mental addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division using proven shortcuts
- Evaluate whether a computed answer is reasonable using estimation as a check
- Solve CSE-style estimation and mental math problems quickly and accurately
- Use estimation strategically to eliminate wrong answer choices under time pressure

### 4.1 What Is Estimation?

**Estimation** is the process of finding an approximate value that is close to the exact answer. It is not guessing — it is a deliberate, systematic technique that uses rounding, number sense, and mathematical reasoning to produce a value that is "good enough" for the purpose at hand.

#### Approximation vs. Exact Answers

| Situation | What You Need | Why |
|-----------|--------------|-----|
| Checking if your grocery total exceeds your budget | Estimate | You need a quick "yes/no," not centavo-level precision |
| Writing a check for a supplier | Exact answer | Financial documents require precision |
| Verifying a colleague's computation | Estimate | You want to catch gross errors, not replicate their work |
| Filing a government disbursement voucher | Exact answer | Audit requirements demand accuracy |
| Eliminating wrong CSE answer choices | Estimate | Speed matters more than precision |
| Computing your final CSE answer | Exact answer | You need the correct choice |

#### When Estimation Is Useful

- **Before computing:** Estimate first so you know what ballpark the answer should be in
- **After computing:** Check your exact answer against an estimate to catch errors
- **When time is short:** If you cannot compute exactly in time, an estimate can guide you to the best answer choice
- **When choices are spread apart:** If the four choices are 200, 2000, 20000, and 200000, a rough estimate is all you need

#### The Estimation Mindset

Estimation requires accepting that "close enough" is sometimes better than "exactly right but too slow." On the CSE, a student who estimates effectively will:
1. Solve easy items in 20–30 seconds (saving time for hard items)
2. Eliminate 2–3 wrong choices on difficult items (improving guessing odds from 25% to 50–100%)
3. Catch arithmetic errors before selecting a wrong answer

### 4.2 Rounding Numbers

Rounding is the most fundamental estimation skill. It replaces a number with a simpler, nearby value that is easier to work with mentally.

#### The Rounding Rule (5-or-More Rule)

To round a number to a given place:
1. Look at the digit **one place to the right** of the target place
2. If that digit is **5 or more**, round the target digit **up** (add 1)
3. If that digit is **less than 5**, round the target digit **down** (keep it the same)
4. Replace all digits to the right of the target place with zeros

#### Rounding to the Nearest Ten

Look at the ones digit:

```
47 → ones digit is 7 (≥ 5) → round up → 50
43 → ones digit is 3 (< 5) → round down → 40
85 → ones digit is 5 (≥ 5) → round up → 90
124 → ones digit is 4 (< 5) → round down → 120
1,678 → ones digit is 8 (≥ 5) → round up → 1,680
```

#### Rounding to the Nearest Hundred

Look at the tens digit:

```
4,783 → tens digit is 8 (≥ 5) → round up → 4,800
4,723 → tens digit is 2 (< 5) → round down → 4,700
1,250 → tens digit is 5 (≥ 5) → round up → 1,300
9,950 → tens digit is 5 (≥ 5) → round up → 10,000
```

**Note:** When rounding up causes a digit to exceed 9, carry to the next place. 9,950 rounded to the nearest hundred: tens digit is 5, so round up the hundreds from 9 to 10, which means 10,000.

#### Rounding to the Nearest Thousand

Look at the hundreds digit:

```
12,456 → hundreds digit is 4 (< 5) → round down → 12,000
12,567 → hundreds digit is 5 (≥ 5) → round up → 13,000
89,500 → hundreds digit is 5 (≥ 5) → round up → 90,000
99,800 → hundreds digit is 8 (≥ 5) → round up → 100,000
```

#### Rounding Decimals

The same rule applies — look at the digit one place to the right of your target:

**To the nearest whole number (ones):** Look at the tenths digit
```
3.7 → tenths is 7 (≥ 5) → 4
3.2 → tenths is 2 (< 5) → 3
12.5 → tenths is 5 (≥ 5) → 13
0.49 → tenths is 4 (< 5) → 0
```

**To the nearest tenth:** Look at the hundredths digit
```
4.67 → hundredths is 7 (≥ 5) → 4.7
4.62 → hundredths is 2 (< 5) → 4.6
9.95 → hundredths is 5 (≥ 5) → 10.0
```

**To the nearest hundredth:** Look at the thousandths digit
```
3.456 → thousandths is 6 (≥ 5) → 3.46
3.452 → thousandths is 2 (< 5) → 3.45
0.999 → thousandths is 9 (≥ 5) → 1.00
```

#### Rounding Money Values

Philippine peso amounts follow decimal rounding rules:

```
₱45.67 to the nearest peso → ₱46 (tenths digit 6 ≥ 5)
₱123.42 to the nearest peso → ₱123 (tenths digit 4 < 5)
₱1,999.50 to the nearest peso → ₱2,000 (tenths digit 5 ≥ 5)
₱89.995 to the nearest centavo → ₱90.00 (thousandths digit 5 ≥ 5)
```

#### Place Value Importance

The most common rounding error is looking at the wrong digit. Always identify:
1. **Which place** you are rounding TO (the target)
2. **Which digit** you look AT (one place to the right of the target)

```
Round 6,749 to the nearest hundred:
- Target place: hundreds (the 7)
- Decision digit: tens (the 4)
- 4 < 5, so round down
- Answer: 6,700
```

#### CSE-Style Rounding Examples

**Easy:** Round 847 to the nearest ten.
- Decision digit: 7 (ones place)
- 7 ≥ 5 → round up
- **Answer: 850**

**Medium:** Round 23,456 to the nearest thousand.
- Decision digit: 4 (hundreds place)
- 4 < 5 → round down
- **Answer: 23,000**

**Hard:** Round 9,975 to the nearest hundred.
- Decision digit: 7 (tens place)
- 7 ≥ 5 → round up hundreds from 9 to 10
- Carry: 9,975 → 10,000
- **Answer: 10,000**

### 4.3 Estimation Techniques

Different estimation techniques work better in different situations. A skilled estimator chooses the right tool for the job.

#### Front-End Estimation

Use the **leading (leftmost) digits** to get a quick approximation, then optionally adjust using the remaining digits.

**Step 1:** Add only the front-end (leftmost) digits
**Step 2:** Estimate the sum of the remaining digits
**Step 3:** Combine

**Example:** Estimate 4,567 + 3,891
- Front-end: 4,000 + 3,000 = 7,000
- Remaining: 567 + 891 ≈ 1,400
- Estimate: 7,000 + 1,400 = **8,400** (exact: 8,458)

**Example:** Estimate 678 + 234 + 891
- Front-end: 600 + 200 + 800 = 1,600
- Remaining: 78 + 34 + 91 ≈ 200
- Estimate: **1,800** (exact: 1,803)

**Best for:** Quick ballpark when you need a rough answer fast.

#### Compatible Numbers

Replace the actual numbers with **nearby numbers that are easy to compute with** — numbers that divide evenly, add to round numbers, or form familiar pairs.

**Example:** Estimate 748 ÷ 25
- 748 is close to 750
- 750 ÷ 25 = 30
- **Estimate: 30** (exact: 29.92)

**Example:** Estimate 47 × 21
- 47 ≈ 50, 21 ≈ 20
- 50 × 20 = 1,000
- **Estimate: 1,000** (exact: 987)

**Example:** Estimate 1,189 + 811
- 1,189 ≈ 1,200 and 811 ≈ 800
- 1,200 + 800 = 2,000
- **Estimate: 2,000** (exact: 2,000 — sometimes compatible numbers give exact answers!)

**Best for:** Division and multiplication where exact computation is complex.

#### Clustering

When several numbers are **close to the same value**, multiply that value by the count of numbers.

**Example:** Estimate 48 + 51 + 49 + 52 + 50
- All five numbers cluster around 50
- 5 × 50 = **250** (exact: 250)

**Example:** Estimate 198 + 205 + 195 + 201
- All four numbers cluster around 200
- 4 × 200 = **800** (exact: 799)

**Best for:** Adding several similar-sized numbers (common in data sets and averages).

#### Rounding Before Computation

Round each number to a convenient place value, then compute with the rounded values.

**Example:** Estimate 3,456 + 2,789
- Round to nearest thousand: 3,000 + 3,000 = 6,000
- Round to nearest hundred: 3,500 + 2,800 = 6,300
- Exact: 6,245
- The hundred-level rounding (6,300) is closer to the exact answer

**Rule of thumb:** Round to one fewer place than the number of digits. For 4-digit numbers, round to the nearest hundred. For 3-digit numbers, round to the nearest ten.

#### Benchmark Estimation

Use **familiar reference points** (benchmarks) to estimate values, especially for fractions and percentages.

Common benchmarks:
```
1/4 = 0.25 = 25%
1/3 ≈ 0.33 = 33%
1/2 = 0.50 = 50%
2/3 ≈ 0.67 = 67%
3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
```

**Example:** Is 5/9 closer to 1/2 or 2/3?
- 1/2 = 4.5/9
- 2/3 = 6/9
- 5/9 is between them but closer to 1/2

**Example:** Estimate 48% of 600
- 48% ≈ 50% (benchmark)
- 50% of 600 = 300
- **Estimate: 300** (exact: 288)

**Best for:** Fraction and percentage problems where exact computation is time-consuming.

#### Choosing the Best Estimation Strategy

| Situation | Best Strategy |
|-----------|--------------|
| Adding 2–3 large numbers | Front-end estimation |
| Dividing by an awkward number | Compatible numbers |
| Adding many similar numbers | Clustering |
| Quick check of any computation | Rounding before computation |
| Fraction/percentage problems | Benchmark estimation |
| Multiple-choice elimination | Any strategy that narrows to 1–2 choices |

### 4.4 Mental Addition

Mental addition means computing sums in your head without writing anything down. The key is breaking problems into simpler pieces your working memory can handle.

#### Breaking Apart Numbers (Decomposition)

Split one or both addends into place-value components, then add sequentially.

**Example:** 67 + 48
```
67 + 48
= 67 + 40 + 8      (break 48 into 40 + 8)
= 107 + 8
= 115
```

**Example:** 256 + 178
```
256 + 178
= 256 + 100 + 70 + 8
= 356 + 70 + 8
= 426 + 8
= 434
```

#### Compensation Method

Round one number to a convenient value, add, then **compensate** (adjust) for the rounding.

**Example:** 99 + 47
```
99 + 47
= 100 + 47 - 1     (rounded 99 up by 1, so subtract 1)
= 147 - 1
= 146
```

**Example:** 298 + 156
```
298 + 156
= 300 + 156 - 2    (rounded 298 up by 2)
= 456 - 2
= 454
```

**Example:** 45 + 197
```
45 + 197
= 45 + 200 - 3     (rounded 197 up by 3)
= 245 - 3
= 242
```

#### Grouping Compatible Numbers

When adding several numbers, look for pairs that sum to 10, 100, or 1000.

**Example:** 27 + 45 + 73 + 55
```
(27 + 73) + (45 + 55)    (pairs that make 100)
= 100 + 100
= 200
```

**Example:** 8 + 3 + 7 + 2 + 6 + 4
```
(8 + 2) + (3 + 7) + (6 + 4)    (pairs that make 10)
= 10 + 10 + 10
= 30
```

#### Adding from Left to Right

Instead of the traditional right-to-left algorithm, add the largest place values first for mental math.

**Example:** 345 + 267
```
300 + 200 = 500     (hundreds first)
40 + 60 = 100       (tens next)
5 + 7 = 12          (ones last)
500 + 100 + 12 = 612
```

This works well mentally because you build the answer from the most significant digits, giving you a running estimate that gets more precise.

### 4.5 Mental Subtraction

Mental subtraction is trickier than addition because borrowing is hard to track in your head. These strategies avoid borrowing entirely.

#### Counting Up Strategy

Instead of subtracting, **count up from the smaller number to the larger number**. The distance you travel is the difference.

**Example:** 83 - 47
```
47 → 50 (add 3)
50 → 80 (add 30)
80 → 83 (add 3)
Total added: 3 + 30 + 3 = 36
```
**Answer: 36**

**Example:** 1,000 - 683
```
683 → 700 (add 17)
700 → 1,000 (add 300)
Total: 17 + 300 = 317
```
**Answer: 317**

This is especially powerful when subtracting from round numbers (100, 1000, etc.).

#### Compensation Technique

Round the number being subtracted to a convenient value, subtract, then adjust.

**Example:** 72 - 29
```
72 - 29
= 72 - 30 + 1      (rounded 29 up to 30, so add 1 back)
= 42 + 1
= 43
```

**Example:** 500 - 198
```
500 - 198
= 500 - 200 + 2    (rounded 198 up to 200, so add 2 back)
= 300 + 2
= 302
```

**Example:** 845 - 397
```
845 - 397
= 845 - 400 + 3
= 445 + 3
= 448
```

#### Subtracting by Place Value

Subtract each place value separately, handling any negative results.

**Example:** 742 - 258
```
700 - 200 = 500
40 - 50 = -10       (tens: 4 - 5 is negative)
2 - 8 = -6          (ones: 2 - 8 is negative)
500 + (-10) + (-6) = 484
```

#### Adjusting Both Numbers

Add or subtract the same amount from both numbers to make the subtraction easier. This works because a - b = (a + c) - (b + c).

**Example:** 83 - 47
```
Add 3 to both: 86 - 50 = 36
```

**Example:** 534 - 278
```
Add 2 to both: 536 - 280
Now: 536 - 280 = 256
```

The goal is to make the subtrahend (number being subtracted) end in zero.

### 4.6 Mental Multiplication

Mental multiplication is one of the highest-value skills for the CSE. Many problems that look complex become trivial with the right shortcut.

#### Multiplying by 10, 100, and 1000

Append zeros (or move the decimal point right):

```
45 × 10 = 450          (append one zero)
45 × 100 = 4,500       (append two zeros)
45 × 1,000 = 45,000    (append three zeros)
3.7 × 10 = 37          (move decimal one place right)
3.7 × 100 = 370        (move decimal two places right)
0.045 × 1,000 = 45     (move decimal three places right)
```

#### Multiplying by 5, 50, and 500

Multiply by 10 and divide by 2 (or divide by 2 and multiply by 10):

```
48 × 5 = 48 × 10 ÷ 2 = 480 ÷ 2 = 240
OR: 48 ÷ 2 × 10 = 24 × 10 = 240

36 × 50 = 36 × 100 ÷ 2 = 3,600 ÷ 2 = 1,800
72 × 500 = 72 × 1,000 ÷ 2 = 72,000 ÷ 2 = 36,000
```

#### Multiplying by 25

Divide by 4 and multiply by 100:

```
48 × 25 = 48 ÷ 4 × 100 = 12 × 100 = 1,200
36 × 25 = 36 ÷ 4 × 100 = 9 × 100 = 900
84 × 25 = 84 ÷ 4 × 100 = 21 × 100 = 2,100
```

#### Doubling and Halving

If one factor is even, halve it and double the other. Repeat until the multiplication is easy.

**Example:** 16 × 35
```
16 × 35
= 8 × 70       (halve 16, double 35)
= 4 × 140      (halve 8, double 70)
= 2 × 280      (halve 4, double 140)
= 1 × 560      (halve 2, double 280)
= 560
```

**Example:** 25 × 48
```
25 × 48
= 50 × 24      (double 25, halve 48)
= 100 × 12     (double 50, halve 24)
= 1,200
```

#### Distributive Property (Breaking Apart)

Split one factor into parts that are easy to multiply:

**Example:** 7 × 48
```
7 × 48
= 7 × (50 - 2)
= 7 × 50 - 7 × 2
= 350 - 14
= 336
```

**Example:** 6 × 97
```
6 × 97
= 6 × (100 - 3)
= 600 - 18
= 582
```

**Example:** 12 × 15
```
12 × 15
= 12 × (10 + 5)
= 120 + 60
= 180
```

#### Multiplying Near Benchmark Numbers

Numbers close to 10, 100, or 1000 can be handled with compensation:

**Example:** 99 × 7
```
99 × 7
= (100 - 1) × 7
= 700 - 7
= 693
```

**Example:** 98 × 45
```
98 × 45
= (100 - 2) × 45
= 4,500 - 90
= 4,410
```

**Example:** 102 × 8
```
102 × 8
= (100 + 2) × 8
= 800 + 16
= 816
```

#### Squaring Numbers Ending in 5

For any number ending in 5: multiply the tens digit by (tens digit + 1), then append 25.

```
15² → 1 × 2 = 2, append 25 → 225
25² → 2 × 3 = 6, append 25 → 625
35² → 3 × 4 = 12, append 25 → 1,225
45² → 4 × 5 = 20, append 25 → 2,025
75² → 7 × 8 = 56, append 25 → 5,625
```

#### Powers of 10 Shortcuts Summary

| Multiply by | Shortcut |
|-------------|----------|
| 10 | Append 0 / move decimal right 1 |
| 100 | Append 00 / move decimal right 2 |
| 1,000 | Append 000 / move decimal right 3 |
| 5 | ×10 ÷ 2 |
| 50 | ×100 ÷ 2 |
| 25 | ÷4 × 100 |
| 9 | ×10 - original |
| 11 | ×10 + original |
| 99 | ×100 - original |
| 101 | ×100 + original |

### 4.7 Mental Division

Mental division relies on recognizing patterns, using compatible numbers, and simplifying before dividing.

#### Division Patterns with Powers of 10

```
450 ÷ 10 = 45       (remove one zero / move decimal left 1)
4,500 ÷ 100 = 45    (remove two zeros / move decimal left 2)
45,000 ÷ 1,000 = 45 (remove three zeros / move decimal left 3)
3.6 ÷ 10 = 0.36     (move decimal left 1)
```

#### Dividing by 5

Multiply by 2 and divide by 10:

```
135 ÷ 5 = 135 × 2 ÷ 10 = 270 ÷ 10 = 27
480 ÷ 5 = 480 × 2 ÷ 10 = 960 ÷ 10 = 96
73 ÷ 5 = 73 × 2 ÷ 10 = 146 ÷ 10 = 14.6
```

#### Dividing by 25

Multiply by 4 and divide by 100:

```
300 ÷ 25 = 300 × 4 ÷ 100 = 1,200 ÷ 100 = 12
175 ÷ 25 = 175 × 4 ÷ 100 = 700 ÷ 100 = 7
```

#### Dividing by 50

Multiply by 2 and divide by 100:

```
350 ÷ 50 = 350 × 2 ÷ 100 = 700 ÷ 100 = 7
1,200 ÷ 50 = 1,200 × 2 ÷ 100 = 2,400 ÷ 100 = 24
```

#### Compatible Numbers for Division

Replace the dividend with the nearest number that divides evenly:

**Example:** 623 ÷ 8
- 623 is close to 624
- 624 ÷ 8 = 78
- **Estimate: 78** (exact: 77.875)

**Example:** 1,590 ÷ 4
- 1,590 is close to 1,600
- 1,600 ÷ 4 = 400
- **Estimate: 400** (exact: 397.5)

#### Simplifying Before Dividing

Cancel common factors from both dividend and divisor:

**Example:** 360 ÷ 12
```
360 ÷ 12
= (360 ÷ 4) ÷ (12 ÷ 4)    (divide both by 4)
= 90 ÷ 3
= 30
```

**Example:** 840 ÷ 24
```
840 ÷ 24
= (840 ÷ 8) ÷ (24 ÷ 8)    (divide both by 8)
= 105 ÷ 3
= 35
```

#### Divisibility Rules (Quick Recognition)

| Divisible by | Rule |
|-------------|------|
| 2 | Last digit is even (0, 2, 4, 6, 8) |
| 3 | Sum of digits is divisible by 3 |
| 4 | Last two digits form a number divisible by 4 |
| 5 | Last digit is 0 or 5 |
| 6 | Divisible by both 2 and 3 |
| 8 | Last three digits form a number divisible by 8 |
| 9 | Sum of digits is divisible by 9 |
| 10 | Last digit is 0 |

**Example:** Is 4,536 divisible by 9?
- Sum of digits: 4 + 5 + 3 + 6 = 18
- 18 ÷ 9 = 2 ✓
- Yes, 4,536 ÷ 9 = 504

### 4.8 Estimation with Decimals and Fractions

Decimals and fractions appear frequently in CSE problems involving money, measurements, and percentages. Estimation with these number types requires knowing benchmark values and rounding strategies.

#### Rounding Decimals for Estimation

Round each decimal to the nearest whole number (or nearest tenth) before computing:

**Example:** Estimate 4.78 + 3.21 + 2.95
```
4.78 ≈ 5
3.21 ≈ 3
2.95 ≈ 3
Estimate: 5 + 3 + 3 = 11 (exact: 10.94)
```

**Example:** Estimate 12.45 × 3.8
```
12.45 ≈ 12
3.8 ≈ 4
Estimate: 12 × 4 = 48 (exact: 47.31)
```

#### Benchmark Fractions

Memorize these fraction-decimal-percent equivalents for instant estimation:

```
1/2 = 0.5 = 50%
1/3 ≈ 0.33 = 33.3%
1/4 = 0.25 = 25%
1/5 = 0.2 = 20%
1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%
1/10 = 0.1 = 10%
2/3 ≈ 0.67 = 66.7%
3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
5/8 = 0.625 = 62.5%
```

#### Estimating Fraction Values

Compare unknown fractions to benchmarks:

**Example:** Is 7/15 closer to 1/3 or 1/2?
- 1/3 = 5/15
- 1/2 = 7.5/15
- 7/15 is between them, closer to 1/2

**Example:** Estimate 5/8 + 3/4
- 5/8 ≈ 0.625 (or "a bit more than 1/2")
- 3/4 = 0.75
- Estimate: about 1.375, or "between 1¼ and 1½"
- Exact: 5/8 + 6/8 = 11/8 = 1⅜ = 1.375 ✓

#### Financial Estimation Examples

**Example:** A government employee earns ₱28,456.50 monthly. Estimate the annual salary.
```
₱28,456.50 ≈ ₱28,500
₱28,500 × 12 = ₱28,500 × 10 + ₱28,500 × 2
= ₱285,000 + ₱57,000
= ₱342,000
(Exact: ₱341,478)
```

**Example:** Estimate 15% tip on a ₱847 dinner.
```
10% of ₱847 = ₱84.70
5% = half of 10% = ₱42.35
15% ≈ ₱84.70 + ₱42.35 = ₱127.05
Quick estimate: ₱847 ≈ ₱850, 15% of ₱850 = ₱127.50
```

#### Measurement Estimation Examples

**Example:** A room measures 4.8 m × 3.2 m. Estimate the area.
```
4.8 ≈ 5, 3.2 ≈ 3
Estimate: 5 × 3 = 15 m² (exact: 15.36 m²)
```

### 4.9 Checking Reasonableness of Answers

On the CSE, checking reasonableness is your last line of defense against careless errors. It takes 5–10 seconds and can save you from selecting a wrong answer.

#### Identifying Impossible Answers

Before computing, ask: "What is the answer definitely NOT?"

**Example:** 456 + 789 = ?
- Both numbers are less than 1,000
- Sum must be less than 2,000
- Both numbers are greater than 400
- Sum must be greater than 800
- **Reasonable range: 800 to 2,000**
- If your answer is 12,450 or 124, you made an error

**Example:** 85 × 12 = ?
- 85 × 10 = 850 (lower bound)
- 85 × 20 = 1,700 (upper bound, since 12 < 20)
- **Reasonable range: 850 to 1,700**
- Exact: 1,020 ✓

#### Estimating Before Solving

Always do a 5-second estimate before detailed computation:

1. Round the numbers
2. Compute with rounded values
3. Note the approximate answer
4. Now compute exactly
5. Compare — if they're wildly different, recheck

#### Checking Magnitude and Signs

**Magnitude check:** Count the number of digits your answer should have.
- 500 × 30 = 15,000 (5 digits)
- If you get 1,500 (4 digits) or 150,000 (6 digits), something is wrong

**Sign check:** For integer problems:
- Positive + Positive = Positive
- Negative + Negative = Negative
- Large positive + small negative = Positive
- Small positive + large negative = Negative

#### Elimination Techniques for Multiple Choice

**Last-digit check:** The last digit of your answer depends only on the last digits of the operands.
```
347 + 568: last digits 7 + 8 = 15, so answer ends in 5
If choices are: a) 905  b) 915  c) 925  d) 935
Only b) and... wait, let's estimate: 350 + 570 = 920
Answer must end in 5 and be near 920 → 915 ✓
```

**Parity check:** 
- Even + Even = Even
- Odd + Odd = Even
- Even + Odd = Odd
- Even × anything = Even
- Odd × Odd = Odd

**Order of magnitude:** If you're multiplying a 3-digit number by a 2-digit number, the answer should have 4 or 5 digits.

#### Quick Validation Methods

| Method | When to Use | How |
|--------|------------|-----|
| Last digit | Addition, multiplication | Compute only the ones digit |
| Casting out 9s | Any arithmetic | Sum of digits should be consistent |
| Reverse operation | After any computation | If a + b = c, then c - b should = a |
| Benchmark comparison | Any problem | "Is this bigger/smaller than [known value]?" |

### 4.10 Real-Life Applications of Estimation

The CSE tests estimation in realistic scenarios because government employees use these skills daily. Here are common contexts:

#### Budgeting Expenses

**Example:** A department head needs to estimate whether the following expenses fit within a ₱50,000 monthly budget: rent ₱18,500, utilities ₱4,800, supplies ₱7,200, transportation ₱3,900, miscellaneous ₱2,100.

Quick estimate:
```
₱18,500 ≈ ₱19,000
₱4,800 ≈ ₱5,000
₱7,200 ≈ ₱7,000
₱3,900 ≈ ₱4,000
₱2,100 ≈ ₱2,000
Total ≈ ₱37,000 — well within budget ✓
```

#### Shopping Discounts

**Example:** A shirt costs ₱1,295 with a 30% discount. Estimate the sale price.
```
30% of ₱1,295 ≈ 30% of ₱1,300 = ₱390
Sale price ≈ ₱1,300 - ₱390 = ₱910
(Exact: ₱1,295 × 0.70 = ₱906.50)
```

#### Payroll Estimation

**Example:** An office has 47 employees each earning approximately ₱22,000 monthly. Estimate the monthly payroll.
```
47 ≈ 50 employees
50 × ₱22,000 = ₱1,100,000
Adjust: we added 3 extra employees × ₱22,000 = ₱66,000
Better estimate: ₱1,100,000 - ₱66,000 ≈ ₱1,034,000
(Exact: 47 × ₱22,000 = ₱1,034,000)
```

#### Travel Time Estimation

**Example:** A government vehicle travels 285 km at approximately 60 km/h. Estimate travel time.
```
285 ÷ 60 ≈ 300 ÷ 60 = 5 hours
Adjust: 285 is 15 less than 300, so about 15 minutes less
Estimate: approximately 4 hours 45 minutes
(Exact: 4 hours 45 minutes)
```

#### Inventory Calculations

**Example:** A warehouse has 12 shelves, each holding approximately 145 boxes. Estimate total capacity.
```
12 × 145
= 12 × 150 - 12 × 5
= 1,800 - 60
= 1,740
(Exact: 1,740)
```

#### Utility Bill Estimation

**Example:** Last month's electricity was ₱4,567. The rate increased by approximately 8%. Estimate the new bill.
```
8% of ₱4,567 ≈ 8% of ₱4,600 = ₱368
New bill ≈ ₱4,600 + ₱368 = ₱4,968
(Exact: ₱4,567 × 1.08 = ₱4,932.36)
```

### 4.11 Speed Techniques for Mental Computation

These advanced techniques help you compute faster under CSE time pressure.

#### Chunking Numbers

Break large computations into manageable "chunks" of 2–3 operations:

**Example:** 15 × 24
```
Chunk 1: 15 × 20 = 300
Chunk 2: 15 × 4 = 60
Combine: 300 + 60 = 360
```

**Example:** 1,250 + 3,780 + 2,470
```
Chunk 1: 1,250 + 3,780 = 5,030
Chunk 2: 5,030 + 2,470 = 7,500
```

#### Pattern Recognition

Recognize patterns that simplify computation:

**Multiplying by 11:**
```
23 × 11: put the sum of digits between them → 2_(2+3)_3 = 253
45 × 11: 4_(4+5)_5 = 495
72 × 11: 7_(7+2)_2 = 792
86 × 11: 8_(8+6)_6 = 8_(14)_6 → carry: 946
```

**Multiplying by 9:**
```
Any number × 9 = number × 10 - number
47 × 9 = 470 - 47 = 423
83 × 9 = 830 - 83 = 747
```

**Difference of squares:** a² - b² = (a+b)(a-b)
```
52² - 48² = (52+48)(52-48) = 100 × 4 = 400
```

#### Rapid Arithmetic Shortcuts

**Adding 9:** Add 10, subtract 1
```
67 + 9 = 67 + 10 - 1 = 76
```

**Subtracting 9:** Subtract 10, add 1
```
83 - 9 = 83 - 10 + 1 = 74
```

**Multiplying by 4:** Double twice
```
37 × 4 = 37 × 2 × 2 = 74 × 2 = 148
```

**Multiplying by 8:** Double three times
```
13 × 8 = 13 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 26 × 2 × 2 = 52 × 2 = 104
```

**Dividing by 4:** Halve twice
```
84 ÷ 4 = 84 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 42 ÷ 2 = 21
```

**Dividing by 8:** Halve three times
```
96 ÷ 8 = 96 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 48 ÷ 2 ÷ 2 = 24 ÷ 2 = 12
```

#### Reducing Unnecessary Computation

On multiple choice, you often do not need the exact answer:

**Example:** Which is closest to 789 × 21?
a) 12,000  b) 15,000  c) 17,000  d) 20,000

Quick estimate: 800 × 20 = 16,000 → closest to c) 17,000
(Exact: 16,569)

You saved time by not computing 789 × 21 exactly.

### 4.12 Common Errors in Estimation and Mental Math

#### Error 1: Rounding the Wrong Digit

```
WRONG: Round 4,783 to the nearest hundred
"The hundreds digit is 7, which is ≥ 5, so round up" → 5,000 ✗

CORRECT: Look at the TENS digit (8), which is ≥ 5
Round the hundreds digit UP from 7 to 8 → 4,800 ✓
```

The target digit is what changes. The decision digit is one place to the right.

#### Error 2: Overestimating or Underestimating Excessively

```
WRONG: Estimate 47 + 52 + 48 + 51
Round all up: 50 + 60 + 50 + 60 = 220 (too high)

CORRECT: Recognize clustering around 50
4 × 50 = 200 (exact: 198)
```

When rounding multiple numbers, try to balance — round some up and some down.

#### Error 3: Place Value Confusion

```
WRONG: 45 × 100 = 450 (forgot a zero)
CORRECT: 45 × 100 = 4,500

WRONG: 3,600 ÷ 100 = 360 (removed only one zero)
CORRECT: 3,600 ÷ 100 = 36
```

Count the zeros carefully when multiplying or dividing by powers of 10.

#### Error 4: Careless Arithmetic in Mental Math

```
WRONG: 67 + 48 = 67 + 40 + 8 = 107 + 8 = 114
CORRECT: 67 + 48 = 67 + 40 + 8 = 107 + 8 = 115
```

Even with shortcuts, verify the final step.

#### Error 5: Mental Sign Mistakes

```
WRONG: 72 - 29 = 72 - 30 + 1 = 42 + 1 = 41
Wait — 72 - 30 = 42 ✓, then 42 + 1 = 43 ✓
Actually the error would be: 72 - 30 = 52 (wrong subtraction)

CORRECT: 72 - 30 = 42, then 42 + 1 = 43
```

When using compensation, be clear about whether you add or subtract the adjustment.

#### Error 6: Misapplying Estimation to Exact-Answer Questions

If the question asks "What is 456 + 789?" and the choices are 1,235, 1,245, 1,255, 1,265 — you need exact computation, not estimation. Estimation tells you the answer is near 1,250, but you still need to determine which specific choice is correct.

### Exam Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Estimate Before Solving Exactly

Spend 5 seconds estimating before you compute. This gives you a target. If your exact answer is far from the estimate, recompute rather than selecting a wrong answer.

#### Strategy 2: Identify Reasonable Answer Choices Quickly

Scan all four choices before computing. If three choices are in the thousands and one is in the hundreds, you can often eliminate the outlier immediately.

#### Strategy 3: Use Elimination Effectively

- **Last-digit elimination:** Compute only the ones digit of the answer. Eliminate choices that don't match.
- **Magnitude elimination:** Estimate the order of magnitude. Eliminate choices that are 10× too large or too small.
- **Sign elimination:** For integer problems, determine the sign first. Eliminate choices with the wrong sign.

#### Strategy 4: Improve Speed Under Time Pressure

- Use mental math shortcuts for "easy" items (save time for hard ones)
- Don't write out full algorithms for simple problems — compute mentally
- If stuck, estimate and move on — come back later if time permits
- Practice mental math daily to build speed (like a muscle)

#### Strategy 5: Balance Speed and Accuracy

- Fast but wrong = zero points
- Slow but right on only half the items = low score
- The sweet spot: use estimation to be fast on easy items, exact computation on medium items, and educated elimination on hard items

### Mini Practice Set

**1.** Round 6,847 to the nearest hundred.

**Answer:** 6,800
**Explanation:** The tens digit is 4 (< 5), so round down. The hundreds digit stays at 8.

**2.** Round 23.56 to the nearest tenth.

**Answer:** 23.6
**Explanation:** The hundredths digit is 6 (≥ 5), so round up the tenths from 5 to 6.

**3.** Estimate 487 + 312 + 198 by rounding to the nearest hundred.

**Answer:** 1,000
**Explanation:** 500 + 300 + 200 = 1,000. (Exact: 997)

**4.** Use compatible numbers to estimate 748 ÷ 25.

**Answer:** 30
**Explanation:** 748 ≈ 750. 750 ÷ 25 = 30. (Exact: 29.92)

**5.** Mentally compute 99 + 47.

**Answer:** 146
**Explanation:** 99 + 47 = 100 + 47 - 1 = 146 (compensation method).

**6.** Mentally compute 500 - 198.

**Answer:** 302
**Explanation:** 500 - 200 + 2 = 302 (compensation: rounded 198 up to 200, add 2 back).

**7.** Mentally compute 25 × 48.

**Answer:** 1,200
**Explanation:** 25 × 48 = (48 ÷ 4) × 100 = 12 × 100 = 1,200.

**8.** Mentally compute 135 ÷ 5.

**Answer:** 27
**Explanation:** 135 × 2 ÷ 10 = 270 ÷ 10 = 27.

**9.** Estimate 4.78 + 3.21 + 2.95.

**Answer:** 11
**Explanation:** 5 + 3 + 3 = 11. (Exact: 10.94)

**10.** Is 7/15 closer to 1/3 or 1/2?

**Answer:** Closer to 1/2
**Explanation:** 1/3 = 5/15, 1/2 = 7.5/15. Since 7/15 is closer to 7.5/15 than to 5/15, it's closer to 1/2.

**11.** What is 67 + 48 mentally?

**Answer:** 115
**Explanation:** 67 + 48 = 67 + 40 + 8 = 107 + 8 = 115 (decomposition method).

**12.** Estimate 47 × 21 using compatible numbers.

**Answer:** 1,000
**Explanation:** 47 ≈ 50, 21 ≈ 20. 50 × 20 = 1,000. (Exact: 987)

**13.** Round 9,975 to the nearest hundred.

**Answer:** 10,000
**Explanation:** Tens digit is 7 (≥ 5), round up hundreds from 9 to 10, causing a carry: 10,000.

**14.** Mentally compute 83 - 47.

**Answer:** 36
**Explanation:** Add 3 to both: 86 - 50 = 36 (adjust-both-numbers method).

**15.** Mentally compute 6 × 97.

**Answer:** 582
**Explanation:** 6 × (100 - 3) = 600 - 18 = 582 (distributive property).

**16.** Estimate the monthly payroll for 48 employees earning ₱21,500 each.

**Answer:** Approximately ₱1,075,000
**Explanation:** 48 ≈ 50. 50 × ₱21,500 = ₱1,075,000. (Exact: ₱1,032,000)

**17.** Mentally compute 45².

**Answer:** 2,025
**Explanation:** Numbers ending in 5: 4 × 5 = 20, append 25 → 2,025.

**18.** Use front-end estimation for 678 + 234 + 891.

**Answer:** 1,800
**Explanation:** Front-end: 600 + 200 + 800 = 1,600. Remaining: 78 + 34 + 91 ≈ 200. Total: 1,800. (Exact: 1,803)

**19.** Mentally compute 360 ÷ 12.

**Answer:** 30
**Explanation:** 360 ÷ 12 = (360 ÷ 4) ÷ 3 = 90 ÷ 3 = 30.

**20.** A bill is ₱4,567. If it increases by 8%, estimate the new amount.

**Answer:** Approximately ₱4,930
**Explanation:** 8% of ₱4,567 ≈ 8% of ₱4,600 = ₱368. New bill ≈ ₱4,600 + ₱368 = ₱4,968 ≈ ₱4,930. (Exact: ₱4,932.36)

### Quick Recap

- **Rounding:** Look at the digit ONE PLACE to the RIGHT of your target. If it's 5 or more, round up; otherwise, round down.
- **Front-end estimation:** Add leading digits, then adjust with remaining digits.
- **Compatible numbers:** Replace awkward numbers with nearby "friendly" numbers.
- **Clustering:** When numbers are similar, multiply the average by the count.
- **Mental addition:** Use decomposition, compensation, or compatible pairs.
- **Mental subtraction:** Count up, compensate, or adjust both numbers.
- **Mental multiplication:** Use powers-of-10 shortcuts, doubling/halving, or the distributive property.
- **Mental division:** Use reciprocal multiplication shortcuts (÷5 = ×2÷10) or simplify first.
- **Reasonableness:** Always check magnitude, sign, and last digit before selecting your answer.

### Memory Aids

- **"5 or more, raise the score; 4 or less, let it rest"** — the rounding rule in one line
- **"Right neighbor decides"** — always look at the digit to the RIGHT of your target place
- **"×5 = ×10 ÷ 2"** and **"÷5 = ×2 ÷ 10"** — the 5-shortcut pair
- **"×25 = ÷4 × 100"** — the 25-shortcut
- **"Compensation: round, compute, adjust"** — the three-step mental math framework
- **"Last digit tells all"** — for multiple choice, compute only the ones digit to eliminate options
- **"Estimate first, compute second, check third"** — the three-phase exam approach
- **"Double-halve until easy"** — keep halving one factor and doubling the other until multiplication is trivial
- **"Count up, don't borrow"** — for subtraction, count from the smaller to the larger number
