# Percentage Mental Math and Shortcuts

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Percentage mental math** is the ability to compute percentage values quickly in your head — without a calculator, without writing long solutions, and without wasting precious exam seconds. In the Philippine Civil Service Examination, where time pressure is intense and every second counts, mental math shortcuts separate high scorers from average ones.

The CSE Numerical Ability section frequently presents percentage problems that *look* like they require full computation but can actually be solved in 5–10 seconds using benchmark percentages, fraction-percent equivalents, and decomposition strategies. Examinees who rely solely on long division and multiplication often run out of time, even when they know the concepts.

Percentage mental math appears constantly in professional and daily life:
- **Shopping** — instantly computing 20% off a ₱1,250 item
- **Taxes** — estimating 12% VAT on a purchase without a calculator
- **Budgeting** — quickly determining what fraction of your salary goes to rent
- **Payroll** — computing deductions, bonuses, and withholding mentally
- **Engineering estimates** — approximating material waste percentages on-site
- **Transportation** — calculating fare increases as percentages
- **Inventory analysis** — estimating stock changes without spreadsheets
- **Government reports** — interpreting budget allocations and statistical data rapidly
- **Tipping** — computing 10% or 15% of a restaurant bill instantly

The CSE tests mental math because government employees must:
1. Verify computations quickly during meetings and briefings
2. Estimate budget impacts without always having a calculator
3. Spot unreasonable numbers in reports and proposals
4. Make rapid numerical decisions under time constraints

**Common mistakes examinees make with shortcuts and estimation:**
1. Misremembering fraction-percent equivalents (e.g., thinking 1/3 = 30% instead of 33.33%)
2. Moving the decimal point in the wrong direction when finding 10% or 1%
3. Forgetting to halve correctly when deriving 5% from 10%
4. Adding benchmark percentages incorrectly (e.g., computing 15% as 10% + 50% instead of 10% + 5%)
5. Over-rounding when estimating, leading to answers that are too far from the exact value
6. Confusing "percent of" with "percent off" in discount problems
7. Applying shortcuts to the wrong base number
8. Not verifying that the mental math answer is reasonable before selecting it

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Identify common fraction-percent equivalents quickly from memory
- Estimate percentage values mentally using rounding and benchmarks
- Apply percentage shortcuts (10%, 5%, 1% methods) accurately
- Decompose complex percentages into simpler benchmark combinations
- Solve percentage computations faster using mental multiplication
- Compute percentage increases and decreases using mental multiplier shortcuts
- Analyze practical percentage situations efficiently without a calculator
- Verify answers using estimation and reasonableness checks
- Solve CSE-style mental math questions under time pressure
- Eliminate unreasonable answer choices using rapid estimation

### 4.1 Understanding Percentage Mental Math

### What Is Mental Math?

**Mental math** means performing calculations in your head — no pencil, no paper, no calculator. For percentages, this means converting, computing, and estimating percentage values using internalized shortcuts rather than formal algorithms.

### Why Mental Math Matters for the CSE

The Civil Service Exam gives you approximately 45–60 seconds per question. Many percentage problems can be solved in under 15 seconds with the right shortcuts, freeing time for harder questions. The difference between a passer and a non-passer is often speed, not knowledge.

### The Three Pillars of Percentage Mental Math

| Pillar | What It Means | Example |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| **Memorization** | Know fraction-percent equivalents by heart | 1/8 = 12.5% — instant recall |
| **Decomposition** | Break complex percentages into easy parts | 35% = 25% + 10% |
| **Estimation** | Round to get a close-enough answer fast | 19% of 500 ≈ 20% of 500 = 100 |

### Balancing Speed and Correctness

Mental math is not about guessing. It is about:
- Using **exact shortcuts** when the numbers cooperate (e.g., 25% of 800 = 200, exactly)
- Using **estimation** when exact computation would take too long
- **Verifying** that your quick answer falls within a reasonable range

**CSE Tip:** In multiple-choice exams, you don't need to compute the exact answer if your estimate eliminates three of the four choices.

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** In multiple-choice formats, the options are pre-selected and often widely separated. Rapid estimation lets you identify the single numerically plausible answer choice in seconds. Rather than doing the full calculation to find that `18.8% of 395` is `74.26`, estimating `20% of 400 = 80` instantly points you to the correct choice (like 74.3) if the other options are far away (like 12, 148, or 320).

### 4.2 Common Fraction-Percent Equivalents

### Why Memorize These?

Every percentage computation becomes faster when you can instantly recognize that a fraction IS a percentage. Instead of dividing, you recall. Instead of multiplying by 100, you know.

### The Essential Equivalents Table

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/3 | 0.333... | 33.33% |
| 2/3 | 0.666... | 66.67% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 2/5 | 0.4 | 40% |
| 3/5 | 0.6 | 60% |
| 4/5 | 0.8 | 80% |
| 1/6 | 0.1667 | 16.67% |
| 5/6 | 0.8333 | 83.33% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 3/8 | 0.375 | 37.5% |
| 5/8 | 0.625 | 62.5% |
| 7/8 | 0.875 | 87.5% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |
| 1/20 | 0.05 | 5% |
| 1/25 | 0.04 | 4% |
| 1/50 | 0.02 | 2% |
| 1/100 | 0.01 | 1% |

### How to Use These Equivalents

**Example (Easy):** What is 25% of 360?
- Recognize: 25% = 1/4
- Compute: 360 ÷ 4 = 90
- Answer: 90 (done in 3 seconds)

**Example (Medium):** What is 12.5% of 480?
- Recognize: 12.5% = 1/8
- Compute: 480 ÷ 8 = 60
- Answer: 60

**Example (Hard):** What is 37.5% of 640?
- Recognize: 37.5% = 3/8
- Compute: 640 ÷ 8 = 80, then 80 × 3 = 240
- Answer: 240

### Pattern Recognition Techniques

**The "Halving" Pattern:**
- 50% → 25% → 12.5% → 6.25% (each is half the previous)
- If you know 50% of a number, halve it for 25%, halve again for 12.5%

**The "Thirds" Pattern:**
- 33.33% = 1/3, 66.67% = 2/3
- To find 33.33% of any number: divide by 3
- To find 66.67%: divide by 3, multiply by 2 (or subtract 1/3 from the whole)

**The "Fifths" Pattern:**
- 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% are all multiples of 1/5
- Find 20% (divide by 5), then multiply for 40%, 60%, 80%

### Memorization Drill

Practice instant recall:
- See "1/4" → think "25%" immediately
- See "37.5%" → think "3/8" immediately
- See "0.125" → think "12.5% = 1/8" immediately

The goal is zero hesitation. These should be as automatic as knowing 7 × 8 = 56.

**3.** If you know 20% of a number is 45, how do you find 60% of that number mentally? → **Multiply by 3** (20% × 3 = 60%, so 45 × 3 = 135)

### 4.3 Benchmark Percentages

### What Are Benchmark Percentages?

**Benchmark percentages** are easy-to-compute reference points: 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, and 75%. Every other percentage can be built from these.

### Computing Benchmarks Instantly

| Benchmark | How to Compute | Example (of 4,200) |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **1%** | Divide by 100 (move decimal 2 left) | 42 |
| **5%** | Find 10%, then halve it | 420 ÷ 2 = 210 |
| **10%** | Divide by 10 (move decimal 1 left) | 420 |
| **25%** | Divide by 4 | 1,050 |
| **50%** | Divide by 2 | 2,100 |
| **75%** | Find 50% + 25% (or subtract 25% from whole) | 2,100 + 1,050 = 3,150 |

### Building Any Percentage from Benchmarks

The decomposition strategy: break any percentage into a sum of benchmarks.

| Target | Decomposition | Mental Steps |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| 15% | 10% + 5% | Find 10%, halve it for 5%, add |
| 20% | 10% × 2 | Find 10%, double it |
| 30% | 10% × 3 | Find 10%, triple it |
| 35% | 25% + 10% | Find 25%, add 10% |
| 40% | 50% − 10% | Find 50%, subtract 10% |
| 45% | 50% − 5% | Find 50%, subtract 5% |
| 60% | 50% + 10% | Find 50%, add 10% |
| 70% | 75% − 5% | Find 75%, subtract 5% |
| 80% | 75% + 5% | Find 75%, add 5% |
| 90% | 100% − 10% | Subtract 10% from the whole |
| 95% | 100% − 5% | Subtract 5% from the whole |
| 99% | 100% − 1% | Subtract 1% from the whole |

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "Since 50% is half of the number, 5% must also be easily computed by moving the decimal point one place to the left from 50%."
>
> **Why it fails:** This is correct in theory, but in practice, examinees often make a decimal placement error under pressure, confusing 5% (0.05) with 50% (0.5). For example, 50% of 800 is 400. Moving the decimal one place left gives 40, which is indeed 5%. But many examinees mistakenly divide by 5 or write 5% as 0.5, leading to a massive error.
>
> **Correct model:** Always derive 5% by first finding 10% (move decimal one place left), and then halving that result. For 800: `10% = 80`, so `5% = 80 ÷ 2 = 40`. This two-step process is virtually foolproof.

### Worked Examples

**Easy:** Find 15% of 600.
- 10% of 600 = 60
- 5% of 600 = 30 (half of 60)
- 15% = 60 + 30 = **90**

**Medium:** Find 35% of 1,200.
- 25% of 1,200 = 300
- 10% of 1,200 = 120
- 35% = 300 + 120 = **420**

**Hard:** Find 72% of 4,500.
- 75% of 4,500 = 3,375
- 3% of 4,500 = 135 (1% = 45, × 3 = 135)
- 72% = 75% − 3% = 3,375 − 135 = **3,240**

### When Decomposition Is Faster Than Direct Computation

Direct computation of 35% of 1,200:
- 0.35 × 1,200 = ? (requires multiplying 35 × 12, then placing decimal)

Decomposition of 35% of 1,200:
- 25% = 300, 10% = 120, sum = 420 (three simple operations)

For most people, decomposition is faster because each step uses division by small numbers (2, 4, 10) rather than multi-digit multiplication.

### 4.4 Estimating Techniques

### When to Estimate vs. When to Compute Exactly

**Estimate when:**
- The answer choices are spread far apart (e.g., 45, 90, 180, 360)
- The problem uses messy numbers that don't divide cleanly
- You're running low on time
- The question asks for "approximately" or "closest to"

**Compute exactly when:**
- The answer choices are close together (e.g., 144, 148, 152, 156)
- The numbers are clean and divide evenly
- You have time and the shortcut gives an exact answer

### Rounding for Estimation

**Strategy:** Round the base number OR the percentage to the nearest friendly value.

**Example:** What is 19% of 503?
- Round: 20% of 500 = 100
- Actual: 19% × 503 = 95.57
- The estimate (100) is close enough to identify the correct answer choice

**Example:** What is 33% of 612?
- Round: 33.33% of 600 = 200
- Actual: 33% × 612 = 201.96
- Estimate is excellent

### Compatible-Number Estimation

Choose numbers that work well together:

**Example:** What is 26% of 398?
- Compatible: 25% of 400 = 100
- Actual: 26% × 398 = 103.48
- Close enough for multiple choice

**Example:** What is 48% of 810?
- Compatible: 50% of 800 = 400
- Actual: 48% × 810 = 388.8
- Estimate gives the right ballpark

### Front-End Estimation

Use only the leading digits:

**Example:** What is 15% of 7,842?
- Front-end: 15% of 7,000 = 1,050
- Better: 15% of 7,800 = 1,170
- Actual: 15% × 7,842 = 1,176.3

### Benchmark Estimation

Compare to known benchmarks:

**Example:** Is 3/7 closer to 40% or 50%?
- 3/7 ≈ 0.4286 ≈ 42.86%
- Closer to 40% (only 2.86% away) than 50% (7.14% away)

### Practical Estimation Applications

**Shopping:** A ₱1,890 item is 30% off. Estimate the discount.
- Round: 30% of ₱1,900 ≈ 30% of ₱2,000 = ₱600
- Better: 30% of ₱1,900 = ₱570
- Actual: 30% × ₱1,890 = ₱567

**Payroll:** Your monthly salary is ₱23,500. Estimate your 3% PhilHealth contribution.
- Round: 3% of ₱24,000 = ₱720
- Actual: 3% × ₱23,500 = ₱705

**Budgeting:** A department spent ₱4.2M out of a ₱12M budget. Approximately what percent?
- 4.2/12 ≈ 4/12 = 1/3 ≈ 33%
- Actual: 4.2/12 = 35%

**Transportation:** A fare increased from ₱9 to ₱12. Estimate the percent increase.
- Change = ₱3, Original = ₱9
- 3/9 = 1/3 ≈ 33%
- Actual: 33.33%

### 4.5 Percentage Shortcuts

### The 10% Shortcut

**Rule:** To find 10% of any number, move the decimal point one place to the left.

```
10% of 450 = 45
10% of 3,200 = 320
10% of 78 = 7.8
10% of 5 = 0.5
```

This is the foundation of all other shortcuts. Master this first.

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Moving the decimal point one place to the left divides a number by 10. Because percent means "per hundred", 10% is equal to 10/100, which simplifies exactly to 1/10. In our base-10 number system, dividing by 10 shifts all digits one place value to the right relative to the decimal point, which is why the shortcut works instantly.

### The 5% Shortcut

**Rule:** Find 10%, then divide by 2.

```
5% of 450: 10% = 45, half = 22.5
5% of 3,200: 10% = 320, half = 160
5% of 78: 10% = 7.8, half = 3.9
```

### The 1% Shortcut

**Rule:** Move the decimal point two places to the left.

```
1% of 450 = 4.5
1% of 3,200 = 32
1% of 78 = 0.78
1% of 15,000 = 150
```

### Building from 1%, 5%, and 10%

Once you have these three, you can build ANY percentage:

| To Find | Method | Example (of 800) |
|---------|--------|-----------------|
| 2% | 1% × 2 | 8 × 2 = 16 |
| 3% | 1% × 3 | 8 × 3 = 24 |
| 4% | 1% × 4 | 8 × 4 = 32 |
| 6% | 5% + 1% | 40 + 8 = 48 |
| 7% | 5% + 2% | 40 + 16 = 56 |
| 8% | 10% − 2% | 80 − 16 = 64 |
| 9% | 10% − 1% | 80 − 8 = 72 |
| 11% | 10% + 1% | 80 + 8 = 88 |
| 12% | 10% + 2% | 80 + 16 = 96 |
| 15% | 10% + 5% | 80 + 40 = 120 |
| 20% | 10% × 2 | 80 × 2 = 160 |
| 25% | ÷ 4 | 800 ÷ 4 = 200 |
| 30% | 10% × 3 | 80 × 3 = 240 |
| 40% | 10% × 4 | 80 × 4 = 320 |
| 50% | ÷ 2 | 800 ÷ 2 = 400 |

### The Doubling and Halving Strategy

**Doubling:** If you know x%, then 2x% is double that value.
- 12.5% of 640 = 80 → 25% of 640 = 160 → 50% of 640 = 320

**Halving:** If you know x%, then x/2% is half that value.
- 50% of 900 = 450 → 25% of 900 = 225 → 12.5% of 900 = 112.5

### The "Multiply by Decimal" Shortcut

For quick mental multiplication, convert the percentage to a simple decimal:
- 25% = × 0.25 = ÷ 4
- 20% = × 0.2 = ÷ 5
- 50% = × 0.5 = ÷ 2
- 75% = × 0.75 = × 3 ÷ 4
- 12.5% = × 0.125 = ÷ 8

### CSE-Style Shortcut Drills

**Easy:** 10% of ₱15,000 = ?
- Move decimal left: ₱1,500

**Medium:** 15% of ₱2,400 = ?
- 10% = 240, 5% = 120, total = ₱360

**Hard:** 8% of ₱37,500 = ?
- 10% = 3,750, 2% = 750, 8% = 3,750 − 750 = ₱3,000

### 4.6 Mental Math for Percentage Increase and Decrease

### The Multiplier Shortcut

Instead of computing the percentage and then adding/subtracting, use a single multiplication:

**For increase:** Multiply by (1 + rate/100)
```
+10% → × 1.1
+20% → × 1.2
+25% → × 1.25
+50% → × 1.5
+5%  → × 1.05
```

**For decrease:** Multiply by (1 − rate/100)
```
−10% → × 0.9
−20% → × 0.8
−25% → × 0.75
−50% → × 0.5
−5%  → × 0.95
```

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "Applying successive percentage changes is the same as adding the percentages together."
>
> **Why it fails:** If a price increases by 25% and then decreases by 20%, many examinees think the net change is `25% − 20% = 5% increase`. But if we start with 100: a 25% increase gives 125. A 20% decrease on 125 is 25, which brings the final price back to 100 — a net change of 0%.
>
> **Correct model:** Successive percentage changes must be multiplied as factors, not added: `1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00` (no change). Always apply each successive percentage to the result of the previous step.

### Mental Multiplier Techniques

**Example (Easy):** A ₱2,000 item has a 10% discount. What's the sale price?
- Multiplier: × 0.9
- Mental: 2,000 × 0.9 = 2,000 − 200 = ₱1,800

**Example (Medium):** A salary of ₱25,000 gets a 20% raise. New salary?
- Multiplier: × 1.2
- Mental: 25,000 × 1.2 = 25,000 + 5,000 = ₱30,000

**Example (Hard):** A ₱4,800 item is marked up 25% then discounted 20%. Final price?
- After 25% increase: 4,800 × 1.25 = 4,800 + 1,200 = ₱6,000
- After 20% decrease: 6,000 × 0.8 = 6,000 − 1,200 = ₱4,800

### Quick Multiplier Recognition

Train yourself to instantly recognize these multipliers:

| Change | Multiplier | Mental Trick |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| +10% | 1.1 | Add 1/10 of the number |
| +20% | 1.2 | Add 1/5 of the number |
| +25% | 1.25 | Add 1/4 of the number |
| +33.33% | 1.333 | Add 1/3 of the number |
| +50% | 1.5 | Add half the number |
| +100% | 2.0 | Double the number |
| −10% | 0.9 | Subtract 1/10 of the number |
| −20% | 0.8 | Subtract 1/5 of the number |
| −25% | 0.75 | Take 3/4 of the number |
| −33.33% | 0.667 | Take 2/3 of the number |
| −50% | 0.5 | Halve the number |

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Multipliers simplify percentage change because they incorporate the base (100% or 1) and the change (rate) into a single decimal factor. Instead of two operations (finding the part, then adding/subtracting it), the multiplier does both in one step. For instance, a 15% increase is `100% + 15% = 115%`, which as a decimal is `1.15`. Multiplying a value by `1.15` scales it directly to its final increased amount.

### Estimation for Percentage Change

When the percentage isn't a clean benchmark:

**Example:** A ₱3,400 item increases by 18%. Estimate the new price.
- 18% ≈ 20% for estimation
- 20% of 3,400 = 680
- Estimated new price: 3,400 + 680 = ₱4,080
- Actual: 3,400 × 1.18 = ₱4,012
- Close enough for multiple choice

### 4.7 Real-Life Applications of Percentage Mental Math

### Shopping Discounts

**Scenario:** A jacket costs ₱3,500 with a 30% discount.
- 10% = 350, so 30% = 350 × 3 = ₱1,050 discount
- Sale price: 3,500 − 1,050 = ₱2,450

### Restaurant Tips

**Scenario:** Your bill is ₱1,280. You want to leave a 10% tip.
- 10% of 1,280 = ₱128
- For 15%: 128 + 64 = ₱192
- For 20%: 128 × 2 = ₱256

### Taxes and VAT

**Scenario:** An item costs ₱5,600 before 12% VAT.
- 10% = 560
- 2% = 112
- 12% VAT = 560 + 112 = ₱672
- Total: 5,600 + 672 = ₱6,272

### Payroll Deductions

**Scenario:** Monthly salary is ₱28,000. Compute 4% Pag-IBIG contribution.
- 1% = 280
- 4% = 280 × 4 = ₱1,120

### Transportation Fare Changes

**Scenario:** Jeepney fare went from ₱13 to ₱15. Estimate percent increase.
- Change = ₱2, Original = ₱13
- 2/13 ≈ 2/12 = 1/6 ≈ 16.67%
- Quick check: 15% of 13 = 1.95, 16% of 13 = 2.08 → about 15.4%

### Engineering Material Estimates

**Scenario:** A project needs 2,400 kg of cement. Add 5% for waste.
- 5% of 2,400 = 120
- Total needed: 2,400 + 120 = 2,520 kg

### Budgeting

**Scenario:** Your ₱35,000 salary — what's 30% for rent?
- 10% = 3,500
- 30% = 3,500 × 3 = ₱10,500

### Government Statistics

**Scenario:** A barangay's population grew from 8,000 to 8,400. What percent?
- Change = 400, Original = 8,000
- 400/8,000 = 4/80 = 1/20 = 5%

### Survey Interpretation

**Scenario:** In a survey of 1,500 respondents, 375 said "Yes." What percent?
- 375/1,500 = 375/1,500
- Simplify: 375 ÷ 375 = 1, 1,500 ÷ 375 = 4
- 1/4 = 25%

### 4.8 Problem-Solving Strategies

### Strategy 1: Identify the Fastest Method

Before computing, ask: "What's the quickest path to the answer?"

| Situation | Best Method |
|-----------|------------|
| Percentage is a benchmark (10%, 25%, 50%) | Direct division |
| Percentage is near a benchmark (9%, 11%, 24%) | Benchmark ± adjustment |
| Numbers are messy but choices are spread apart | Estimation |
| Numbers are clean and choices are close | Exact shortcut computation |

### Strategy 2: Work Backward from Choices

If the choices are 120, 150, 180, 240 — and you need 25% of 600:
- 25% = 1/4
- 600 ÷ 4 = 150
- Select 150 immediately

### Strategy 3: Simplify Before Computing

**Example:** What is 75% of 840?
- Don't compute 0.75 × 840
- Instead: 75% = 3/4, so 840 ÷ 4 = 210, then 210 × 3 = 630

### Strategy 4: Use Complementary Percentages

To find 85% of something, it may be easier to find 15% and subtract:
- 85% of 2,000 = 2,000 − 15% of 2,000 = 2,000 − 300 = 1,700

### Strategy 5: The "Nice Numbers" Check

If your answer doesn't come out to a clean number and the choices are all clean numbers, you likely made an error. CSE problems are designed to have neat answers.

### 4.9 Estimation and Answer Checking

### Verifying Answers Mentally

After computing, always do a quick sanity check:

**Reasonableness checks:**
- 25% of something should be about 1/4 of it
- 10% of 5,000 must be 500 — if your answer is 50 or 5,000, you moved the decimal wrong
- A 20% discount on ₱1,000 should give ₱800, not ₱200 (that's the discount, not the price)

### Detecting Impossible Answers

**Red flags:**
- Percentage of a number is LARGER than the number itself (unless the percentage > 100%)
- A discount makes the price higher
- A percentage increase gives a number smaller than the original
- The answer is negative when the context requires a positive value

### Rapid Elimination Strategy

In multiple choice, eliminate answers that fail the reasonableness test:

**Example:** 15% of 3,200 = ?
Choices: (A) 48  (B) 480  (C) 4,800  (D) 320

- 10% of 3,200 = 320
- 15% must be more than 320 but less than 50% (1,600)
- Only (B) 480 fits

### Benchmark Comparison Method

Compare your answer to a known benchmark:

**Example:** You computed 23% of 450 = 103.5
- Check: 25% of 450 = 112.5
- 23% should be slightly less than 112.5
- 103.5 is reasonable ✓

### 4.10 Common Errors in Percentage Mental Math

### Error 1: Incorrect Rounding

**Wrong:** 19% of 500 ≈ 20% of 500 = 100, so the answer is exactly 100
**Right:** The estimate is 100, but the exact answer is 95. Use estimation to eliminate choices, not as the final answer when choices are close.

### Error 2: Decimal-Place Mistakes

**Wrong:** 1% of 3,500 = 350 (moved decimal only one place)
**Right:** 1% of 3,500 = 35 (move decimal TWO places left)

### Error 3: Confusing Benchmark Percentages

**Wrong:** 15% = 10% + 50% (confusing 5% with 50%)
**Right:** 15% = 10% + 5% (5% is HALF of 10%, not 50%)

### Error 4: Arithmetic Carelessness in Decomposition

**Wrong:** 35% of 800 = 25% + 10% = 200 + 8 = 208
**Right:** 35% of 800 = 25% + 10% = 200 + 80 = 280

The error: computing 10% of 80 instead of 10% of 800.

### Error 5: Overestimating or Underestimating

**Wrong:** 49% of 600 ≈ 50% of 600 = 300, so the answer is 300
**Right:** If choices include both 294 and 300, you need the exact answer: 49% × 600 = 294

### Error 6: Shortcut Misuse — Wrong Base

**Wrong:** "What is 20% more than 500?" → 20% of 600 = 120 (used wrong base)
**Right:** 20% of 500 = 100, so 20% more than 500 = 600

### Exam Strategies

- **Memorize the fraction-percent table.** This alone saves 2-3 minutes across the entire exam.
- **Always find 10% first.** It's the gateway to every other percentage.
- **Estimate before computing.** Narrow down to 1-2 choices, then verify.
- **Use complementary percentages.** Finding 90% is easier as "100% minus 10%."
- **Recognize benchmark patterns instantly.** If you see 1/4, think 25% without computing.
- **Decompose complex percentages.** 35% = 25% + 10%, not 35 × something.
- **Check decimal placement.** The #1 mental math error is moving the decimal the wrong number of places.
- **Don't over-round.** If choices are close together, you need precision, not estimation.
- **Work backward from choices.** Sometimes it's faster to test each choice than to compute forward.
- **Time yourself.** Mental math problems should take 10-20 seconds. If you're past 30 seconds, switch to estimation and eliminate.

**1.** What is 25% of 1,600?
**Answer:** 400
**Explanation:** 25% = 1/4. 1,600 ÷ 4 = 400.

**2.** What is 10% of 8,750?
**Answer:** 875
**Explanation:** Move decimal one place left: 8,750 → 875.

**3.** What is 5% of 4,200?
**Answer:** 210
**Explanation:** 10% = 420, half of that = 210.

**4.** What is 15% of 3,000?
**Answer:** 450
**Explanation:** 10% = 300, 5% = 150, total = 450.

**5.** What is 12.5% of 960?
**Answer:** 120
**Explanation:** 12.5% = 1/8. 960 ÷ 8 = 120.

**6.** What is 33.33% of 2,700?
**Answer:** 900
**Explanation:** 33.33% = 1/3. 2,700 ÷ 3 = 900.

**7.** What is 75% of 2,400?
**Answer:** 1,800
**Explanation:** 75% = 3/4. 2,400 ÷ 4 = 600, × 3 = 1,800.

**8.** Estimate 19% of 5,100.
**Answer:** Approximately 969 (exact: 969)
**Explanation:** 20% of 5,100 = 1,020. Subtract 1% (51): 1,020 − 51 = 969.

**9.** A ₱2,500 item is 20% off. What's the sale price?
**Answer:** ₱2,000
**Explanation:** 20% of 2,500 = 500. Sale price = 2,500 − 500 = ₱2,000.

**10.** What is 8% of 6,250?
**Answer:** 500
**Explanation:** 10% = 625, 2% = 125, 8% = 625 − 125 = 500.

**11.** What fraction-percent equivalent is 0.375?
**Answer:** 37.5% = 3/8
**Explanation:** 0.375 × 100 = 37.5%. As a fraction: 375/1000 = 3/8.

**12.** What is 40% of 1,750?
**Answer:** 700
**Explanation:** 10% = 175, × 4 = 700.

**13.** A salary of ₱32,000 gets a 25% raise. New salary?
**Answer:** ₱40,000
**Explanation:** 25% of 32,000 = 8,000. New: 32,000 + 8,000 = ₱40,000.

**14.** What is 1% of 45,000?
**Answer:** 450
**Explanation:** Move decimal two places left: 45,000 → 450.

**15.** Estimate 48% of 820.
**Answer:** Approximately 394 (exact: 393.6)
**Explanation:** 50% of 820 = 410. Subtract 2% (16.4 ≈ 16): 410 − 16 = 394.

**16.** What is 60% of 3,500?
**Answer:** 2,100
**Explanation:** 50% = 1,750, 10% = 350, total = 2,100.

**17.** 375 is what percent of 1,500?
**Answer:** 25%
**Explanation:** 375/1,500 = 1/4 = 25%.

**18.** What is 90% of 4,400?
**Answer:** 3,960
**Explanation:** 100% − 10% = 4,400 − 440 = 3,960.

**19.** A ₱6,000 item increases by 15%. New price?
**Answer:** ₱6,900
**Explanation:** 10% = 600, 5% = 300, 15% = 900. New: 6,000 + 900 = ₱6,900.

**20.** What is 66.67% of 4,500?
**Answer:** 3,000
**Explanation:** 66.67% = 2/3. 4,500 ÷ 3 = 1,500, × 2 = 3,000.

### Quick Recap

| Concept | Key Rule | Quick Example |
|---------|----------|--------------|
| **10% shortcut** | Move decimal 1 place left | 10% of 4,500 = 450 |
| **5% shortcut** | Half of 10% | 5% of 4,500 = 225 |
| **1% shortcut** | Move decimal 2 places left | 1% of 4,500 = 45 |
| **25% shortcut** | Divide by 4 | 25% of 4,500 = 1,125 |
| **50% shortcut** | Divide by 2 | 50% of 4,500 = 2,250 |
| **Decomposition** | Break into benchmarks | 35% = 25% + 10% |
| **Fraction equivalents** | Memorize the table | 3/8 = 37.5% |
| **Estimation** | Round to friendly numbers | 19% ≈ 20% |
| **Multiplier (increase)** | × (1 + rate/100) | +20% → × 1.2 |
| **Multiplier (decrease)** | × (1 − rate/100) | −20% → × 0.8 |

**Essential reminders:**
- 10% is your anchor — derive everything else from it
- Fraction-percent equivalents eliminate computation entirely
- Estimation is a tool, not a guess — it narrows choices
- Decomposition beats direct multiplication for most people
- Always verify: does the answer make sense given the benchmark?
- CSE problems are designed for clean answers — trust neat numbers

### Memory Aids

### The "10-5-1" Foundation
```
10% = move decimal 1 left
 5% = half of 10%
 1% = move decimal 2 left
```
Think: "**T**en-**F**ive-**O**ne, **T**hat's **F**or **O**peners" — always start here.

### Fraction-Percent Pairs (Rhyming Mnemonic)
```
Half is fifty, that's the start
Quarter's twenty-five (one part in four)
A fifth is twenty, nice and clean
An eighth is twelve-point-five, in between
A third is thirty-three and a third
```

### The Decomposition Rule
```
Any % = sum of benchmarks
15% = 10% + 5%
35% = 25% + 10%
72% = 75% − 3%
```
Think: "**B**reak **B**ig into **B**ites" (BBB rule)

### The Complement Shortcut
```
To find 85%, subtract 15% from 100%
To find 90%, subtract 10% from 100%
To find 95%, subtract 5% from 100%
```
Think: "**S**ubtract the **S**mall piece" — it's faster when the percentage is above 50%.

### Decimal Movement Reminder
```
÷ 10  → decimal moves 1 LEFT  (10%)
÷ 100 → decimal moves 2 LEFT  (1%)
× 10  → decimal moves 1 RIGHT
× 100 → decimal moves 2 RIGHT
```
Think: "**L**eft for **L**ess" — dividing makes numbers smaller, decimal goes left.

### Multiplier Memory
```
Increase: 1 + (percent ÷ 100)
Decrease: 1 − (percent ÷ 100)
```
Think: "**A**dd for **A**scend, **S**ubtract for **S**hrink" (A-A, S-S)

### Which Method?

For each problem, identify the type and solve.

**1.** Compute 37.5% of ₱480.
- **Type:** Fraction-percent equivalent (37.5% = 3/8)
- **Answer:** ₱480 ÷ 8 = 60; 60 × 3 = **₱180**
- **Why:** 37.5% is a standard eighths family benchmark, which divides cleanly.

**2.** Compute 19% of ₱505 where choices are: A) ₱42, B) ₱96, C) ₱212, D) ₱405.
- **Type:** Estimation by rounding (19% of 505 ≈ 20% of 500)
- **Answer:** 20% of 500 = **₱100** → Select B
- **Why:** The choices are widely separated, making exact calculation a waste of time.

**3.** Compute 8% of ₱1,200.
- **Type:** Benchmark subtraction (10% − 2%) or 1% shortcut (1% × 8)
- **Answer:** 1% = 12; 12 × 8 = **₱96** (or 120 − 24 = ₱96)
- **Why:** Single-digit percentage is easiest to scale from the 1% anchor.
