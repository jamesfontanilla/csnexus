# Profit, Loss, and Tax

## Introduction

**Profit, loss, and tax** problems form a critical subset of percentage-based questions in the Numerical Ability section of the Philippine Civil Service Examination. These problems require you to compute financial outcomes — how much a seller gains or loses on a transaction, and how taxes affect the final price a buyer pays.

Unlike pure percentage problems that deal with abstract rates and bases, profit-loss-tax problems are grounded in **real commercial transactions**. Every question tells a story: someone bought something at one price, sold it at another, and you must determine the financial result.

**Focus Areas:**
- Profit and Loss (computing gains and losses from transactions)
- Sales Tax and VAT (computing tax amounts and tax-inclusive prices)

**Why these concepts matter in daily life and government work:**

- **Retail and business** — every store owner computes profit margins daily
- **Government procurement** — public servants evaluate bids, compute markups, and verify supplier pricing
- **Payroll and budgeting** — understanding tax deductions on salaries and benefits
- **Transportation** — fare adjustments, fuel surcharges, and VAT on tickets
- **Engineering projects** — material cost overruns, contractor profit margins
- **Office supplies** — computing VAT on purchases, verifying receipts
- **Public service finance** — budget allocation, revenue collection, tax compliance
- **Online shopping** — comparing pre-tax and post-tax prices
- **Restaurant billing** — understanding service charges and VAT breakdowns
- **Personal finance** — computing gains/losses on investments, understanding tax obligations

**Why these concepts appear on the Civil Service Exam:**

Government employees routinely handle financial documents — purchase orders, receipts, budget reports, and tax computations. The CSE tests whether candidates can:
1. Accurately compute profit or loss on transactions
2. Determine percentage gains and losses for reporting
3. Apply correct tax rates to compute final amounts
4. Verify financial computations in procurement documents
5. Interpret tax-inclusive vs. tax-exclusive pricing

**Common mistakes examinees make:**
1. Confusing **cost price** and **selling price** when computing profit percentage
2. Using selling price as the base for profit percentage (it should be cost price)
3. Forgetting that VAT is computed on the **base price**, not on a previously taxed amount
4. Adding tax percentages directly to discount percentages (they operate on different bases)
5. Misidentifying whether a problem asks for the amount or the percentage
6. Arithmetic errors when working with 12% VAT (common Philippine rate)
7. Not reading whether the given price is tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive
8. Confusing markup (based on cost) with margin (based on selling price)

## Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Compute profit and loss amounts accurately given cost price and selling price
- Determine profit percentage and loss percentage using the correct base
- Compute sales tax and VAT amounts given a rate and base price
- Determine total prices including taxes
- Distinguish between tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive pricing
- Analyze real-life financial situations involving combined profit/loss and tax
- Solve multi-step CSE-style business mathematics problems efficiently
- Apply the multiplier method for quick tax and markup computations
- Estimate answers mentally to verify computations and eliminate wrong choices

## 4.1 Understanding Profit, Loss, and Tax

### What Are Profit, Loss, and Tax?

Every commercial transaction involves a **cost price** (what the seller paid to acquire the item) and a **selling price** (what the buyer pays to purchase it). The relationship between these two prices determines whether the seller earns a profit or suffers a loss.

**Profit** occurs when the selling price is greater than the cost price. The seller receives more money than they spent.

**Loss** occurs when the selling price is less than the cost price. The seller receives less money than they spent.

**Tax** is an additional amount collected by the government on transactions. In the Philippines, the most common form is the **Value-Added Tax (VAT)** at 12%. Tax increases the total amount the buyer pays but does not directly affect the seller's profit calculation (the seller remits the tax to the government).

### Key Terminology

| Term | Symbol | Meaning |
|------|--------|---------|
| Cost Price | CP | The amount paid to acquire or produce an item |
| Selling Price | SP | The amount received when the item is sold |
| Marked Price | MP | The listed/displayed price before any discount |
| Profit | P | The gain from a transaction (SP − CP when SP > CP) |
| Loss | L | The deficit from a transaction (CP − SP when CP > SP) |
| VAT | — | Value-Added Tax, currently 12% in the Philippines |
| Tax Amount | T | The peso value of tax on a transaction |
| Total Price | TP | The final amount paid including tax |

### The Relationship Among CP, SP, and Tax

Think of a transaction as a flow:

```
Seller buys item → CP (cost price)
Seller sets price → SP (selling price)
Government adds tax → TP = SP + Tax Amount
Buyer pays → Total Price (TP)
```

The seller's profit or loss depends only on CP vs. SP. The tax is a separate layer that affects what the buyer ultimately pays.

### When Is It Profit vs. Loss?

- If SP > CP → **Profit** (the seller gained money)
- If SP < CP → **Loss** (the seller lost money)
- If SP = CP → **Break-even** (no gain, no loss)

**Example:** A government office bought a printer for ₱12,000 and later sold it as surplus for ₱8,000.
- CP = ₱12,000, SP = ₱8,000
- Since SP < CP, this is a **loss** of ₱12,000 − ₱8,000 = ₱4,000

## 4.2 Profit

### Computing Profit Amount

The profit amount is simply the difference between selling price and cost price:

```
Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price
P = SP − CP
```

This formula applies only when SP > CP. If SP < CP, the result is negative, which means it is a loss (covered in the next section).

**Easy Example:**
A sari-sari store owner bought a case of canned goods for ₱1,200 and sold all cans for a total of ₱1,500.

- CP = ₱1,200
- SP = ₱1,500
- Profit = ₱1,500 − ₱1,200 = **₱300**

**Medium Example:**
An office supply dealer purchased 50 reams of bond paper at ₱180 per ream and sold them at ₱210 per ream.

- Total CP = 50 × ₱180 = ₱9,000
- Total SP = 50 × ₱210 = ₱10,500
- Profit = ₱10,500 − ₱9,000 = **₱1,500**

**Hard Example:**
A contractor bid ₱450,000 for a government project. His actual expenses were: materials ₱280,000, labor ₱95,000, permits ₱15,000, and transportation ₱12,000.

- Total CP = ₱280,000 + ₱95,000 + ₱15,000 + ₱12,000 = ₱402,000
- SP (contract price) = ₱450,000
- Profit = ₱450,000 − ₱402,000 = **₱48,000**

### Computing Profit Percentage

Profit percentage tells you how much profit was earned *relative to the cost price*. It answers: "For every ₱100 spent, how many pesos were gained?"

```
Profit Percentage = (Profit ÷ Cost Price) × 100%
Profit % = (P ÷ CP) × 100%
```

**CSE Tip:** The base (denominator) for profit percentage is ALWAYS the cost price, not the selling price. This is the most common error on the exam.

**Easy Example:**
CP = ₱500, SP = ₱600
- Profit = ₱600 − ₱500 = ₱100
- Profit % = (₱100 ÷ ₱500) × 100% = **20%**

**Medium Example:**
A canteen operator spent ₱3,500 on ingredients and earned ₱4,200 from food sales.
- Profit = ₱4,200 − ₱3,500 = ₱700
- Profit % = (₱700 ÷ ₱3,500) × 100% = **20%**

**Hard Example:**
An electronics store bought a laptop for ₱32,000 and sold it for ₱38,400.
- Profit = ₱38,400 − ₱32,000 = ₱6,400
- Profit % = (₱6,400 ÷ ₱32,000) × 100% = **20%**

### Finding Selling Price Given Profit Percentage

Sometimes the exam gives you CP and profit %, and asks for SP:

```
SP = CP × (1 + Profit%/100)
```

**Example:** CP = ₱2,000, Profit % = 25%
- SP = ₱2,000 × (1 + 25/100) = ₱2,000 × 1.25 = **₱2,500**

### Finding Cost Price Given SP and Profit Percentage

```
CP = SP ÷ (1 + Profit%/100)
```

**Example:** SP = ₱3,600, Profit % = 20%
- CP = ₱3,600 ÷ 1.20 = **₱3,000**

## 4.3 Loss

### Computing Loss Amount

Loss is the opposite of profit — it occurs when the selling price is less than the cost price:

```
Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price
L = CP − SP
```

This formula applies only when CP > SP.

**Easy Example:**
A vendor bought fruits for ₱800 but could only sell them for ₱600 before they spoiled.
- CP = ₱800, SP = ₱600
- Loss = ₱800 − ₱600 = **₱200**

**Medium Example:**
A government agency purchased office chairs at ₱4,500 each. After 3 years, they sold 10 chairs as surplus at ₱2,700 each.
- CP per chair = ₱4,500, SP per chair = ₱2,700
- Loss per chair = ₱4,500 − ₱2,700 = ₱1,800
- Total loss = 10 × ₱1,800 = **₱18,000**

**Hard Example:**
A transport cooperative bought a jeepney for ₱850,000. After operating costs of ₱120,000 and earning ₱780,000 in fares over its lifetime, they sold it for scrap at ₱95,000.
- Total cost = ₱850,000 + ₱120,000 = ₱970,000
- Total revenue = ₱780,000 + ₱95,000 = ₱875,000
- Loss = ₱970,000 − ₱875,000 = **₱95,000**

### Computing Loss Percentage

Loss percentage tells you how much was lost *relative to the cost price*:

```
Loss Percentage = (Loss ÷ Cost Price) × 100%
Loss % = (L ÷ CP) × 100%
```

**CSE Tip:** Just like profit percentage, the base for loss percentage is the cost price.

**Easy Example:**
CP = ₱1,000, SP = ₱800
- Loss = ₱1,000 − ₱800 = ₱200
- Loss % = (₱200 ÷ ₱1,000) × 100% = **20%**

**Medium Example:**
A bookstore bought textbooks at ₱350 each and had to clear them at ₱280 each.
- Loss per book = ₱350 − ₱280 = ₱70
- Loss % = (₱70 ÷ ₱350) × 100% = **20%**

**Hard Example:**
An engineering firm invested ₱1,250,000 in equipment that they later sold for ₱937,500.
- Loss = ₱1,250,000 − ₱937,500 = ₱312,500
- Loss % = (₱312,500 ÷ ₱1,250,000) × 100% = **25%**

### Finding Selling Price Given Loss Percentage

```
SP = CP × (1 − Loss%/100)
```

**Example:** CP = ₱5,000, Loss % = 15%
- SP = ₱5,000 × (1 − 15/100) = ₱5,000 × 0.85 = **₱4,250**

### Finding Cost Price Given SP and Loss Percentage

```
CP = SP ÷ (1 − Loss%/100)
```

**Example:** SP = ₱6,800, Loss % = 15%
- CP = ₱6,800 ÷ 0.85 = **₱8,000**

## 4.4 Sales Tax and VAT

### What Is Sales Tax?

**Sales tax** is a percentage-based charge added to the selling price of goods and services. The buyer pays the tax on top of the item's price, and the seller remits it to the government.

### What Is VAT?

**Value-Added Tax (VAT)** is the specific form of sales tax used in the Philippines. The standard rate is **12%**. VAT applies to most goods and services, with some exemptions (basic commodities, educational services, etc.).

For CSE purposes, unless stated otherwise, assume the Philippine VAT rate of 12%.

### Computing Tax Amount

```
Tax Amount = Base Price × Tax Rate
T = Price × (Rate ÷ 100)
```

For Philippine VAT:
```
VAT Amount = Base Price × 0.12
```

**Easy Example:**
An item costs ₱500 before tax. VAT = 12%.
- Tax Amount = ₱500 × 0.12 = **₱60**

**Medium Example:**
Office supplies totaling ₱8,450 are subject to 12% VAT.
- Tax Amount = ₱8,450 × 0.12 = **₱1,014**

**Hard Example:**
A construction company purchases materials worth ₱245,000 (VAT-exclusive). Compute the VAT.
- VAT = ₱245,000 × 0.12 = **₱29,400**

### Computing Total Price (Tax-Inclusive)

```
Total Price = Base Price + Tax Amount
TP = Price + (Price × Rate/100)
TP = Price × (1 + Rate/100)
```

For 12% VAT:
```
Total Price = Base Price × 1.12
```

**Easy Example:**
A meal costs ₱350 before VAT.
- Total Price = ₱350 × 1.12 = **₱392**

**Medium Example:**
A government office orders furniture worth ₱56,000 (VAT-exclusive).
- Total Price = ₱56,000 × 1.12 = **₱62,720**

**Hard Example:**
An engineering firm bills ₱1,800,000 for services (VAT-exclusive).
- Total Price = ₱1,800,000 × 1.12 = **₱2,016,000**

### Extracting Base Price from a VAT-Inclusive Amount

Sometimes the exam gives you the total (VAT-inclusive) price and asks for the base price or the VAT amount:

```
Base Price = Total Price ÷ (1 + Rate/100)
```

For 12% VAT:
```
Base Price = VAT-Inclusive Price ÷ 1.12
VAT Amount = VAT-Inclusive Price − Base Price
```

**Example:** A receipt shows ₱1,120 (VAT-inclusive). Find the base price and VAT.
- Base Price = ₱1,120 ÷ 1.12 = ₱1,000
- VAT = ₱1,120 − ₱1,000 = **₱120**

**CSE Tip:** A common trap is computing 12% of the VAT-inclusive price. That gives the wrong answer. You must divide by 1.12 first, then subtract.

**Wrong approach:** ₱1,120 × 0.12 = ₱134.40 ← INCORRECT
**Correct approach:** ₱1,120 ÷ 1.12 = ₱1,000, then VAT = ₱120 ← CORRECT

## 4.5 Identifying Profit, Loss, and Tax Problems

### Recognizing Problem Types by Clue Words

CSE questions use specific language that signals which formula to apply:

| Clue Words | Problem Type | Formula Direction |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| bought for... sold for... | Profit or Loss | Compare SP vs CP |
| gained, earned, profit, markup | Profit | SP − CP or Profit % |
| lost, deficit, below cost | Loss | CP − SP or Loss % |
| VAT, tax, surcharge, inclusive | Tax | Price × Rate |
| total amount paid, final price | Tax-inclusive | Price × (1 + Rate) |
| net amount, before tax | Base price | Total ÷ (1 + Rate) |
| percent gain, percent profit | Profit % | (P ÷ CP) × 100 |
| percent loss | Loss % | (L ÷ CP) × 100 |

### Identifying Known and Unknown Values

Step-by-step approach for any profit/loss/tax problem:

1. **Read the problem completely** before computing anything
2. **Identify what is given:** CP? SP? Rate? Percentage? Total?
3. **Identify what is asked:** Amount? Percentage? Original price? Final price?
4. **Select the correct formula** based on the relationship
5. **Compute and verify** — does the answer make sense?

**Example of identification:**
"A store bought a TV for ₱15,000 and sold it at a 30% profit. What was the selling price?"
- Given: CP = ₱15,000, Profit % = 30%
- Asked: SP = ?
- Formula: SP = CP × (1 + Profit%/100) = ₱15,000 × 1.30 = ₱19,500

## 4.6 Multi-Step Financial Problems

### Problems Involving Profit and Tax Together

Many CSE questions combine profit and tax in a single problem. The key is to handle them in the correct order:

1. First compute the selling price (based on cost + profit)
2. Then add tax to get the total price the buyer pays

```
Step 1: SP = CP × (1 + Profit%/100)
Step 2: Total Price = SP × (1 + Tax Rate/100)
```

**Example:**
A retailer buys a washing machine for ₱18,000 and marks it up by 25%. The buyer must also pay 12% VAT. What is the total amount the buyer pays?

Step 1: SP = ₱18,000 × 1.25 = ₱22,500
Step 2: Total = ₱22,500 × 1.12 = **₱25,200**

### Problems Involving Loss and Tax

Even when a seller takes a loss, the buyer still pays VAT on the selling price:

**Example:**
A store sells damaged inventory originally costing ₱5,000 at a 40% loss. VAT of 12% applies. What does the buyer pay?

Step 1: SP = ₱5,000 × (1 − 0.40) = ₱5,000 × 0.60 = ₱3,000
Step 2: Total = ₱3,000 × 1.12 = **₱3,360**

### Problems Combining Discount and Tax

A common CSE scenario: an item has a discount, then tax is applied to the discounted price.

```
Step 1: Discounted Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount%/100)
Step 2: Total Price = Discounted Price × (1 + Tax Rate/100)
```

**Example:**
A laptop marked at ₱45,000 is on 20% sale. 12% VAT applies after discount. Final price?

Step 1: Discounted = ₱45,000 × 0.80 = ₱36,000
Step 2: Total = ₱36,000 × 1.12 = **₱40,320**

### Organizing Multi-Step Computations

For complex problems, use a systematic layout:

**Example:**
A wholesaler sells goods costing ₱120,000 at a 15% profit. The buyer gets a 5% loyalty discount on the selling price, then pays 12% VAT. What is the final amount?

Step 1: SP = ₱120,000 × 1.15 = ₱138,000
Step 2: After discount = ₱138,000 × 0.95 = ₱131,100
Step 3: After VAT = ₱131,100 × 1.12 = **₱146,832**

## 4.7 Practical Applications of Profit, Loss, and Tax

### Retail Pricing

A clothing store buys shirts at ₱250 each and sells them at ₱400 each.
- Profit per shirt = ₱400 − ₱250 = ₱150
- Profit % = (₱150 ÷ ₱250) × 100% = 60%

### Payroll Deductions

An employee's gross salary is ₱35,000. After 12% withholding tax:
- Tax = ₱35,000 × 0.12 = ₱4,200
- Net pay = ₱35,000 − ₱4,200 = ₱30,800

### Government Procurement

A government agency receives a bid of ₱500,000 (VAT-exclusive) for office renovation.
- VAT = ₱500,000 × 0.12 = ₱60,000
- Total contract value = ₱560,000

### Transportation Ticket Pricing

A bus company's base fare is ₱180. With 12% VAT:
- Total fare = ₱180 × 1.12 = ₱201.60

### Engineering Project Budgeting

Materials cost ₱2,400,000. The contractor adds a 20% profit margin, and the client pays 12% VAT on the total.
- Contractor price = ₱2,400,000 × 1.20 = ₱2,880,000
- Client pays = ₱2,880,000 × 1.12 = ₱3,225,600

### Online Shopping

An item is listed at ₱1,499 (VAT-inclusive). What is the actual item price?
- Base price = ₱1,499 ÷ 1.12 = ₱1,338.39

### Restaurant Billing

A meal subtotal is ₱850. Service charge is 10%, then 12% VAT on the total.
- After service charge = ₱850 × 1.10 = ₱935
- After VAT = ₱935 × 1.12 = ₱1,047.20

## 4.8 Successive Percentage Effects in Business

### Why Percentages Cannot Simply Be Added

A common error is thinking that a 25% markup followed by 12% VAT equals a 37% increase. This is wrong because each percentage operates on a **different base**.

- 25% markup is applied to the cost price
- 12% VAT is applied to the selling price (which is already marked up)

**Proof:**
CP = ₱1,000
- After 25% markup: ₱1,000 × 1.25 = ₱1,250
- After 12% VAT: ₱1,250 × 1.12 = ₱1,400
- Actual total increase: (₱1,400 − ₱1,000) ÷ ₱1,000 = 40% (not 37%)

### The Multiplier Method

Instead of computing each step separately, multiply all factors together:

**For successive increases:**
```
Final = Original × (1 + r₁/100) × (1 + r₂/100) × ...
```

**For successive decreases:**
```
Final = Original × (1 − r₁/100) × (1 − r₂/100) × ...
```

**For mixed (increase then decrease):**
```
Final = Original × (1 + r₁/100) × (1 − r₂/100)
```

**Example:** Cost = ₱10,000, markup = 30%, discount = 10%, VAT = 12%
- Combined multiplier = 1.30 × 0.90 × 1.12 = 1.3104
- Final price = ₱10,000 × 1.3104 = **₱13,104**

### Profit After Discount

If a seller marks up by 40% then gives a 10% discount:
- Effective multiplier = 1.40 × 0.90 = 1.26
- Effective profit = 26% (not 30%)

### Tax-Inclusive Pricing Systems

Some businesses quote prices that already include VAT. To find the pre-tax price:
- Divide by 1.12 (for 12% VAT)

To find the VAT portion of a tax-inclusive price:
- VAT = Price − (Price ÷ 1.12)
- Or equivalently: VAT = Price × (12/112)

**Example:** Receipt shows ₱5,600 (VAT-inclusive)
- Base = ₱5,600 ÷ 1.12 = ₱5,000
- VAT = ₱5,600 − ₱5,000 = ₱600
- Quick check: ₱5,600 × (12/112) = ₱600 ✓

## 4.9 Problem-Solving Strategies

### Step-by-Step System for Any Profit/Loss/Tax Problem

1. **Read** the entire problem — identify the story
2. **Label** all given values (CP, SP, rate, etc.)
3. **Determine** what is being asked
4. **Choose** the correct formula
5. **Compute** carefully
6. **Verify** — does the answer make logical sense?

### Quick Formula Selection Guide

| Given | Asked | Formula |
|-------|-------|---------|
| CP, SP | Profit | P = SP − CP |
| CP, SP | Profit % | (SP − CP) ÷ CP × 100 |
| CP, Profit % | SP | CP × (1 + r/100) |
| SP, Profit % | CP | SP ÷ (1 + r/100) |
| CP, SP | Loss | L = CP − SP |
| CP, SP | Loss % | (CP − SP) ÷ CP × 100 |
| CP, Loss % | SP | CP × (1 − r/100) |
| SP, Loss % | CP | SP ÷ (1 − r/100) |
| Price, Tax % | Tax Amount | Price × r/100 |
| Price, Tax % | Total | Price × (1 + r/100) |
| Total (inclusive) | Base Price | Total ÷ (1 + r/100) |

### Estimation Before Solving

Before computing, estimate to eliminate obviously wrong choices:

- 12% VAT on ₱10,000 ≈ ₱1,200 → total ≈ ₱11,200
- 25% profit on ₱8,000 → profit ≈ ₱2,000 → SP ≈ ₱10,000
- 10% loss on ₱50,000 → loss ≈ ₱5,000 → SP ≈ ₱45,000

If a choice is wildly different from your estimate, eliminate it immediately.

## 4.10 Estimation and Mental Math Techniques

### Benchmark Percentages for Quick Computation

Memorize these for speed:

| Percentage | Multiplier | Mental Shortcut |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| 10% | × 0.10 | Move decimal one place left |
| 12% (VAT) | × 0.12 | 10% + 2% (or 10% + one-fifth of 10%) |
| 15% | × 0.15 | 10% + half of 10% |
| 20% | × 0.20 | Divide by 5 |
| 25% | × 0.25 | Divide by 4 |
| 50% | × 0.50 | Divide by 2 |

### Quick VAT Computation (12%)

For 12% of any amount:
1. Find 10% (move decimal left)
2. Find 2% (divide 10% by 5, or find 1% and double)
3. Add them together

**Example:** 12% of ₱8,500
- 10% = ₱850
- 2% = ₱170
- 12% = ₱850 + ₱170 = **₱1,020**

### Quick Profit/Loss Estimation

For profit percentage, think in fractions:
- 20% profit = gained 1/5 of cost
- 25% profit = gained 1/4 of cost
- 33.3% profit = gained 1/3 of cost
- 50% profit = gained 1/2 of cost

**Example:** CP = ₱12,000, Profit = ₱3,000
- ₱3,000 is 1/4 of ₱12,000 → Profit % = 25%

### Elimination Technique

On multiple choice, if CP = ₱10,000 and profit % = 20%:
- SP must be MORE than ₱10,000 (eliminate anything ≤ ₱10,000)
- SP = ₱10,000 × 1.20 = ₱12,000
- Any choice far from ₱12,000 is wrong

## 4.11 Common Errors in Profit, Loss, and Tax Problems

### Error 1: Using the Wrong Base

**Wrong:** Profit % = Profit ÷ Selling Price × 100
**Correct:** Profit % = Profit ÷ Cost Price × 100

The base for profit/loss percentage is ALWAYS the cost price.

### Error 2: Confusing Markup with Margin

- **Markup** = Profit ÷ Cost Price (based on what you paid)
- **Margin** = Profit ÷ Selling Price (based on what you received)

CSE problems almost always ask for markup (profit percentage based on cost).

### Error 3: Computing VAT on a VAT-Inclusive Price

**Wrong:** VAT = ₱1,120 × 0.12 = ₱134.40
**Correct:** Base = ₱1,120 ÷ 1.12 = ₱1,000; VAT = ₱120

### Error 4: Adding Successive Percentages

**Wrong:** 20% markup + 12% VAT = 32% total increase
**Correct:** Multiplier = 1.20 × 1.12 = 1.344 → 34.4% total increase

### Error 5: Forgetting Which Price Includes Tax

Read carefully whether the given price is:
- "VAT-exclusive" (tax must be added)
- "VAT-inclusive" (tax is already inside)

### Error 6: Arithmetic Carelessness with Decimals

When multiplying by 1.12:
- ₱5,000 × 1.12 = ₱5,600 (not ₱5,120 — that's × 1.024)
- Double-check by estimating: 12% of ₱5,000 = ₱600, so total ≈ ₱5,600 ✓

## Exam Strategies

### Recognizing Question Types Quickly

- See "bought" and "sold" → Profit or Loss problem
- See "VAT" or "tax" → Tax computation
- See "percent profit" or "percent loss" → Percentage formula (base = CP)
- See "total amount paid" → Likely needs tax addition
- See "before tax" or "net" → Likely needs tax extraction

### Solving Under Time Pressure

1. Skip the story — go straight to the numbers
2. Identify CP, SP, rate — label them mentally
3. Use the multiplier method for multi-step problems (faster than computing each step)
4. Estimate first, then compute only if needed to distinguish between close choices

### Using Answer Choices to Your Advantage

If CP = ₱20,000 and the question asks for SP after 15% profit:
- SP must be between ₱20,000 and ₱25,000 (15% of 20K = 3K)
- Exact: ₱20,000 × 1.15 = ₱23,000
- If only one choice is in that range, pick it without full computation

### Percentage Shortcuts for Common Rates

- **12% VAT:** multiply by 1.12 (or add 10% + 2%)
- **25% profit:** multiply by 1.25 (or add one-quarter)
- **20% loss:** multiply by 0.80 (or subtract one-fifth)
- **10% discount:** multiply by 0.90 (or subtract one-tenth)

## Mini Practice Set

**1.** A store bought a calculator for ₱450 and sold it for ₱540. What is the profit?
**Answer:** ₱90
**Explanation:** Profit = SP − CP = ₱540 − ₱450 = ₱90.

**2.** A vendor bought mangoes for ₱2,000 and sold them for ₱2,500. What is the profit percentage?
**Answer:** 25%
**Explanation:** Profit = ₱500. Profit % = (₱500 ÷ ₱2,000) × 100% = 25%.

**3.** An item costing ₱800 was sold for ₱640. What is the loss percentage?
**Answer:** 20%
**Explanation:** Loss = ₱800 − ₱640 = ₱160. Loss % = (₱160 ÷ ₱800) × 100% = 20%.

**4.** A phone costs ₱15,000 before VAT (12%). What is the total price?
**Answer:** ₱16,800
**Explanation:** Total = ₱15,000 × 1.12 = ₱16,800.

**5.** A receipt shows ₱2,240 (VAT-inclusive at 12%). What is the base price?
**Answer:** ₱2,000
**Explanation:** Base = ₱2,240 ÷ 1.12 = ₱2,000.

**6.** CP = ₱6,000, Profit % = 30%. Find SP.
**Answer:** ₱7,800
**Explanation:** SP = ₱6,000 × 1.30 = ₱7,800.

**7.** SP = ₱4,800, Loss % = 20%. Find CP.
**Answer:** ₱6,000
**Explanation:** CP = ₱4,800 ÷ 0.80 = ₱6,000.

**8.** An item marked at ₱10,000 has a 15% discount, then 12% VAT. Final price?
**Answer:** ₱9,520
**Explanation:** After discount = ₱10,000 × 0.85 = ₱8,500. After VAT = ₱8,500 × 1.12 = ₱9,520.

**9.** A seller buys goods for ₱25,000 and sells at 20% profit. Buyer pays 12% VAT. Total paid?
**Answer:** ₱33,600
**Explanation:** SP = ₱25,000 × 1.20 = ₱30,000. Total = ₱30,000 × 1.12 = ₱33,600.

**10.** What is 12% VAT on ₱7,500?
**Answer:** ₱900
**Explanation:** VAT = ₱7,500 × 0.12 = ₱900.

**11.** A dealer bought 100 items at ₱50 each and sold 80 at ₱70 each. The remaining 20 were sold at ₱30 each. Total profit?
**Answer:** ₱1,200
**Explanation:** Total CP = 100 × ₱50 = ₱5,000. Total SP = (80 × ₱70) + (20 × ₱30) = ₱5,600 + ₱600 = ₱6,200. Profit = ₱6,200 − ₱5,000 = ₱1,200.

**12.** A laptop costs ₱35,000. After 25% markup and 12% VAT, what does the buyer pay?
**Answer:** ₱49,000
**Explanation:** SP = ₱35,000 × 1.25 = ₱43,750. Total = ₱43,750 × 1.12 = ₱49,000.

**13.** A government surplus item originally cost ₱120,000 and was sold at 65% loss. Selling price?
**Answer:** ₱42,000
**Explanation:** SP = ₱120,000 × (1 − 0.65) = ₱120,000 × 0.35 = ₱42,000.

**14.** If profit is ₱1,800 and profit percentage is 15%, what is the cost price?
**Answer:** ₱12,000
**Explanation:** CP = Profit ÷ (Profit%/100) = ₱1,800 ÷ 0.15 = ₱12,000.

**15.** A VAT-inclusive price is ₱33,600. If VAT is 12%, what is the VAT amount?
**Answer:** ₱3,600
**Explanation:** Base = ₱33,600 ÷ 1.12 = ₱30,000. VAT = ₱33,600 − ₱30,000 = ₱3,600.

**16.** An item is bought for ₱4,000 and sold at a 12.5% profit. What is the selling price?
**Answer:** ₱4,500
**Explanation:** SP = ₱4,000 × 1.125 = ₱4,500.

**17.** A store marks up goods by 40% then offers a 20% discount. What is the effective profit percentage?
**Answer:** 12%
**Explanation:** Multiplier = 1.40 × 0.80 = 1.12. Effective profit = 12%.

**18.** Total sales for the day were ₱89,600 (all VAT-inclusive at 12%). What is the total VAT collected?
**Answer:** ₱9,600
**Explanation:** Base = ₱89,600 ÷ 1.12 = ₱80,000. VAT = ₱89,600 − ₱80,000 = ₱9,600.

**19.** A trader sold an item for ₱7,200 and made a 20% profit. What was the cost price?
**Answer:** ₱6,000
**Explanation:** CP = SP ÷ (1 + Profit%/100) = ₱7,200 ÷ 1.20 = ₱6,000.

**20.** Materials cost ₱500,000. Contractor adds 18% profit, then 12% VAT applies. Total project cost?
**Answer:** ₱660,800
**Explanation:** After profit = ₱500,000 × 1.18 = ₱590,000. After VAT = ₱590,000 × 1.12 = ₱660,800.

## Quick Recap

| Concept | Formula | Key Point |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Profit | SP − CP | SP must exceed CP |
| Loss | CP − SP | CP must exceed SP |
| Profit % | (P ÷ CP) × 100 | Base is always CP |
| Loss % | (L ÷ CP) × 100 | Base is always CP |
| Tax Amount | Price × Rate | Rate as decimal |
| Total (with tax) | Price × (1 + Rate) | Multiplier method |
| Base from inclusive | Total ÷ (1 + Rate) | Divide, don't subtract % |
| Successive changes | Multiply all factors | Never add percentages |

## Memory Aids

- **"Profit Percentage = Profit over Purchase"** — both start with P, and "purchase" reminds you the base is cost price
- **"1.12 for VAT"** — memorize this multiplier; multiply to add VAT, divide to remove VAT
- **"SP > CP = Smile (Profit), SP < CP = Sad (Loss)"** — quick emotional check
- **"Don't add, multiply"** — for successive percentages, always multiply the factors
- **"Divide to find the base"** — whenever you have a tax-inclusive amount, divide by (1 + rate)
- **"12% = 10% + 2%"** — break VAT into easy mental pieces
