# Percentage Word Problems

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Percentage word problems** represent the most challenging and most frequently tested category within the Numerical Ability section of the Philippine Civil Service Examination. Unlike straightforward percentage computations (where numbers and operations are explicitly stated), word problems require you to **translate everyday language into mathematical equations**, identify hidden operations, and execute multi-step solutions under time pressure.

These problems simulate the kind of quantitative reasoning that government employees perform daily:
- **Shopping and commerce** — computing final prices after successive discounts, comparing deals
- **Taxes and VAT** — determining tax-inclusive prices, computing withholding amounts
- **Salaries and payroll** — calculating net pay after multiple deductions, computing raises
- **Business and finance** — profit margins, commission structures, break-even analysis
- **Transportation** — fare adjustments, fuel cost changes, route efficiency
- **Population and demographics** — growth rates, survey interpretation, census data
- **Engineering and construction** — material waste percentages, project completion rates
- **Government budgets** — allocation changes, year-over-year comparisons, deficit calculations
- **Education** — passing rates, grade computations, scholarship thresholds
- **Banking** — simple interest approximations, loan computations, savings growth

The CSE specifically tests percentage word problems because they assess two skills simultaneously: **reading comprehension** (understanding what is being asked) and **mathematical reasoning** (selecting and executing the correct operations). Many examinees who can compute 25% of 800 easily will struggle when the same problem is embedded in a three-sentence scenario about a government procurement discount.

**Common mistakes examinees make:**
1. Misidentifying which value is the base (whole) in the problem context
2. Performing only one step when the problem requires two or three operations
3. Confusing "percent OF" with "percent MORE THAN" or "percent LESS THAN"
4. Applying a percentage to the wrong quantity (e.g., applying discount to the discounted price)
5. Ignoring contextual constraints (e.g., computing a discount on an already-reduced item)
6. Adding percentages directly when they apply to different bases
7. Failing to read what the question actually asks (finding the discount amount when asked for the final price)
8. Not converting between "what remains" and "what was removed" (e.g., 30% off means 70% remains)

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Translate percentage word problems into mathematical equations accurately
- Identify the correct percentage operation from contextual clues
- Solve multi-step percentage problems involving successive operations
- Analyze real-life percentage situations involving discounts, taxes, commissions, and salaries
- Distinguish between problems asking for the part, the whole, or the rate
- Apply the complement strategy (100% − x%) for efficiency
- Interpret percentage comparison statements correctly
- Solve CSE-style percentage word problems within time constraints

### 4.1 Translating Word Problems into Equations

### The Translation Process

The single most important skill in percentage word problems is **translation** — converting English sentences into mathematical expressions. Every percentage word problem, no matter how complex, reduces to one or more applications of the core relationship:

```
Part = Rate × Whole
```

The challenge is identifying which words map to which component.

### Keyword-to-Operation Mapping

| Keyword/Phrase | Mathematical Meaning | Example |
|----------------|---------------------|---------|
| "percent of" | Multiply (rate × whole) | "25% of 400" → 0.25 × 400 |
| "is" or "equals" | = (equals sign) | "What is 30% of 50?" → x = 0.30 × 50 |
| "what" or "how much" | Unknown variable (x) | "What percent..." → x% |
| "increased by" | Add the percentage amount | "increased by 10%" → original + 10% of original |
| "decreased by" | Subtract the percentage amount | "decreased by 15%" → original − 15% of original |
| "more than" | Addition context | "20% more than 500" → 500 + 0.20(500) |
| "less than" | Subtraction context | "30% less than 200" → 200 − 0.30(200) |
| "of the original" | The original is the base | Base = original value |
| "of the new price" | The new value is the base | Base = new value |
| "remaining" or "left" | Complement (100% − removed%) | "After 40% discount, what remains?" → 60% |
| "total" or "altogether" | Sum of parts | Add all components |

### The Three-Step Translation System

**Step 1: Identify what is being asked.**
Read the last sentence first. What does the question want? The final price? The percentage? The original amount?

**Step 2: Identify the given values.**
Highlight or underline all numbers and their roles. Which is the base? Which is the rate?

**Step 3: Build the equation.**
Map the verbal relationship to Part = Rate × Whole (or its rearrangements).

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** Translating word problems into algebraic equations works because the English structure mirrors mathematical operations exactly. The word "of" indicates multiplication, "is" represents the equals sign, and the percentage acts as a rate. By breaking the verbal description down, you create a direct map to one of the three variations of `Part = Rate × Whole`.

### Worked Translation Examples

**Example 1 (Easy):**
"A government office has 250 employees. If 60% are permanent, how many permanent employees are there?"

Translation:
- Asked: the part (number of permanent employees)
- Given: whole = 250, rate = 60%
- Equation: Part = 0.60 × 250 = **150**

**Example 2 (Medium):**
"Out of 400 applicants, 340 passed the eligibility exam. What percentage passed?"

Translation:
- Asked: the rate (percentage)
- Given: part = 340, whole = 400
- Equation: Rate = 340 ÷ 400 = 0.85 = **85%**

**Example 3 (Hard):**
"After a 20% salary deduction, an employee takes home ₱24,000. What was the original salary?"

Translation:
- Asked: the whole (original salary)
- Given: rate remaining = 80% (since 100% − 20% = 80%), part = ₱24,000
- Equation: 24,000 = 0.80 × Whole → Whole = 24,000 ÷ 0.80 = **₱30,000**

**CSE Tip:** When a problem says "after X% is removed," the remaining amount equals (100% − X%) of the original. This complement approach avoids a two-step calculation.

### 4.2 Identifying the Correct Operation

### Why Keywords Alone Are Not Enough

Many examinees memorize keyword lists and apply operations mechanically. This fails when problems use indirect language or when the same keyword appears in different contexts.

Consider these two problems:
- "What is 25% of 800?" → Multiply: 0.25 × 800 = 200
- "200 is 25% of what number?" → Divide: 200 ÷ 0.25 = 800

Both contain "25% of," but the operations are different because the unknown is in a different position.

### The Position-Based Decision System

The operation depends on **which component is unknown**:

| Unknown | Formula | Operation |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Part (P) | P = R × W | Multiply rate by whole |
| Rate (R) | R = P ÷ W | Divide part by whole |
| Whole (W) | W = P ÷ R | Divide part by rate |

### Recognizing Increase vs. Decrease Contexts

**Increase indicators:** raised, grew, added, bonus, markup, surcharge, appreciation, gained, expanded, rose
**Decrease indicators:** discount, reduction, deduction, loss, depreciation, cut, declined, shrunk, dropped

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "In a markup or tax problem, you should subtract the percentage from 100% since it's an extra cost."
>
> **Why it fails:** Markup and tax increase the total amount. Subtracting the percentage from 100% (e.g., `100% − 12% = 88%`) results in a price decrease, which violates the logic of tax. For example, if a ₱500 item has 12% VAT, the multiplier should be `1.12` (increasing the price to ₱560), not `0.88` (which decreases it to ₱440).
>
> **Correct model:** For increases (markup, tax, raises), the multiplier is `1 + Rate`. For decreases (discounts, deductions, loss), the multiplier is `1 − Rate`.

**Example (Medium):**
"A store marks up its products by 40% over cost. If a product costs ₱500, what is the selling price?"

Analysis:
- "marks up by 40%" = increase context
- Base (cost) = ₱500
- Markup amount = 0.40 × 500 = ₱200
- Selling price = 500 + 200 = **₱700**

Alternatively using the multiplier: 500 × 1.40 = **₱700**

### Operation-Selection Flowchart

```
START: Read the problem
  │
  ├─ Is the problem asking for a PART (amount)?
  │     → MULTIPLY: Rate × Whole
  │
  ├─ Is the problem asking for a RATE (percentage)?
  │     → DIVIDE: Part ÷ Whole, then × 100
  │
  └─ Is the problem asking for the WHOLE (base/original)?
        → DIVIDE: Part ÷ Rate
```

**Common Trap:** "What percent MORE" is not the same as "what percent OF."
- "150 is what percent of 200?" → 150 ÷ 200 = 75%
- "150 is what percent more than 100?" → (150 − 100) ÷ 100 = 50%

**1.** A government clerk processed 180 out of 240 files today. What percentage of files did he process? → **75%** (180 ÷ 240 × 100 = 75%)

### 4.3 Multi-Step Percentage Problems

### What Makes a Problem Multi-Step?

A multi-step percentage problem requires **two or more percentage operations** to reach the final answer. The output of one step becomes the input for the next.

### Types of Multi-Step Problems

**Type 1: Successive Changes (Same Direction)**
Two or more increases, or two or more decreases, applied sequentially.

**Example:** A product priced at ₱1,000 receives a 20% discount, then an additional 10% discount on the reduced price. What is the final price?

Step 1: First discount → ₱1,000 × 0.80 = ₱800
Step 2: Second discount → ₱800 × 0.90 = **₱720**

**Warning:** The answer is NOT ₱1,000 × 0.70 = ₱700. You cannot add 20% + 10% = 30% because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price, not the original.

Using multipliers: ₱1,000 × 0.80 × 0.90 = **₱720**

**Type 2: Successive Changes (Opposite Directions)**
An increase followed by a decrease, or vice versa.

**Example:** A salary of ₱25,000 is increased by 10%, then the new salary is decreased by 10%. What is the final salary?

Step 1: Increase → ₱25,000 × 1.10 = ₱27,500
Step 2: Decrease → ₱27,500 × 0.90 = **₱24,750**

**Warning:** The final salary is NOT ₱25,000. A 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease does NOT return to the original because the decrease applies to the larger amount.

Net multiplier: 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99 → net effect is a 1% decrease.

**Type 3: Finding an Intermediate Value**
The problem gives the final result and asks for an earlier value.

**Example:** After a 25% discount, a laptop costs ₱36,000. What was the original price?

Analysis: The ₱36,000 represents 75% of the original (100% − 25% = 75%).
Equation: 0.75 × Original = 36,000
Solution: Original = 36,000 ÷ 0.75 = **₱48,000**

### The Multiplier Method

For successive percentage changes, convert each change to a **multiplier** and multiply them together:

| Change | Multiplier |
|--------|-----------|
| Increase by x% | 1 + x/100 |
| Decrease by x% | 1 − x/100 |
| +10% | 1.10 |
| +15% | 1.15 |
| +20% | 1.20 |
| +25% | 1.25 |
| −10% | 0.90 |
| −15% | 0.85 |
| −20% | 0.80 |
| −25% | 0.75 |
| −30% | 0.70 |
| −50% | 0.50 |

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** The multiplier method works because multiplication is associative: `(Base × Multiplier₁) × Multiplier₂ = Base × (Multiplier₁ × Multiplier₂)`. This means we can chain any number of sequential changes together as a single product. It also reveals the net percentage change immediately: in the example below, `1.15 × 0.90 = 1.035`, which tells us the net budget grew by exactly `3.5%` over two years, completely bypassing intermediate calculations.

**Example (Hard):**
A government project budget of ₱2,000,000 is increased by 15% in Year 2, then decreased by 10% in Year 3. What is the Year 3 budget?

Multiplier chain: ₱2,000,000 × 1.15 × 0.90
= ₱2,000,000 × 1.035
= **₱2,070,000**

Net effect: 3.5% increase from the original.

### 4.4 Real-Life Percentage Word Problem Contexts

### Shopping and Discounts

**Scenario:** A department store offers a "Buy 1, Get 50% Off the 2nd Item" promotion. If each item costs ₱1,200, how much do you pay for two items?

Solution:
- First item: ₱1,200 (full price)
- Second item: ₱1,200 × 0.50 = ₱600
- Total: ₱1,200 + ₱600 = **₱1,800**

Effective discount on two items: (₱2,400 − ₱1,800) ÷ ₱2,400 = 25%

### Taxes and VAT

**Scenario:** A restaurant bill before VAT is ₱2,500. If VAT is 12%, what is the total bill?

Solution: ₱2,500 × 1.12 = **₱2,800**

**Reverse problem:** A receipt shows a VAT-inclusive total of ₱3,360. What is the pre-VAT amount?

Solution: ₱3,360 ÷ 1.12 = **₱3,000**

### Salaries and Deductions

**Scenario:** An employee earns ₱35,000 monthly. Deductions are: 4% for Pag-IBIG, 4% for PhilHealth, and 5% for GSIS. What is the net take-home pay?

Solution:
- Total deduction rate: 4% + 4% + 5% = 13%
- Net rate: 100% − 13% = 87%
- Take-home: ₱35,000 × 0.87 = **₱30,450**

**Note:** When multiple deductions are all computed from the same base (gross salary), you CAN add the percentages. This is different from successive discounts where each applies to a different base.

### Commissions

**Scenario:** A real estate agent earns a 3% commission on sales. If she sold a property worth ₱4,500,000, how much is her commission?

Solution: ₱4,500,000 × 0.03 = **₱135,000**

### Population and Surveys

**Scenario:** A barangay has 12,000 residents. A survey shows 65% are registered voters, and 80% of registered voters actually voted in the last election. How many residents voted?

Solution:
- Registered voters: 12,000 × 0.65 = 7,800
- Actual voters: 7,800 × 0.80 = **6,240**

This is a multi-step problem where the second percentage applies to the result of the first.

### Business Profit and Loss

**Scenario:** A vendor buys goods for ₱8,000 and sells them for ₱10,400. What is the profit percentage?

Solution:
- Profit = ₱10,400 − ₱8,000 = ₱2,400
- Profit % = 2,400 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = **30%**

**Important:** Profit percentage is always computed relative to the COST (buying price), not the selling price.

### Government Budget Reports

**Scenario:** A city's education budget was ₱50,000,000 last year. This year it was increased to ₱58,000,000. What percentage increase is this?

Solution:
- Increase = ₱58,000,000 − ₱50,000,000 = ₱8,000,000
- Percentage increase = 8,000,000 ÷ 50,000,000 × 100 = **16%**

### Check Your Understanding

**1.** A LGU has 20,000 residents. If 60% are adults, and 30% of adults are registered senior citizens, how many registered senior citizens are there? → **3,600** (20,000 × 0.60 × 0.30 = 3,600)
**2.** A store offers a 20% discount on a ₱1,500 bag. Members get an additional 10% off the reduced price. What does a member pay? → **₱1,080** (₱1,500 × 0.80 × 0.90 = ₱1,080)
**3.** A laptop worth ₱40,000 before VAT is sold with 12% VAT applied. Find the final price. → **₱44,800** (₱40,000 × 1.12 = ₱44,800)

### 4.5 Percentage Comparison and Interpretation

### Comparing Percentage Values

Percentage comparisons require careful attention to what each percentage refers to.

**Example:** Store A offers 30% off a ₱2,000 item. Store B offers 25% off a ₱2,200 item. Which store gives the lower final price?

Store A: ₱2,000 × 0.70 = ₱1,400
Store B: ₱2,200 × 0.75 = ₱1,650

**Store A** gives the lower price (₱1,400 vs ₱1,650).

> ⚠️ **Misconception:** "You can compare percentages directly without knowing their bases."
>
> **Why it fails:** A percentage is a relative ratio, not an absolute quantity. It only holds physical meaning when applied to its base. As shown in the example below, a 5% raise is larger than a 10% raise because the base of the first (₱50,000) is much larger than the second (₱20,000). Direct percentage comparisons are only valid when the bases are identical.
>
> **Correct model:** Always compute the absolute amounts (`Part = Whole × Rate`) before making size comparisons, unless the bases are explicitly stated to be the same.

### Percentage vs. Actual Amount

A common CSE trap: a larger percentage does not always mean a larger amount.

**Example:** Company A gave a 5% raise on a ₱50,000 salary. Company B gave a 10% raise on a ₱20,000 salary. Which raise is larger in pesos?

Company A: ₱50,000 × 0.05 = ₱2,500
Company B: ₱20,000 × 0.10 = ₱2,000

Company A's raise is larger (₱2,500 > ₱2,000) despite the lower percentage.

### Interpreting "Percent More Than" vs. "Percent Of"

These two phrases have different mathematical meanings:

- "A is 25% MORE THAN B" means A = B + 0.25B = 1.25B
- "A is 25% OF B" means A = 0.25B

**Example:** If Juan's salary is 20% more than Maria's salary of ₱30,000:
- Juan's salary = ₱30,000 × 1.20 = ₱36,000

If Juan's salary is 20% of Maria's salary of ₱30,000:
- Juan's salary = ₱30,000 × 0.20 = ₱6,000

These are vastly different answers from similar-sounding phrases.

### 4.6 Problem-Solving Strategies

### The READ-PLAN-SOLVE-CHECK System

**READ:** Read the entire problem twice. Underline numbers and circle the question.

**PLAN:** Identify:
- What is given? (numbers and their roles)
- What is asked? (the unknown)
- What operation(s) are needed?

**SOLVE:** Execute the computation step by step. Write intermediate results.

**CHECK:** Does the answer make sense? Is it reasonable given the context?

### Strategy 1: Work Backwards from the Answer Choices

When stuck, test each answer choice to see which satisfies the problem conditions.

**Example:** After a 20% discount, a TV costs ₱16,000. What was the original price?
- A) ₱18,000 → 20% of 18,000 = 3,600 → 18,000 − 3,600 = 14,400 ✗
- B) ₱19,200 → 20% of 19,200 = 3,840 → 19,200 − 3,840 = 15,360 ✗
- C) ₱20,000 → 20% of 20,000 = 4,000 → 20,000 − 4,000 = 16,000 ✓
- D) ₱21,000 → 20% of 21,000 = 4,200 → 21,000 − 4,200 = 16,800 ✗

Answer: **C) ₱20,000**

### Strategy 2: The Complement Approach

Instead of computing the discount/deduction and subtracting, compute what REMAINS directly.

- "30% off" → multiply by 0.70 (what remains)
- "15% deduction" → multiply by 0.85 (what remains)
- "45% failed" → 55% passed

This saves one arithmetic step and reduces error opportunities.

> 🤔 **Why does this work?** The complement approach works due to the distributive property: `Base − (Base × Rate) = Base × (1 − Rate)`. By subtracting the rate from 1 (or 100%) first, you combine subtraction and multiplication into a single step. This is a massive time-saver on the CSE where doing `3,500 × 0.70` mentally is much faster and less error-prone than finding `3,500 × 0.30 = 1,050` and then computing `3,500 − 1,050 = 2,450`.

### Strategy 3: Estimation Before Computation

Before computing, estimate the answer to eliminate obviously wrong choices.

**Example:** What is 23% of ₱4,800?
- Estimate: 25% of 4,800 = 1,200, so 23% should be slightly less than 1,200
- Exact: 0.23 × 4,800 = 1,104
- If choices are: A) ₱1,104  B) ₱1,204  C) ₱984  D) ₱1,344
- Estimation immediately eliminates B and D.

### Strategy 4: Convert Difficult Percentages to Friendly Fractions

| Percentage | Friendly Fraction | Mental Math |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------|
| 10% | 1/10 | Divide by 10 |
| 20% | 1/5 | Divide by 5 |
| 25% | 1/4 | Divide by 4 |
| 33⅓% | 1/3 | Divide by 3 |
| 50% | 1/2 | Divide by 2 |
| 75% | 3/4 | Multiply by 3, divide by 4 |
| 12.5% | 1/8 | Divide by 8 |
| 5% | 1/20 | Divide by 20 (or 10% ÷ 2) |
| 15% | 3/20 | 10% + 5% |
| 30% | 3/10 | 3 × (divide by 10) |

**Example:** 25% of ₱3,600 = ₱3,600 ÷ 4 = **₱900** (faster than 0.25 × 3,600)

### 4.7 Estimation and Answer Checking

### Why Estimation Matters on the CSE

The CSE is timed. Estimation helps you:
1. Eliminate 2-3 wrong choices immediately
2. Verify your computed answer is reasonable
3. Catch arithmetic errors before submitting
4. Save time on difficult computations

### Benchmark Percentages for Quick Estimation

| If the percentage is... | The answer is approximately... |
|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Close to 10% | About 1/10 of the base |
| Close to 25% | About 1/4 of the base |
| Close to 33% | About 1/3 of the base |
| Close to 50% | About half the base |
| Close to 67% | About 2/3 of the base |
| Close to 75% | About 3/4 of the base |
| Close to 100% | About equal to the base |

### Reasonableness Checks

After solving, verify:
- A percentage increase should give a result LARGER than the original
- A percentage decrease should give a result SMALLER than the original
- A part should be LESS than the whole (unless the rate exceeds 100%)
- A rate should be between 0% and 100% for standard problems
- The answer should match the units asked (pesos, people, items, percent)

### Identifying Impossible Answers

Quick elimination rules:
- If asked for a discounted price, the answer must be LESS than the original price
- If asked for a price after markup, the answer must be MORE than the cost
- If asked "what percent of 200 is 50," the answer must be less than 100%
- If the base is ₱10,000 and the rate is 15%, the part cannot exceed ₱10,000

### 4.8 Common Errors in Percentage Word Problems

### Error 1: Wrong Base (Most Common)

**Wrong:** "A shirt is marked up 40% to ₱700. What was the cost?"
Incorrect approach: 40% of 700 = 280, so cost = 700 − 280 = 420 ✗

**Correct:** The ₱700 is 140% of the cost (100% + 40% markup).
Cost = 700 ÷ 1.40 = **₱500** ✓

The markup percentage is based on the COST, not the selling price.

### Error 2: Adding Successive Percentages

**Wrong:** "20% off then 10% off = 30% off" ✗
**Correct:** Multiplier = 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, so the effective discount is 28%, not 30%.

### Error 3: Confusing "Percent Of" with "Percent More Than"

**Wrong:** "Juan earns 20% more than Maria's ₱25,000" → 20% of 25,000 = 5,000 → Juan earns ₱5,000 ✗
**Correct:** Juan earns ₱25,000 + ₱5,000 = **₱30,000** (or ₱25,000 × 1.20)

### Error 4: Applying Percentage to Wrong Quantity

**Wrong:** "A ₱500 item has 12% VAT. The customer pays ₱500 − 12% of 500 = ₱440" ✗
**Correct:** VAT is ADDED: ₱500 + 12% of 500 = ₱500 + 60 = **₱560**

### Error 5: Forgetting What the Question Asks

The problem says "how much is the discount?" but you compute the final price. Always re-read the question before selecting your answer.

### Error 6: Decimal Point Errors

- 15% = 0.15 (not 1.5 or 0.015)
- 5% = 0.05 (not 0.5)
- 0.5% = 0.005 (not 0.5)

**CSE Tip:** If your answer seems unreasonably large or small, check your decimal conversion first.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Finding the Part (Easy)

**Problem:** A government agency allocates 35% of its ₱2,000,000 budget to personnel services. How much is allocated to personnel services?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. Identify: Rate = 35%, Whole = ₱2,000,000, find the Part
2. Convert: 35% = 0.35
3. Compute: 0.35 × 2,000,000 = 700,000

**Answer:** ₱700,000

### Example 2: Finding the Whole (Medium)

**Problem:** A student answered 45 questions correctly, which is 75% of the total. How many questions are on the test?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. Identify: Part = 45, Rate = 75%, find the Whole
2. Convert: 75% = 0.75
3. Compute: Whole = 45 ÷ 0.75 = 60

**Answer:** 60 questions

### Example 3: Multi-Step with Tax (Medium)

**Problem:** A laptop costs ₱45,000. It is on a 10% discount, and then 12% VAT is applied to the discounted price. What is the final price?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. Discounted price: ₱45,000 × 0.90 = ₱40,500
2. VAT amount: ₱40,500 × 0.12 = ₱4,860
3. Final price: ₱40,500 + ₱4,860 = ₱45,360

Or using multipliers: ₱45,000 × 0.90 × 1.12 = **₱45,360**

**Answer:** ₱45,360

### Example 4: Reverse Percentage (Hard)

**Problem:** After receiving a 15% salary increase, an employee now earns ₱34,500. What was the original salary?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. The new salary (₱34,500) represents 115% of the original (100% + 15%)
2. Original = ₱34,500 ÷ 1.15 = ₱30,000

**Answer:** ₱30,000

### Example 5: Successive Discounts (Hard)

**Problem:** A store offers a 20% discount on all items. Members get an additional 15% off the discounted price. If a bag originally costs ₱3,000, how much does a member pay?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. First discount: ₱3,000 × 0.80 = ₱2,400
2. Member discount: ₱2,400 × 0.85 = ₱2,040

Or: ₱3,000 × 0.80 × 0.85 = ₱3,000 × 0.68 = **₱2,040**

**Answer:** ₱2,040

Effective total discount: 100% − 68% = 32% (not 35%)

### Example 6: Commission Problem (Medium)

**Problem:** A sales agent earns a 5% commission on all sales plus a fixed ₱8,000 monthly base pay. If she sold ₱600,000 worth of products this month, what is her total income?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. Commission: ₱600,000 × 0.05 = ₱30,000
2. Total income: ₱8,000 + ₱30,000 = ₱38,000

**Answer:** ₱38,000

### Example 7: Population Growth (Hard)

**Problem:** A municipality's population was 80,000 in 2020. It grew by 5% in 2021 and by 8% in 2022. What was the population at the end of 2022?

**Step-by-step solution:**
1. After 2021: 80,000 × 1.05 = 84,000
2. After 2022: 84,000 × 1.08 = 90,720

Or: 80,000 × 1.05 × 1.08 = 80,000 × 1.134 = **90,720**

**Answer:** 90,720

### Quick Recap

- Every percentage word problem reduces to Part = Rate × Whole — the challenge is identifying which value plays which role
- Translation is the critical skill: convert English phrases into mathematical operations before computing
- Multi-step problems require applying percentages sequentially; never add percentages that apply to different bases
- The multiplier method (1 + x/100 for increases, 1 − x/100 for decreases) streamlines successive-change problems
- Reverse problems (given the result, find the original) require dividing by the multiplier, not subtracting the percentage
- Always verify: increases produce larger values, decreases produce smaller values, and parts should be less than wholes
- Estimation eliminates wrong choices quickly — use benchmark fractions (10% = 1/10, 25% = 1/4, 50% = 1/2)
- The most common error is using the wrong base; always ask "percent of WHAT?"

---

### Exam Strategies

- Read the last sentence first to identify what is being asked before processing the scenario
- Convert percentages to friendly fractions for mental math (25% = ÷4, 20% = ÷5, 10% = ÷10)
- Use the complement approach: "30% off" means "multiply by 0.70" — one step instead of two
- When stuck, plug answer choices back into the problem to verify which one works
- For successive changes, multiply the multipliers rather than adding percentages
- Estimate first, compute second — if your answer doesn't match your estimate, recheck
- Watch for "percent of" vs. "percent more than" — these are different operations
- On timed exams, skip problems that require complex multi-step computation and return to them later

### Which Method?

For each problem, identify the type and solve.

**1.** A property's price increases from ₱4,000,000 to ₱4,600,000. Find the percent increase.
- **Type:** Percentage change / Rate computation (R = P ÷ W)
- **Answer:** Increase = ₱600,000. Rate = 600,000 ÷ 4,000,000 × 100 = **15%**
- **Why:** Finding the rate of increase relative to the original base price.

**2.** An LGU budget allocates 45% to operations and 30% to personnel. If ₱300,000 remains for other expenses, find the total budget.
- **Type:** Reverse whole computation (W = P ÷ R)
- **Answer:** Remaining % = 100% − 45% − 30% = 25%. Total = 300,000 ÷ 0.25 = **₱1,200,000**
- **Why:** The remaining amount represents the remaining percentage complement of the total budget.

**3.** A bag costs ₱3,000 with successive discounts of 20% then 15%. What is the final price?
- **Type:** Successive decreases (multiplier method)
- **Answer:** ₱3,000 × 0.80 × 0.85 = **₱2,040**
- **Why:** The second discount applies to the already reduced price, requiring sequential multiplication of complements.
