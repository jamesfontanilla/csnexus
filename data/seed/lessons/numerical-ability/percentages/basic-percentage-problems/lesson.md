# Basic Percentage Problems

## Introduction

**Basic percentage problems** are the bread and butter of the Numerical Ability section in the Philippine Civil Service Examination. These problems ask you to find one of three values — the **percentage (rate)**, the **part**, or the **whole (base)** — when the other two are given. Unlike the previous subtopic (Fundamentals of Percentages) which focused on conversions between fractions, decimals, and percentages, this subtopic focuses on *applying* percentages to solve real-world problems.

Percentage problems appear in virtually every aspect of government work:
- **Shopping and commerce** — computing discounts, markups, and sale prices
- **Taxes** — calculating VAT (12%), withholding tax, income tax brackets
- **Grades and passing rates** — determining scores needed to pass (80% of 150 items)
- **Payroll** — computing salary deductions, overtime pay, bonuses
- **Budgeting** — allocating percentages of a total budget to departments
- **Statistics** — interpreting survey results, population growth, approval ratings
- **Engineering** — accuracy rates, defect percentages, completion rates
- **Business** — profit margins, commission rates, sales targets
- **Government reports** — percentage of GDP, literacy rates, employment figures
- **Transportation** — occupancy rates, on-time performance percentages

The CSE tests basic percentage problems because they require both conceptual understanding and computational accuracy. You must identify *what* is being asked, select the correct formula, and execute the arithmetic — all under time pressure.

**Common mistakes examinees make:**
1. Confusing the part with the whole (e.g., treating the discount amount as the original price)
2. Forgetting to convert the percentage to a decimal before multiplying
3. Dividing when they should multiply (or vice versa)
4. Using the wrong number as the base in "percent of what" problems
5. Misreading the question — finding the percentage when asked for the part
6. Arithmetic errors in decimal multiplication and division
7. Not checking whether the answer is reasonable (e.g., getting a part larger than the whole)
8. Confusing "what percent OF" with "what percent MORE THAN"

## Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Identify the three components of any percentage problem (rate, part, whole)
- Find the percentage/rate when given the part and the whole
- Find the part when given the percentage and the whole
- Find the whole/base when given the part and the percentage
- Analyze CSE-style percentage word problems efficiently
- Solve multi-step percentage problems involving successive operations
- Compare and interpret percentage data in practical contexts
- Use estimation to verify answers and eliminate unreasonable choices
- Avoid common traps in percentage problem wording

## 4.1 Understanding the Three Components

Every basic percentage problem involves exactly three quantities. If you can identify which two are given and which one is missing, you can solve any percentage problem.

### The Three Components

| Component | Also Called | What It Represents | Example |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Rate (R)** | Percentage, percent | The proportion expressed as a percent | 25% |
| **Part (P)** | Portion, amount, percentage amount | The piece of the whole | 20 |
| **Whole (W)** | Base, total, original amount | The complete quantity | 80 |

### The Relationship

The three components are connected by one fundamental relationship:

```
Part = Rate × Whole
```

Or equivalently:
```
P = R × W       (Finding the Part)
R = P ÷ W       (Finding the Rate)
W = P ÷ R       (Finding the Whole)
```

**⚠️ Critical Rule:** When using these formulas, the Rate must be in decimal form (not percent form). Convert 25% to 0.25 before multiplying or dividing.

### The Percentage Triangle

A helpful memory device is the **percentage triangle**:

```
        [ P ]
       /     \
     [ R ] × [ W ]
```

- Cover the value you want to find
- If P is covered: multiply R × W
- If R is covered: divide P ÷ W
- If W is covered: divide P ÷ R

### Identifying Components in Word Problems

The hardest part of percentage problems is often not the math — it is identifying which number is the rate, which is the part, and which is the whole.

**Clue words for the WHOLE (Base):**
- "of" — the number after "of" is usually the whole
- "original," "total," "entire," "full"
- The starting amount before any change

**Clue words for the PART:**
- "is" — the number near "is" is often the part
- "amount," "portion," "how much," "how many"
- The result after applying the percentage

**Clue words for the RATE:**
- The number with the % symbol
- "what percent," "what rate," "what proportion"

#### Pattern Recognition

| Question Pattern | Missing Value | Formula |
|-----------------|---------------|---------|
| What is 25% of 80? | Part | P = 0.25 × 80 |
| 20 is what percent of 80? | Rate | R = 20 ÷ 80 |
| 20 is 25% of what? | Whole | W = 20 ÷ 0.25 |

**💡 CSE Tip:** In the exam, quickly scan the question for the % symbol. Then identify which of the remaining two numbers is the "of" number (whole) and which is the "is" number (part). This takes 5 seconds and prevents formula errors.

## 4.2 Finding the Percentage (Rate)

**Formula:**
```
Rate = Part ÷ Whole
Rate (%) = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
```

You use this formula when the question asks: "What percent...?" or "What is the rate...?"

### Step-by-Step Procedure

```
Step 1: Identify the Part (the portion or amount)
Step 2: Identify the Whole (the total or base)
Step 3: Divide the Part by the Whole
Step 4: Multiply by 100 to convert to percentage
```

### Easy Examples

**Example 1:** What percentage of 80 is 20?
```
Part = 20, Whole = 80
Rate = 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25
Rate = 0.25 × 100 = 25%
Answer: 25%
```

**Example 2:** A student scored 45 out of 50 on a quiz. What is the percentage score?
```
Part = 45, Whole = 50
Rate = 45 ÷ 50 = 0.90
Rate = 0.90 × 100 = 90%
Answer: 90%
```

**Example 3:** In a class of 40 students, 32 passed the exam. What percentage passed?
```
Part = 32, Whole = 40
Rate = 32 ÷ 40 = 0.80
Rate = 0.80 × 100 = 80%
Answer: 80%
```

### Medium Examples

**Example 4:** A government office has 250 employees. If 175 are female, what percentage of employees are female?
```
Part = 175, Whole = 250
Rate = 175 ÷ 250 = 0.70
Rate = 0.70 × 100 = 70%
Answer: 70%
```

**Example 5:** A factory produced 1,200 items and 36 were defective. What is the defect rate?
```
Part = 36, Whole = 1,200
Rate = 36 ÷ 1,200 = 0.03
Rate = 0.03 × 100 = 3%
Answer: 3%
```

**Example 6:** Out of ₱45,000 monthly salary, ₱5,400 goes to taxes. What percentage is deducted for taxes?
```
Part = 5,400, Whole = 45,000
Rate = 5,400 ÷ 45,000 = 0.12
Rate = 0.12 × 100 = 12%
Answer: 12%
```

### Hard Examples

**Example 7:** A survey of 2,400 residents found that 1,560 support a new policy. What percentage support the policy?
```
Part = 1,560, Whole = 2,400
Rate = 1,560 ÷ 2,400 = 0.65
Rate = 0.65 × 100 = 65%
Answer: 65%
```

**Example 8:** A construction project budgeted ₱8,500,000 and has spent ₱6,375,000. What percentage of the budget has been used?
```
Part = 6,375,000, Whole = 8,500,000
Rate = 6,375,000 ÷ 8,500,000 = 0.75
Rate = 0.75 × 100 = 75%
Answer: 75%
```

**Example 9:** An engineer measured 847 acceptable parts out of 1,100 total. What is the acceptance rate?
```
Part = 847, Whole = 1,100
Rate = 847 ÷ 1,100 = 0.77
Rate = 0.77 × 100 = 77%
Answer: 77%
```

### CSE-Style Example

**A government agency received 3,600 applications. After screening, 2,880 were found eligible. What percentage of applications were eligible?**

```
Step 1: Part = 2,880 (eligible applications)
Step 2: Whole = 3,600 (total applications)
Step 3: Rate = 2,880 ÷ 3,600 = 0.80
Step 4: Rate = 0.80 × 100 = 80%
```

**Answer: 80%**

**Verification:** 80% of 3,600 = 0.80 × 3,600 = 2,880 ✓

## 4.3 Finding the Part

**Formula:**
```
Part = Rate × Whole
Part = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Whole
```

You use this formula when the question asks: "What is X% of Y?" or "How much/many...?"

### Step-by-Step Procedure

```
Step 1: Identify the Rate (the percentage given)
Step 2: Convert the Rate to decimal form (divide by 100)
Step 3: Identify the Whole (the total or base amount)
Step 4: Multiply the decimal rate by the Whole
```

### Easy Examples

**Example 1:** What is 25% of 200?
```
Rate = 25% = 0.25, Whole = 200
Part = 0.25 × 200 = 50
Answer: 50
```

**Example 2:** What is 10% of 450?
```
Rate = 10% = 0.10, Whole = 450
Part = 0.10 × 450 = 45
Answer: 45
```

**Example 3:** A store offers a 20% discount on a ₱500 item. How much is the discount?
```
Rate = 20% = 0.20, Whole = 500
Part = 0.20 × 500 = 100
Answer: ₱100
```

### Medium Examples

**Example 4:** A company allocates 35% of its ₱2,000,000 budget to salaries. How much is allocated to salaries?
```
Rate = 35% = 0.35, Whole = 2,000,000
Part = 0.35 × 2,000,000 = 700,000
Answer: ₱700,000
```

**Example 5:** An employee receives a 5.5% salary increase on a ₱28,000 monthly salary. How much is the increase?
```
Rate = 5.5% = 0.055, Whole = 28,000
Part = 0.055 × 28,000 = 1,540
Answer: ₱1,540
```

**Example 6:** A city has 850,000 registered voters. If 72% voted in the election, how many voted?
```
Rate = 72% = 0.72, Whole = 850,000
Part = 0.72 × 850,000 = 612,000
Answer: 612,000 voters
```

### Hard Examples

**Example 7:** A government project is 87.5% complete. If the total project involves 1,600 tasks, how many tasks are done?
```
Rate = 87.5% = 0.875, Whole = 1,600
Part = 0.875 × 1,600 = 1,400
Answer: 1,400 tasks
```

**Example 8:** A loan of ₱350,000 charges 18% annual interest. How much interest is charged in one year?
```
Rate = 18% = 0.18, Whole = 350,000
Part = 0.18 × 350,000 = 63,000
Answer: ₱63,000
```

**Example 9:** In a survey of 4,500 respondents, 33⅓% preferred Option A. How many preferred Option A?
```
Rate = 33⅓% = 1/3, Whole = 4,500
Part = (1/3) × 4,500 = 1,500
Answer: 1,500 respondents
```

### CSE-Style Example

**A barangay has a total population of 12,500. If 8% of the population are senior citizens, how many senior citizens are in the barangay?**

```
Step 1: Rate = 8% = 0.08
Step 2: Whole = 12,500
Step 3: Part = 0.08 × 12,500 = 1,000
```

**Answer: 1,000 senior citizens**

**Verification:** 1,000 ÷ 12,500 = 0.08 = 8% ✓

## 4.4 Finding the Whole (Base)

**Formula:**
```
Whole = Part ÷ Rate
Whole = Part ÷ (Percentage ÷ 100)
```

You use this formula when the question asks: "X is Y% of what number?" or "What is the total/original amount?"

### Step-by-Step Procedure

```
Step 1: Identify the Part (the known portion)
Step 2: Identify the Rate (the percentage given)
Step 3: Convert the Rate to decimal form (divide by 100)
Step 4: Divide the Part by the decimal rate
```

### Easy Examples

**Example 1:** 15 is 25% of what number?
```
Part = 15, Rate = 25% = 0.25
Whole = 15 ÷ 0.25 = 60
Answer: 60
```

**Example 2:** 36 is 50% of what number?
```
Part = 36, Rate = 50% = 0.50
Whole = 36 ÷ 0.50 = 72
Answer: 72
```

**Example 3:** A student answered 42 items correctly, which is 70% of the total. How many items are on the test?
```
Part = 42, Rate = 70% = 0.70
Whole = 42 ÷ 0.70 = 60
Answer: 60 items
```

### Medium Examples

**Example 4:** A salesperson earned ₱4,500 in commission, which represents 6% of total sales. What were the total sales?
```
Part = 4,500, Rate = 6% = 0.06
Whole = 4,500 ÷ 0.06 = 75,000
Answer: ₱75,000
```

**Example 5:** A department spent ₱180,000, which is 15% of the annual budget. What is the annual budget?
```
Part = 180,000, Rate = 15% = 0.15
Whole = 180,000 ÷ 0.15 = 1,200,000
Answer: ₱1,200,000
```

**Example 6:** In an election, a candidate received 13,500 votes, which is 45% of the total votes cast. How many total votes were cast?
```
Part = 13,500, Rate = 45% = 0.45
Whole = 13,500 ÷ 0.45 = 30,000
Answer: 30,000 votes
```

### Hard Examples

**Example 7:** A company's tax payment of ₱2,625,000 represents 12.5% of its gross revenue. What is the gross revenue?
```
Part = 2,625,000, Rate = 12.5% = 0.125
Whole = 2,625,000 ÷ 0.125 = 21,000,000
Answer: ₱21,000,000
```

**Example 8:** An engineer reports that 1,890 defective units represent 2.7% of total production. What is the total production?
```
Part = 1,890, Rate = 2.7% = 0.027
Whole = 1,890 ÷ 0.027 = 70,000
Answer: 70,000 units
```

**Example 9:** A scholarship fund disbursed ₱840,000, which is 33⅓% of the total endowment. What is the total endowment?
```
Part = 840,000, Rate = 33⅓% = 1/3
Whole = 840,000 ÷ (1/3) = 840,000 × 3 = 2,520,000
Answer: ₱2,520,000
```

### CSE-Style Example

**A government employee's monthly tax deduction is ₱3,750. If this represents 15% of the gross monthly salary, what is the gross monthly salary?**

```
Step 1: Part = ₱3,750 (tax deduction)
Step 2: Rate = 15% = 0.15
Step 3: Whole = 3,750 ÷ 0.15 = 25,000
```

**Answer: ₱25,000**

**Verification:** 15% of 25,000 = 0.15 × 25,000 = 3,750 ✓

## 4.5 Identifying the Components of a Percentage Problem

The most critical skill in percentage problems is correctly identifying which value is the rate, which is the part, and which is the whole. Misidentification leads to using the wrong formula.

### Decision Framework

Ask yourself these three questions in order:

```
1. Which number has the % symbol (or the word "percent")? → That's the RATE
2. Which number follows "of"? → That's the WHOLE
3. Which number is near "is" or represents the result? → That's the PART
```

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Problem | Rate | Part | Whole | Formula Needed |
|---------|------|------|-------|---------------|
| What is 30% of 500? | 30% | ? | 500 | P = R × W |
| 150 is what % of 500? | ? | 150 | 500 | R = P ÷ W |
| 150 is 30% of what? | 30% | 150 | ? | W = P ÷ R |
| 40% of 250 is what? | 40% | ? | 250 | P = R × W |
| What % of 80 is 60? | ? | 60 | 80 | R = P ÷ W |
| 72 is 90% of what? | 90% | 72 | ? | W = P ÷ R |

### Tricky Wording Patterns

Some CSE questions use non-standard phrasing. Here's how to decode them:

| Phrasing | Translation | Missing |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| "How much is 15% of 800?" | Part = 0.15 × 800 | Part |
| "₱120 is what fraction of ₱800?" | Rate = 120 ÷ 800 | Rate |
| "If 45 represents 9%, find the total" | Whole = 45 ÷ 0.09 | Whole |
| "What part of 600 is 150?" | Rate = 150 ÷ 600 | Rate |
| "60% of what gives 420?" | Whole = 420 ÷ 0.60 | Whole |

### Practice: Identify Before You Solve

For each problem below, identify the Rate, Part, and Whole before solving:

1. **"A class has 50 students. 80% attended the field trip. How many attended?"**
   - Rate = 80%, Whole = 50, Part = ? → P = 0.80 × 50 = 40

2. **"Out of 200 applicants, 150 passed the screening. What percent passed?"**
   - Part = 150, Whole = 200, Rate = ? → R = 150 ÷ 200 = 0.75 = 75%

3. **"₱6,000 is 12% of the total budget. What is the total budget?"**
   - Part = 6,000, Rate = 12%, Whole = ? → W = 6,000 ÷ 0.12 = 50,000

## 4.6 Percentage Problems in Real-Life Contexts

### Discounts and Sales

**Pattern:** Discount Amount = Rate × Original Price

**Example:** A laptop originally costs ₱45,000. It is on sale at 15% off. How much is the discount? What is the sale price?
```
Discount = 0.15 × 45,000 = ₱6,750
Sale Price = 45,000 - 6,750 = ₱38,250
```

### VAT and Taxes

**Pattern:** Tax Amount = Tax Rate × Taxable Amount

**Example:** A purchase costs ₱8,500 before VAT. If VAT is 12%, how much is the total including VAT?
```
VAT = 0.12 × 8,500 = ₱1,020
Total = 8,500 + 1,020 = ₱9,520
```

### Grades and Passing Rates

**Pattern:** Score Percentage = Items Correct ÷ Total Items × 100

**Example:** To pass the CSE, you need at least 80% on a 100-item test. What is the minimum number of correct answers?
```
Minimum = 0.80 × 100 = 80 correct answers
```

### Payroll Deductions

**Pattern:** Deduction = Rate × Gross Salary

**Example:** An employee earns ₱32,000 monthly. Deductions include 3% for Pag-IBIG and 4% for PhilHealth. How much is deducted total?
```
Pag-IBIG = 0.03 × 32,000 = ₱960
PhilHealth = 0.04 × 32,000 = ₱1,280
Total Deductions = 960 + 1,280 = ₱2,240
```

### Salary Increases

**Pattern:** Increase Amount = Rate × Current Salary

**Example:** A government employee earning ₱25,000 receives a 10% salary increase. What is the new salary?
```
Increase = 0.10 × 25,000 = ₱2,500
New Salary = 25,000 + 2,500 = ₱27,500
```

### Business Profits

**Pattern:** Profit = Profit Rate × Cost

**Example:** A vendor buys goods for ₱15,000 and sells them at a 40% profit. What is the selling price?
```
Profit = 0.40 × 15,000 = ₱6,000
Selling Price = 15,000 + 6,000 = ₱21,000
```

### Commission

**Pattern:** Commission = Commission Rate × Total Sales

**Example:** A real estate agent earns 5% commission. If a property sold for ₱3,200,000, how much commission does the agent earn?
```
Commission = 0.05 × 3,200,000 = ₱160,000
```

### Survey and Statistics

**Pattern:** Number in Category = Percentage × Total Surveyed

**Example:** A survey of 1,500 government employees found that 68% are satisfied with their work conditions. How many are satisfied?
```
Satisfied = 0.68 × 1,500 = 1,020 employees
```

### Inventory Management

**Pattern:** Damaged Items = Damage Rate × Total Inventory

**Example:** A warehouse has 8,000 units in stock. After inspection, 2.5% were found damaged. How many units are damaged?
```
Damaged = 0.025 × 8,000 = 200 units
```

### Transportation Statistics

**Example:** A bus company operates 120 routes. If 85% of routes are on schedule, how many routes are on schedule?
```
On schedule = 0.85 × 120 = 102 routes
```

## 4.7 Multi-Step Percentage Problems

Multi-step problems require two or more percentage operations to reach the final answer. These are common in the CSE because they test both procedural fluency and logical organization.

### Type 1: Successive Percentages

**Example:** A shirt costs ₱1,200. It is discounted by 20%, and then an additional 10% is taken off the discounted price. What is the final price?

```
Step 1: First discount = 0.20 × 1,200 = ₱240
        Price after first discount = 1,200 - 240 = ₱960

Step 2: Second discount = 0.10 × 960 = ₱96
        Final price = 960 - 96 = ₱864
```

**⚠️ Common Mistake:** Adding the percentages (20% + 10% = 30%) and applying once. This gives 0.30 × 1,200 = ₱360 discount → ₱840 final price. This is WRONG because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price, not the original.

### Type 2: Finding Remaining After Multiple Deductions

**Example:** An employee's gross salary is ₱40,000. Deductions are: 12% income tax, 4% PhilHealth, and 2% Pag-IBIG. What is the net take-home pay?

```
Income tax = 0.12 × 40,000 = ₱4,800
PhilHealth = 0.04 × 40,000 = ₱1,600
Pag-IBIG = 0.02 × 40,000 = ₱800
Total deductions = 4,800 + 1,600 + 800 = ₱7,200
Net pay = 40,000 - 7,200 = ₱32,800
```

**Alternative method:** Total deduction rate = 12% + 4% + 2% = 18%
```
Net pay = (1 - 0.18) × 40,000 = 0.82 × 40,000 = ₱32,800
```

Note: Adding rates works here because all deductions are based on the SAME whole (gross salary). This is different from successive discounts where each applies to a different base.

### Type 3: Finding the Original from a Changed Value

**Example:** After a 20% increase, an employee's salary is ₱36,000. What was the original salary?

```
New salary = Original + 20% of Original = 1.20 × Original
36,000 = 1.20 × Original
Original = 36,000 ÷ 1.20 = ₱30,000
```

**⚠️ Common Mistake:** Computing 20% of 36,000 (= 7,200) and subtracting. This gives 28,800, which is WRONG because 36,000 is not the base — the unknown original salary is the base.

### Type 4: Combining Percentage and Arithmetic

**Example:** A store sells 3 items at ₱450 each with a 10% bulk discount on the total. How much does the customer pay?

```
Step 1: Total before discount = 3 × 450 = ₱1,350
Step 2: Discount = 0.10 × 1,350 = ₱135
Step 3: Amount paid = 1,350 - 135 = ₱1,215
```

### CSE-Style Multi-Step Example

**A government office has a budget of ₱5,000,000. It allocates 40% to personnel, 25% to operations, and the rest to capital outlay. How much is allocated to capital outlay?**

```
Step 1: Personnel + Operations = 40% + 25% = 65%
Step 2: Capital outlay percentage = 100% - 65% = 35%
Step 3: Capital outlay = 0.35 × 5,000,000 = ₱1,750,000
```

**Answer: ₱1,750,000**

## 4.8 Percentage Comparison and Interpretation

### Comparing Rates

When comparing percentages, ensure they refer to the same base. A common CSE trap is presenting two percentages with different bases.

**Example:** Department A has 120 employees with 90% attendance. Department B has 80 employees with 95% attendance. Which department has more employees present?

```
Department A: 0.90 × 120 = 108 present
Department B: 0.95 × 80 = 76 present
Department A has more employees present (108 > 76)
```

Even though Department B has a higher percentage, Department A has more people present because it has a larger base.

### Interpreting Survey Results

**Example:** In a survey, 60% of 500 respondents in City A support a policy, while 45% of 800 respondents in City B support it. Which city has more supporters?

```
City A: 0.60 × 500 = 300 supporters
City B: 0.45 × 800 = 360 supporters
City B has more supporters (360 > 300)
```

### Percentage vs. Actual Quantity

**Key Insight:** A higher percentage does not always mean a larger quantity. Always check the base before drawing conclusions.

| Scenario | Percentage | Base | Actual Quantity |
|----------|-----------|------|----------------|
| Store A: 30% profit on ₱100,000 sales | 30% | 100,000 | ₱30,000 |
| Store B: 20% profit on ₱200,000 sales | 20% | 200,000 | ₱40,000 |

Store B earns more profit despite having a lower profit rate.

## 4.9 Problem-Solving Strategies

### The 5-Step System

For any percentage problem on the CSE:

```
Step 1: READ — Read the entire problem. Identify what is being asked.
Step 2: IDENTIFY — Label the Rate, Part, and Whole. Mark the unknown.
Step 3: FORMULA — Select the correct formula based on the unknown.
Step 4: COMPUTE — Convert percentage to decimal. Execute the arithmetic.
Step 5: CHECK — Verify the answer is reasonable. Use estimation.
```

### Mental Math Shortcuts

**10% shortcut:** Move the decimal one place left.
```
10% of 450 = 45
10% of 3,200 = 320
10% of 87 = 8.7
```

**5% shortcut:** Find 10%, then halve it.
```
5% of 600 = half of 60 = 30
5% of 1,400 = half of 140 = 70
```

**25% shortcut:** Divide by 4.
```
25% of 200 = 200 ÷ 4 = 50
25% of 840 = 840 ÷ 4 = 210
```

**50% shortcut:** Divide by 2.
```
50% of 350 = 175
50% of 1,600 = 800
```

**1% shortcut:** Move the decimal two places left.
```
1% of 5,000 = 50
1% of 230 = 2.3
```

**Combining shortcuts:**
```
15% of 400 = 10% + 5% = 40 + 20 = 60
35% of 200 = 25% + 10% = 50 + 20 = 70
12% of 500 = 10% + 2×(1%) = 50 + 10 = 60
```

### Estimation Before Computing

Before doing exact calculations, estimate the answer to eliminate obviously wrong choices.

**Example:** What is 23% of 812?
- Estimate: 23% ≈ 25% = 1/4, and 812 ≈ 800
- Estimate: 800 ÷ 4 = 200
- So the answer should be close to 200 (slightly less, since 23% < 25% and 812 > 800)
- Exact: 0.23 × 812 = 186.76

If the choices are (A) 18.68, (B) 186.76, (C) 1,867.6, (D) 86.76 — you can immediately eliminate A, C, and D.

## 4.10 Estimation and Answer Checking

### Reasonableness Checks

After solving, verify your answer makes sense:

| If you're finding... | The answer should be... |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Part (when rate < 100%) | Less than the Whole |
| Part (when rate > 100%) | Greater than the Whole |
| Rate | Between 0% and 100% (usually) |
| Whole | Greater than the Part (when rate < 100%) |

### Benchmark Percentages for Quick Checking

Memorize these for instant verification:

| Percentage | Equivalent | Quick Check |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| 10% | ÷ 10 | Move decimal left 1 |
| 20% | ÷ 5 | Double 10% |
| 25% | ÷ 4 | Quarter of the whole |
| 33⅓% | ÷ 3 | One-third |
| 50% | ÷ 2 | Half |
| 66⅔% | × 2/3 | Two-thirds |
| 75% | × 3/4 | Three-quarters |
| 100% | × 1 | The whole thing |
| 200% | × 2 | Double |

### Elimination Strategy

On the CSE, if you can estimate the answer within 10-20%, you can often eliminate 2-3 wrong choices immediately. This is especially useful when time is short.

**Example:** 18% of 4,500 = ?
- Quick estimate: 20% of 4,500 = 900, so 18% should be slightly less than 900
- If choices are: (A) 81, (B) 810, (C) 8,100, (D) 180 → Answer is (B)

## 4.11 Common Errors in Basic Percentage Problems

### Error 1: Confusing Part and Whole

**Wrong:** "20 is what percent of 5?" → Student computes 5 ÷ 20 = 0.25 = 25%
**Right:** Rate = Part ÷ Whole = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 = 400%

The number after "of" is the whole. 20 is 400% of 5 (it's four times as large).

### Error 2: Forgetting Decimal Conversion

**Wrong:** 25% of 80 = 25 × 80 = 2,000
**Right:** 25% of 80 = 0.25 × 80 = 20

Always convert the percentage to a decimal (divide by 100) before multiplying.

### Error 3: Dividing Instead of Multiplying

**Wrong:** "What is 40% of 250?" → 250 ÷ 0.40 = 625
**Right:** Part = Rate × Whole = 0.40 × 250 = 100

"What is X% of Y?" always means multiply.

### Error 4: Multiplying Instead of Dividing

**Wrong:** "60 is 20% of what?" → 60 × 0.20 = 12
**Right:** Whole = Part ÷ Rate = 60 ÷ 0.20 = 300

"X is Y% of what?" always means divide.

### Error 5: Using the Changed Value as the Base

**Wrong:** "After a 25% increase, the price is ₱500. What was the original?"
→ Student computes: 25% of 500 = 125, then 500 - 125 = 375

**Right:** 500 = 1.25 × Original → Original = 500 ÷ 1.25 = ₱400

The 25% increase was applied to the ORIGINAL price, not to ₱500.

### Error 6: Adding Successive Percentages

**Wrong:** "A 20% discount followed by a 10% discount" = 30% total discount
**Right:** After 20% off: price × 0.80. Then 10% off that: × 0.90.
Net effect: 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, which is a 28% total discount, not 30%.

### Error 7: Misreading "Percent OF" vs. "Percent MORE THAN"

- "A is 25% of B" means A = 0.25 × B
- "A is 25% more than B" means A = 1.25 × B

These are completely different statements. The first says A is a quarter of B. The second says A exceeds B by a quarter.

## Exam Strategies

1. **Scan for the % symbol first** — it immediately tells you the rate and narrows down what's being asked.

2. **Identify "of" and "is"** — "of" points to the whole, "is" points to the part. This works in 90% of CSE percentage questions.

3. **Estimate before computing** — use benchmark percentages (10%, 25%, 50%) to get a ballpark answer. Eliminate choices that are off by a factor of 10.

4. **Check units and context** — if the question asks about money, your answer should be in pesos. If it asks about people, your answer should be a whole number.

5. **Verify with reverse computation** — after finding your answer, plug it back into the relationship to confirm. If Part = Rate × Whole, check that your three values satisfy this equation.

6. **Watch for "remaining" questions** — "What percentage did NOT pass?" = 100% minus the passing rate. Don't solve for the passing rate and select it as your answer.

7. **Time management** — basic percentage problems should take 30-45 seconds each. If you're spending more than a minute, estimate and move on.

## Quick Recap

| To Find | Formula | Key Word Clue |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| **Rate** | R = P ÷ W × 100 | "what percent," "what rate" |
| **Part** | P = R × W | "what is X% of," "how much/many" |
| **Whole** | W = P ÷ R | "X is Y% of what," "find the total" |

**Essential reminders:**
- Always convert percentage to decimal before computing (÷ 100)
- "Of" signals multiplication; the number after "of" is the whole
- "Is" signals the part (the result)
- Successive percentages cannot be simply added — each applies to a different base
- Estimate first, compute second, verify third
- If the part > whole and rate < 100%, something is wrong
- Use benchmark percentages (10%, 25%, 50%) for mental math

## Memory Aids

### The "IS-OF-PERCENT" Triangle
```
        [ IS ]
       /      \
   [ OF ]  ×  [ % ]
```
- IS = the Part
- OF = the Whole
- % = the Rate (as decimal)

### Mnemonic: "PER-CENT = PER HUNDRED"
- To convert TO percent: × 100 (make it per hundred)
- To convert FROM percent: ÷ 100 (undo the per hundred)

### Formula Memory Trick
- **P**art = **R**ate × **W**hole → "**P**eople **R**ead **W**ords"
- **R**ate = **P**art ÷ **W**hole → "**R**eading **P**aper **W**orks"
- **W**hole = **P**art ÷ **R**ate → "**W**rite **P**ractice **R**eports"

### Decimal Conversion Reminder
- Percent → Decimal: "**P**oint moves **L**eft **2**" (PL2)
- Decimal → Percent: "**P**oint moves **R**ight **2**" (PR2)
