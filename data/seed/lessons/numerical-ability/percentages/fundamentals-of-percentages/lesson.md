# Fundamentals of Percentages

## Introduction

**Percentages** are one of the most practical and frequently tested mathematical concepts in the Philippine Civil Service Examination. The word "percent" comes from the Latin *per centum*, meaning "per hundred" — so a percentage is simply a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100.

Percentages appear everywhere in daily life and government work:
- **Discounts and sales tax** — computing VAT (12%) on purchases
- **Interest rates** — understanding loan terms and savings growth
- **Grades and test scores** — interpreting academic performance (e.g., 85/100 = 85%)
- **Surveys and statistics** — reading election results, approval ratings, population data
- **Business reports** — profit margins, growth rates, budget allocations
- **Salary computations** — tax brackets, deductions, pay increases
- **Engineering and quality control** — accuracy rates, defect percentages
- **Government budgeting** — percentage of GDP, allocation ratios across departments

The CSE tests percentage fundamentals because every government employee must interpret statistical reports, compute budget shares, verify payroll deductions, and communicate numerical data clearly. Errors in percentage interpretation can lead to misallocated funds, incorrect tax computations, or misleading public reports.

**Common mistakes examinees make when solving percentage problems:**
1. Forgetting to multiply or divide by 100 during conversions
2. Moving the decimal point in the wrong direction
3. Not simplifying fractions after converting from percentages
4. Confusing "percent of" with "percent increase"
5. Misinterpreting percentages greater than 100%
6. Rounding too early in multi-step problems
7. Mixing up which number is the base vs. the part

## Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Define percentages correctly and explain their relationship to fractions and decimals
- Convert fractions into percentages accurately (proper, improper, and mixed numbers)
- Convert decimals into percentages using the multiply-by-100 rule
- Convert percentages into decimals by dividing by 100
- Convert percentages into simplified fractions
- Recognize and recall common benchmark equivalents instantly
- Compare equivalent forms of numbers across fractions, decimals, and percentages
- Apply percentage conversions in practical CSE-style scenarios
- Use estimation and mental math to verify percentage answers quickly
- Identify and avoid common conversion errors under exam time pressure

## 4.1 What Is a Percentage?

A **percentage** is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. The symbol **%** means "out of 100" or "per hundred."

```
50% means 50 out of 100 → 50/100 → 0.50
25% means 25 out of 100 → 25/100 → 0.25
100% means 100 out of 100 → the whole thing
```

Think of a percentage as a standardized way to describe proportions. Instead of saying "3 out of 4 students passed," you can say "75% of students passed." Both convey the same information, but percentages make comparisons easier because everything is measured against the same base: 100.

#### Visual Understanding

Imagine a 10 × 10 grid with 100 squares:
- If you shade **50 squares**, you have shaded **50%** of the grid
- If you shade **25 squares**, you have shaded **25%** of the grid
- If you shade **all 100 squares**, you have shaded **100%** of the grid
- If you shade **1 square**, you have shaded **1%** of the grid

#### Real-Life Percentage Examples

| Statement | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| A store offers a 20% discount | For every ₱100 of the original price, you save ₱20 |
| Your test score is 85% | You got 85 correct answers out of every 100 items |
| The population grew by 3% | For every 100 people last year, there are now 103 |
| VAT is 12% | For every ₱100 spent, ₱12 goes to tax |
| A battery is at 40% | 40 out of every 100 units of capacity remain |

#### The Three Forms of a Number

Every number can be written in three equivalent forms:

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|----------|---------|------------|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |

**CSE Tip:** The exam frequently asks you to identify which of these three forms is equivalent to a given value. Memorizing the common equivalents saves time.

## 4.2 Understanding Fractions, Decimals, and Percents

Fractions, decimals, and percentages are three different notations for the same concept: **a part of a whole**. Converting between them is the foundation of all percentage work.

#### The Relationship

```
Fraction → Decimal → Percentage
  1/4    →  0.25   →   25%

Divide numerator    Multiply
by denominator      by 100
```

The conversion chain works in both directions:
- **Fraction to Decimal:** Divide the numerator by the denominator
- **Decimal to Percentage:** Multiply by 100 (move decimal point 2 places right)
- **Percentage to Decimal:** Divide by 100 (move decimal point 2 places left)
- **Percentage to Fraction:** Write over 100, then simplify

#### Equivalency Table — Common Values

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|----------|---------|------------|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/3 | 0.333... | 33.33% |
| 2/3 | 0.666... | 66.67% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 2/5 | 0.4 | 40% |
| 3/5 | 0.6 | 60% |
| 4/5 | 0.8 | 80% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 3/8 | 0.375 | 37.5% |
| 5/8 | 0.625 | 62.5% |
| 7/8 | 0.875 | 87.5% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |
| 1/20 | 0.05 | 5% |
| 1/25 | 0.04 | 4% |
| 1/50 | 0.02 | 2% |

**Important:** When a fraction produces a repeating decimal (like 1/3 = 0.333...), the percentage is also repeating (33.33...%). In CSE multiple-choice items, these are typically rounded to one or two decimal places (33.33% or 33⅓%).

## 4.3 Converting Fractions to Percentages

To convert any fraction to a percentage, follow this procedure:

```
Step 1: Divide the numerator by the denominator
Step 2: Multiply the result by 100
Step 3: Attach the % symbol
```

#### Method 1: Direct Division

```
3/4 → 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 → 0.75 × 100 = 75%
```

#### Method 2: Make the Denominator 100

If the denominator divides evenly into 100, multiply both numerator and denominator:

```
3/5 → (3 × 20)/(5 × 20) = 60/100 = 60%
7/25 → (7 × 4)/(25 × 4) = 28/100 = 28%
```

#### Easy Examples

| Fraction | Calculation | Percentage |
|----------|-------------|------------|
| 1/2 | 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5 → × 100 | 50% |
| 3/4 | 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 → × 100 | 75% |
| 2/5 | 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 → × 100 | 40% |
| 7/10 | 7 ÷ 10 = 0.7 → × 100 | 70% |
| 9/20 | 9 ÷ 20 = 0.45 → × 100 | 45% |

#### Medium Examples (Repeating Decimals)

| Fraction | Calculation | Percentage |
|----------|-------------|------------|
| 1/3 | 1 ÷ 3 = 0.3333... → × 100 | 33.33% |
| 2/3 | 2 ÷ 3 = 0.6666... → × 100 | 66.67% |
| 1/6 | 1 ÷ 6 = 0.1666... → × 100 | 16.67% |
| 5/6 | 5 ÷ 6 = 0.8333... → × 100 | 83.33% |
| 1/7 | 1 ÷ 7 = 0.1428... → × 100 | 14.29% |

#### Hard Examples (Improper Fractions and Mixed Numbers)

**Improper fractions** (numerator > denominator) produce percentages greater than 100%:

```
5/4 → 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25 → 1.25 × 100 = 125%
7/2 → 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 → 3.5 × 100 = 350%
```

**Mixed numbers** — convert to improper fraction first:

```
2 1/4 → 9/4 → 9 ÷ 4 = 2.25 → 2.25 × 100 = 225%
1 3/5 → 8/5 → 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6 → 1.6 × 100 = 160%
```

**CSE Tip:** If a fraction has a denominator that is a factor of 100 (2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50), use Method 2 — it is faster and avoids decimal division entirely.

**Common Mistake:** Forgetting to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first. "2 1/4" is NOT "2.14" — it is 2 + 1/4 = 2.25 = 225%.

## 4.4 Converting Decimals to Percentages

Converting a decimal to a percentage is the simplest conversion: **multiply by 100** (or equivalently, move the decimal point two places to the right).

```
Decimal × 100 = Percentage

0.75 × 100 = 75%
0.08 × 100 = 8%
1.5 × 100 = 150%
```

#### Why This Works

A percentage is "per hundred." Multiplying by 100 scales the decimal up to show how many parts per hundred it represents.

#### Terminating Decimals

| Decimal | Move decimal 2 right | Percentage |
|---------|---------------------|------------|
| 0.5 | 50. | 50% |
| 0.25 | 25. | 25% |
| 0.75 | 75. | 75% |
| 0.1 | 10. | 10% |
| 0.05 | 5. | 5% |
| 0.01 | 1. | 1% |
| 0.125 | 12.5 | 12.5% |
| 0.875 | 87.5 | 87.5% |
| 0.333 | 33.3 | 33.3% |

#### Decimals Greater Than 1

When the decimal is greater than 1, the percentage exceeds 100%:

| Decimal | Percentage | Meaning |
|---------|------------|---------|
| 1.0 | 100% | The whole amount |
| 1.5 | 150% | One and a half times the original |
| 2.0 | 200% | Double the original |
| 3.25 | 325% | More than triple |
| 0.005 | 0.5% | Half of one percent |

#### Money-Related Examples

| Situation | Decimal | Percentage |
|-----------|---------|------------|
| VAT rate | 0.12 | 12% |
| Withholding tax | 0.02 | 2% |
| Savings interest | 0.035 | 3.5% |
| Loan interest | 0.18 | 18% |
| Discount | 0.15 | 15% |

**Common Mistake:** Moving the decimal point in the wrong direction. Remember: decimal → percentage means the number gets BIGGER (multiply by 100, move RIGHT). If your answer is smaller than the original decimal, you moved the wrong way.

## 4.5 Converting Percentages to Decimals

To convert a percentage to a decimal: **divide by 100** (or move the decimal point two places to the left).

```
Percentage ÷ 100 = Decimal

75% ÷ 100 = 0.75
8% ÷ 100 = 0.08
150% ÷ 100 = 1.5
```

#### Basic Examples

| Percentage | Move decimal 2 left | Decimal |
|------------|--------------------:|---------|
| 50% | 0.50 | 0.5 |
| 25% | 0.25 | 0.25 |
| 10% | 0.10 | 0.1 |
| 5% | 0.05 | 0.05 |
| 1% | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| 100% | 1.00 | 1.0 |

#### Tricky Cases

| Percentage | Decimal | Note |
|------------|---------|------|
| 8% | 0.08 | Need a leading zero placeholder |
| 0.5% | 0.005 | Half a percent — very small |
| 0.1% | 0.001 | One-tenth of a percent |
| 250% | 2.5 | Greater than 100% → greater than 1 |
| 1000% | 10.0 | Ten times the original |
| 33.33% | 0.3333 | Repeating decimal |
| 12.5% | 0.125 | One-eighth |

#### Practical Examples

```
A 15% discount → 0.15 (multiply price by 0.15 to find discount amount)
A 12% VAT → 0.12 (multiply price by 0.12 to find tax)
A 3.5% raise → 0.035 (multiply salary by 0.035 to find increase)
An 85% passing rate → 0.85 (multiply total by 0.85 to find passers)
```

**CSE Tip:** When computing "X% of Y" on the exam, always convert the percentage to a decimal first, then multiply. This is faster and less error-prone than working with fractions.

**Common Mistake:** Writing 8% as 0.8 instead of 0.08. Count the decimal places: 8% = 8.0% → move two left → 0.08. The number must get SMALLER when converting percentage to decimal.

## 4.6 Converting Percentages to Fractions

To convert a percentage to a fraction:

```
Step 1: Write the percentage over 100
Step 2: Simplify the fraction to lowest terms
```

#### Easy Examples

| Percentage | Over 100 | Simplified | Process |
|------------|----------|------------|---------|
| 25% | 25/100 | 1/4 | ÷ 25 |
| 50% | 50/100 | 1/2 | ÷ 50 |
| 75% | 75/100 | 3/4 | ÷ 25 |
| 20% | 20/100 | 1/5 | ÷ 20 |
| 40% | 40/100 | 2/5 | ÷ 20 |
| 60% | 60/100 | 3/5 | ÷ 20 |
| 80% | 80/100 | 4/5 | ÷ 20 |
| 10% | 10/100 | 1/10 | ÷ 10 |

#### Medium Examples

| Percentage | Over 100 | Simplified | GCF |
|------------|----------|------------|-----|
| 35% | 35/100 | 7/20 | 5 |
| 45% | 45/100 | 9/20 | 5 |
| 15% | 15/100 | 3/20 | 5 |
| 65% | 65/100 | 13/20 | 5 |
| 8% | 8/100 | 2/25 | 4 |
| 12% | 12/100 | 3/25 | 4 |
| 36% | 36/100 | 9/25 | 4 |

#### Hard Examples (Decimal Percentages)

When the percentage contains a decimal, multiply both numerator and denominator to eliminate it:

```
12.5% → 12.5/100 → (12.5 × 2)/(100 × 2) = 25/200 = 1/8
37.5% → 37.5/100 → (37.5 × 2)/(100 × 2) = 75/200 = 3/8
6.25% → 6.25/100 → (6.25 × 4)/(100 × 4) = 25/400 = 1/16
33.33% → 33⅓/100 = (100/3)/100 = 100/300 = 1/3
66.67% → 66⅔/100 = (200/3)/100 = 200/300 = 2/3
```

#### Percentages Greater Than 100%

```
125% → 125/100 = 5/4 (improper fraction) or 1 1/4 (mixed number)
150% → 150/100 = 3/2 or 1 1/2
200% → 200/100 = 2/1 = 2
350% → 350/100 = 7/2 or 3 1/2
```

**CSE Tip:** To simplify quickly, find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) of the numerator and 100. Common GCFs with 100: 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50.

**Common Mistake:** Not simplifying completely. 60/100 simplified by 10 gives 6/10, but this is NOT fully reduced — divide again by 2 to get 3/5.

```
1/5 = 20%, 2/5 = 40%, 3/5 = 60%, 4/5 = 80%, 5/5 = 100%
```

```
1/8 = 12.5%, 2/8 = 25%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 4/8 = 50%
5/8 = 62.5%, 6/8 = 75%, 7/8 = 87.5%, 8/8 = 100%
```

```
1/3 ≈ 33.33%, 2/3 ≈ 66.67%, 3/3 = 100%
```

## 4.8 Practical Applications of Percentages

Percentages are not abstract — they appear in real scenarios that government employees encounter daily.

#### Shopping and Commerce

```
Original price: ₱1,200
Discount: 25%
Discount amount: ₱1,200 × 0.25 = ₱300
Sale price: ₱1,200 − ₱300 = ₱900
```

#### VAT Computation (12%)

```
Item price (VAT-exclusive): ₱500
VAT: ₱500 × 0.12 = ₱60
Total price: ₱500 + ₱60 = ₱560
```

#### Grades and Test Scores

```
A student answered 42 out of 50 items correctly.
Score as percentage: 42/50 = 0.84 = 84%
```

#### Salary Increase

```
Current salary: ₱25,000/month
Raise: 8%
Increase amount: ₱25,000 × 0.08 = ₱2,000
New salary: ₱25,000 + ₱2,000 = ₱27,000
```

#### Survey and Election Results

```
Total voters: 5,000
Candidate A received 2,350 votes
Percentage: 2,350/5,000 = 0.47 = 47%
```

#### Budget Allocation

```
Total department budget: ₱10,000,000
Personnel services: 60% → ₱6,000,000
MOOE: 30% → ₱3,000,000
Capital outlay: 10% → ₱1,000,000
```

#### Engineering Accuracy

```
A machine produces 500 items per hour.
Defect rate: 2%
Defective items: 500 × 0.02 = 10 items
Good items: 500 − 10 = 490 items
```

**CSE Tip:** In word problems, identify the BASE (the whole), the RATE (the percentage), and the PART (the portion). The relationship is always: Part = Base × Rate.

## 4.9 Percentage Comparison and Interpretation

Understanding how to compare percentages and interpret percentage statements is critical for government work and the CSE.

#### Comparing Percentages

To compare values expressed in different forms, convert them all to the same form:

**Example:** Which is greater: 3/8, 0.36, or 35%?
```
3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
0.36 = 36%
35% = 35%

Order: 3/8 (37.5%) > 0.36 (36%) > 35%
```

#### Interpreting Survey Results

**Statement:** "60% of employees prefer flexible work schedules."

This means:
- 60 out of every 100 employees surveyed prefer flexible schedules
- The majority (more than half) prefer flexible schedules
- 40% do NOT prefer flexible schedules (the complement)

#### Absolute vs. Relative Thinking

**Warning:** Percentages can be misleading without context.

**Example:** "Department A reduced errors by 50%. Department B reduced errors by 25%."

This does NOT necessarily mean Department A improved more:
- If A had 4 errors and reduced by 50% → now has 2 errors (reduced by 2)
- If B had 100 errors and reduced by 25% → now has 75 errors (reduced by 25)

Department B eliminated more actual errors despite a lower percentage reduction.

#### Percentage Points vs. Percent Change

- "The approval rating went from 40% to 50%" — this is a **10 percentage point** increase
- But it is a **25% increase** relative to the original (10/40 = 0.25 = 25%)

The CSE may test whether you can distinguish between these two interpretations.

#### Complementary Percentages

If 35% of applicants passed, then 100% − 35% = **65% failed**. The complement always adds up to 100%.

## 4.10 Problem-Solving Strategies

When facing percentage conversion problems on the CSE, use this systematic approach:

#### Step-by-Step System

```
Step 1: Identify what form the number is currently in (fraction, decimal, or %)
Step 2: Identify what form the question asks for
Step 3: Apply the correct conversion rule
Step 4: Simplify if needed
Step 5: Verify by converting back
```

#### Conversion Rules Summary

| From | To | Rule |
|------|-----|------|
| Fraction | Percentage | Divide, then × 100 |
| Decimal | Percentage | × 100 (move decimal right 2) |
| Percentage | Decimal | ÷ 100 (move decimal left 2) |
| Percentage | Fraction | Write over 100, simplify |
| Fraction | Decimal | Divide numerator by denominator |
| Decimal | Fraction | Write as fraction over power of 10, simplify |

#### Verification Technique

Always check your answer by reversing the conversion:
```
Claim: 3/8 = 37.5%
Check: 37.5% → 37.5/100 = 375/1000 = 3/8 ✓
```

#### Elimination Strategy for Multiple Choice

If the question asks "What is 7/20 as a percentage?" and the choices are:
- A) 3.5%
- B) 14%
- C) 35%
- D) 70%

**Quick estimate:** 7/20 is slightly more than 1/3 (which is 33.33%), so the answer should be around 35%. Select C.

**Verification:** 7 ÷ 20 = 0.35 → 35% ✓

#### Avoiding Careless Errors

1. **Direction check:** Fraction/decimal → percentage = number gets BIGGER
2. **Direction check:** Percentage → fraction/decimal = number gets SMALLER
3. **Reasonableness:** 1/4 should be 25%, not 250% or 2.5%
4. **Simplification:** Always reduce fractions to lowest terms
5. **Units:** Don't forget the % symbol when expressing percentages

## 4.11 Estimation and Mental Math Techniques

Speed matters on the CSE. These mental math shortcuts help you solve percentage problems faster and verify answers without full computation.

#### Benchmark Estimation

Use known benchmarks to estimate unknown values:

```
What is 3/7 as a percentage?
Think: 3/7 is slightly less than 3/6 = 1/2 = 50%
       and slightly more than 3/8 = 37.5%
Estimate: around 42-43%
Actual: 3 ÷ 7 = 0.4286 = 42.86% ✓
```

#### The 10% Shortcut

Finding 10% is easy — just move the decimal one place left. Build other percentages from 10%:

```
10% of 450 = 45
5% of 450 = 22.5 (half of 10%)
20% of 450 = 90 (double 10%)
15% of 450 = 45 + 22.5 = 67.5 (10% + 5%)
1% of 450 = 4.5 (one-tenth of 10%)
```

#### Halving and Doubling

```
50% = half the number
25% = half of 50% = quarter
12.5% = half of 25% = eighth
75% = 50% + 25%
```

#### Fraction-to-Percent Mental Shortcuts

- **Denominator is 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50:** Convert directly (these divide evenly into 100)
- **Denominator is 3, 6, 7, 9, 11:** Use known benchmarks or estimate

#### Elimination by Estimation

On multiple-choice items, estimate first to eliminate impossible answers:

```
Question: What is 5/12 as a percentage?
Estimate: 5/12 is slightly less than 5/10 = 50%, and more than 5/15 = 33%
          So the answer is between 33% and 50%.
Choices: A) 24%  B) 41.67%  C) 58.33%  D) 60%
Only B falls in the 33-50% range. Select B.
```

## 4.12 Common Errors in Percentages

Awareness of these pitfalls helps you avoid losing easy points on the CSE.

#### Error 1: Wrong Decimal Direction

```
WRONG: 0.45 as a percentage → 0.0045% (moved LEFT instead of RIGHT)
RIGHT: 0.45 as a percentage → 45% (moved RIGHT two places)
```

#### Error 2: Forgetting the Zero Placeholder

```
WRONG: 5% as a decimal → 0.5
RIGHT: 5% as a decimal → 0.05

WRONG: 3% as a decimal → 0.3
RIGHT: 3% as a decimal → 0.03
```

#### Error 3: Incomplete Fraction Simplification

```
WRONG: 60% = 60/100 = 6/10 (stopped too early)
RIGHT: 60% = 60/100 = 6/10 = 3/5 (fully simplified)
```

#### Error 4: Mishandling Percentages Over 100%

```
WRONG: 150% as a decimal → 0.15 (treated 150 like 15)
RIGHT: 150% as a decimal → 1.50 = 1.5
```

#### Error 5: Confusing Percent OF vs. Percent MORE

```
"120% of 50" = 50 × 1.20 = 60
"20% more than 50" = 50 + (50 × 0.20) = 50 + 10 = 60

These happen to be the same, but the reasoning differs.
"120% of 50" ≠ "120% more than 50"
"120% more than 50" = 50 + (50 × 1.20) = 50 + 60 = 110
```

#### Error 6: Arithmetic Carelessness with Repeating Decimals

```
WRONG: 1/3 = 0.33 = 33% (rounded too aggressively)
RIGHT: 1/3 = 0.3333... = 33.33% (or 33⅓%)
```

**CSE Tip:** On the exam, if you see 33.33% or 33⅓% among the choices, it represents 1/3. If you see 66.67% or 66⅔%, it represents 2/3.

## Mini Practice Set

**1.** Convert 3/5 to a percentage.
**Answer:** 60%
**Explanation:** 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6 → 0.6 × 100 = 60%.

**2.** Convert 0.875 to a percentage.
**Answer:** 87.5%
**Explanation:** 0.875 × 100 = 87.5%.

**3.** Convert 45% to a decimal.
**Answer:** 0.45
**Explanation:** 45 ÷ 100 = 0.45.

**4.** Convert 72% to a fraction in lowest terms.
**Answer:** 18/25
**Explanation:** 72/100 → GCF is 4 → 72 ÷ 4 = 18, 100 ÷ 4 = 25 → 18/25.

**5.** What is 1/8 expressed as a percentage?
**Answer:** 12.5%
**Explanation:** 1 ÷ 8 = 0.125 → 0.125 × 100 = 12.5%.

**6.** Convert 0.04 to a percentage.
**Answer:** 4%
**Explanation:** 0.04 × 100 = 4%.

**7.** Express 250% as a decimal.
**Answer:** 2.5
**Explanation:** 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5.

**8.** Convert 5/8 to a percentage.
**Answer:** 62.5%
**Explanation:** 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625 → 0.625 × 100 = 62.5%.

**9.** What fraction is equivalent to 35%?
**Answer:** 7/20
**Explanation:** 35/100 → GCF is 5 → 35 ÷ 5 = 7, 100 ÷ 5 = 20 → 7/20.

**10.** Convert 2/3 to a percentage.
**Answer:** 66.67%
**Explanation:** 2 ÷ 3 = 0.6667 → 0.6667 × 100 = 66.67%.

**11.** Express 0.005 as a percentage.
**Answer:** 0.5%
**Explanation:** 0.005 × 100 = 0.5%.

**12.** Convert 6% to a fraction in lowest terms.
**Answer:** 3/50
**Explanation:** 6/100 → GCF is 2 → 6 ÷ 2 = 3, 100 ÷ 2 = 50 → 3/50.

**13.** Which is greater: 2/5 or 38%?
**Answer:** 2/5 (which equals 40%)
**Explanation:** 2/5 = 0.4 = 40%, and 40% > 38%.

**14.** Convert 7/4 to a percentage.
**Answer:** 175%
**Explanation:** 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75 → 1.75 × 100 = 175%.

**15.** Express 0.333 as a percentage.
**Answer:** 33.3%
**Explanation:** 0.333 × 100 = 33.3%.

**16.** Convert 125% to a fraction.
**Answer:** 5/4 or 1 1/4
**Explanation:** 125/100 → GCF is 25 → 125 ÷ 25 = 5, 100 ÷ 25 = 4 → 5/4.

**17.** What is 9/25 as a percentage?

## Quick Recap

- A **percentage** means "per hundred" — it expresses a number as a fraction of 100
- **Fraction → Percentage:** Divide numerator by denominator, multiply by 100
- **Decimal → Percentage:** Multiply by 100 (move decimal 2 places right)
- **Percentage → Decimal:** Divide by 100 (move decimal 2 places left)
- **Percentage → Fraction:** Write over 100, simplify to lowest terms
- Memorize benchmark equivalents (halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, eighths, tenths)
- Use estimation to verify answers and eliminate wrong choices quickly
- Watch for common traps: wrong decimal direction, incomplete simplification, missing zero placeholders

## Memory Aids

- **"Percent = Per Hundred"** — always think "out of 100"
- **"Right to Percent, Left to Decimal"** — decimal point moves RIGHT when converting TO percentage, LEFT when converting FROM percentage
- **"Bigger to Percent, Smaller to Decimal"** — the number gets bigger as a percentage, smaller as a decimal
- **"Divide and Multiply by 100"** — fraction→% uses ×100; %→decimal uses ÷100
- **"GCF with 100"** — to simplify %→fraction, find the GCF of the number and 100
- **"Fifths = 20s, Eighths = 12.5s, Thirds = 33.3s"** — count by these intervals for quick recall
- **"Factor of 100 = Easy Convert"** — if denominator is 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, or 50, multiply to get /100 directly
