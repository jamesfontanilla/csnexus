# Prefixes

## Explanations

### Introduction

Prefixes are word parts added to the beginning of a base word. They change meaning without replacing the whole word. That makes them one of the fastest tools for unlocking unfamiliar vocabulary in Civil Service Examination verbal ability items.

On the ladder of word meaning, prefixes come early. They help you see whether a word means not, again, before, after, between, under, beyond, or many other common ideas. If you can spot the prefix quickly, you already have a strong clue about the answer.

### Why This Topic Is Tested in the CSE

The CSE likes prefix questions because they test more than memory. They test whether you can break a word into parts, connect those parts to meaning, and still check the sentence for the final answer.

That matters in government work too. A report, memo, notice, or instruction can change meaning fast if one prefix is missed. A single part at the start of a word can flip the idea completely.

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

Examinees often make these errors:

- guessing the prefix meaning without checking the whole word
- confusing a prefix with the base word
- treating every word that starts with the same letters as a true prefix word
- forgetting that some prefixes can have more than one meaning
- ignoring context when the word can be read in more than one way

These mistakes happen because many learners memorize word lists without learning how words behave in real sentences. This lesson fixes that by focusing on meaning, usage, and context together.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify common prefixes quickly
- explain what a prefix usually means
- split a word into prefix and base
- use context to confirm the right sense
- avoid false prefix guesses

## The Prefix Ladder

### Overview

Think of prefixes as the first rung of the word-meaning ladder. They appear at the front of a word and immediately narrow the meaning.

### Core Principle

A prefix adds a clue, but it does not work alone. The base word still matters. If you know the prefix meaning and the base meaning, you can often solve the word faster than by guessing from the whole word alone.

### Visualization

| Step | What you learn | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A prefix sits at the beginning of a word | `un` + `happy` |
| 2 | Common prefixes have common meanings | `re-` = again |
| 3 | The base word still matters | `sub` + `marine` |
| 4 | Context decides the exact answer | "The submarine stayed below the surface." |

### Common Mistakes

- reading only the first two letters and assuming the meaning
- forgetting that a prefix can attach to more than one type of base word
- confusing a prefix with part of the root
- ignoring the sentence when two meanings look possible

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does `un-` mean in `unfair`?

Reasoning:

1. The base word is `fair`.
2. The prefix `un-` usually means not or opposite of.
3. `unfair` therefore means not fair.

The prefix changes the meaning in a simple but powerful way.

## Common Prefix Families

### Overview

Some prefix families appear again and again in exam vocabulary. Learning those families gives you the biggest return for the least memorization.

### Core Principle

Do not try to memorize every possible word. Learn the most common families and their most common meanings. That is enough to break open many CSE items.

### Visualization

| Family | Common meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `un-`, `in-`, `im-`, `il-`, `ir-`, `dis-`, `non-`, `anti-` | not; opposite of; against | `unfair`, `invisible`, `disapprove`, `nonprofit` |
| `re-`, `pre-`, `post-`, `fore-`, `mid-`, `semi-` | again; before; after; front; middle; half | `rewrite`, `preview`, `postpone`, `foretell`, `midpoint`, `semiformal` |
| `sub-`, `inter-`, `trans-`, `over-`, `under-`, `extra-`, `intra-` | under; between; across; too much; too little; beyond; within | `submarine`, `interact`, `transport`, `overwork`, `underpay`, `extracurricular`, `intramural` |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest trap is assuming every word that starts with the same letters uses the prefix in the way you expect. Some words just happen to begin with those letters, and some prefixes have more than one meaning.

The prefix points directly to quantity.

## What Prefixes Change

### Overview

Prefixes usually change one of a few big ideas: negation, repetition, time, place, size, amount, direction, or relation.

### Core Principle

A prefix does not just decorate a word. It steers the meaning. The same base word can become a different idea depending on the prefix attached to it.

### Visualization

| Change | Common prefixes | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negation | `un-`, `in-`, `im-`, `il-`, `ir-`, `dis-`, `non-` | `unfair`, `impossible`, `disagree` |
| Repetition | `re-` | `rewrite`, `reopen` |
| Time or order | `pre-`, `post-`, `fore-`, `mid-`, `semi-` | `preview`, `postpone`, `foretell`, `midterm`, `semiformal` |
| Place or relation | `sub-`, `inter-`, `trans-`, `intra-` | `submarine`, `interact`, `transport`, `intramural` |
| Size or amount | `micro-`, `multi-`, `hyper-`, `uni-`, `bi-`, `tri-` | `microscope`, `multilingual`, `hyperactive`, `biweekly` |
| Self or together | `auto-`, `co-`, `com-`, `con-` | `autobiography`, `cooperate`, `connect` |

### Common Mistakes

Some prefixes have more than one job. For example, `over-` can mean above or too much, and `under-` can mean below or too little. The sentence decides which shade fits best.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does `re-` mean in `rewrite`?

Reasoning:

1. The base word is `write`.
2. `re-` often means again or back.
3. `rewrite` means write again.

The prefix shows repetition.

### Key Insight

If you know the job a prefix usually does, you can narrow the answer very quickly. The best prefix readers do not just identify the beginning of the word; they identify the meaning shift.

## How To Decode A Word

### Overview

Use a fixed order when you meet an unfamiliar prefixed word.

### Core Principle

Read the word in parts, then check the sentence. The fastest solution is not random guessing. It is a repeatable method.

### Visualization

| Step | What you do | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find the prefix | shows the meaning clue |
| 2 | Say the base word | reveals the core idea |
| 3 | Recall the prefix meaning | narrows the answer |
| 4 | Read the sentence | confirms the exact sense |
| 5 | Eliminate the wrong choices | saves time and reduces error |

### Common Mistakes

- answering before reading the sentence
- assuming the first meaning that comes to mind is correct
- forgetting that some prefixes can be ambiguous
- not checking whether the answer choice preserves the base word meaning

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does `postpone` suggest?

Reasoning:

1. The prefix `post-` usually means after.
2. The base idea is to place or time something.
3. `postpone` means to put something after the planned time.

The prefix helps you see why the word means delay.

### Quick Check

Question: What should you do first when decoding a word
Choices:
- Find the prefix
- before
- after
Answer: Find the prefix
Rationale: The prefix clue changes the meaning before the base word.

Question: Why is the base word important
Choices:
- before
- The root carries the core meaning
- after
Answer: The root carries the core meaning
Rationale: The prefix clue changes the meaning before the base word.

Question: Why should you check the sentence
Choices:
- before
- after
- The sentence confirms the exact sense
Answer: The sentence confirms the exact sense
Rationale: The prefix clue changes the meaning before the base word.

### Key Insight

A step-by-step routine is better than memory alone. If you can identify the prefix, the base word, and the sentence clue, you can solve many vocabulary questions without stress.

## Quick Signals To Remember

### Overview

Some prefix clues are so common that you should be able to recognize them almost instantly.

### Core Principle

Think of these as fast signals, not final answers. They help you move quickly, but context still has the final say.

### Visualization

- `un-` often signals not
- `re-` often signals again
- `pre-` often signals before
- `post-` often signals after
- `sub-` often signals under
- `inter-` often signals between
- `multi-` often signals many
- `auto-` often signals self

### Common Mistakes

The danger is overtrusting the signal and skipping the rest of the word. A quick clue is useful only when it fits the full sentence.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does `auto-` suggest in `autobiography`?

Reasoning:

1. `auto-` means self.
2. `bio-` relates to life.
3. `autobiography` is a life story written by oneself.

The signal helps you decode the whole word.

Rationale: The prefix clue changes the meaning before the base word.

Question: What does `pre-` usually mean

### Key Insight

Fast prefix signals are exam-friendly because they save time. Just remember that they are starting points, not shortcuts that replace thinking.

## Key Takeaways

- A prefix is added to the beginning of a word.
- Prefixes can change meaning, time, place, size, or amount.
- The most useful exam prefixes are the common ones: `un-`, `re-`, `pre-`, `sub-`, `inter-`, `multi-`, and similar families.
- A prefix is a clue, not the whole answer.
- The sentence still decides the best meaning.
- A good test taker checks both the word parts and the context.
- Learning a small prefix ladder can help with many vocabulary items at once.

## Summary

Prefixes are one of the fastest ways to break open new words. They give you clues about opposite meaning, repetition, time, place, number, size, and direction. In the CSE, that kind of clue can turn an unfamiliar word into a manageable one.

If you remember only one idea from this lesson, remember this: do not read the beginning of the word in isolation. Match the prefix to its usual meaning, read the base word, and confirm everything in the sentence. That habit will help you in word meaning, context clues, and other verbal ability items.
