# Multiple Meaning Words

## Explanations

### Introduction

Multiple meaning words are words that have more than one sense. The spelling stays the same, but the meaning changes depending on the sentence. In Civil Service Exam verbal ability items, that matters because a familiar-looking word can still be wrong if you choose the wrong sense.

This topic is important because the exam does not test memory alone. It tests whether you can read carefully, use context, and choose the meaning that actually fits the sentence.

### Why This Topic Is Tested

Official CSC advisories list **word meaning** under verbal ability, and sentence completion uses the same skill. Words with multiple meanings are especially useful for test writers because they force you to rely on context instead of guessing from the first meaning that comes to mind.

That is why this topic shows up often in exam practice. A word may look ordinary, but the sentence can make it mean something different.

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often make these errors:

- choosing the first dictionary meaning without reading the sentence
- forgetting that a word can be a noun in one sentence and a verb in another
- ignoring clue words like *but*, *or*, *in other words*, and *such as*
- assuming the most common meaning is always correct
- confusing literal meaning with a different sense of the same word
- reading too fast and missing the part of speech

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain what multiple meaning words are
- recognize when a word changes meaning because of context
- use clue words and sentence logic to choose the right sense
- identify when a word is being used as a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb
- eliminate answer choices that fit another meaning of the same word

## What Multiple Meaning Words Are

### Overview

A multiple meaning word is a word that has two or more meanings. Sometimes the meanings are closely related. Sometimes they are very different. The important point is that the sentence decides which meaning is active.

Examples:

- **bank** can mean a financial institution or the side of a river
- **light** can mean not heavy, illumination, or to make something burn
- **file** can mean a folder of papers, a metal tool, or to arrange papers in order
- **spring** can mean a season, a coil, or to jump suddenly
- **watch** can mean a timepiece or to look carefully

### Core Principle

Ask this question every time:

**Which meaning makes the whole sentence work?**

That is the meaning you want. Not the most common meaning. Not the first meaning you remember. The one that fits the sentence.

### Common Mistakes

Many students think dictionary study alone is enough. It is not. A dictionary shows several meanings, but the sentence chooses only one.

Another mistake is treating every meaning as equally likely. In exam items, the context usually gives strong clues that rule out the wrong sense.

### Key Insight

The word does not carry one fixed meaning in every sentence. The sentence gives the meaning.

## Why Context Matters

### Overview

Context is the surrounding language that helps define the word. If the sentence contains clue words, punctuation, or clear topic clues, those details help you identify the correct meaning.

The same word can switch sense because the surrounding words create a different situation.

### Core Principle

The sentence may tell you the meaning through:

- a direct explanation
- a restatement in simpler words
- a contrast with another idea
- examples that point to a category
- grammar and part of speech
- the general logic of the sentence

### Visualization

| Clue type | How it helps | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| definition | directly names the meaning | "or a financial institution" |
| restatement | repeats the idea more simply | "in other words" |
| contrast | points to the opposite sense | "but" or "however" |
| grammar | shows whether the word is a noun, verb, or adjective | "to file" vs "a file" |
| topic clue | tells you the subject area | money, river, school, work |

### Common Mistakes

Some test takers look only at the target word and ignore the sentence around it. That is the fastest way to miss the answer.

Another mistake is using a meaning that is correct in general but wrong in that sentence. On the CSE, the exact context matters more than a dictionary memorization trick.

### Key Insight

Context is the rulebook. The sentence decides which meaning is allowed.

## Clue Types That Help

### Definition and Restatement Clues

Some sentences explain the meaning directly.

Example:

"She went to the bank, a financial institution, after lunch."

The phrase *a financial institution* gives the meaning directly.

### Contrast Clues

Sometimes the sentence shows the other meaning first and then shifts away from it.

Example:

"The bank by the river was muddy, but the bank where he deposited money was closed."

The word *but* helps you see that the second meaning is different from the first.

### Grammar Clues

Part of speech matters.

- **to file** is a verb
- **a file** is a noun
- **light** can be an adjective, noun, or verb depending on the sentence

If the answer choice does not fit the grammar, it is wrong.

### Topic Clues

The subject of the sentence often points to the meaning.

- money words suggest the banking sense
- river words suggest the river-edge sense
- school or office words suggest paper or record senses

### Key Insight

You do not have to guess randomly. The sentence gives enough clues if you slow down and read carefully.

## How To Choose the Correct Meaning

### Step-by-Step Method

Use this method every time:

1. Identify the target word.
2. Read the whole sentence first.
3. Decide what part of speech the word is.
4. Look for clue words and topic clues.
5. Test each meaning in the sentence.
6. Eliminate choices that do not fit grammar or context.
7. Choose the meaning that makes the sentence correct and natural.

### What To Ask Yourself

- Does this meaning fit the sentence?
- Is this the right part of speech?
- Does the surrounding context support it?
- Would the sentence still make sense if I replace the word with this choice?

### Shortcut Rule

If two meanings seem possible, choose the one that fits the whole sentence best, not just one word nearby.

### Exam-Day Tips

- do not trust the first meaning you remember
- reread the sentence after each elimination
- pay attention to small words like *but*, *or*, and *in*
- check whether the word is being used as a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb
- if the word still feels unclear, test the choices one by one

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Question: What does **bank** mean in the sentence below?

"She deposited her paycheck at the bank before lunch."

Options:

1. river edge
2. financial institution
3. steep slope
4. collection of clouds

Reasoning:

- the sentence mentions *deposited her paycheck*
- that points to money and accounts
- the correct meaning is **financial institution**

### Example 2

Question: What does **light** mean in the sentence below?

"The box was light enough to carry with one hand."

Options:

1. bright
2. not heavy
3. to ignite
4. weak in color

Reasoning:

- the sentence says the box was easy to carry
- that means it was **not heavy**

### Example 3

Options:

1. metal tool
2. arrange in order
3. stack loosely

Reasoning:

- the sentence talks about reports and alphabetically
- the correct sense is **arrange in order**

### Example 4

Options:

1. jump suddenly

3. the season after winter

Reasoning:

- the sentence mentions flowers blooming
- that points to the season
- the correct answer is **the season after winter**

## Sentence Completion Connection

Multiple meaning words are also important in sentence completion. The blank may allow more than one word in theory, but only one meaning will fit the grammar and the logic of the full sentence.

That is why the same habit matters in both topics:

- read the full sentence
- check the clue words
- test the part of speech
- eliminate choices that do not fit the meaning



## Key Takeaways

- Multiple meaning words have more than one sense.
- The sentence decides which meaning is correct.
- Part of speech is a major clue.
- Context clues and topic clues help you eliminate wrong choices.
- Do not choose the first dictionary meaning automatically.
- Test the answer inside the sentence before you commit.

## Summary

Multiple meaning words are a common CSE trap because they look familiar but change meaning with context. The same spelling can point to different senses, and the only safe way to answer is to read the full sentence carefully.

If you remember one thing from this lesson, remember this: **the correct meaning is the one that fits the sentence, not the one you remember first.**

Answer: look after or keep an eye on.
