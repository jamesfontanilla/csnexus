# Suffixes

## Explanations

### Introduction

Suffixes are word parts added to the end of a base word. They do not just decorate the word. They often change the word's part of speech, tense, degree, or meaning. In the CSE, that makes suffixes a fast way to unlock unfamiliar vocabulary.

If prefixes help you read the front of a word, suffixes help you read the back. That matters because many exam words look unfamiliar only until you notice the ending. A word like `helpful`, `happiness`, or `carelessly` becomes much easier once you spot the suffix.

### Why This Topic Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Commission likes word-part questions because they test reasoning, not just memorization. A test taker who knows suffixes can decode many words quickly, compare answer choices more accurately, and avoid guessing from a single familiar-looking ending.

Suffix knowledge also helps in workplace reading. Government memos, notices, reports, and forms often use words that change form through suffixes. If you understand what the ending is doing, you can read faster and with fewer mistakes.

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

Many examinees make the same errors:

- treating every word ending as a real suffix
- guessing the meaning from the ending alone and ignoring the full word
- forgetting that one suffix can have more than one job
- mixing up noun-forming, adjective-forming, and adverb-forming endings
- missing spelling changes such as dropping `e` or changing `y` to `i`

The fix is simple: read the whole word, identify the suffix, ask what job it usually does, and then confirm the meaning in the sentence.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify common suffixes in words
- explain what a suffix usually does to meaning or grammar
- recognize when a suffix changes part of speech
- spot common spelling shifts caused by suffixes
- solve CSE-style word-meaning questions using suffix clues and context

### Overview

Think of suffixes as a ladder of meaning:

| 2 | Common suffixes carry common meanings | `-less` = without |
| 3 | Some suffixes change grammar | `teach` â†’ `teacher` |
| 4 | Some suffixes change degree or tense | `kind` â†’ `kinder` / `walk` â†’ `walked` |
| 5 | Context confirms the final meaning | "The careful worker" |

When you climb this ladder, you move from simple recognition to actual exam solving.

### Core Principle

The main rule is this: a suffix adds meaning at the end, but the base word still matters. The best answer is usually the one that fits both the suffix and the sentence.

That is why suffix questions are not only vocabulary questions. They are also grammar and context questions. A suffix may tell you the word is a noun, adjective, or adverb, but the sentence still decides whether that word makes sense.

### Visualization

Picture a word as a train:

- the base word is the engine
- the suffix is the last car
- the suffix changes the train's function

| Base word | Suffix | New word | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `kind` | `-ness` | `kindness` | turns an adjective into a noun |
| `hope` | `-ful` | `hopeful` | turns a noun into an adjective |
| `quick` | `-ly` | `quickly` | turns an adjective into an adverb |
| `teach` | `-er` | `teacher` | turns a verb into a person noun |

### Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is seeing the ending and assuming the whole word has that meaning. That is risky. For example, not every word ending in `-ly` is an adverb, and not every word ending in `-er` means "one who."

Another mistake is forgetting that the same suffix can do different jobs. `-er` can mean "one who" in `teacher`, but it can also mean "more" in `bigger`.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does the suffix `-less` suggest in `careless`?

Think it through:

1. The base word is `care`.
2. The suffix `-less` usually means without.
3. `careless` therefore means without care or not careful.

So the suffix signals lack or absence.

## Common Suffix Families

### Overview

Some suffixes appear over and over in exam vocabulary. These are the families worth learning first because they unlock many words quickly.

### Core Principle

The CSE does not expect you to memorize every ending in the language. It expects you to know the high-yield ones and use them correctly. The best suffix families are the ones that regularly show up in reading, office writing, and vocabulary items.

### Visualization

| --- | --- | --- |
| `-less` | without; lacking | `hopeless`, `careless` |
| `-ful` | full of; having | `helpful`, `careful` |
| `-ness` | state; quality; condition | `happiness`, `darkness` |
| `-ment` | act; process; result | `movement`, `payment` |
| `-tion` / `-sion` | act; result; state | `creation`, `decision` |
| `-able` / `-ible` | capable of; fit to be | `readable`, `visible` |
| `-ly` | in a manner; like | `quickly`, `softly` |
| `-er` / `-or` | one who; person who; more | `teacher`, `actor`, `bigger` |
| `-est` | most | `brightest`, `largest` |
| `-al` / `-ial` / `-ic` | relating to; having the nature of | `national`, `social`, `scientific` |
| `-ous` / `-y` / `-ish` | full of; like; somewhat | `dangerous`, `rainy`, `childish` |
| `-ship` / `-hood` / `-dom` | state; condition; relation | `friendship`, `childhood`, `kingdom` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not assume every suffix family has only one meaning. Some endings are flexible. For example, `-er` can mean a person who does something, but it can also show comparison. That is why you should always read the full word.

So the best answer is the noun-forming family.

## What Suffixes Change

### Overview

Suffixes can change more than one thing. They may change part of speech, show tense, show degree, or add a quality meaning.

### Core Principle

There are two broad jobs to remember:

- **Inflectional** suffixes change grammar without creating a new dictionary word class in the same way. Examples include tense and degree endings.
- **Derivational** suffixes create new words or new parts of speech. Examples include `-ness`, `-ful`, `-less`, `-ment`, and `-tion`.

On the CSE, you do not need a technical grammar lecture. You do need to know what the ending is doing.

### Visualization

| Change | Common suffixes | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tense | `-ed`, `-ing` | `walked`, `walking` |
| Degree | `-er`, `-est` | `larger`, `largest` |
| Adverb | `-ly` | `carefully` |
| Noun | `-ness`, `-ment`, `-tion`, `-ship` | `happiness`, `movement`, `creation`, `friendship` |
| Adjective | `-ful`, `-less`, `-ous`, `-al`, `-ic`, `-y`, `-ish` | `hopeful`, `careless`, `dangerous`, `national`, `scientific`, `rainy`, `childish` |
| Verb | `-en`, `-ize`, `-fy` | `strengthen`, `modernize`, `simplify` |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest trap is confusing a suffix's job with the word's final meaning. For example, `-ly` often makes an adverb, but not every `-ly` word is an adverb. Some are adjectives, and some are nouns or proper names.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: In `teacher`, what does the suffix `-er` do?

Reasoning:

1. The base word is `teach`.
2. `-er` can form a noun meaning a person who does the action.
3. A teacher is a person who teaches.

So the suffix creates a person noun.

## Spelling Shifts

### Overview

Adding a suffix often changes spelling. The spelling change is not random. It usually follows a pattern.

### Core Principle

Three common spelling patterns matter most:

- drop the silent `e` before certain suffixes
- change `y` to `i` before many suffixes
- sometimes double the final consonant in short words

These patterns help you recognize the derived word and avoid spelling traps.

### Visualization

| Base | Suffix | Result | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hope` | `-ful` | `hopeful` | keep the `e` when needed for the base |
| `care` | `-less` | `careless` | keep the base shape clear |
| `happy` | `-ness` | `happiness` | `y` changes to `i` |
| `simple` | `-ly` | `simply` | silent `e` drops |
| `run` | `-ing` | `running` | final consonant doubles |

### Common Mistakes

Students often overgeneralize the spelling rules. Not every suffix changes every word the same way, and some words preserve their spelling for historical reasons. The safe move is to learn the common pattern and then check whether the word actually exists.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which is the correct form: `happy + ness` or `happy + ly`?

Reasoning:

1. `happy` is an adjective.
2. `-ness` turns it into a noun: `happiness`.
3. `-ly` would make an adverb: `happily`.
4. If the question asks for a noun, the correct form is `happiness`.

This shows why spelling and grammar work together.

Spelling changes are clues, not obstacles. If you know the common pattern, the derived word becomes easier to recognize and easier to spell correctly on the exam.

## Solving CSE Suffix Questions

### Overview

Suffix questions become manageable when you use the same method every time.

### Core Principle

Use this sequence:

1. Spot the suffix.
2. Identify the base word.
3. Recall the suffix's common job.
4. Check whether the word class changed.
5. Read the sentence to confirm the meaning.

That process works for direct vocabulary questions and for context-based items.

### Visualization

| Step | What you do | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find the ending | reveals the suffix family |
| 2 | Remove the ending mentally | shows the base word |
| 3 | Name the suffix job | tells you the likely meaning |
| 4 | Check part of speech | avoids grammar traps |
| 5 | Test in context | confirms the final answer |

### Common Mistakes

The worst mistake is guessing from memory without checking the sentence. Another mistake is choosing the answer that sounds impressive instead of the one that fits the suffix's job.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does `quickly` suggest?

Reasoning:

1. The base word is `quick`.
2. `-ly` usually forms an adverb.
3. An adverb tells how something is done.
4. So `quickly` means in a quick manner.

The suffix tells you the word is describing an action, not a person or thing.

## Key Takeaways

- A suffix is added to the end of a word.
- Suffixes can change meaning, tense, degree, or part of speech.
- Common suffixes include `-less`, `-ful`, `-ness`, `-ment`, `-tion`, `-able`, `-ly`, `-er`, `-est`, `-al`, `-ic`, `-ous`, `-ship`, `-hood`, and `-dom`.
- Some suffixes are flexible and can do more than one job.
- Spelling changes like `y` to `i` and silent-`e` dropping are important clues.
- The base word still matters, so do not look only at the ending.
- Context confirms whether your suffix analysis is correct.
- A simple step-by-step method is better than guessing.

## Summary

Suffixes are one of the fastest ways to decode unfamiliar words. They tell you whether a word is acting like a noun, adjective, adverb, or verb, and they often give a direct clue about meaning. In CSE word-meaning questions, that is a powerful advantage.

If you remember only one idea from this lesson, remember this: a suffix is a clue, not the whole answer. Read the base word, identify the suffix, and then check the sentence. That habit will help you in vocabulary, grammar, and reading comprehension items.
