# Elimination Strategy

## Explanations

### Introduction

Elimination strategy means using the clues in the sentence to rule out choices that cannot fit. In sentence completion, you do not have to prove every option right. You only need to prove the wrong options wrong until the best answer is left standing.

That is why strong test takers read the sentence as a whole, not just the blank. They look for meaning clues, grammar clues, tone clues, logic clues, and punctuation clues. Each clue can remove one or more wrong choices.

Think of the process as narrowing a field. The first pass removes obvious mismatches. The second pass checks the close choices. The final pass confirms the best fit.

### Why Elimination Strategy Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination uses plausible distractors. Some answers look familiar. Some sound formal. Some are grammatically possible at first glance but fail once the whole sentence is read carefully.

Elimination strategy matters because the CSE checks whether you can:

- read fast without losing accuracy
- reject choices that clash with meaning
- reject choices that break grammar
- reject choices that do not match tone or register
- reject choices that point in the wrong logical direction
- compare the final two choices carefully

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

Examinees often make these mistakes:

- choosing the first word that sounds familiar
- reading only the choices and not the full sentence
- eliminating too quickly without checking the clue
- stopping after removing one bad option
- ignoring grammar after finding a good meaning
- ignoring tone after finding a good dictionary definition
- trusting a close distractor without a final recheck

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the clue that matters most in a sentence
- eliminate answers by meaning, grammar, tone, logic, and punctuation
- compare the final pair instead of guessing
- explain why a distractor is wrong
- choose the best answer with less hesitation

## Read the Sentence Frame

### Overview

The sentence frame tells you what kind of word the blank needs. Before looking at the choices, ask what role the blank plays in the sentence. Is it a noun, verb, adjective, adverb, phrase, connector, or pronoun? Is it part of a formal statement, a casual remark, or a logical link?

### Core Principle

The blank must fit the sentence frame first. If the frame asks for a verb, a noun cannot win. If the frame asks for a reason connector, a contrast word cannot win. If the frame asks for a formal phrase, a casual word cannot win.

### Visualization

| What the frame shows | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| verb slot | tense, number, voice |
| noun slot | singular or plural, countable or uncountable |
| adjective slot | describes a noun directly |
| adverb slot | modifies a verb, adjective, or clause |
| connector slot | reason, result, contrast, condition |
| pronoun slot | antecedent and number |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is jumping to the choices before identifying the blank type. Another mistake is thinking that a word can be correct just because it looks sophisticated. The frame, not the vocabulary level, decides the answer.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

`The report was ____ after the figures were checked.`

Options:

1. finalized
2. final
3. finally
4. finality

Reasoning:

- `was ____` needs a form that fits a verb phrase
- `finalized` works as the completed action
- `final` is only an adjective and sounds incomplete here
- `finally` is an adverb
- `finality` is a noun

Answer: **finalized**

## Eliminate by Meaning

### Overview

Meaning clues tell you which option makes sense in the situation. If a choice clashes with the sentence idea, remove it immediately. This is the fastest kind of elimination because a wrong meaning is often obvious once the sentence is read carefully.

### Core Principle

Ask whether the option matches the sentence idea exactly. If the clue says exact, complete, careful, urgent, or temporary, the answer must match that idea and not merely sound nice.

### Visualization

| Meaning clue | What to remove |
| --- | --- |
| exact facts | vague or opposite words |
| short-term situation | permanent or long-term words |
| immediate need | slow or optional words |
| careful detail | careless or rough words |
| all required parts | incomplete or empty words |

### Common Mistakes

The common mistake is choosing a synonym that is close but not exact. Another mistake is ignoring the clue words in the sentence, such as `without mistakes`, `for a short time`, or `needed right away`.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

`The office memo was ____ because it gave the facts without mistakes.`

Options:

1. accurate
2. vague
3. noisy
4. broken

Reasoning:

- the clue `gave the facts without mistakes` points to exactness
- `accurate` fits the meaning
- `vague` is the opposite of exact
- `noisy` does not fit the idea of a memo
- `broken` does not fit the meaning of clarity or correctness

Answer: **accurate**

### Quick Check

Question: What should you remove first if the meaning clashes
Choices:
- Meaning
- grammar
- tone
Answer: Meaning
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: a near-synonym is always safe
Choices:
- True
- False
- It depends
Answer: False
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What clue word tells you the answer must be exact
Choices:
- meaning
- grammar
- Exact
Answer: Exact
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

If the choice does not match the idea, it cannot survive no matter how good it sounds.

## Eliminate by Grammar

### Overview

Grammar clues tell you what form the word must take. The sentence may need the correct verb tense, the correct subject-verb agreement, the correct pronoun form, or the correct phrase shape.

### Core Principle

When the frame is grammatical, the wrong forms can be removed fast. A singular subject does not take a plural verb. A past-time clue does not take present tense. A pronoun must match its antecedent. A noun slot cannot take an adverb.

### Visualization

| Grammar clue | What it eliminates |
| --- | --- |
| plural subject | singular verb |
| past time clue | present or future verb |
| pronoun slot | noun or adjective forms |
| adjective slot | adverbs and nouns |
| phrase slot | full clause forms |

### Common Mistakes

The usual mistake is seeing a word that fits the meaning and forgetting that the form is wrong. Another mistake is treating grammar as secondary when it is actually the first filter.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

`The files ____ ready for filing.`

Options:

1. are
2. is
3. was
4. has

Reasoning:

- `files` is plural
- the blank needs a plural verb
- `are` matches the subject
- `is` and `was` are singular
- `has` does not fit the slot

Answer: **are**

### Quick Check

Question: What should match the subject
Choices:
- Grammar
- meaning
- tone
Answer: Grammar
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What should match the antecedent
Choices:
- meaning
- Pronoun
- grammar
Answer: Pronoun
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: grammar can eliminate a choice even if the meaning looks fine
Choices:
- False
- It depends
- True
Answer: True
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Grammar gives you hard boundaries. If the form is wrong, the choice is out.

## Eliminate by Tone and Register

### Overview

Tone and register tell you how formal, casual, official, academic, or technical the sentence should sound. A word may be correct in meaning but still wrong if it sounds too casual for a memo or too stiff for a friendly message.

### Core Principle

Match the voice of the sentence to the setting. Office notices, memos, and circulars usually need formal language. Friendly messages need relaxed language. Academic or technical contexts need precise wording.

### Visualization

| Setting clue | Likely register |
| --- | --- |
| memo, notice, circular | formal or official |
| chat, message, note to a friend | casual |
| study, report, analysis | academic |
| manual, device, procedure | technical |
| clinic, office help, consultation | consultative |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is choosing the strongest-sounding word instead of the best-fitting one. Another mistake is confusing politeness with formality. A polite sentence is not always official, and a casual sentence is not always careless.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

`In the memo, the supervisor ____ the staff to submit the form.`

Options:

1. requests
2. asks
3. begs
4. jokes

Reasoning:

- `memo` signals a formal office setting
- `requests` matches the tone best
- `asks` is possible, but it is less formal
- `begs` is too emotional
- `jokes` does not fit the register

Answer: **requests**

### Quick Check

Question: What does register tell you
Choices:
- Tone

- grammar
Answer: Tone
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: a word can be correct in meaning but wrong in tone
Choices:
- False

Answer: True
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What kind of language usually fits a memo
Choices:

### Key Insight

If the voice is wrong, the choice is wrong even if the dictionary meaning looks close.

## Eliminate by Logic and Punctuation

### Overview

Some choices fail because they point in the wrong logical direction. A reason connector cannot replace a result connector. A contrast word cannot replace a cause word. Punctuation can also reveal what fits, especially with semicolons, commas, and conjunctive adverbs.

### Core Principle

Read the relationship before choosing the word. Ask whether the blank needs a cause, a result, a contrast, or a condition. Then check the punctuation around it.

### Visualization

| Relationship | Common signal |
| --- | --- |
| cause | because, since |
| result | so, therefore, as a result |
| contrast | however, nevertheless |
| condition | unless, if |
| example | for example, for instance |

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often choose a connector because it is familiar, not because it fits the direction. Another mistake is ignoring punctuation. A conjunctive adverb may need a semicolon before it and a comma after it.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

`The roads were flooded; ____, the meeting was postponed.`

Options:

1. therefore
2. because
3. unless
4. meanwhile

Reasoning:

- the first clause gives the reason
- the second clause gives the result
- `therefore` fits the formal result
- `because` would reverse the direction
- `unless` shows condition
- `meanwhile` shows time overlap, not consequence

Answer: **therefore**

### Quick Check

Question: What does a result connector show
Choices:
- A result
- meaning
- grammar
Answer: A result
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What does a contrast connector show
Choices:
- meaning
- A contrast
- grammar
Answer: A contrast
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: punctuation can help eliminate the wrong connector
Choices:
- False
- It depends
- True
Answer: True
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Logic and punctuation work together. If the direction is wrong, the choice cannot stay.

## Recheck the Final Pair

### Overview

After elimination, two choices may still look close. That is normal. The last step is to compare the remaining options directly against the sentence frame. Read each one in the blank and ask which one fits more precisely.

### Core Principle

Do not stop when only two choices remain. Check whether one option is more exact in meaning, more correct in grammar, or more natural in tone. The final pair often differs by a small but important clue.

### Visualization

| Final check | What to compare |
| --- | --- |
| meaning | exactness versus near-match |
| grammar | correct form versus close form |
| tone | formal versus casual voice |
| logic | correct direction versus wrong direction |
| punctuation | correct pause versus wrong pause |

### Common Mistakes

The common mistake is guessing when only two choices remain. Another mistake is trusting the more familiar word even when the sentence frame favors the other one. The final pair deserves a slower reread.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

`The clerk ____ the report before lunch.`

Options:

1. submitted
2. submitting
3. submit
4. submission

Reasoning:

- the sentence needs a past-tense verb
- `submitted` fits the slot exactly
- `submitting` is a participle, not the main verb here
- `submit` is the base form
- `submission` is a noun

Answer: **submitted**

### Quick Check

Question: What should you do when two choices remain
Choices:
- Test both in the sentence
- meaning
- grammar
Answer: Test both in the sentence
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What can still separate the final pair
Choices:
- meaning

- grammar
Answer: Tone
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: the last two choices should be guessed without checking
Choices:
- True

Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

The final pair is where careful readers win. Slow down just enough to see the exact fit.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The notice was ____ because it gave every required step clearly.`

Options:

1. complete
2. completed
3. completely
4. completeness

Reasoning:

- the blank needs an adjective describing the notice
- `complete` fits the meaning and grammar
- `completed` suggests the notice performed an action
- `completely` is an adverb
- `completeness` is a noun

Answer: **complete**

### Example 2

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The office lost power; ____, the briefing moved to the lobby.`

Options:

1. therefore
2. because
3. unless
4. even

Reasoning:

- the second clause is the result
- `therefore` fits the logic and the punctuation
- `because` reverses the direction
- `unless` shows condition
- `even` does not complete the relationship

Answer: **therefore**

### Example 3

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence below?

`The report was long, but ____ was clear.`

Options:

1. it
2. they
3. he
4. she

Reasoning:

- the antecedent is `The report`
- `report` is singular and inanimate
- `it` matches the antecedent
- the other choices refer to people or plural nouns

Answer: **it**

## Key Takeaways

- Elimination strategy means removing choices that cannot fit.
- The sentence frame tells you what kind of word the blank needs.
- Meaning, grammar, tone, logic, and punctuation can each eliminate choices.
- The final pair still needs a careful recheck.
- Strong test takers do not guess early; they narrow the field step by step.

## Summary

Elimination strategy is a practical way to solve sentence completion quickly and accurately. Start with the sentence frame. Remove choices that clash with meaning. Remove choices that fail grammar. Remove choices that sound wrong for the tone. Remove choices that point the wrong way logically. Then compare the final pair carefully. The more disciplined your elimination, the less you have to rely on luck.
