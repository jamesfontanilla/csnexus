# Grammar Fit

## Explanations

### Introduction

Grammar fit means the blank must match the sentence's grammar skeleton. In sentence completion, that often matters before meaning does. A choice can be related in meaning and still be wrong if it breaks subject-verb agreement, pronoun case, tense, parallel structure, or a fixed word pattern.

The safest way to approach grammar-fit items is to read the whole sentence, identify the blank's grammatical role, and test which choice keeps the sentence smooth and correct.

### Why Grammar Fit Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can handle formal English accurately. That matters in government work because memos, notices, reports, and instructions all depend on clear grammar.

Grammar-fit questions test whether you can:

- match a verb to its subject
- choose the correct pronoun form
- keep tense consistent
- keep lists and paired structures parallel
- use articles, prepositions, and comparisons correctly

The exam is not only asking whether you know English words. It is checking whether you can build a sentence that actually works.

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

Examinees often make the same errors:

- choosing a word that sounds right but does not fit the grammar
- matching the blank to a nearby noun instead of the real subject
- using the wrong pronoun case after a verb or preposition
- shifting tense without a good reason
- mixing different forms in a list
- ignoring articles, prepositions, and comparison markers

These mistakes happen because sentence completion feels like vocabulary work at first. Grammar fit reminds you that structure matters just as much.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the grammatical job of the blank
- apply subject-verb agreement correctly
- choose the right pronoun form
- keep verb tense consistent
- recognize parallel structures and fix mismatched forms
- choose the best article, preposition, or comparison form

## Subject-Verb Agreement

### Overview

The subject tells the verb whether to act like singular or plural. In sentence completion, that is often the first thing you should check. If the subject is singular, the verb should usually be singular. If the subject is plural, the verb should usually be plural.

### Core Principle

Find the real subject of the sentence, not the nearest noun. Prepositional phrases, extra descriptions, and long modifiers can hide the true subject. Once you find it, match the verb to that subject.

### Visualization

| Subject type | Example | Verb shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| singular | the officer | `is`, `reviews`, `checks` |
| plural | the officers | `are`, `review`, `check` |
| singular with a phrase | the list of forms | singular verb |
| plural with a phrase | the forms on the desk | plural verb |

### Common Mistakes

One common trap is the phrase between the subject and the verb. For example, in "The list of forms on the desk ___," the subject is `list`, not `forms`.

Another trap is a compound structure such as `either...or` or `neither...nor`. In those items, the nearer subject often controls the verb.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

"The list of forms ____ on the desk."

Options:

1. is
2. are
3. were
4. have

Reasoning:

- the real subject is `list`, which is singular
- the verb must also be singular
- `is` fits
- `are` and `were` are plural
- `have` does not fit the structure

Answer: **is**

### Quick Check

Question: What should you find first in a subject-verb item
Choices:
- The subject controls agreement
- nearest noun
- plural verb
Answer: The subject controls agreement
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: the nearest noun is always the subject
Choices:
- nearest noun
- The subject controls agreement
- plural verb
Answer: The subject controls agreement
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which is correct for a singular subject, `is` or `are`

- nearest noun

Answer: The subject controls agreement
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Subject-verb questions become easier when you stop listening to the nearest noun and start listening to the grammatical subject. Find the subject first, then match the verb.

## Pronoun Fit and Case

### Overview

Pronouns must fit their role in the sentence. A pronoun used as a subject should have subject case. A pronoun after a verb or preposition should usually have object case. Possessive pronouns must show ownership, not action.

### Core Principle

Use the pronoun form that matches the job:

- subject: `I`, `he`, `she`, `we`, `they`
- object: `me`, `him`, `her`, `us`, `them`
- possessive determiner: `my`, `his`, `her`, `our`, `their`
- possessive pronoun: `mine`, `his`, `hers`, `ours`, `theirs`
- reflexive: `myself`, `himself`, `herself`, `ourselves`, `themselves`

### Visualization

| Sentence slot | Correct form example | Wrong form trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before the verb | `She` completed the form. | `Her` completed the form. |
| after a verb | The director praised `her`. | The director praised `she`. |
| after a preposition | between you and `me` | between you and `I` |
| before a noun | `their` report | `theirs` report |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest trap is the old phrase `between you and I`. After a preposition like `between`, the object form is needed.

Another trap is confusing possessive determiners with possessive pronouns. `Their report` is correct before a noun, but `The report is theirs` is correct when the pronoun stands alone.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

"The supervisor thanked ____ after the meeting."

Options:

1. he
2. him
3. his
4. himself

Reasoning:

- the blank comes after the verb `thanked`
- that position needs an object pronoun
- `him` fits
- `he` is subject form
- `his` is possessive
- `himself` is reflexive and does not fit the meaning

Answer: **him**

### Quick Check

Question: Which form usually comes after a verb like `thanked`
Choices:
- The subject controls agreement
- nearest noun
- plural verb
Answer: The subject controls agreement
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which is correct: `between you and me` or `between you and I`
Choices:
- `between you and me`
- The subject controls agreement
- `between you and I`
Answer: The subject controls agreement
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: `their` and `theirs` do the same job in every sentence
Choices:
- nearest noun
- plural verb
- The subject controls agreement
Answer: The subject controls agreement
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Pronoun questions are easier when you ask one simple question: what job is the pronoun doing? Once you know the job, the case usually follows.

## Verb Tense Consistency

### Overview

Verb tense shows time. In sentence completion, the tense must fit the time clue in the sentence and stay consistent with the rest of the statement.

### Core Principle

Look for time markers such as:

- `yesterday`, `last week`, `ago`
- `every day`, `usually`, `always`
- `now`, `currently`, `at present`
- `by the time`, `before`, `after`, `since`
- `tomorrow`, `next week`, `by the end of`

The time clue tells you which tense belongs in the blank.

### Visualization

| Time clue | Usual tense | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| habitual action | simple present | `checks` |
| completed past action | simple past | `checked` |
| action already completed before another past action | past perfect | `had checked` |
| action continuing from the past to now | present perfect | `has checked` |
| future action | future tense | `will check` |

### Common Mistakes

Some examinees switch tense halfway through a sentence without noticing the time clue. That makes the sentence inconsistent.

Another mistake is using the most familiar form of a verb instead of the form that matches the time marker.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

"Yesterday, the clerk ____ the records before lunch."

Options:

1. checks
2. checked
3. will check
4. has checked

Reasoning:

- `Yesterday` points to the past
- the action happened before lunch, still in the past
- `checked` fits
- `checks` is present tense
- `will check` is future tense
- `has checked` does not match the past time clue as well

Answer: **checked**

### Quick Check

Question: Which time clue usually signals simple past
Choices:
- yesterday
- nearest noun
- plural verb
Answer: yesterday
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What does `by the time` often suggest
Choices:
- nearest noun
- an earlier completed action
- plural verb
Answer: an earlier completed action
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: tense should stay consistent unless the time changes
Choices:
- False
- It depends
- True
Answer: True
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Tense fit is a time-line problem. Once you read the time clue correctly, the verb form becomes much easier to choose.

## Parallelism

### Overview

Parallelism means matching grammatical shape. If a sentence lists words, phrases, or clauses that share the same role, they should look alike. Sentence completion often tests whether you can keep that balance.

### Core Principle

When ideas are equal, their grammar should be equal too. That means nouns should match nouns, verbs should match verbs, infinitives should match infinitives, and clauses should match clauses.

### Visualization

| Parallel type | Good form | Bad form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nouns | accuracy, clarity, and fairness | accuracy, clear, and fairness |
| verbs | file, stamp, and sign | file, stamping, and sign |
| infinitives | to read, to write, and to summarize | to read, writing, and to summarize |
| clauses | because it was clear, because it was brief, and because it was accurate | because it was clear, brief, and because it was accurate |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is mixing forms inside a list. Another mistake is forgetting that correlative pairs such as `either...or` and `not only...but also` need matching structures on both sides.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

"The clerk had to file the papers, stamp the forms, and ____ the log."

Options:

1. archive
2. archived
3. archiving
4. archive's

Reasoning:

- the list uses base verbs after `to`
- `archive` matches the other verbs
- `archived` is past tense
- `archiving` is a participle
- `archive's` is possessive and does not fit

Answer: **archive**

### Quick Check

Question: What should a parallel list have
Choices:
- matching forms
- nearest noun
- plural verb
Answer: matching forms
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which pair needs balance on both sides
Choices:
- nearest noun
- parallel structure
- plural verb
Answer: parallel structure
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: nouns and verbs can freely mix in the same list
Choices:
- True
- It depends
- False
Answer: False
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Parallelism is about balance. If one item in the list looks different, that is often the wrong choice.

## Modifiers, Articles, and Prepositions

### Overview

Some blanks are solved by natural word fit rather than big grammar rules. Articles, prepositions, adverbs, and adjective forms must still be correct. These items often feel small, but they can decide the whole sentence.

### Core Principle

Use the form that matches the word around it:

- `a` / `an` depends on sound
- `the` points to something specific
- prepositions show relationship
- adjectives describe nouns
- adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs

### Visualization

| Choice type | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| article | introduces a noun | `a report`, `an officer`, `the report` |
| preposition | shows relationship | `in the folder`, `on the desk`, `at noon` |
| adjective | describes a noun | `careful officer` |
| adverb | describes an action | `carefully completed` |

### Common Mistakes

One common mistake is writing `an university` instead of `a university`. The choice depends on sound, not spelling.

Another mistake is choosing a preposition by translation alone. Many prepositions are fixed by common usage, not by literal meaning.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

"She is ____ honest officer."

Options:

1. a
2. an
3. the
4. no article

Reasoning:

- `honest` starts with a vowel sound
- `an` fits before vowel sounds
- `a` is used before consonant sounds
- `the` is specific, but the sentence needs an indefinite article
- no article does not fit

Answer: **an**

### Quick Check

Question: What matters more for `a` or `an`, spelling or sound
Choices:
- sound
- nearest noun
- plural verb
Answer: sound
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which is correct: `interested in` or `interested on`
Choices:
- `interested on`
- interested in
- nearest noun
Answer: interested in
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: an adverb usually describes a noun
Choices:
- nearest noun
- plural verb
- The root carries the core meaning
Answer: The root carries the core meaning
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Small words are not small mistakes. Articles, prepositions, and modifiers often sound obvious only after you know the rule.

## Comparison and Mixed Grammar Checks

### Overview

Comparison questions test symmetry. Mixed grammar checks combine more than one rule, so you have to slow down and verify the whole sentence, not just one word.

### Core Principle

Use the correct comparison pattern:

- `than` compares two things
- `as...as` keeps two parts equal
- `between` is for two
- `among` is for three or more
- `fewer` usually goes with countable nouns
- `less` usually goes with uncountable nouns

### Visualization

| Pattern | Correct use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `than` | comparison | `clearer than` |
| `as...as` | equal comparison | `as clear as` |
| `between` | two items | `between the two reports` |
| `among` | three or more items | `among the reports` |
| `fewer` | countable items | `fewer errors` |
| `less` | uncountable quantity | `less time` |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is using `less` with countable nouns like `errors` or `books`. Another mistake is breaking symmetry by making only one side of a comparison grammatical.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which word best completes the sentence below?

"The second report was ____ than the first."

Options:

1. clearer
2. clear
3. clearest
4. clearly

Reasoning:

- the sentence compares two reports
- a comparative form is needed
- `clearer` fits
- `clear` is not a comparison
- `clearest` is superlative, used for three or more
- `clearly` is an adverb

Answer: **clearer**

### Quick Check

Question: Which word is used for a comparison between two things
Choices:
- between
- nearest noun
- plural verb
Answer: between
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which is correct: `fewer errors` or `less errors`
Choices:
- `less errors`
- fewer errors
- nearest noun
Answer: fewer errors
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: `between` is usually used for more than two items
Choices:
- True
- It depends
- False
Answer: False
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Comparison items reward careful symmetry. Mixed items reward careful checking. In both cases, the sentence has to sound complete and balanced.

1. had reviewed

- `By the time` shows one action finished before another past action

## Key Takeaways

- Grammar fit means the blank must match the sentence structure.
- Subject-verb agreement, pronoun case, tense, parallelism, articles, prepositions, and comparisons are the main traps.
- The nearest noun is not always the real subject.
- A pronoun must match its job in the sentence.
- Lists must stay parallel.
- Small grammar words can decide the answer.

## Summary

Grammar fit is the part of sentence completion that tests structure first. The sentence may give you a meaning clue, but the final choice still has to obey grammar. Once you learn to check subject, pronoun, tense, parallelism, and word pattern in order, the blank becomes much easier to solve.

If you remember only one thing from this lesson, remember this: the best answer is the one that keeps the sentence both grammatical and natural.
