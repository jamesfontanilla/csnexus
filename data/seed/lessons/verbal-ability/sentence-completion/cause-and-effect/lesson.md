# Cause and Effect

## Explanations

### Introduction

Cause and effect shows a reason and its result. One idea explains why something happened. The other idea shows what happened because of it. In sentence completion, the blank may need a reason connector, a result connector, or a phrase that points to the cause.

This lesson stays close to the way the CSE actually tests the skill. The exam does not only ask whether you know a connector. It asks whether you can read the direction of the sentence. If the sentence is asking for a reason, choose a reason signal. If the sentence is asking for a result, choose a result signal. If the blank needs a phrase instead of a full clause, the grammar itself will tell you.

Think of cause and effect as a chain. The cause comes first in logic, but not always first in the sentence. The effect may appear before the cause, and the connector has to make that relationship clear.

### Why Cause and Effect Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read formal English accurately and quickly. That matters in government work because reports, notices, memos, and instructions often explain why a decision was made or what happened after an event.

Cause-and-effect questions test whether you can:

- identify a reason or a result
- match a connector to the sentence's direction
- tell a cause clause from a cause phrase
- recognize punctuation clues in formal result connectors
- avoid confusing cause with time, purpose, or sequence

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

Examinees often make these mistakes:

- choosing a result connector when the sentence needs a reason
- choosing a reason connector when the sentence needs a result
- using a clause connector where a phrase connector is needed
- confusing `since` as cause with `since` as time
- confusing `so` as result with `so that` as purpose
- ignoring punctuation around `therefore`, `thus`, `hence`, and `consequently`

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the cause and the effect in a sentence
- choose connectors for reasons and results
- distinguish clause-level and phrase-level cause signals
- use punctuation to narrow the correct answer
- avoid time and purpose traps
- explain why distractors fail

## What Cause and Effect Means

### Overview

Cause and effect is a relationship between events or ideas. The cause is the reason. The effect is the outcome. In sentence completion, the blank must fit that relationship exactly.

### Core Principle

Ask two questions:

- What happened first in logic?
- Is the blank showing why something happened or what happened because of it?

### Visualization

| Relationship | What it does | Example signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cause | explains why something happened | `because`, `since`, `for` |
| effect | shows the result | `so`, `therefore`, `thus`, `hence`, `consequently` |
| cause phrase | gives a noun phrase reason | `because of`, `due to`, `owing to`, `on account of` |
| purpose | shows an intended result | `so that`, `in order to` |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is treating every connector as if it works both ways. It does not. A reason connector and a result connector point in opposite directions.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The meeting was postponed, ____ the roads were flooded."

Options:

1. because
2. therefore
3. so
4. unless

Reasoning:

- the second clause gives the reason
- the blank needs a reason connector
- `because` fits the explanatory relationship
- `therefore` and `so` show result, not reason
- `unless` shows condition

Answer: **because**

### Quick Check

Question: What is the cause
Choices:
- The reason
- contrast
- result
Answer: The reason
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: What is the effect
Choices:
- contrast
- The result
- result
Answer: The result
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: cause and effect always appear in that order in the sentence
Choices:
- True
- It depends
- False
Answer: False
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

The blank is not asking for a word that sounds formal. It is asking for the direction of the relationship.

## Reason Connectors

### Overview

Reason connectors explain why something happened. The most common ones in sentence completion are `because`, `since`, `as`, and `for`.

### Core Principle

Use a reason connector when the blank introduces the explanation for an event or condition.

- `because` is the clearest and most direct reason connector
- `since` can mean `because` when it introduces a reason
- `as` can also mean `because` in formal writing, but the context must be clear
- `for` is a formal reason connector that usually joins two independent clauses

### Visualization

| Connector | Common use | Grammar clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `because` | direct reason | usually followed by a clause |
| `since` | reason or time | check the time clue carefully |
| `as` | reason in formal writing | usually followed by a clause |
| `for` | explanatory reason | usually joins independent clauses |

### Common Mistakes

The most common error is choosing `since` when the sentence is clearly about time. Another error is using `for` in a sentence that does not have two independent clauses.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The office opened a second counter ____ the queue was long."

Options:

1. because
2. therefore
3. so
4. unless

Reasoning:

- the second clause explains why the office acted
- the blank needs a reason connector
- `because` fits the clause
- `therefore` and `so` show result
- `unless` shows condition

Answer: **because**

### Quick Check

Question: Which connector is the safest direct reason word
Choices:
- because
- contrast
- result
Answer: because
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which connector may also mean time
Choices:
- contrast
- since
- result
Answer: since
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which connector is formal and often joins two independent clauses
Choices:
- contrast
- result
- for
Answer: for
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Reason connectors explain the cause. If the sentence is showing why, look here first.

## Result Connectors

### Overview

Result connectors show what happened because of the cause. Common result connectors include `so`, `therefore`, `thus`, `hence`, `consequently`, and `as a result`.

### Core Principle

Use a result connector when the blank introduces the outcome or consequence.

- `so` is the most direct result connector
- `therefore`, `thus`, `hence`, and `consequently` are more formal
- `as a result` often appears as a phrase showing consequence

### Visualization

| Connector | Common use | Punctuation clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `so` | direct result | often follows a comma |
| `therefore` | formal conclusion | often follows a semicolon or period, then a comma |
| `thus` | formal result | often follows a semicolon or period, then a comma |
| `hence` | formal result | often follows a semicolon or period, then a comma |
| `consequently` | formal consequence | often follows a semicolon or period, then a comma |
| `as a result` | result phrase | often follows a semicolon or period, then a comma |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is using a reason connector when the sentence is asking for the outcome. Another mistake is ignoring punctuation before a conjunctive adverb like `therefore`.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The queue was long, ____ the office opened a second counter."

Options:

1. so
2. because
3. unless
4. for

Reasoning:

- the second clause shows the result
- the blank needs a result connector
- `so` fits the cause-to-effect flow
- `because` would reverse the direction
- `unless` shows condition
- `for` gives a reason, not a result

Answer: **so**

### Quick Check

Question: Which connector usually shows a direct result
Choices:
- so
- contrast
- result
Answer: so
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which connector often appears after a semicolon
Choices:
- contrast
- therefore
- result
Answer: therefore
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: `therefore` is usually followed by a comma in formal writing
Choices:
- False
- It depends
- True
Answer: True
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Result connectors point to the outcome. They tell the reader what followed from the cause.

## Phrase Versus Clause Clues

### Overview

Some cause signals need a full clause. Others need only a noun phrase. That difference matters a lot in the CSE because the wrong connector may be logically right but grammatically impossible.

### Core Principle

If the blank is followed by a subject and verb, a clause connector is usually needed. If it is followed by a noun phrase, a phrase connector is usually needed.

- clause connectors: `because`, `since`, `as`, `for`
- phrase connectors: `because of`, `due to`, `owing to`, `on account of`

### Visualization

| Form | What follows it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `because` | clause | `because the roads were flooded` |
| `since` | clause | `since the roads were flooded` |
| `for` | clause | `for the roads were flooded` |
| `because of` | noun phrase | `because of the flooded roads` |
| `due to` | noun phrase | `due to the flooded roads` |
| `owing to` | noun phrase | `owing to the flooded roads` |
| `on account of` | noun phrase | `on account of the flooded roads` |

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often write `because of` before a full clause, which breaks the grammar. Another mistake is using `because` before a noun phrase when a phrase connector is needed.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"____ the flooded roads, the meeting was postponed."

Options:

1. because of
2. because
3. so
4. therefore

Reasoning:

- the blank is followed by a noun phrase
- `because of` fits the phrase pattern
- `because` would need a full clause after it
- `so` and `therefore` show result, not cause phrase

Answer: **because of**

### Quick Check

Question: Which connector usually takes a noun phrase
Choices:
- because of
- contrast
- result
Answer: because of
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which connector usually takes a full clause
Choices:
- contrast
- because
- result
Answer: because
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: `because of the rain` is a phrase-level cause
Choices:
- False
- It depends
- True
Answer: True
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

The grammar around the blank often decides the answer before the meaning does.

## Since, Time, and Purpose Traps

### Overview

Some connectors can do more than one job. `Since` can mean reason or time. `As` can also be causal or temporal. `So` can show result, but `so that` shows purpose.

### Core Principle

Use the timeline and the sentence logic to separate these meanings.

- `since` as reason: explains why something happened
- `since` as time: means from a point in the past until now
- `as` as reason: explains why something happened
- `as` as time: means while or when
- `so` as result: shows consequence
- `so that` as purpose: shows intention or goal

### Visualization

| Word or phrase | Possible meaning | How to tell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `since` | reason or time | check whether the sentence is about cause or a time span |
| `as` | reason or time | use context to identify the meaning |
| `so` | result | the second idea is the consequence |
| `so that` | purpose | the second idea is the intended goal |

### Common Mistakes

The most dangerous trap is choosing `since` when the sentence is really talking about time. Another trap is choosing `so` when the sentence really means intention, not result.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The office opened a second counter ____ the queue was long."

Options:

1. since
2. because of
3. therefore
4. unless

Reasoning:

- the blank introduces the reason
- `since` fits the causal clause
- `because of` would need a noun phrase, not a clause
- `therefore` shows result
- `unless` shows condition

Answer: **since**

### Quick Check

Question: Which word can mean either reason or time
Choices:
- since
- contrast
- result
Answer: since
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: Which phrase shows purpose, not result
Choices:
- contrast
- so that
- result
Answer: so that
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

Question: True or false: `so that` and `so` mean the same thing
Choices:
- True
- It depends
- False
Answer: False
Rationale: The sentence relationship determines the correct choice.

### Key Insight

Ambiguous words are not impossible. They just demand more careful reading.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The roads were flooded, ____ the meeting was postponed."

Answer: **so**

Reasoning: The second clause is the result of the first clause.

### Example 2

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"____ the roads were flooded, the meeting was postponed."

Answer: **because**

Reasoning: The blank introduces the reason for the postponement.

### Example 3

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The meeting was postponed, ____ the roads were flooded."

Answer: **for**

Reasoning: The sentence uses a formal explanatory connector between two independent clauses.

### Example 4

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"____ the flooded roads, the meeting was postponed."

Answer: **because of**

Reasoning: The blank is followed by a noun phrase, so a phrase-level cause connector is needed.

### Example 5

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"The roads were flooded; ____, the meeting was postponed."

Answer: **therefore**

Reasoning: The semicolon plus the conclusion calls for a formal result connector.

### Example 6

Question: Which connector best completes the sentence below?

"Since the queue was long, the office opened a second counter."

Answer: **since**

Reasoning: The sentence clearly uses since as a reason connector, not a time marker.







## Key Takeaways

- Cause explains why something happened.
- Effect shows what happened because of the cause.
- `Because`, `since`, and `for` can introduce reasons.
- `Because of`, `due to`, `owing to`, and `on account of` usually introduce noun-phrase causes.
- `So`, `therefore`, `thus`, `hence`, and `consequently` show results.
- `Since` can also mean time, so context matters.
- `So that` shows purpose, not simple result.

## Summary

Cause-and-effect items ask you to follow the logic of the sentence. The blank may need a reason, a result, or a phrase that names the cause. If you can read the direction of the idea, the answer becomes much easier.

The main habit is simple: read the sentence's logic first, then test the grammar around the blank. That is how you avoid the most common traps and choose the connector that truly fits.
