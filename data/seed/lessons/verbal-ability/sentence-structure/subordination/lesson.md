# Subordination

## Explanations

### Introduction

Subordination joins an independent clause with a dependent clause. In CSE sentence-structure items, subordination is tested because it shows whether you can spot the clause that cannot stand alone, choose the right subordinating conjunction, and punctuate the sentence correctly when the dependent clause comes first.

### Why Subordination Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read sentence relationships with precision. Subordination is tested because it shows whether you can:

- identify dependent clauses quickly
- distinguish complex sentences from compound and simple sentences
- choose the subordinating conjunction that matches the relationship
- punctuate introductory dependent clauses correctly
- recognize relative clauses and clause functions

### Common Mistakes

Candidates often make these mistakes:

- thinking a dependent clause is complete because it has a subject and verb
- confusing subordinating conjunctions with coordinating conjunctions
- leaving out the comma after an introductory dependent clause
- inserting a comma before a dependent clause that follows the main clause
- treating relative clauses as separate sentences

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify independent and dependent clauses
- recognize common subordinating conjunctions
- choose the right conjunction for time, cause, condition, contrast, purpose, or place
- punctuate subordinate clauses correctly
- tell subordination apart from coordination

## What Subordination Means

### Overview

Subordination connects ideas of unequal rank. The dependent clause adds information, but it cannot stand alone as a sentence.

### Core Principle

Ask whether the clause can stand by itself.

- if it can stand alone, it is independent
- if it depends on another clause, it is subordinate

### Visualization

| Clause type | Example | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dependent clause | `Because the roads were flooded` | cannot stand alone |
| independent clause | `The bus arrived late.` | can stand alone |
| complex sentence | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` | combines both |

### Common Mistakes

Do not assume a clause is complete just because it has a subject and a verb. A dependent clause still needs an independent clause to finish the thought.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Is `Because the roads were flooded` a complete sentence?

Reasoning:

- the clause has a subject and a verb
- but it begins with `because`
- it leaves the thought unfinished

Answer: **No. It is a dependent clause.**

### Key Insight

Subordination makes one idea rely on another.

### Quick Check

Question: Which clause cannot stand alone?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded
- The bus arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded
Rationale: The clause is dependent and needs a main clause.

Question: Which sentence is complex?
Choices:
- The bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
Rationale: One independent clause and one dependent clause form a complex sentence.

Question: Which option shows subordination?
Choices:
- Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
- The office was busy, and the clerk stayed calm.
- The office was busy.
Answer: Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
Rationale: The dependent clause begins with a subordinating conjunction.

## Independent and Dependent Clauses

### Overview

An independent clause is a complete thought. A dependent clause has a subject and a verb, but it cannot stand alone. In CSE items, this difference often decides whether a sentence is simple, compound, or complex.

### Core Principle

Use this test:

- complete thought = independent clause
- incomplete thought = dependent clause

### Visualization

| Example | Clause type | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `The assistant sent the notice.` | independent | complete by itself |
| `After the meeting ended` | dependent | needs a main clause |
| `After the meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.` | complex | dependent + independent |

### Common Mistakes

Do not label a clause independent only because it has a subject and a verb. If the thought is unfinished, it is still dependent.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: In `After the meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.`, which part is dependent?

Reasoning:

- `After the meeting ended` cannot stand alone
- `the assistant sent the notice` can stand alone
- the first part is subordinate

Answer: **After the meeting ended**

### Key Insight

The ability to stand alone is what makes a clause independent.

### Quick Check

Question: Which clause is independent?
Choices:
- After the meeting ended
- The assistant sent the notice.
- After the meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.
Answer: The assistant sent the notice.
Rationale: It expresses a complete thought on its own.

Question: Which sentence includes a dependent clause?
Choices:
- The assistant sent the notice.
- After the meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.
- The assistant sent the notice, and the clerk archived the notes.
Answer: After the meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.
Rationale: The introductory clause depends on the main clause.

Question: Which option is a fragment?
Choices:
- After the meeting ended.
- The assistant sent the notice.
- The assistant sent the notice, and the clerk archived the notes.
Answer: After the meeting ended.
Rationale: The dependent clause is incomplete by itself.

## Subordinating Conjunctions

### Overview

Subordinating conjunctions begin dependent clauses. They show relationships such as cause, contrast, condition, time, purpose, or place. Common examples include `because`, `although`, `if`, `unless`, `after`, `before`, `when`, `until`, `where`, and `so that`.

### Core Principle

Choose the conjunction that matches the relationship between the ideas.

- cause: `because`, `since`
- contrast: `although`, `though`
- condition: `if`, `unless`
- time: `after`, `before`, `when`, `until`
- purpose: `so that`
- place: `where`

### Visualization

| Relationship | Example |
| --- | --- |
| cause | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` |
| contrast | `Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.` |
| condition | `Unless the report is ready, do not send it.` |
| time | `After the meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.` |
| purpose | `So that the bridge could open safely, the crew worked carefully.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not swap a subordinating conjunction for a coordinating conjunction. Also, do not choose a conjunction only because it is familiar; it must match the relationship in the sentence.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which conjunction best fits `The crew worked carefully ___ the bridge could open safely.`?

Reasoning:

- the second idea shows purpose
- `so that` expresses purpose
- the sentence becomes a clear complex sentence

Answer: **so that**

### Key Insight

The conjunction is not just a connector. It tells the reader how the two clauses relate.

### Quick Check

Question: Which word is the subordinating conjunction in `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.`?
Choices:
- because
- and
- but
Answer: because
Rationale: `Because` introduces the dependent clause.

Question: Which conjunction shows condition?
Choices:
- if
- and
- so
Answer: if
Rationale: `If` introduces a condition.

Question: Which conjunction shows contrast?
Choices:
- although
- because
- until
Answer: although
Rationale: `Although` introduces a contrasting idea.

## Introductory Clauses and Punctuation

### Overview

When a dependent clause comes first, put a comma after it. When the dependent clause follows the main clause, the comma is usually omitted. This is one of the most common punctuation patterns tested in CSE sentence-structure items.

### Core Principle

Use this pattern:

- dependent clause first = comma + independent clause
- independent clause first = usually no comma before the dependent clause

### Visualization

| Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- |
| correct introductory clause | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` |
| correct postposed clause | `The bus arrived late because the roads were flooded.` |
| comma splice | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, the passengers waited quietly.` |
| fused sentence | `Because the roads were flooded the bus arrived late.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not forget the comma after an introductory dependent clause. Do not place a comma before a dependent clause that follows the main clause unless the sentence needs one for a separate reason.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which revision is punctuated correctly?

`The assistant sent the notice after the meeting ended.`

Reasoning:

- the main clause comes first
- the dependent clause follows
- no comma is needed before `after`

Answer: **The assistant sent the notice after the meeting ended.**

### Key Insight

Clause order changes punctuation.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded the bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, the passengers waited quietly.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
Rationale: An introductory dependent clause should be followed by a comma.

Question: Which sentence does not need a comma before the dependent clause?
Choices:
- The bus arrived late because the roads were flooded.
- The bus arrived late, because the roads were flooded.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
Answer: The bus arrived late because the roads were flooded.
Rationale: The dependent clause follows the independent clause.

Question: Which punctuation belongs after an introductory dependent clause?
Choices:
- comma
- semicolon
- apostrophe
Answer: comma
Rationale: The introductory clause should be separated from the main clause with a comma.

## Clause Functions and Relative Pronouns

### Overview

Subordinate clauses can function as adverb clauses, adjective clauses, or noun clauses. Relative words such as `who`, `whom`, `whose`, `that`, `where`, and `when` often introduce adjective clauses.

### Core Principle

Ask what the clause does in the sentence.

- adverb clause = modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb
- adjective clause = describes a noun or pronoun
- noun clause = acts like a noun

### Visualization

| Clause function | Example | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| adverb clause | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` | explains why |
| adjective clause | `The bus that arrived late left the station.` | describes bus |
| noun clause | `I know that the memo is ready.` | acts as object |

### Common Mistakes

Do not treat every `that` clause the same way. In one sentence it may introduce a noun clause, and in another it may introduce an adjective clause.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: What does `who stayed calm` do in `The clerk who stayed calm answered the phone.`?

Reasoning:

- the clause describes `the clerk`
- it acts like an adjective
- it is a subordinate clause

Answer: **adjective clause**

### Key Insight

The function of the clause tells you why it is subordinate.

### Quick Check

Question: Which word introduces the relative clause in `The bus that arrived late left the station.`?
Choices:
- that
- because
- and
Answer: that
Rationale: `That` introduces the adjective clause.

Question: What is the function of `that the memo is ready` in `I know that the memo is ready.`?
Choices:
- noun clause
- adjective clause
- independent clause
Answer: noun clause
Rationale: The clause acts as the object of `know`.

Question: Which sentence uses an adjective clause?
Choices:
- The gardener who watered the plants left early.
- Because the gardener was late, the plants needed water.
- The gardener was late, and the plants needed water.
Answer: The gardener who watered the plants left early.
Rationale: The clause describes the noun `gardener`.

## Subordination Versus Coordination

### Overview

Subordination makes one idea depend on another. Coordination keeps ideas at the same level. In CSE sentence-structure items, you must notice whether the sentence is complex, compound, or compound-complex.

### Core Principle

Use this test:

- equal ideas = coordination
- dependent plus independent = subordination
- both together = compound-complex

### Visualization

| Structure | Example | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| complex | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` | dependent + independent |
| compound | `The roads were flooded, so the bus arrived late.` | equal clauses |
| compound-complex | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.` | both kinds |

### Common Mistakes

Do not call a sentence compound just because it has `and` or `so`. If a dependent clause is present, the structure may be complex or compound-complex instead.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which sentence is compound-complex?

Reasoning:

- one clause is dependent
- two clauses are independent
- both subordination and coordination appear together

Answer: **Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.**

### Key Insight

Sentence type depends on clause relationships, not on length.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence is complex rather than compound?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, so the bus arrived late.
- The bus arrived late.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
Rationale: The first clause is dependent, so the sentence is complex.

Question: Which sentence is compound-complex?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
- The bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
Rationale: The sentence has both a dependent clause and two independent clauses.

Question: Which sentence keeps two ideas at different grammatical levels?
Choices:
- Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
- The office was busy, but the clerk stayed calm.
- The office was busy.
Answer: Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
Rationale: The first clause depends on the second, so the ideas are not equal.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` is a complex sentence. The dependent clause begins with `because`, so it cannot stand alone.

### Example 2

`The bus arrived late because the roads were flooded.` is also complex. The dependent clause comes after the main clause, so no comma is needed.

### Example 3

`The bus that arrived late left the station.` uses a relative clause. The clause `that arrived late` describes `bus`.

### Example 4

`I know that the memo is ready.` contains a noun clause. The clause `that the memo is ready` works as the object of `know`.

## Key Takeaways

- A dependent clause cannot stand alone.
- Subordinating conjunctions show relationships such as cause, contrast, time, condition, purpose, and place.
- Introductory dependent clauses usually take a comma after them.
- Dependent clauses that follow the main clause usually do not need a comma.
- Relative and noun clauses are also forms of subordination.
- Coordination and subordination are different structural relationships.

## Summary

Subordination is one of the most important sentence-structure patterns in the Civil Service Examination. It tests whether you can identify dependent clauses, choose the right subordinating conjunction, and punctuate complex sentences correctly. If you first ask whether the clause can stand alone, the structure becomes much easier to classify.
