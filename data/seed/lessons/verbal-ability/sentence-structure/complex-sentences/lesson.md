# Complex Sentences

## Explanations

### Introduction

A complex sentence contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. In CSE sentence-structure items, complex sentences test whether you can tell a complete thought from an incomplete one, recognize subordinating conjunctions, and punctuate an introductory dependent clause correctly.

### Why Complex Sentences Are Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read beyond the surface of a sentence. Complex sentences are tested because they show whether you can:

- identify a dependent clause quickly
- tell a complex sentence from a simple, compound, or compound-complex sentence
- recognize common subordinating conjunctions
- place a comma after an introductory dependent clause
- repair a fragment by attaching it to a main clause

### Common Mistakes

Candidates often make these mistakes:

- treating any sentence that starts with `because`, `although`, or `when` as complete by itself
- forgetting the comma after an opening dependent clause
- confusing a complex sentence with a compound sentence
- reading only the first clause and missing the main clause
- choosing a conjunction that shows the wrong relationship

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify complex sentences quickly
- spot dependent clauses and main clauses
- recognize subordinating conjunctions
- punctuate introductory dependent clauses correctly
- distinguish complex sentences from nearby sentence types

## What Makes a Sentence Complex

### Overview

A complex sentence has one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses. The dependent clause cannot stand alone, so it must attach to a main clause to complete the thought.

### Core Principle

Count the independent clauses first.

If there is one complete thought and one incomplete thought, the sentence is complex.

### Visualization

| Sentence type | Clause pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| simple sentence | 1 independent clause | `The clerk filed the memo.` |
| compound sentence | 2 independent clauses | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` |
| complex sentence | 1 independent + 1 dependent clause | `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.` |
| compound-complex sentence | 2 independent + 1 dependent clause | `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized, and the supervisor helped.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not call a sentence complex just because it has more than one verb. Also, do not call it complex just because it begins with a dependent marker. The sentence still needs one complete clause and one dependent clause.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Is `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.` a complex sentence?

Reasoning:

- `Because the memo was late` is dependent.
- `the clerk apologized` is independent.
- The sentence has one dependent clause and one independent clause.

Answer: **Yes. It is a complex sentence.**

### Key Insight

Complex means one independent clause plus at least one dependent clause.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence is complex?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo.
- Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Answer: Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
Rationale: It has one dependent clause and one independent clause.

Question: How many independent clauses are in `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.`?
Choices:
- 1
- 2
- 3
Answer: 1
Rationale: The opening clause is dependent, so only one clause is independent.

Question: Which sentence is not complex?
Choices:
- Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
Answer: The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Rationale: That sentence is compound, not complex.

## Dependent Clauses and Subordinators

### Overview

A dependent clause has a subject and a verb, but it cannot stand alone. It often begins with a subordinating conjunction such as `because`, `although`, `when`, `if`, `while`, `since`, `before`, `after`, `until`, or `unless`.

### Core Principle

The subordinating conjunction makes one clause dependent. It also shows the relationship between the dependent clause and the main clause, such as cause, contrast, time, or condition.

### Visualization

| Marker | Common relationship | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `because` | reason | `Because the roads were flooded` |
| `although` | contrast | `Although the office was busy` |
| `when` | time | `When the meeting ended` |
| `if` | condition | `If the supervisor approves` |
| `while` | simultaneous action | `While the trainer explained the rule` |
| `since` | reason | `Since the memo was short` |
| `after` | time | `After the audit finished` |
| `before` | time | `Before the office opened` |
| `until` | time limit | `Until the printer is repaired` |
| `unless` | condition | `Unless the manager calls` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not confuse a dependent clause with a complete sentence. Do not confuse a subordinating conjunction with a coordinating conjunction. And do not assume that `because` or `although` can join two independent clauses by itself.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Why is `Because the roads were flooded` a dependent clause?

Reasoning:

- It has a subject and a verb.
- It begins with `because`.
- It does not finish the thought.

Answer: **It needs a main clause to complete the idea.**

### Key Insight

If a clause begins with a subordinating conjunction, expect it to depend on another clause.

### Quick Check

Question: Which option is the dependent clause?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded
- The bus arrived late.
- The bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded
Rationale: It starts with a subordinating conjunction and cannot stand alone.

Question: Which word most clearly signals a dependent clause?
Choices:
- because
- and
- comma
Answer: because
Rationale: `Because` is a subordinating conjunction.

Question: Which choice best shows a condition?
Choices:
- if the supervisor approves
- the supervisor approves
- the supervisor approved
Answer: if the supervisor approves
Rationale: `If` introduces a conditional dependent clause.

## Introductory Dependent Clauses

### Overview

When a dependent clause comes first in a complex sentence, it is usually followed by a comma before the main clause begins.

### Core Principle

Use this pattern:

dependent clause, main clause.

### Visualization

| Pattern | Correct example |
| --- | --- |
| dependent clause first | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` |
| dependent clause first | `When the meeting ended, the team filed out.` |
| dependent clause first | `If the supervisor approves, the office will print the memo.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not forget the comma after the opening dependent clause. Do not use a semicolon when the first part is not an independent clause. And do not place the comma before the subordinating conjunction.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which revision correctly fixes `Because the roads were flooded the bus arrived late.`?

Reasoning:

- The opening clause is dependent.
- A comma should separate it from the main clause.
- The correct revision is `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.`

Answer: **Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.**

### Key Insight

If the dependent clause starts the sentence, the comma usually comes right after it.

### Quick Check

Question: Which revision correctly punctuates the introductory dependent clause?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded the bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded; the bus arrived late.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
Rationale: An introductory dependent clause usually takes a comma after it.

Question: Which punctuation mark should appear after the introductory clause?
Choices:
- comma
- apostrophe
- colon
Answer: comma
Rationale: The dependent clause should be separated from the main clause by a comma.

Question: Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
Choices:
- When the meeting ended, the team filed out.
- When the meeting ended the team filed out.
- When, the meeting ended, the team filed out.
Answer: When the meeting ended, the team filed out.
Rationale: The comma belongs after the introductory dependent clause.

## Complex Sentences and Nearby Sentence Types

### Overview

Complex sentences are easy to confuse with simple, compound, and compound-complex sentences. The safest strategy is to count the independent clauses and then check whether a dependent clause is present.

### Core Principle

Use two questions:

1. How many independent clauses are there?
2. Is any clause dependent?

If there is one independent clause and one dependent clause, the sentence is complex.

### Visualization

| Sentence type | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| simple | 1 independent | `The bus arrived late.` |
| compound | 2 independent | `The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.` |
| complex | 1 independent + 1 dependent | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` |
| compound-complex | 2 independent + 1 dependent | `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not assume that a sentence is complex just because it starts with a subordinating conjunction. Also, do not confuse a compound-complex sentence with a plain complex sentence.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which sentence is compound-complex rather than complex?

Reasoning:

- `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` has one independent clause and one dependent clause.
- `Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.` has one dependent clause and two independent clauses.

Answer: **Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.**

### Key Insight

Count clauses before you trust the punctuation.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence is simple?
Choices:
- The bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.
Answer: The bus arrived late.
Rationale: It has one independent clause and no dependent clause.

Question: Which sentence is compound?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
Answer: The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.
Rationale: It has two independent clauses joined correctly.

Question: Which sentence is compound-complex?
Choices:
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, and the bus arrived late.
- Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late, and the passengers waited quietly.
Rationale: It has one dependent clause and two independent clauses.

## Revising Complex Sentences

### Overview

Complex-sentence items often ask you to revise a fragment, fix punctuation, or pick the sentence that already works. The best revision is the one that keeps the meaning clear and the clause relationship correct.

### Core Principle

To repair a complex sentence:

- attach a dependent clause to a main clause
- place a comma after an introductory dependent clause
- keep the subordinating conjunction that matches the relationship
- avoid turning the sentence into a run-on or a compound sentence by mistake

### Visualization

| Problem | Better fix |
| --- | --- |
| dependent clause alone | attach a main clause |
| missing comma | add a comma after the opening dependent clause |
| wrong relationship word | choose the correct subordinating conjunction |
| unclear clause boundary | separate the clauses correctly |

### Common Mistakes

Do not “fix” a complex sentence by changing it into a compound sentence if the original idea needs subordination. And do not leave a dependent clause hanging without a main clause.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which revision fixes `If the supervisor approves.`?

Reasoning:

- The original is only a dependent clause.
- It needs a main clause to finish the thought.
- `If the supervisor approves, the office will print the memo.` is a complete complex sentence.

Answer: **If the supervisor approves, the office will print the memo.**

### Key Insight

A complex sentence needs both parts working together, not one part standing alone.

### Quick Check

Question: Which revision correctly turns the fragment into a complex sentence?
Choices:
- If the supervisor approves, the office will print the memo.
- If the supervisor approves the office will print the memo.
- The office will print the memo, and the supervisor approves.
Answer: If the supervisor approves, the office will print the memo.
Rationale: The dependent clause is attached to a main clause and punctuated correctly.

Question: Which sentence needs no correction?
Choices:
- Since the memo was short, the branch sent a reply.
- Since the memo was short the branch sent a reply.
- Since the memo was short; the branch sent a reply.
Answer: Since the memo was short, the branch sent a reply.
Rationale: The dependent clause and comma placement are correct.

Question: Which clause should be added to complete the idea?
Choices:
- the team filed out
- because the meeting ended
- the meeting ended
Answer: the team filed out
Rationale: The dependent clause needs a main clause to become a complete sentence.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` is a complex sentence. The opening clause is dependent, and the main clause carries the complete thought.

### Example 2

`The report that the clerk revised was approved.` uses a restrictive clause. The sentence is still complex because it contains one independent clause and one dependent clause.

### Example 3

`If the supervisor approves, the office will print the memo.` is complex and correctly punctuated because the dependent clause comes first and takes a comma after it.

### Example 4

`Because the roads were flooded` is not a complete sentence. It is only a dependent clause, so it needs a main clause to finish the idea.

## Key Takeaways

- A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.
- A dependent clause has a subject and a verb but cannot stand alone.
- Subordinating conjunctions such as `because`, `although`, and `when` make a clause dependent.
- Introductory dependent clauses usually need a comma after them.
- Counting clauses is the fastest way to tell complex sentences from nearby sentence types.

## Summary

Complex sentences are one of the most important sentence-structure patterns in the Civil Service Examination. They test whether you can see how a dependent clause connects to a main clause, whether you can place the comma in the right position, and whether you can tell complex sentences apart from compound and compound-complex sentences. If you learn to spot the dependent marker first, the rest of the sentence usually becomes easier to read.
