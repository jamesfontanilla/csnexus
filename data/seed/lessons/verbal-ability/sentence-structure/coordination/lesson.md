# Coordination

## Explanations

### Introduction

Coordination joins units of equal grammatical rank. In CSE sentence-structure items, coordination shows up when a writer connects words, phrases, or clauses that belong on the same level. The most common signals are coordinating conjunctions, especially the FANBOYS words.

### Why Coordination Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read sentence relationships with precision. Coordination is tested because it shows whether you can:

- spot equal grammatical units quickly
- recognize coordinating conjunctions and correlative conjunctions
- punctuate coordinated clauses correctly
- distinguish coordination from subordination
- keep paired structures parallel and clean

### Common Mistakes

Candidates often make these mistakes:

- assuming every `and` sentence is automatically correct
- forgetting the comma before a coordinating conjunction that joins two independent clauses
- using a subordinating conjunction when coordination is needed
- mixing unequal grammatical forms in a correlative pair
- confusing coordination of words or phrases with coordination of clauses

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify coordination in words, phrases, and clauses
- recognize the seven coordinating conjunctions
- choose the right conjunction for the relationship between ideas
- punctuate coordinated clauses correctly
- tell coordination apart from subordination

## What Coordination Means

### Overview

Coordination connects units that are equal in structure. The joined parts can be words, phrases, or clauses, but they should sit at the same grammatical level.

### Core Principle

Ask whether the joined parts are equal.

If the answer is yes, coordination is likely present.

### Visualization

| Coordinated unit | Example | What is joined |
| --- | --- | --- |
| words | `The clerk and the supervisor signed the memo.` | two nouns |
| phrases | `The clerk worked quickly and quietly.` | two adverbs |
| clauses | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` | two independent clauses |

### Common Mistakes

Do not call a sentence coordinated just because you see a conjunction. The conjunction has to connect equal units. If one part depends on another, that is subordination, not coordination.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Is `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` an example of coordination?

Reasoning:

- The sentence joins two independent clauses.
- The two clauses are equal in rank.
- The conjunction links them correctly.

Answer: **Yes. It is coordination of clauses.**

### Key Insight

Coordination means equality of structure, not just the presence of a conjunction.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence shows coordination of words?
Choices:
- The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
- The clerk filed the memo.
Answer: The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.
Rationale: Two nouns are joined at the same grammatical level.

Question: Which sentence shows coordination of clauses?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
- The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.
Answer: The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Rationale: Two independent clauses are joined correctly.

Question: Which sentence is not coordination?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- The clerk worked quickly and quietly.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
Answer: Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
Rationale: The dependent clause makes the sentence complex, not coordinated.

## Coordinating Conjunctions

### Overview

The seven coordinating conjunctions are the FANBOYS words: `for`, `and`, `nor`, `but`, `or`, `yet`, and `so`. Each one shows a different relationship between the coordinated units.

### Core Principle

Choose the conjunction that matches the relationship:

- `and` adds
- `but` and `yet` contrast
- `or` shows choice
- `so` shows result
- `for` shows reason
- `nor` adds a negative idea

### Visualization

| Conjunction | Relationship | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `and` | addition | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` |
| `but` | contrast | `The office was busy, but the clerk stayed calm.` |
| `yet` | contrast | `The schedule changed, yet everyone stayed calm.` |
| `or` | choice | `You may call now, or you may send an email later.` |
| `so` | result | `The printer jammed, so the staff waited.` |
| `for` | reason | `The editor left early, for the deadline was near.` |
| `nor` | negative addition | `The team did not delay, nor did it complain.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not use a subordinating conjunction such as `because` or `although` when the sentence needs a coordinating conjunction. Also, do not choose a conjunction that signals the wrong relationship.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which conjunction best fits `The printer jammed, ___ the staff waited.`?

Reasoning:

- The second clause gives the result.
- `so` shows result.
- The sentence becomes a correct coordinated clause pair.

Answer: **so**

### Key Insight

The conjunction should reveal how the two equal units are related.

### Quick Check

Question: Which word is the coordinating conjunction in `The office was busy, but the clerk stayed calm.`?
Choices:
- but
- because
- although
Answer: but
Rationale: `But` is one of the FANBOYS conjunctions.

Question: Which conjunction best shows choice?
Choices:
- or
- so
- nor
Answer: or
Rationale: `Or` presents an alternative.

Question: Which word is not a coordinating conjunction?
Choices:
- and
- because
- yet
Answer: because
Rationale: `Because` is subordinating, not coordinating.

## Coordination in Clauses and Punctuation

### Overview

When coordination joins two independent clauses, the most common pattern is:

independent clause, coordinating conjunction independent clause.

A semicolon can also join two closely related independent clauses when no coordinating conjunction is used.

### Core Principle

Use the right boundary mark for the structure you want.

- Use a comma before FANBOYS when both clauses are independent.
- Use a semicolon when two independent clauses are closely related and no coordinating conjunction appears.
- Do not use only a comma between two independent clauses.

### Visualization

| Pattern | Correct example |
| --- | --- |
| comma + conjunction | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` |
| semicolon | `The clerk filed the memo; the supervisor signed it.` |
| comma splice | `The clerk filed the memo, the supervisor signed it.` |
| fused sentence | `The clerk filed the memo the supervisor signed it.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not place the comma after the conjunction. Do not use a comma by itself to connect two complete clauses. And do not use a semicolon if one side is not an independent clause.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which revision correctly fixes `The lights flickered the office lost power.`?

Reasoning:

- The sentence has two independent clauses.
- A semicolon can join them cleanly.
- The correct revision is `The lights flickered; the office lost power.`

Answer: **The lights flickered; the office lost power.**

### Key Insight

Coordination is not just about the conjunction. It is also about the punctuation that supports the structure.

### Quick Check

Question: Which revision correctly uses a comma before the conjunction?
Choices:
- The printer jammed, so the staff waited.
- The printer jammed so the staff waited.
- The printer jammed, the staff waited.
Answer: The printer jammed, so the staff waited.
Rationale: Two independent clauses need a comma before the coordinating conjunction.

Question: Which revision correctly uses a semicolon?
Choices:
- The meeting ended; the assistant sent the notice.
- The meeting ended, and the assistant sent the notice.
- The meeting ended, the assistant sent the notice.
Answer: The meeting ended; the assistant sent the notice.
Rationale: Two closely related independent clauses can be linked with a semicolon.

Question: Which sentence is punctuated correctly as a coordinated clause pair?
Choices:
- The office was busy, but the clerk stayed calm.
- The office was busy but the clerk stayed calm.
- The office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
Answer: The office was busy, but the clerk stayed calm.
Rationale: The comma correctly appears before the coordinating conjunction.

## Coordination of Words, Phrases, and Correlative Pairs

### Overview

Coordination can join more than clauses. It can also join words and phrases. Correlative conjunctions are paired coordinators such as `either...or`, `neither...nor`, `both...and`, and `not only...but also`.

### Core Principle

Keep the units balanced.

- words should be joined with words
- phrases should be joined with phrases
- clauses should be joined with clauses
- correlative pairs should stay parallel

### Visualization

| Unit type | Example | What is equal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| words | `The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.` | nouns |
| phrases | `The clerk worked quickly and quietly.` | adverbs |
| correlative pair | `Either the clerk or the supervisor will sign the memo.` | paired alternatives |
| correlative pair | `Both the clerk and the supervisor signed the memo.` | paired nouns |

### Common Mistakes

Do not mix different forms inside a correlative pair. For example, do not pair a noun with a clause if the sentence calls for two nouns. Also, do not forget the second part of the pair.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which sentence uses a correlative pair correctly?

Reasoning:

- `Either the clerk or the supervisor will sign the memo.` has a balanced pair.
- The two alternatives are parallel.
- The sentence is clear and complete.

Answer: **Either the clerk or the supervisor will sign the memo.**

### Key Insight

Coordination is strongest when the joined parts are matched in form.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence shows coordination of phrases?
Choices:
- The clerk worked quickly and quietly.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
Answer: The clerk worked quickly and quietly.
Rationale: Two adverbs are joined at the phrase level.

Question: Which pair is correlative?
Choices:
- either...or
- and...because
- but...although
Answer: either...or
Rationale: Correlative conjunctions work in pairs.

Question: Which sentence uses a correlative pair correctly?
Choices:
- Both the clerk and the supervisor signed the memo.
- The clerk and the supervisor signed the memo, and the manager approved it.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
Answer: Both the clerk and the supervisor signed the memo.
Rationale: The paired structure is parallel and complete.

## Coordination Versus Subordination

### Overview

Coordination keeps ideas at the same level. Subordination makes one idea depend on another. In CSE sentence-structure items, this difference matters because the wrong choice can turn a coordinated sentence into a complex sentence or vice versa.

### Core Principle

Ask whether the sentence is joining equals or making one part dependent.

- equal units = coordination
- dependent plus independent = subordination

### Visualization

| Structure | Example | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| coordination | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` | equal clauses |
| coordination | `The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.` | equal nouns |
| subordination | `Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.` | one clause depends on another |

### Common Mistakes

Do not think `because` and `although` are interchangeable with FANBOYS words. Also, do not assume a sentence is coordinated just because it is long or contains more than one verb.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which sentence uses coordination rather than subordination?

Reasoning:

- `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` joins equal clauses.
- `Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.` makes one clause dependent.
- Coordination is the better label for the first sentence.

Answer: **The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.**

### Key Insight

Coordination keeps the relationship level and balanced. Subordination makes one part less equal.

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence is compound rather than complex?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
- Although the office was busy, the clerk stayed calm.
Answer: The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Rationale: Two independent clauses are joined by a coordinating conjunction.

Question: Which word is a subordinating conjunction, not a coordinating conjunction?
Choices:
- because
- and
- or
Answer: because
Rationale: `Because` introduces a dependent clause.

Question: Which sentence keeps the ideas at equal grammatical rank?
Choices:
- The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.
- Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.
- The clerk filed the memo because the supervisor signed it.
Answer: The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.
Rationale: The two nouns are coordinated at the same level.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` is coordinated at the clause level. The comma appears before the coordinating conjunction because both clauses are independent.

### Example 2

`Either the clerk or the supervisor will sign the memo.` uses a correlative pair. The two alternatives are parallel, so the structure stays balanced.

### Example 3

`Because the memo was ready, the clerk filed it.` is not coordination. It is subordination, because the opening clause depends on the main clause.

### Example 4

`The clerk and the supervisor filed the memo.` coordinates words rather than clauses. The equal units are the two nouns.

## Key Takeaways

- Coordination joins units that are equal in rank.
- The FANBOYS conjunctions are `for`, `and`, `nor`, `but`, `or`, `yet`, and `so`.
- Coordinated independent clauses usually need a comma before the conjunction.
- Semicolons can join closely related independent clauses without a conjunction.
- Correlative pairs must stay parallel.
- Coordination is not the same as subordination.

## Summary

Coordination helps writers connect equal ideas clearly. In the Civil Service Examination, coordination questions often test whether you can identify a coordinating conjunction, choose the right connector, place the comma correctly, and keep paired structures parallel. If you first ask whether the units are equal, the correct answer usually becomes easier to see.
