# Clauses

## Explanations

### Introduction

A clause is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb. Some clauses can stand alone as complete sentences, while others cannot. In the CSE, clauses matter because they help you tell complete sentences from fragments, classify sentence structure, and punctuate connected ideas correctly.

### Why Clauses Are Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read grammar carefully and handle formal English accurately. Clauses are tested because they show whether you can:

- distinguish independent clauses from dependent clauses
- identify simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences
- recognize noun clauses, adjective clauses, and adverb clauses
- use commas, semicolons, and conjunctions correctly between clauses
- repair fragments, run-ons, and comma splices

These skills show up in sentence structure and error recognition items, where one small clause error can change the whole meaning of a sentence.

### Common Mistakes

Candidates often make these mistakes:

- treating a dependent clause as a complete sentence
- missing the comma after an introductory dependent clause
- joining two independent clauses with only a comma
- confusing noun, adjective, and adverb clauses
- misreading a relative clause as a phrase
- ignoring the true subject because a long clause comes first

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify independent and dependent clauses
- classify sentences by clause count and structure
- recognize noun, adjective, and adverb clauses
- punctuate connected clauses correctly
- repair clause-based fragments and run-ons

## What a Clause Is

### Overview

A clause is a word group built around a subject and a verb. The key difference is whether the clause expresses a complete thought. If it does, it is an independent clause. If it does not, it is a dependent clause.

### Core Principle

Ask three questions:

1. Does the group have a subject?
2. Does it have a verb?
3. Does it express a complete thought on its own?

If the answer to all three is yes, the group is an independent clause. If it has a subject and verb but still feels unfinished, it is a dependent clause.

### Visualization

| Type | Example | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| independent clause | `The clerk filed the memo.` | can stand alone |
| dependent clause | `Because the clerk filed the memo` | needs another clause |
| phrase | `in the filing room` | lacks subject or verb |

### Common Mistakes

Do not confuse “has a subject and verb” with “is complete.” A dependent clause also has a subject and verb, but it cannot stand alone.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which group is a clause?

`Because the office closed early`

Reasoning:

- It has a subject and a verb.
- It does not express a complete thought by itself.
- It is a dependent clause.

Answer: **Because the office closed early**

### Quick Check

Question: Which group is an independent clause?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo.
- Because the clerk filed the memo.
- in the filing room
Answer: The clerk filed the memo.
Rationale: It has a subject, a verb, and a complete thought.

Question: Which group is a dependent clause?
Choices:
- The supervisor signed the report.
- Although the supervisor signed the report
- before lunch
Answer: Although the supervisor signed the report
Rationale: It has a subject and a verb, but it cannot stand alone.

Question: Which group is not a clause?
Choices:
- the updated report
- The updated report was filed.
- The report was updated.
Answer: the updated report
Rationale: It has no finite verb, so it is a phrase.

### Key Insight

If the group has a subject and a verb but still sounds unfinished, it is probably a dependent clause.

## Independent Clauses

### Overview

An independent clause expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a sentence. In CSE items, independent clauses are important because they are the building blocks of simple sentences, compound sentences, and many clause-combination questions.

### Core Principle

Count independent clauses first. A simple sentence has one independent clause. A compound sentence has two or more independent clauses. A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.

### Visualization

| Sentence type | Clause count | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| simple | 1 independent | `The clerk filed the memo.` |
| compound | 2 independent | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` |
| complex | 1 independent + 1 dependent | `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.` |
| compound-complex | 2 independent + 1 dependent | `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized, and the supervisor helped.` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not call a sentence compound just because it is long. Also, do not forget that a sentence may contain more than one clause even if it looks simple at first glance.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which sentence is compound?

`The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.`

Reasoning:

- It has two independent clauses.
- A comma and coordinating conjunction connect them.
- It is compound.

Answer: **The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.**

### Quick Check

Question: Which sentence is simple?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
Answer: The clerk filed the memo.
Rationale: It has only one independent clause.

Question: Which sentence is compound?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo.
- Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Answer: The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Rationale: It has two independent clauses joined correctly.

Question: Which sentence is complex?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo.
- Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Answer: Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized.
Rationale: It has one dependent clause and one independent clause.

### Key Insight

If you can count the independent clauses correctly, you can classify most sentence types quickly.

## Dependent Clauses

### Overview

A dependent clause has a subject and a verb, but it cannot stand alone as a complete sentence. It often begins with a dependent marker such as because, although, when, while, if, since, before, or after.

### Core Principle

Dependent markers turn a clause into an incomplete thought. The clause must attach to an independent clause to become complete.

### Visualization

| Marker type | Example | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| time | `When the office reopened` | tells when |
| reason | `Because the file was missing` | tells why |
| condition | `If the manager approves` | tells under what condition |
| contrast | `Although the room was full` | shows contrast |

### Common Mistakes

Do not stop reading as soon as you see a subject and verb. If the clause starts with a dependent marker, it still may not be complete.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Why is `Because the staff arrived late` a dependent clause?

Reasoning:

- It has a subject and a verb.
- It starts with `because`, which makes it dependent.
- It does not finish the thought.

Answer: **It needs a main clause to complete the idea.**

### Quick Check

Question: Which option is a dependent clause?
Choices:
- Because the staff arrived late
- The staff arrived late.
- at the front desk
Answer: Because the staff arrived late
Rationale: It starts with a dependent marker and cannot stand alone.

Question: Which word most clearly signals a dependent clause?
Choices:
- because
- and
- period
Answer: because
Rationale: `Because` is a dependent marker.

Question: Which sentence starts with an introductory dependent clause?
Choices:
- The staff arrived late because the roads were flooded.
- Because the roads were flooded, the staff arrived late.
- The roads were flooded, and the staff arrived late.
Answer: Because the roads were flooded, the staff arrived late.
Rationale: The opening clause is dependent and needs a comma before the main clause.

### Key Insight

If a clause begins with a dependent marker, expect it to attach to another clause.

## Clause Types in Complex Sentences

### Overview

Dependent clauses can work like nouns, adjectives, or adverbs. In the CSE, this is why some clause items ask about meaning, while others ask about function.

### Core Principle

Ask what the clause does:

- noun clause: acts like a noun
- adjective clause: modifies a noun
- adverb clause: modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb

Relative clauses are adjective clauses introduced by words like who, whom, whose, that, and which. In formal writing, restrictive clauses usually use `that` and do not take commas, while nonrestrictive clauses usually use `which` and do take commas.

### Visualization

| Clause type | Example | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| noun clause | `What the clerk decided` | acts as a noun |
| adjective clause | `that the clerk revised` | modifies a noun |
| adverb clause | `Because the road was flooded` | modifies the main clause |

### Common Mistakes

Do not confuse noun clauses with adjective clauses just because both may begin with `that`, `who`, or `which`. The function in the sentence decides the type.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which clause is the noun clause in `I know that the report is late`?

Reasoning:

- `that the report is late` is the clause that completes the verb `know`.
- It acts like a noun.
- It is the noun clause.

Answer: **that the report is late**

### Quick Check

Question: Which option is the noun clause?
Choices:
- that the report is late
- the late report
- because the report is late
Answer: that the report is late
Rationale: It acts like the object of the verb `know`.

Question: Which option is the adjective clause?
Choices:
- because the report was late
- that the clerk revised
- in the filing room
Answer: that the clerk revised
Rationale: It modifies a noun, so it works as an adjective clause.

Question: Which option is the adverb clause?
Choices:
- the clerk revised the report
- before lunch
- because the roads were flooded
Answer: because the roads were flooded
Rationale: It shows reason and modifies the main clause.

### Key Insight

Clause type is determined by function, not by length or by the first word alone.

## Clauses in Punctuation and Repair

### Overview

Clauses often decide where punctuation belongs. An introductory dependent clause usually takes a comma after it. Two independent clauses can be joined with a comma and a coordinating conjunction, or with a semicolon when the connection is close enough.

### Core Principle

Use the clause relationship to choose punctuation:

- dependent clause first, then comma
- two independent clauses with FANBOYS, use comma before the conjunction
- two independent clauses without a conjunction, use semicolon or separate sentences
- restrictive relative clauses usually do not take commas
- nonrestrictive relative clauses usually do take commas

### Visualization

| Pattern | Correct form |
| --- | --- |
| introductory dependent clause | `Because the office was closed, the clerk went home.` |
| compound sentence | `The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` |
| semicolon join | `The clerk filed the memo; the supervisor signed it.` |
| nonrestrictive relative clause | `The supervisor, who arrived early, opened the office.` |

### Common Mistakes

The most common clause error is a comma splice. Another common mistake is forgetting that a dependent clause at the start of a sentence usually needs a comma after it.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which revision is correct?

`Because the file was missing the clerk searched the drawer.`

Reasoning:

- The opening clause is dependent.
- It should be followed by a comma.
- The corrected sentence is complete and clear.

Answer: **Because the file was missing, the clerk searched the drawer.**

### Quick Check

Question: Which revision correctly punctuates the introductory dependent clause?
Choices:
- Because the file was missing, the clerk searched the drawer.
- Because the file was missing the clerk searched the drawer.
- The clerk searched the drawer because, the file was missing.
Answer: Because the file was missing, the clerk searched the drawer.
Rationale: Introductory dependent clauses usually take a comma after them.

Question: Which revision correctly joins the two independent clauses?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
- The clerk filed the memo and the supervisor signed it.
- The clerk filed the memo; and the supervisor signed it.
Answer: The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Rationale: Two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction need a comma before the conjunction.

Question: Which revision correctly uses a nonrestrictive relative clause?
Choices:
- The supervisor, who arrived early, opened the office.
- The supervisor who arrived early, opened the office.
- The supervisor who arrived early opened the office,
Answer: The supervisor, who arrived early, opened the office.
Rationale: A nonessential relative clause is separated by commas.

### Key Insight

Once you recognize the clause type, punctuation becomes much easier to choose.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`Because the roads were flooded, the bus arrived late.` is a complex sentence. The opening clause is dependent, and the main clause carries the complete thought.

### Example 2

`The report that the clerk revised was approved.` uses a restrictive relative clause. The clause is essential to identify which report is being discussed, so no commas are needed.

### Example 3

`The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.` is a compound sentence. The comma before `and` correctly joins two independent clauses.

### Example 4

`What the manager decided surprised the staff.` contains a noun clause. The clause acts as the subject of the sentence.

## Key Takeaways

- An independent clause has a subject, a verb, and a complete thought.
- A dependent clause has a subject and a verb but cannot stand alone.
- Sentence type depends on how many independent and dependent clauses are present.
- Noun, adjective, and adverb clauses each have different functions.
- Introductory dependent clauses usually need a comma.
- Two independent clauses need the right joiner or punctuation.
- Restrictive and nonrestrictive relative clauses are punctuated differently.

## Summary

Clauses are the backbone of sentence structure. In the Civil Service Examination, they help you decide whether a sentence is complete, how sentence parts relate to each other, and what punctuation belongs between ideas. If you can identify clause boundaries, clause type, and clause function quickly, you can handle many CSE grammar questions with more confidence.

## Final Challenge

### Mixed Practice Set

Question: Which clause is the dependent clause in `Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized`?
Choices:
- Because the memo was late
- the clerk apologized
- the memo was late
Answer: Because the memo was late
Rationale: It starts with a dependent marker and cannot stand alone.

Question: Which sentence is compound-complex?
Choices:
- The clerk filed the memo.
- Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized, and the supervisor helped.
- The clerk filed the memo, and the supervisor signed it.
Answer: Because the memo was late, the clerk apologized, and the supervisor helped.
Rationale: It has one dependent clause and two independent clauses.

Question: Which revision correctly fixes the fragment `When the office reopened`?
Choices:
- When the office reopened, the clerk filed the memo.
- When the office reopened the clerk filed the memo.
- The clerk filed the memo when, the office reopened.
Answer: When the office reopened, the clerk filed the memo.
Rationale: The dependent clause is attached to a main clause and punctuated correctly.
