# Active and Passive Voice

## 1. Introduction

**Voice** in grammar refers to the relationship between the subject of a sentence and the action expressed by the verb. It tells us whether the subject is *doing* the action or *receiving* the action.

There are two voices in English:

- **Active Voice** — The subject performs the action.
- **Passive Voice** — The subject receives the action.

### Why This Matters for the Civil Service Exam

Voice is one of the most frequently tested grammar concepts in the Philippine Civil Service Examination (CSE). Questions typically ask you to:

- Identify whether a sentence is in active or passive voice
- Convert sentences from one voice to another
- Spot grammatically incorrect passive constructions
- Choose the clearest or most appropriate sentence structure

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

- Confusing the subject with the object when converting voice
- Using the wrong form of the "be" verb in passive constructions
- Forgetting to use the past participle (not the simple past)
- Creating awkward or wordy passive sentences when active is clearer
- Losing tense consistency during conversion

## 3. Core Concept Explanations

---

### 3.1 What Is Voice in Grammar?

**Voice** describes the direction of action in a sentence — specifically, the relationship between the verb and the subject.

| Voice | Relationship |
|-------|-------------|
| Active | Subject → does the action → to the object |
| Passive | Subject ← receives the action ← from the agent |

Think of it as a camera angle:
- **Active voice** = the camera is on the doer
- **Passive voice** = the camera is on the receiver

#### Examples by Difficulty

**Easy:**
- Active: *The cat chased the mouse.*
- Passive: *The mouse was chased by the cat.*

**Medium:**
- Active: *The committee has approved the new policy.*
- Passive: *The new policy has been approved by the committee.*

**Hard:**
- Active: *The auditor should have been reviewing the financial statements.*
- Passive: *The financial statements should have been being reviewed by the auditor.*

**CSE-Style:**
- Which sentence is in passive voice?
  - (A) The supervisor assigned the tasks.
  - (B) The tasks were assigned by the supervisor. ✓
  - (C) The supervisor is assigning tasks.
  - (D) Tasks need assignment.

---

### 3.2 Active Voice

In active voice, the **subject performs the action** expressed by the verb. The sentence follows a direct, natural order:

```
Subject (doer) + Verb (action) + Object (receiver)
```

#### Examples

| Sentence | Subject (Doer) | Verb | Object (Receiver) |
|----------|----------------|------|-------------------|
| The manager approved the proposal. | The manager | approved | the proposal |
| Maria solved the problem. | Maria | solved | the problem |
| The government implements new policies. | The government | implements | new policies |
| Engineers designed the bridge. | Engineers | designed | the bridge |

#### Advantages of Active Voice

1. **Clarity** — The reader immediately knows who is doing what.
2. **Conciseness** — Active sentences are typically shorter.
3. **Directness** — Stronger, more confident communication.
4. **Readability** — Easier to process mentally.

#### When Active Voice Is Stronger

- *Weak passive:* The report was submitted by the employee.
- *Strong active:* The employee submitted the report.

The active version is 2 words shorter and immediately tells you who did what.

---

### 3.3 Passive Voice

In passive voice, the **subject receives the action**. The doer (called the "agent") either appears after "by" or is omitted entirely.

```
Subject (receiver) + Be verb + Past Participle + (by + Agent)
```

#### Examples

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| The manager approved the proposal. | The proposal was approved by the manager. |
| Maria solved the problem. | The problem was solved by Maria. |
| Someone stole my wallet. | My wallet was stolen. |
| They will announce the results. | The results will be announced. |

#### Key Characteristics

1. **Emphasis shifts** to the receiver of the action
2. **The agent (doer) becomes optional** — you can drop the "by" phrase
3. **Formal tone** — common in official, scientific, and legal writing
4. **The verb phrase always contains a form of "be" + past participle**

#### When Passive Voice Is Appropriate

- The doer is unknown: *My bag was stolen.* (We don't know who did it.)
- The doer is obvious: *The suspect was arrested.* (Obviously by police.)
- The action matters more than the doer: *The law was enacted in 2019.*
- Formal/official writing: *Applications must be submitted before June 30.*

---

### 3.4 Structure of Passive Voice

This is the most critical section for exam success. Memorize this formula:

```
Subject + BE verb (conjugated to match tense) + PAST PARTICIPLE + (by + agent)
```

#### The "Be" Verb Forms

| Tense | Be Form |
|-------|---------|
| Simple Present | am / is / are |
| Simple Past | was / were |
| Present Continuous | am being / is being / are being |
| Past Continuous | was being / were being |
| Present Perfect | has been / have been |
| Past Perfect | had been |
| Future | will be |
| Future Perfect | will have been |
| Modal | can be / should be / must be / may be |

#### Past Participle Reminders

- Regular verbs: add **-ed** (approved, submitted, completed)
- Irregular verbs: memorize the third form (written, taken, done, seen, given)

#### Agreement Rules

The "be" verb must agree with the **new subject** (the original object):

- *The letter **was** sent.* (singular subject → was)
- *The letters **were** sent.* (plural subject → were)
- *The report **has been** submitted.* (singular → has)
- *The reports **have been** submitted.* (plural → have)

---

### 3.5 Active to Passive Conversion

Follow these steps exactly:

#### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Identify the subject** (doer) in the active sentence
2. **Identify the verb** and its tense
3. **Identify the object** (receiver)
4. **Move the object** to the subject position
5. **Add the appropriate "be" verb** matching the original tense
6. **Change the main verb** to its past participle form
7. **Add "by + original subject"** (optional — omit if the agent is obvious or unknown)

#### Worked Example 1 (Simple Past)

Active: *The teacher explained the lesson.*

1. Subject (doer): The teacher
2. Verb: explained (simple past)
3. Object (receiver): the lesson
4. New subject: The lesson
5. Be verb (simple past): was
6. Past participle: explained
7. Agent: by the teacher

**Passive: *The lesson was explained by the teacher.***

#### Worked Example 2 (Present Perfect)

Active: *The committee has reviewed the applications.*

1. Subject: The committee
2. Verb: has reviewed (present perfect)
3. Object: the applications
4. New subject: The applications
5. Be verb (present perfect): have been
6. Past participle: reviewed
7. Agent: by the committee

**Passive: *The applications have been reviewed by the committee.***

#### Worked Example 3 (Future)

Active: *The president will sign the executive order.*

1. Subject: The president
2. Verb: will sign (future)
3. Object: the executive order
4. New subject: The executive order
5. Be verb (future): will be
6. Past participle: signed
7. Agent: by the president

**Passive: *The executive order will be signed by the president.***

#### Worked Example 4 (Modal)

Active: *The staff must complete the training.*

1. Subject: The staff
2. Verb: must complete (modal)
3. Object: the training
4. New subject: The training
5. Be verb (modal): must be
6. Past participle: completed
7. Agent: by the staff

**Passive: *The training must be completed by the staff.***

---

### 3.6 Passive to Active Conversion

Converting passive back to active restores directness and clarity.

#### Steps

1. **Find the agent** (after "by") — this becomes your new subject
2. **If no agent is stated**, supply a logical one (someone, they, the authorities, etc.)
3. **Identify the main verb** (the past participle)
4. **Determine the tense** from the "be" verb
5. **Conjugate the main verb** in that tense with the new subject
6. **Move the passive subject** to the object position

#### Worked Example

Passive: *The documents were signed by the director.*

1. Agent: the director → new subject
2. Past participle: signed
3. Tense: simple past (were)
4. Conjugate: signed (simple past)
5. Original subject → object: the documents

**Active: *The director signed the documents.***

#### When the Agent Is Missing

Passive: *The window was broken.*

- No agent stated. Supply one: Someone, A person, They
- **Active: *Someone broke the window.***

### 3.7 Voice in Different Tenses

This is the master reference table. Study it carefully.

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Simple Present | She writes the report. | The report is written by her. |
| Present Continuous | She is writing the report. | The report is being written by her. |
| Present Perfect | She has written the report. | The report has been written by her. |
| Simple Past | She wrote the report. | The report was written by her. |
| Past Continuous | She was writing the report. | The report was being written by her. |
| Past Perfect | She had written the report. | The report had been written by her. |
| Simple Future | She will write the report. | The report will be written by her. |
| Future Perfect | She will have written the report. | The report will have been written by her. |

#### Key Patterns to Notice

- **Simple tenses**: subject + is/was/will be + past participle
- **Continuous tenses**: subject + is being/was being + past participle
- **Perfect tenses**: subject + has been/had been/will have been + past participle

> ⚠️ **There is no commonly used passive form for future continuous or perfect continuous tenses.** If you see one on an exam, it's likely a distractor.

---

### 3.8 Passive Voice with Modal Verbs

Modal verbs (can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must) follow a simple pattern in passive:

```
Subject + modal + be + past participle
```

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| You can solve this problem. | This problem can be solved. |
| They should submit the form. | The form should be submitted. |
| We must complete the project. | The project must be completed. |
| She may approve the request. | The request may be approved. |
| He could finish the task. | The task could be finished. |
| They would accept the offer. | The offer would be accepted. |

#### Perfect Modal Passive

```
Subject + modal + have been + past participle
```

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| They should have completed it. | It should have been completed. |
| She could have written the letter. | The letter could have been written. |
| He must have taken the file. | The file must have been taken. |

---

### 3.9 When to Use Active Voice

Use active voice when:

1. **You want clarity and directness**
   - ✓ *The supervisor approved the leave request.*
   - ✗ *The leave request was approved by the supervisor.*

2. **The doer is important information**
   - ✓ *The president signed the law.*

3. **You want concise writing**
   - Active sentences are typically 20–30% shorter.

4. **In everyday communication**
   - Instructions, emails, memos, reports

5. **On the CSE, when asked to "improve" a sentence**
   - The exam often considers active voice the "better" construction unless context demands passive.

---

### 3.10 When to Use Passive Voice

Use passive voice when:

1. **The doer is unknown**
   - *The files were deleted.* (We don't know who did it.)

2. **The doer is irrelevant**
   - *The building was constructed in 1985.* (Who built it doesn't matter here.)

3. **You want to emphasize the action or result**
   - *Three employees were terminated.* (Focus on what happened, not who did it.)

4. **In formal, scientific, or official writing**
   - *The experiment was conducted under controlled conditions.*
   - *Applications must be filed before the deadline.*

5. **To avoid blame or sound diplomatic**
   - *Mistakes were made.* (Avoids naming who made them.)

---

### 3.11 Common Voice Errors

#### Error 1: Wrong "Be" Verb Form

- ✗ *The report **is wrote** by the analyst.*
- ✓ *The report **was written** by the analyst.*

"Wrote" is simple past, not past participle. The past participle of "write" is "written."

#### Error 2: Using Simple Past Instead of Past Participle

- ✗ *The package was **took** to the office.*
- ✓ *The package was **taken** to the office.*

#### Error 3: Wrong Tense of "Be"

- ✗ *The documents **is** reviewed yesterday.*
- ✓ *The documents **were** reviewed yesterday.*

#### Error 4: Missing "Be" Verb Entirely

- ✗ *The letter **signed** by the manager.*
- ✓ *The letter **was signed** by the manager.*

#### Error 5: Double Passive

- ✗ *The report was been submitted.*
- ✓ *The report has been submitted.* OR *The report was submitted.*

#### Error 6: Incorrect Agent Preposition

- ✗ *The cake was baked **from** my mother.*
- ✓ *The cake was baked **by** my mother.*

---

### 3.12 Identifying Awkward Passive Constructions

Some passive sentences are grammatically correct but stylistically poor. The CSE may ask you to identify the "best" or "clearest" version.

#### Signs of Awkward Passive

1. **Unnecessary wordiness**
   - Awkward: *It is believed by many experts that the policy should be revised.*
   - Better: *Many experts believe the policy should be revised.*

2. **Buried agent**
   - Awkward: *The decision to postpone the meeting was made by the committee after the report was reviewed by the members.*
   - Better: *The committee decided to postpone the meeting after the members reviewed the report.*

3. **Dangling or unclear agent**
   - Awkward: *The project was completed.* (By whom? If it matters, say so.)

4. **Chain of passives**
   - Awkward: *The form was filled out, was reviewed, and was approved.*
   - Better: *The staff filled out, reviewed, and approved the form.*

#### Correction Strategy

Ask yourself:
1. Who is doing the action? → Make them the subject.
2. Is the passive adding value (emphasis, formality, unknown doer)? → Keep it.
3. Is it just making the sentence longer and harder to read? → Convert to active.

## 4. Step-by-Step Rules Summary

### Rule 1: Identify Voice Instantly

- Does the subject DO the action? → **Active**
- Does the subject RECEIVE the action? → **Passive**
- Is there a "be + past participle" combo? → Likely **Passive**

### Rule 2: Passive Formula

```
Subject + be (tense-matched) + past participle + (by + agent)
```

### Rule 3: Tense Must Be Preserved

If the active sentence is in simple past, the passive must also reflect simple past:
- Active (simple past): *She wrote the letter.*
- Passive (simple past): *The letter was written by her.*

### Rule 4: Subject-Verb Agreement in Passive

The "be" verb agrees with the NEW subject (the original object):
- *The book was returned.* (singular)
- *The books were returned.* (plural)

### Rule 5: Past Participle Is Non-Negotiable

Always use the **third form** of the verb in passive:
- write → written (NOT wrote)
- take → taken (NOT took)
- do → done (NOT did)
- give → given (NOT gave)
- see → seen (NOT saw)

---

## 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Wrong | Correct |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Using past tense instead of past participle | was broke | was broken |
| Wrong "be" verb for the tense | is completed yesterday | was completed yesterday |
| Forgetting "be" verb | The letter signed. | The letter was signed. |
| Wrong preposition | done from him | done by him |
| Passive with intransitive verbs | was happened | happened (no passive possible) |
| Tense shift during conversion | writes → was written | writes → is written |

### Intransitive Verbs Cannot Be Passive

Verbs that don't take an object cannot form passive voice:
- ✗ *The accident was happened.*
- ✓ *The accident happened.*

Other intransitive verbs: arrive, die, exist, occur, sleep, appear, fall

---

## 6. Exam Strategies

### Strategy 1: Spot the "Be + Past Participle" Pattern

If you see any form of "be" followed by a past participle, it's passive. This takes 2 seconds to identify.

### Strategy 2: Ask "Who Is Doing the Action?"

- If the subject is doing it → active
- If the subject is receiving it → passive

### Strategy 3: Check for "By + Agent"

The presence of "by + noun" after the verb phrase is a strong passive indicator (though not all passives include it).

### Strategy 4: Elimination on Conversion Questions

When asked to convert, eliminate choices that:
- Change the meaning
- Use the wrong tense
- Use simple past instead of past participle
- Have subject-verb disagreement

### Strategy 5: "Best Sentence" Questions

When asked which sentence is "best" or "most clear":
- Active is usually preferred unless the context demands passive
- Shorter is usually better
- The one with the clearest subject-verb-object order wins

## 7. Tense Conversion Quick Reference

| If Active Tense Is... | Passive Uses... |
|----------------------|-----------------|
| V1 / V1+s (simple present) | am/is/are + V3 |
| V2 (simple past) | was/were + V3 |
| is/are + V-ing (present continuous) | is/are + being + V3 |
| was/were + V-ing (past continuous) | was/were + being + V3 |
| has/have + V3 (present perfect) | has/have + been + V3 |
| had + V3 (past perfect) | had + been + V3 |
| will + V1 (simple future) | will + be + V3 |
| will + have + V3 (future perfect) | will + have + been + V3 |
| modal + V1 | modal + be + V3 |
| modal + have + V3 | modal + have + been + V3 |

*(V1 = base form, V2 = past tense, V3 = past participle)*

## 8. Real CSE-Like Examples with Analysis

### Example 1 (Easy)

**Question:** Which sentence is in passive voice?
- (A) The clerk filed the documents.
- (B) The documents were filed by the clerk.
- (C) The clerk is filing documents.
- (D) Filing documents is the clerk's job.

**Answer:** B

**Analysis:** "The documents" (receiver) + "were filed" (be + past participle) + "by the clerk" (agent). Classic passive structure.

---

### Example 2 (Medium)

**Question:** Choose the correct passive form of: *"The team has completed the project."*
- (A) The project has been completed by the team.
- (B) The project have been completed by the team.
- (C) The project was completed by the team.
- (D) The project has completed by the team.

**Answer:** A

**Analysis:** Present perfect active (has completed) → present perfect passive (has been + V3). "Project" is singular → "has" (not "have"). Choice C changes the tense to simple past. Choice D omits "been."

---

### Example 3 (Hard)

**Question:** Which sentence contains an error in passive construction?
- (A) The memo should have been distributed earlier.
- (B) The results are being tabulated by the committee.
- (C) The application was been reviewed by the panel.
- (D) New guidelines will be implemented next month.

**Answer:** C

**Analysis:** "Was been" is incorrect. You cannot combine "was" (simple past be) with "been" (perfect participle of be). It should be either "was reviewed" (simple past passive) or "has been reviewed" (present perfect passive).

**3.** Convert to active: *The award was given by the principal.*
- Answer: *The principal gave the award.*

**4.** Identify the error: *The letter was wrote by the manager.*
- Answer: "wrote" should be "written" (past participle)

**5.** Which is passive? (a) She teaches English. (b) English is taught by her.
- Answer: (b)

**6.** Convert to passive: *They are building a new office.*
- Answer: *A new office is being built (by them).*

**7.** Convert to passive: *The president will announce the decision.*
- Answer: *The decision will be announced by the president.*

**8.** Identify the voice: *Someone left the door open.*
- Answer: Active (subject "someone" performs the action)

**9.** Convert to active: *The exam results have been released.*
- Answer: *They have released the exam results.* (or *The school has released...*)

**10.** Identify the error: *The documents is being reviewed by the team.*
- Answer: "is" should be "are" (documents is plural)

**11.** Convert to passive: *You must submit the form before Friday.*
- Answer: *The form must be submitted before Friday.*

**12.** Which is more appropriate for formal writing?
- (a) We conducted the survey last month.
- (b) The survey was conducted last month.
- Answer: (b) — passive is preferred in formal/official contexts when the doer is less important.

**13.** Convert to passive: *The mechanic had repaired the vehicle.*
- Answer: *The vehicle had been repaired by the mechanic.*

**14.** Identify the voice: *The cake has been eaten.*
- Answer: Passive (has been + past participle)

**15.** Fix the error: *The project completed by the team last week.*
- Answer: *The project **was** completed by the team last week.* (missing "be" verb)

**16.** Convert to passive: *She can solve this equation.*
- Answer: *This equation can be solved by her.*

**17.** Which is awkward? (a) The manager approved the budget. (b) The budget was approved by the manager after it was reviewed by the finance team and was recommended by the director.

- Answer: *You/They should have submitted the files yesterday.*

## 10. Quick Recap

| Concept | Key Point |
|---------|-----------|
| Active Voice | Subject does the action. Direct and concise. |
| Passive Voice | Subject receives the action. Uses be + past participle. |
| Conversion | Move object to subject, add "be" in correct tense, use past participle. |
| Tense Preservation | The passive must reflect the same tense as the active original. |
| Agreement | "Be" verb agrees with the new subject (original object). |
| Past Participle | Always V3 — never V2. (written, not wrote; taken, not took) |
| Intransitive Verbs | Cannot form passive (happen, arrive, die, exist). |
| Exam Tip | "Be + V3" = passive. Ask "who does the action?" for quick ID. |

## 11. Memory Aids

### Mnemonic: "BPP" — Be + Past Participle = Passive

If you see any form of BE followed by a PAST PARTICIPLE, it's passive. Period.

### The "By" Test

Can you add "by someone" at the end and it still makes sense? → Passive.
- *The report was submitted [by someone].* ✓ Passive
- *The manager submitted the report [by someone].* ✗ Doesn't work → Active

### Tense Ladder for Passive

```
Simple:      be + V3
Continuous:  be + being + V3
Perfect:     have + been + V3
Modal:       modal + be + V3
```

### Irregular Past Participle Groups

- **-en group:** written, taken, given, driven, eaten, spoken, broken, chosen, frozen
- **-t group:** built, sent, spent, left, kept, slept, felt, dealt
- **Same form:** put, cut, shut, set, hit, let, hurt
- **-ne/-wn:** done, gone, shown, known, grown, drawn, thrown
