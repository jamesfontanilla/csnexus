# Subject-Verb Agreement

## Explanations

### Introduction

Subject-verb agreement means the verb must match the subject in number. A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb. In CSE error-recognition items, this rule is often hidden inside a long sentence, so the real job is not just grammar knowledge but careful sentence reading.

The easiest way to think about it is this: find the true subject first, then choose the verb that matches that subject. Do not let nearby words distract you. Do not let long phrases, commas, or extra details change the number of the subject.

### Why Subject-Verb Agreement Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read formal English accurately and quickly. Subject-verb agreement is tested because it shows whether you can:

- identify the real subject of a sentence
- ignore words that only describe the subject
- notice agreement errors in formal writing
- handle sentences with compound subjects, pronouns, and special noun forms
- make precise decisions under time pressure

This skill matters in government documents, memos, notices, and reports, where even small grammar errors can weaken clarity and professionalism.

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often make the same mistakes:

- matching the verb to the nearest noun instead of the true subject
- forgetting that subjects joined by `and` usually take a plural verb
- forgetting that `either ... or` and `neither ... nor` follow the nearer subject
- being fooled by phrases that come between the subject and verb
- treating words like `everyone` or `each` as plural because they refer to many people
- using a plural verb with words like `news`, `mathematics`, or `scissors`
- forgetting that `there is` and `there are` depend on the noun that follows the verb

These mistakes happen because subject-verb agreement is often mixed with long sentence structure. The rule is simple, but the sentence can be designed to hide it.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the head subject in a sentence
- match singular subjects with singular verbs and plural subjects with plural verbs
- apply the rules for compound subjects, `or`/`nor` structures, and intervening phrases
- recognize singular indefinite pronouns and special singular nouns
- handle collective nouns, quantity expressions, and `there` constructions correctly

## Number of the Subject

### Overview

The first step in subject-verb agreement is finding the number of the subject. The verb must agree with the main subject, not with a nearby noun, phrase, or modifier.

### Core Principle

Ask one question: what is the grammatical subject of the sentence?

- If the subject is singular, use a singular verb.
- If the subject is plural, use a plural verb.

The head noun matters most. A phrase after `of`, `in`, `with`, or `for` does not usually change the number of the subject.

### Visualization

| Subject type | Verb example | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| singular subject | `is`, `was`, `has`, `reports` | singular verb |
| plural subject | `are`, `were`, `have`, `report` | plural verb |
| head noun singular, extra phrase after it | `The box of files is...` | singular |
| head noun plural, extra phrase after it | `The files in the box are...` | plural |

### Common Mistakes

Do not count the words inside a phrase and assume the nearest noun controls the verb. In subject-verb agreement, the main subject controls the verb. A sentence may contain many nouns, but only one of them is the true subject.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The list of requirements ____ on the table.`

Options:

1. is
2. are
3. were
4. have

Reasoning:

- The head noun is `list`, which is singular.
- The phrase `of requirements` does not change the number of `list`.
- The correct verb is `is`.

Answer: **is**

### Quick Check

Question: In `The pile of documents ____ missing`, what is the head noun
Choices:
- pile
- singular verb
- plural verb
Answer: pile
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: If the head noun is singular, what kind of verb should follow
Choices:
- singular verb
- A singular verb
- plural verb
Answer: A singular verb
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: True or false: the noun closest to the verb always controls agreement
Choices:
- True
- It depends

Answer: False
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

### Key Insight

Find the head noun first. That noun decides the verb.

## Compound Subjects and Coordination

### Overview

When two or more subjects are joined by `and`, they usually form a plural subject and take a plural verb. This is one of the most reliable agreement rules in English.

### Core Principle

If the sentence says `A and B`, the subject is usually plural.

- `Mia and Paulo are...`
- `The clerk and the supervisor were...`
- `The report and the memo have...`

Do not confuse `and` with phrases such as `as well as`, `along with`, or `together with`. Those phrases do not make the subject plural.

### Visualization

| Structure | Usual verb number |
| --- | --- |
| `A and B` | plural |
| `A and B and C` | plural |
| `A, as well as B` | matches `A` |
| `A, together with B` | matches `A` |

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often see two nouns and think they must count both separately. That is correct when the nouns are joined by `and`, but not when they are linked by `as well as`, `with`, or `including`. The word that controls the verb is still the true subject.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The manager and the assistant ____ the final report yesterday.`

Options:

1. submits
2. submit
3. was submitting
4. has submit

Reasoning:

- The subject has two nouns joined by `and`.
- That makes the subject plural.
- The correct verb form is `submit`.

Answer: **submit**

### Quick Check

Question: What verb number usually follows `The teacher and the student`
Choices:
- Words in the same family share a common base meaning
- singular verb
- plural verb
Answer: Words in the same family share a common base meaning
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: Does `as well as` usually make the subject plural
Choices:
- Yes
- No
- Not always
Answer: No
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: True or false: `The captain, together with the crew, are ready` is correct
Choices:
- True
- It depends
- False
Answer: False
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

### Key Insight

`And` usually means plural. Extra side phrases usually do not.

## Intervening Phrases and Attractors

### Overview

Long phrases can separate a subject from its verb. These phrases are called intervening phrases, and they often contain nouns that try to distract you. The verb still agrees with the real subject, not with the attractor noun inside the phrase.

### Core Principle

Ignore the phrase between the subject and the verb when you decide agreement.

- `The box of old papers is...`
- `The people who live nearby are...`
- `The captain, along with the sailors, is...`

The phrase may be long, but the head noun still controls the verb.

### Visualization

| Sentence part | Role |
| --- | --- |
| head noun | controls the verb |
| phrase after comma or preposition | does not control the verb |
| relative clause | does not change number |
| attractor noun | can mislead, but is not the subject |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest trap is the attractor noun. If you see a plural noun inside a phrase, do not immediately choose a plural verb. First locate the subject of the whole sentence. Then test the verb against that subject only.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The bouquet of roses ____ on the desk beside the register.`

Options:

1. are
2. is
3. were
4. have been

Reasoning:

- The head noun is `bouquet`, which is singular.
- `of roses` is only a phrase and does not control the verb.
- The correct verb is `is`.

Answer: **is**

### Quick Check

Question: In `The people in the front row ____ applauding`, what controls the verb
Choices:
- The root carries the core meaning
- singular verb
- plural verb
Answer: The root carries the core meaning
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: Does the noun in a phrase after `of` usually control agreement
Choices:
- Yes
- No
- Not always
Answer: No
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: True or false: `The list of names are long` is correct
Choices:
- True
- It depends
- False
Answer: False
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

### Key Insight

Phrases can be long, but grammar still follows the head noun.

## Indefinite Pronouns and Special Singular Nouns

### Overview

Some pronouns and nouns look plural in meaning but are treated as singular in grammar. Others look plural in form but require plural verbs. These are favorite CSE traps because they look familiar but behave in special ways.

### Core Principle

Treat these words carefully:

- singular indefinite pronouns: `each`, `everybody`, `everyone`, `anyone`, `someone`, `nobody`, `no one`, `either`, `neither`
- special singular nouns: `news`, `mathematics`, `civics`, `measles`
- special plural nouns: `scissors`, `trousers`, `tweezers`, `shears`
- quantity wording can change the rule, so read the whole phrase

### Visualization

| Word or phrase | Usual verb number |
| --- | --- |
| `each` | singular |
| `everyone` | singular |
| `nobody` | singular |
| `news` | singular |
| `mathematics` | singular |
| `scissors` | plural |
| `trousers` | plural |

### Common Mistakes

Do not let meaning override grammar. `Everyone` refers to many people, but it still takes a singular verb. `Scissors` looks like one tool, but it takes a plural verb because it has two parts. `News` may feel like a collection of items, but it behaves as singular.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`Each of the applicants ____ the form before leaving.`

Options:

1. submit
2. submits
3. submitted
4. submitting

Reasoning:

- `Each` is singular.
- The verb must also be singular.
- The correct answer is `submits`.

Answer: **submits**

### Quick Check

Question: Is `Everyone in the room are ready` correct
Choices:
- False
- True
- It depends
Answer: False
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: Which is correct: `The scissors is` or `The scissors are`
Choices:
- `The scissors is`
- are
- `The scissors are`
Answer: are
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: Does `news` usually take a singular verb
Choices:
- No
- Not always
- Yes
Answer: Yes
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

### Key Insight

Words that sound plural may still demand singular verbs, and words that look singular may still demand plural verbs.

## Collective Nouns, Quantities, and There Constructions

### Overview

Collective nouns, quantity expressions, and `there is` / `there are` sentences can all hide the real subject. These structures test whether you can look beyond the first word and focus on the grammatical core.

### Core Principle

- Collective nouns such as `team`, `committee`, `class`, and `family` are usually singular when treated as a unit.
- `A number of` is plural, but `the number of` is singular.
- Amounts and measures are often singular when treated as one total.
- In `there is` / `there are` sentences, `there` is not the subject. The noun after the verb controls agreement.

### Visualization

| Structure | Usual verb number |
| --- | --- |
| `The committee` | singular |
| `A number of volunteers` | plural |
| `The number of volunteers` | singular |
| `There are many...` | plural noun after the verb |
| `There is a...` | singular noun after the verb |

### Common Mistakes

The most common error is treating `there` as the subject. It is not. Another common error is treating every collective noun as plural. In most CSE items, a collective noun like `committee` or `team` is singular unless the sentence clearly focuses on individual members.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`There ____ several reasons for the delay in processing.`

Options:

1. is
2. are
3. was
4. has

Reasoning:

- `there` is not the subject.
- The real subject is `reasons`, which is plural.
- The correct verb is `are`.

Answer: **are**

### Quick Check

Question: Is `The committee are meeting` the usual CSE answer
Choices:
- No
- Yes
- Not always
Answer: No
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: Which is correct: `A number of employees are` or `A number of employees is`
Choices:
- `A number of employees are`
- are
- `A number of employees is`
Answer: are
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

Question: Which is correct: `The number of employees are` or `The number of employees is`
Choices:
- `The number of employees are`
- `The number of employees is`
- is
Answer: is
Rationale: The head subject controls agreement.

### Key Insight

Do not let the first word fool you. In these patterns, the real subject often comes later.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`The committee, along with the advisers, ____ meeting after lunch.`

Choices:

1. are
2. is
3. were
4. have

Answer: **is**

Reasoning: `along with the advisers` is an intervening phrase. The true subject is `committee`, which is singular.

### Example 2

`Neither the interns nor the coordinator ____ prepared for the announcement.`

Choices:

1. are
2. were
3. is
4. have

Answer: **is**

Reasoning: With `neither ... nor`, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. The nearer subject is `coordinator`, which is singular.

### Example 3

`The news from the provincial office ____ being reviewed this morning.`

Choices:

1. are
2. were
3. is
4. have

Answer: **is**

Reasoning: `news` is a special singular noun, so it takes a singular verb.

## Key Takeaways

- Find the head noun first.
- `And` usually makes a compound subject plural.
- `Or` and `nor` usually follow the nearer subject.
- Ignore phrases that sit between the subject and verb.
- Memorize the special singular and plural words that CSE items like to test.
- `There is` and `there are` depend on the noun after the verb.
- Collective nouns are usually singular when treated as one unit.

## Summary

Subject-verb agreement is a basic grammar rule, but CSE test writers use long sentences and tricky wording to hide it. The safest method is always the same: identify the real subject, determine whether it is singular or plural, then choose the verb that matches. Once you can ignore attractors, handle compound subjects, and recognize special nouns, these items become much easier to solve.

## Final Challenge

### Mixed Practice Set

1. `The stack of application forms ____ on the counter.`
   - Answer: `is`
   - Why: `stack` is the head noun and it is singular.

2. `My sister and I ____ the final copies to the office.`
   - Answer: `submit`
   - Why: subjects joined by `and` take a plural verb.

3. `Either the supervisor or the assistants ____ handling the calls.`
   - Answer: `are`
   - Why: the nearer subject is `assistants`, which is plural.

4. `Each of the officers ____ the report before noon.`
   - Answer: `reviews`
   - Why: `each` is singular.

5. `There ____ three envelopes in the filing tray.`
   - Answer: `are`
   - Why: the noun after `there` is plural.
