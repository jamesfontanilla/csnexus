# Pronoun Agreement

## Explanations

### Introduction

Pronoun agreement means a pronoun must match its antecedent in number, person, and clarity. If the antecedent is singular, the pronoun should be singular. If the antecedent is plural, the pronoun should be plural. If the sentence switches person or makes the reference unclear, the pronoun is wrong even if it sounds natural at first glance.

In CSE error-recognition items, pronoun agreement is often hidden inside a long sentence. The noun may appear far from the pronoun, or the sentence may contain several nouns that compete for attention. Your job is to find the true antecedent first, then check whether the pronoun matches it cleanly.

### Why Pronoun Agreement Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read formal English accurately and quickly. Pronoun agreement is tested because it shows whether you can:

- match pronouns to the correct antecedent
- avoid shifts in number and person
- detect vague or ambiguous reference
- handle singular and plural antecedents correctly
- choose the safest form in a formal sentence

This matters in government writing, where unclear pronouns can confuse the reader and weaken the sentence.

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often make these mistakes:

- matching a pronoun with the nearest noun instead of the true antecedent
- using a singular pronoun for a plural antecedent
- using a plural pronoun for a singular antecedent
- switching from first person to third person in the same sentence
- using `it` or `they` when the reference is unclear
- forgetting that words like `each`, `everyone`, and `one` are singular in grammar
- treating collective or quantity expressions as though they all behave the same way

These mistakes are common because pronouns are short and easy to overlook. But on the exam, short words still matter. A tiny pronoun can make the entire sentence wrong.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the antecedent of a pronoun
- match singular antecedents with singular pronouns and plural antecedents with plural pronouns
- maintain person agreement within a sentence
- handle indefinite pronouns, compound antecedents, and collective nouns correctly
- recognize when a pronoun is too vague or ambiguous for a formal sentence

## What Pronoun Agreement Means

### Overview

A pronoun takes the place of a noun. Because it stands in for another word, it has to agree with that word. Pronoun agreement is not just about choosing `he`, `she`, `it`, or `they`. It also includes possessive forms, object forms, reflexives, and clarity.

### Core Principle

Ask three questions:

- What noun does the pronoun replace?
- Is that noun singular or plural?
- Is the pronoun clear enough for the reader to follow?

If the antecedent is singular, the pronoun should be singular. If the antecedent is plural, the pronoun should be plural. If the antecedent is first person, second person, or third person, the pronoun should stay in that same person.

### Visualization

| Antecedent type | Correct pronoun pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| singular noun | singular pronoun | `The folder lost its label.` |
| plural noun | plural pronoun | `The files lost their labels.` |
| first person | first-person pronoun | `I checked my schedule.` |
| second person | second-person pronoun | `You should bring your form.` |
| third person | third-person pronoun | `The officer checked his or her badge.` |

### Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is treating pronoun agreement like word choice only. It is not. A pronoun must match the noun it replaces and must do so clearly. If the reader has to guess, the pronoun is not doing its job.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The envelope lost ____ seal during transport.`

Options:

1. their
2. its
3. them
4. theirs

Reasoning:

- `envelope` is singular and nonhuman.
- The pronoun must be singular.
- The possessive adjective that matches is `its`.

Answer: **its**

### Quick Check

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The report lost ____ cover in the rain.`
Choices:
- it
- its
- them
Answer: `its`
Rationale: The antecedent `report` is singular, so the possessive pronoun must be singular.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The files were sorted, and ____ were sent to records.`
Choices:
- it
- they
- itself
Answer: `they`
Rationale: `files` is plural, so the subject pronoun must be plural.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The machine reset ____ after the outage.`
Choices:
- itself
- themselves
- his or herself
Answer: `itself`
Rationale: The antecedent is singular and nonhuman, so the reflexive pronoun must be singular.

### Key Insight

Pronoun agreement starts with the antecedent. If you identify the noun correctly, the pronoun choice becomes much easier.

## Number Agreement with Singular and Plural Antecedents

### Overview

The most basic pronoun-agreement rule is number. Singular antecedents take singular pronouns, and plural antecedents take plural pronouns. This sounds simple, but exam items often hide the antecedent inside a long sentence or place distracting nouns near the pronoun.

### Core Principle

Use singular pronouns for one antecedent and plural pronouns for more than one antecedent.

- singular: `it`, `its`, `he`, `she`, `him`, `her`, `his`, `hers`
- plural: `they`, `them`, `their`, `theirs`, `themselves`

Do not let a nearby plural noun pull you away from a singular antecedent. Do not let a singular noun inside a phrase trick you into using a singular pronoun for a plural antecedent.

### Visualization

| Antecedent | Singular? | Typical pronoun |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `the folder` | yes | `it`, `its` |
| `the files` | no | `they`, `their` |
| `the report` | yes | `it`, `its` |
| `the records` | no | `them`, `theirs` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not confuse grammatical number with meaning. `Files` may be a group of documents, but it is still plural. `Committee` may represent many people, but when it is treated as one unit it is singular. Always check how the noun functions in the sentence.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The records were reviewed, and ____ were sent to the archive.`

Options:

1. it
2. they
3. its
4. himself or herself

Reasoning:

- `records` is plural.
- The pronoun must be plural.
- `they` is the correct subject pronoun.

Answer: **they**

### Quick Check

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The package was sealed, and ____ was placed on the shelf.`
Choices:
- they
- it
- their
Answer: `it`
Rationale: `package` is singular, so the pronoun must be singular.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The forms were checked, and ____ were filed by noon.`
Choices:
- it
- they
- itself
Answer: `they`
Rationale: `forms` is plural, so the pronoun must be plural.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The memo lost ____ stamp during delivery.`
Choices:
- their
- its
- them
Answer: `its`
Rationale: `memo` is singular, and the possessive adjective must match that number.

### Key Insight

Singular noun, singular pronoun. Plural noun, plural pronoun. That rule solves many CSE items by itself.

## Person Agreement and Pronoun Case

### Overview

Pronoun agreement also includes person. First person refers to the speaker, second person refers to the listener, and third person refers to someone or something else. In formal writing, you should not shift from one person to another without a clear reason.

### Core Principle

Keep the person consistent.

- first person: `I`, `we`, `my`, `our`
- second person: `you`, `your`
- third person: `he`, `she`, `it`, `they`, `his`, `her`, `its`, `their`

The pronoun must also fit its grammatical job. A subject pronoun, object pronoun, possessive adjective, possessive pronoun, and reflexive pronoun are not interchangeable.

### Visualization

| Function | Singular example | Plural example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| subject | `he`, `she`, `it` | `they` |
| object | `him`, `her`, `it` | `them` |
| possessive adjective | `his`, `her`, `its` | `their` |
| possessive pronoun | `his`, `hers`, `its` | `theirs` |
| reflexive | `himself`, `herself`, `itself` | `themselves` |

### Common Mistakes

Two errors appear often. First, the writer shifts person in the middle of the sentence. Second, the writer uses the wrong pronoun form, such as an object pronoun where a subject pronoun is needed. Even if the number is correct, the sentence is still wrong if the pronoun case does not match the grammar.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`When the applicant arrives, ____ should sign in at the desk.`

Options:

1. they
2. he or she
3. them
4. their

Reasoning:

- The antecedent `applicant` is singular.
- The sentence needs a subject pronoun.
- `he or she` keeps the number and person clear.

Answer: **he or she**

### Quick Check

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The officer asked ____ to wait outside.`
Choices:
- he
- him
- his
Answer: `him`
Rationale: The pronoun is the object of the verb `asked`.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The candidate checked ____ ID before entering.`
Choices:
- their
- his or her
- them
Answer: `his or her`
Rationale: The sentence needs a possessive adjective that matches a singular antecedent.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `You should bring ____ documents to the interview.`
Choices:
- your
- theirs
- him
Answer: `your`
Rationale: The sentence is in second person, so the pronoun should stay in second person.

### Key Insight

Number matters, but person and pronoun form matter too. A pronoun that is the wrong case is still wrong.

## Indefinite Pronouns and Compound Antecedents

### Overview

Some antecedents look plural in meaning but are singular in grammar. Others are joined by `and`, `or`, or `nor` and require careful checking. These are classic CSE traps because the sentence can feel logical while still being grammatically wrong.

### Core Principle

Memorize the common singular indefinite pronouns:

- `each`
- `everyone`
- `everybody`
- `anyone`
- `somebody`
- `someone`
- `nobody`
- `no one`
- `either`
- `neither`
- `one`

These usually take singular pronouns. Compound antecedents joined by `and` are usually plural. With `or` and `nor`, the pronoun follows the nearer antecedent when the sentence is checking number.

### Visualization

| Structure | Typical pronoun |
| --- | --- |
| `each of the applicants` | `he or she` |
| `everyone in the room` | `he or she` |
| `one of the officers` | `he or she` |
| `the manager and the assistant` | `they` |
| `either the clerk or the assistants` | `they` |
| `neither the guards nor the supervisor` | depends on the nearer noun |

### Common Mistakes

Do not let meaning override grammar. `Everyone` means many people, but it is singular in grammar. Do not forget the nearest-antecedent rule in `either/or` and `neither/nor` structures. The pronoun has to match the grammatical subject, not the idea in your head.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`Each of the applicants must present ____ ID before entering.`

Options:

1. their
2. his or her
3. them
4. theirs

Reasoning:

- `each` is singular.
- The sentence needs a singular possessive adjective.
- `his or her` is the best match in formal writing.

Answer: **his or her**

### Quick Check

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `Everyone in the room should place ____ bag under the chair.`
Choices:
- their
- his or her
- them
Answer: `his or her`
Rationale: `everyone` is singular in grammar, so the pronoun should be singular.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The manager and the assistant said ____ would finish the forms tonight.`
Choices:
- they
- it
- himself
Answer: `they`
Rationale: A compound subject joined by `and` is plural.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `Neither of the routes was safe, so ____ was chosen.`
Choices:
- they
- it
- their
Answer: `it`
Rationale: `neither` is singular, and the sentence refers to a route, not a group.

### Key Insight

Indefinite pronouns and compound antecedents are small grammar traps. Once you learn their patterns, they become easy points.

## Clear Reference and Safe Exam Choices

### Overview

Even if a pronoun matches in number and person, it can still be wrong if the reference is unclear. A formal sentence should not leave the reader guessing what the pronoun means. This is why pronoun agreement also includes clarity.

### Core Principle

A good pronoun must:

- match the antecedent in number
- stay in the correct person
- point clearly to one noun

When the reference is vague, the sentence needs a clearer noun or a better pronoun phrase. Purdue OWL also notes that singular they is increasingly accepted in modern usage, but in exam writing the safest answer is the one that makes the reference unmistakable.

### Visualization

| Problem | Better approach |
| --- | --- |
| unclear `it` | replace with a clear noun |
| vague `they` | identify the exact antecedent |
| mixed person | keep the same point of view |
| awkward singular reference | use `he or she` or a clear noun |

### Common Mistakes

Examinees sometimes choose a pronoun simply because it sounds smoother. But if the pronoun could refer to more than one noun, the sentence is weak. Clarity matters as much as agreement. A correct pronoun must help the reader, not make the reader stop and guess.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The committee said that ____ would release the schedule on Friday.`

Options:

1. it
2. they
3. them
4. their

Reasoning:

- The committee is being treated as one unit.
- The pronoun should be singular.
- `it` is the clearest choice in formal writing.

Answer: **it**

### Quick Check

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `Although the motorcycle hit the tree, ____ was not damaged.`
Choices:
- it
- they
- their
Answer: `it`
Rationale: The pronoun should clearly refer to the motorcycle, which is singular.

Question: Which sentence is clearest?
Choices:
- `The student thanked her teacher because she was helpful.`
- `The student thanked the teacher because she was helpful.`
- `The student thanked the teacher because the teacher was helpful.`
Answer: `The student thanked the teacher because the teacher was helpful.`
Rationale: Repeating the noun removes ambiguity.

Question: Which pronoun best completes the sentence: `The staff submitted ____ reports before the deadline.`
Choices:
- its
- their
- itself
Answer: `their`
Rationale: `staff` is treated as a plural group here, so the possessive adjective is plural.

### Key Insight

The best pronoun is the one that is both grammatically correct and unmistakably clear.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1

`The folder was sealed, and ____ was stored in the vault.`

Choices:

1. they
2. it
3. their
4. themselves

Answer: **it**

Reasoning: `folder` is singular, so the subject pronoun must be singular.

### Example 2

`The assistant asked the applicant to show ____ identification card.`

Choices:

1. his or her
2. their
3. them
4. itself

Answer: **his or her**

Reasoning: `applicant` is singular, and the sentence needs a possessive adjective.

### Example 3

`A number of volunteers said ____ would arrive early.`

Choices:

1. it
2. they
3. its
4. himself or herself

Answer: **they**

Reasoning: `a number of` means several, so the antecedent is plural.

## Key Takeaways

- Start with the antecedent.
- Check number first, then person, then pronoun form.
- Singular antecedents take singular pronouns.
- Plural antecedents take plural pronouns.
- Indefinite pronouns like `each`, `everyone`, and `one` are usually singular.
- Compound antecedents joined by `and` are usually plural.
- A pronoun must be clear, not just grammatically close.
- In formal exam writing, choose the clearest correct form.

## Summary

Pronoun agreement is about more than picking a short word that sounds right. You have to identify the antecedent, check whether it is singular or plural, keep the same person, and make sure the pronoun is clear. CSE items often hide the real antecedent or use tricky singular forms like `each`, `everyone`, or `one of the`. Once you slow down and match the pronoun to the noun step by step, the error becomes much easier to spot.
