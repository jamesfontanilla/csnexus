# Prepositions

## Explanations

### Introduction

Prepositions are small words that show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word in the sentence. They often show time, place, direction, or a more specific relationship such as cause, object, or association. In a sentence, a preposition usually begins a prepositional phrase.

In CSE error-recognition items, preposition errors are easy to miss because the wrong word is usually short and familiar. The sentence may look fine at first glance, but the wrong preposition can change the meaning or break a fixed grammar pattern. Your task is to check the relation being expressed, not just the surface shape of the sentence.

### Why Prepositions Are Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Examination checks whether you can read formal English accurately and quickly. Prepositions are tested because they show whether you can:

- choose the correct word for time, place, and direction
- follow fixed verb, adjective, and noun patterns
- recognize common collocations
- avoid extra or missing prepositions
- tell the difference between similar expressions such as `between` and `among`

This matters in government writing, where small grammar errors can make a sentence unclear or awkward.

### Common Mistakes

Examinees often make these mistakes:

- using `in`, `on`, and `at` interchangeably
- confusing `to`, `into`, `onto`, and `toward`
- mixing up `between` and `among`
- adding an extra preposition after a fixed verb
- dropping a preposition that a verb or adjective requires
- translating directly from another language instead of following English usage

These mistakes are common because prepositions are easy to hear in speech but harder to check in formal writing.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the time, place, or direction relationship in a sentence
- choose the correct preposition for common time expressions
- choose the correct preposition for location and movement
- recognize fixed verb, adjective, and noun patterns
- spot common preposition traps in CSE-style items

## What Prepositions Do

### Overview

A preposition introduces a phrase that links a noun or pronoun to another part of the sentence. Purdue OWL notes that prepositions begin prepositional phrases and can act like adjectives or adverbs by modifying verbs, adjectives, or nouns.

### Core Principle

Ask one simple question: What relationship is the blank showing?

- time: when something happens
- place: where something is
- direction: where something is moving
- relation: how one word connects to another

If you can name the relationship, the preposition usually becomes easier to choose.

### Visualization

| Relationship | Common function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| time | when | `on Monday`, `at noon`, `in March` |
| place | where | `in the drawer`, `on the table`, `at the gate` |
| direction | movement | `to the office`, `into the room`, `onto the shelf` |
| relation | fixed pattern | `depend on`, `interested in`, `different from` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not assume every blank before a noun needs a preposition. Some verbs, adjectives, and nouns require a specific preposition, while others do not. Also, do not choose a preposition only because it sounds common. The sentence must match the intended relationship.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`She sat ____ the red carpet while reading.`

Options:

1. on
2. in
3. at

Reasoning:

- The phrase shows surface location.
- `on` is the correct preposition for a surface.

Answer: **on**

### Quick Check

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The keys were left ____ the table.`
Choices:
- in
- on
- at
Answer: `on`
Rationale: A table is a surface, so `on` is correct.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The file was stored ____ the cabinet.`
Choices:
- in
- on
- at
Answer: `in`
Rationale: A cabinet contains the file.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The guard waited ____ the gate.`
Choices:
- in
- on
- at
Answer: `at`
Rationale: `At` is used for a general location or point.

### Key Insight

Prepositions are relationship words. First identify the relationship, then choose the preposition.

## Time Prepositions

### Overview

Time prepositions show when something happens. The most common exam patterns are `on`, `at`, `in`, `since`, and `for`. `During` and `within` can also appear in extended-time expressions. Purdue OWL and British Council both emphasize that `at`, `in`, and `on` are used for time in different ways.

### Core Principle

Use these time patterns:

- `on` for days and dates
- `at` for exact times and specific moments
- `in` for months, years, seasons, and parts of the day
- `since` for a starting point that continues to now
- `for` for a duration of time
- `during` for something happening within a period or event

### Visualization

| Time pattern | Best preposition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| days and dates | `on` | `on Monday`, `on July 14` |
| exact times | `at` | `at noon`, `at 8:15 p.m.` |
| months, years, seasons | `in` | `in March`, `in 2022` |
| starting point to now | `since` | `since 2019` |
| duration | `for` | `for two hours` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not use `at` with months or years. Do not use `in` with days or exact clock times. Do not confuse `since` and `for`: `since` points to the start, while `for` shows the length of time.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The report is due ____ Monday.`

Options:

1. at
2. on
3. in

Reasoning:

- `Monday` is a day.
- `on` is the correct preposition for days.

Answer: **on**

### Quick Check

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The train arrives ____ noon.`
Choices:
- on
- at
- in
Answer: `at`
Rationale: `Noon` is an exact time.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The branch opened ____ 2022.`
Choices:
- on
- at
- in
Answer: `in`
Rationale: Years take `in`.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The clerk has worked here ____ 2019.`
Choices:
- since
- for
- during
Answer: `since`
Rationale: `Since` marks the starting point.

### Key Insight

Time prepositions are easiest when you match the word to the size of the time unit: day, time, month, year, start point, or duration.

## Place and Direction

### Overview

Place prepositions show where something is. Direction prepositions show movement from one place to another. This group includes `in`, `on`, `at`, `between`, `among`, `to`, `into`, `onto`, `toward`, and `from`.

### Core Principle

Use these place and movement patterns:

- `in` for containment
- `on` for a surface
- `at` for a general location or point
- `between` for two distinct places or items
- `among` for a group or several items
- `to` for destination
- `into` for movement to the inside
- `onto` for movement to a surface
- `toward` for movement in the direction of something
- `from` for source or starting point

### Visualization

| Meaning | Best preposition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| contained inside | `in` | `in the drawer` |
| on a surface | `on` | `on the table` |
| general location | `at` | `at the gate` |
| two separate points | `between` | `between the bank and the school` |
| part of a group | `among` | `among the papers` |
| movement to a destination | `to` | `to the office` |
| movement into something | `into` | `into the room` |
| movement onto a surface | `onto` | `onto the shelf` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not confuse `between` and `among`. Use `between` for two separate items or clearly defined points, and `among` for a group. Also do not confuse `to`, `into`, and `onto`: `to` shows destination, `into` shows movement inside, and `onto` shows movement onto a surface.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The box was moved ____ the shelf.`

Options:

1. to
2. onto
3. into

Reasoning:

- The box moves onto a surface.
- `onto` is the best choice.

Answer: **onto**

### Quick Check

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The map was found ____ the drawer.`
Choices:
- in
- on
- at
Answer: `in`
Rationale: The drawer contains the map.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The house stood ____ the bank and the school.`
Choices:
- among
- between
- to
Answer: `between`
Rationale: The sentence refers to two distinct places.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The clerk walked ____ the records room.`
Choices:
- to
- into
- on
Answer: `to`
Rationale: The sentence shows a destination.

### Key Insight

Place and direction prepositions are about movement and location. Always ask whether the sentence is describing position, source, destination, or motion.

## Verbs, Adjectives, and Fixed Patterns

### Overview

Many preposition choices are fixed by the word before them. Certain verbs, adjectives, and nouns regularly take the same preposition in formal English. British Council specifically highlights verbs and adjectives that commonly take `for`, `from`, `in`, `of`, `on`, `to`, and `with`.

### Core Principle

Learn the common pattern, not just the isolated preposition:

- verbs: `depend on`, `wait for`, `listen to`, `look at`, `approve of`, `consist of`
- adjectives: `interested in`, `responsible for`, `afraid of`, `similar to`, `different from`
- fixed phrases: `met with`, `comply with`, `free from`, `subject to`

### Visualization

| Word type | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| verb | `depend on` | `depend on records` |
| verb | `wait for` | `wait for the courier` |
| verb | `listen to` | `listen to the supervisor` |
| adjective | `interested in` | `interested in public service` |
| adjective | `responsible for` | `responsible for the records` |
| adjective | `different from` | `different from the old version` |

### Common Mistakes

Do not translate literally from another language. English often uses a fixed collocation instead. Also do not add an extra preposition when the verb already has its required pattern. A sentence can look logical and still be wrong if the preposition does not match the established phrase.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The committee approved ____ the plan.`

Options:

1. on
2. of
3. with

Reasoning:

- The verb pattern is `approve of`.
- `of` is the correct preposition.

Answer: **of**

### Quick Check

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The office depends ____ accurate records.`
Choices:
- on
- for
- at
Answer: `on`
Rationale: The fixed verb pattern is `depend on`.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The applicant is interested ____ public service.`
Choices:
- in
- to
- for
Answer: `in`
Rationale: The adjective pattern is `interested in`.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The supervisor is responsible ____ the records.`
Choices:
- to
- for
- of
Answer: `for`
Rationale: The adjective pattern is `responsible for`.

### Key Insight

In many preposition questions, the answer is simply the fixed pattern that English speakers use with the word before the blank.

## Common Traps and Safe Exam Choices

### Overview

Preposition items often test whether you can avoid extra words, missing words, or mistaken pairings. The best exam move is to check the whole pattern, not just the nearest noun.

### Core Principle

Use these safe checks:

- if the sentence shows a fixed phrase, trust the fixed phrase
- if the sentence shows movement, use the correct direction preposition
- if the sentence shows a surface, use `on`
- if the sentence shows containment, use `in`
- if the sentence shows a general location, use `at`
- if the sentence compares two items, use `between`

### Visualization

| Trap | Safer check |
| --- | --- |
| extra preposition after a fixed verb | check the verb pattern |
| wrong time word | match day, time, month, year, or duration |
| `between` vs `among` | two items vs group |
| `to` vs `into` vs `onto` | destination vs inside vs onto a surface |
| literal translation | use natural English collocation |

### Common Mistakes

Typical traps include `discuss about`, `married with`, `rely of`, and other literal-sounding forms. In formal English, `discuss` usually does not need `about`, `married` normally takes `to`, and `rely` takes `on`. The exam often rewards the safest, most standard pattern.

### Worked Mini Example

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence below?

`The team was different ____ the other branch.`

Options:

1. to
2. from
3. on

Reasoning:

- The fixed pattern is `different from`.
- `from` is the safest formal choice.

Answer: **from**

### Quick Check

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The clerk discussed the delay ____ the supervisor.`
Choices:
- with
- about
- to
Answer: `with`
Rationale: The natural pattern is `discuss with someone`.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The book was moved ____ the cart.`
Choices:
- on
- onto
- into
Answer: `onto`
Rationale: The movement is onto a surface.

Question: Which preposition best completes the sentence: `The office was different ____ the old branch.`
Choices:
- from
- to
- with
Answer: `from`
Rationale: The standard pattern is `different from`.

### Key Insight

When in doubt, look for the standard English pattern rather than the most literal-sounding option.

## Key Takeaways

- Prepositions show time, place, direction, or relation.
- `On`, `at`, and `in` are the core time and place prepositions.
- `Since` marks a starting point; `for` marks a duration.
- `Between` is for two points; `among` is for a group.
- `To`, `into`, `onto`, `toward`, and `from` show movement.
- Many answers depend on fixed verb, adjective, or noun patterns.
- Literal translation is risky; standard English collocation is safer.
- The best answer is the one that matches the relationship in the sentence.

## Summary

Prepositions are short words, but they carry a lot of grammatical weight. They can show time, place, direction, or a fixed relationship with a verb, adjective, or noun. In the CSE, the most common traps are `in/on/at`, `to/into/onto`, `between/among`, and collocations like `depend on`, `interested in`, or `different from`. A good strategy is to identify the relationship first, then test whether the preposition matches the established English pattern.
