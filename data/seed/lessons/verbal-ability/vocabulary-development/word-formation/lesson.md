# Word Formation

## Explanations

### Introduction

**Word formation** is the study of how words are created, modified, and expanded through the combination of meaningful parts — roots, prefixes, and suffixes. It is a branch of morphology, the area of linguistics concerned with the internal structure of words. Understanding word formation gives you the ability to decode unfamiliar vocabulary by analyzing its components rather than relying on memorization alone.

Word formation mastery is essential for the Philippine Civil Service Examination because:
- The CSE Verbal Ability section consistently includes questions that require examinees to infer word meanings from structural analysis — recognizing that "irrevocable" contains "ir-" (not) + "revoc" (call back) + "-able" (capable of) eliminates the need to have memorized the word
- Government documents use Latinate and Greek-derived vocabulary extensively — "promulgate," "adjudicate," "circumscribe" — and structural analysis is the fastest path to comprehension
- Word formation knowledge multiplies your effective vocabulary exponentially: knowing 50 roots, 30 prefixes, and 20 suffixes gives you decoding access to thousands of English words
- The exam tests both direct morphology questions (identifying prefix meanings, recognizing root words) and indirect application (using word structure to eliminate wrong answers in synonym and context clue items)
- Civil servants encounter specialized terminology daily in memoranda, executive orders, and legal texts where word-part analysis is often the only available decoding strategy

### Why Word Formation Is Tested in the CSE

The Civil Service Exam tests word formation because:
- It measures analytical vocabulary skills — the ability to reason about language rather than simply recall definitions
- Government communication relies on precise, formal vocabulary built from Latin and Greek morphemes; recognizing these parts is a professional competency
- Word formation questions efficiently test depth of linguistic knowledge in a single item — one question about "anti-" tests understanding applicable to dozens of words
- The skill transfers directly to reading comprehension, where unfamiliar academic vocabulary must be decoded in real time
- Examinees who understand morphology can eliminate wrong answers structurally, even when they have never encountered the target word before
- The CSE rewards systematic thinkers — word formation rewards pattern recognition over rote memorization

### Common Mistakes Examinees Make

1. **Memorizing words as whole units** without understanding their internal structure — this limits vocabulary to only words previously encountered
2. **Confusing similar prefixes** — mixing up "anti-" (against) with "ante-" (before), or "inter-" (between) with "intra-" (within)
3. **Ignoring suffix function** — not recognizing that suffixes change grammatical category (e.g., "-ize" creates verbs, "-tion" creates nouns)
4. **Misidentifying root boundaries** — splitting "prescription" as "pre-script-ion" is correct, but splitting "president" as "pre-sident" misidentifies the root (it is "praesidere" = to sit before)
5. **Assuming all similar-looking words share roots** — "incredible" and "incremental" both start with "in-" but have completely different roots (cred = believe vs. crementum = growth)
6. **Overlooking context** — a word's structural meaning and its contextual meaning can diverge; "awful" structurally means "full of awe" but now means "terrible"
7. **Treating prefixes as absolute** — "inflammable" does NOT mean "not flammable"; the "in-" here is an intensifier, not a negator

### Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you should be able to:
- Define word formation and explain its role in vocabulary development
- Identify root words, prefixes, and suffixes in complex vocabulary
- Analyze how prefixes modify meaning (negation, direction, quantity, time)
- Recognize how suffixes change grammatical category (noun, verb, adjective, adverb)
- Map word family relationships across grammatical forms
- Distinguish derivational morphology from inflectional morphology
- Apply structural analysis to infer meanings of unfamiliar words
- Identify compound words, blends, clippings, and acronyms
- Recognize commonly tested affixes in CSE-style questions
- Use elimination strategies based on word structure to solve exam items accurately

### 4.1 What Is Word Formation?

**Word formation** refers to the processes by which new words are created or existing words are modified in a language. It is the central concern of **morphology** — the branch of linguistics that studies the internal structure of words.

Every word in English can be analyzed as either:
- A **simple word** (one morpheme): "run," "book," "green"
- A **complex word** (multiple morphemes): "un-break-able," "re-construct-ion," "dis-agree-ment"

A **morpheme** is the smallest unit of meaning in a language. There are two types:
- **Free morphemes** — can stand alone as words: "book," "help," "kind"
- **Bound morphemes** — cannot stand alone; must attach to another morpheme: "un-," "-ness," "-ing"

#### How Words Are Built

Words are constructed by combining morphemes in predictable patterns:

| Structure | Example | Breakdown |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Root only | help | help (free morpheme) |
| Prefix + Root | unhelpful | un- + help + -ful |
| Root + Suffix | helpful | help + -ful |
| Prefix + Root + Suffix | unhelpful | un- + help + -ful |
| Prefix + Prefix + Root + Suffix | unpredict-able | un- + pre- + dict + -able |

#### Why This Matters for the CSE

When you encounter an unfamiliar word on the exam, you can:
1. Identify the root → get the core meaning
2. Identify the prefix → understand how the meaning is modified
3. Identify the suffix → determine the grammatical category
4. Combine these insights → arrive at the word's meaning

**Example:** "The committee's decision was **irreversible**."
- ir- = not (prefix, negation)
- re- = back (prefix, direction)
- vers = turn (root, from Latin "vertere")
- -ible = capable of (suffix, adjective-forming)
- Combined: not capable of being turned back = permanent, cannot be undone

#### Examples by Difficulty

**Easy:**
- "unhappy" → un- (not) + happy = not happy
- "redo" → re- (again) + do = do again
- "teacher" → teach + -er (one who) = one who teaches

**Medium:**
- "misinterpret" → mis- (wrongly) + interpret = to understand wrongly
- "unforeseeable" → un- (not) + fore- (before) + see + -able = not able to be seen beforehand
- "discontinuation" → dis- (not) + con- (together) + tinu (hold) + -ation (act of) = the act of not continuing

**Hard:**
- "antidisestablishmentarianism" → anti- (against) + dis- (reverse) + establish + -ment (result) + -arian (believer) + -ism (doctrine)
- "circumnavigation" → circum- (around) + navig (sail) + -ation (act of) = the act of sailing around
- "incomprehensibility" → in- (not) + com- (together) + prehens (grasp) + -ibility (quality of being) = the quality of not being graspable/understandable

### 4.2 Root Words

A **root word** (also called a **base** or **stem**) is the core morpheme that carries the primary meaning of a word. All prefixes and suffixes attach to the root. Knowing common roots allows you to decode hundreds of English words.

#### How Roots Help Determine Meaning

**Strategy:** When you see an unfamiliar word, look for a recognizable root. The root gives you the core meaning; prefixes and suffixes modify it.

**Example 1:** "The witness was asked to **corroborate** the testimony."
- Root: "robor" = strength (Latin)
- "cor-" = together (prefix)
- "-ate" = to make (suffix)
- Meaning: to make strong together = to confirm, to support with evidence

**Example 2:** "The agency must **circumscribe** its authority to the designated areas."
- Root: "scrib" = write, draw (Latin)
- "circum-" = around (prefix)
- Meaning: to draw a line around = to limit, to restrict

**Example 3:** "The senator's speech was full of **rhetoric**."
- Root: "rhet" = speak (Greek, from "rhetor" = speaker)
- "-ic" = relating to (suffix)
- Meaning: the art of speaking/persuasion

### 4.3 Prefixes

A **prefix** is a bound morpheme added to the beginning of a root word to modify its meaning. Prefixes never change the grammatical category of a word — they only change its meaning.

#### Negation Prefixes

These prefixes reverse or negate the meaning of the root.

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| un- | not, opposite of | unhappy, unable, unfair, undo, unlock |
| in-/im-/il-/ir- | not | invisible, impossible, illegal, irregular |
| dis- | not, opposite of | disagree, disappear, disconnect, disqualify |
| non- | not | nonsense, nonprofit, nonessential, noncompliant |
| a-/an- | without | amoral, anonymous, atypical, anarchy |
| mis- | wrongly, badly | misunderstand, misplace, misinform, mismanage |
| anti- | against | antisocial, antibacterial, anticlimactic |
| counter- | against, opposite | counterproductive, counterargument, counterfeit |

**Important distinction:** "in-" has TWO meanings:
- "not" → invisible, incredible, inadequate
- "into/in" → inject, include, inspire, inflame

Context determines which meaning applies. On the CSE, if the root word is an adjective, "in-" usually means "not."

#### Direction and Position Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| pre- | before | predict, prevent, precede, prerequisite |
| post- | after | postpone, postgraduate, postmortem |
| re- | again, back | return, review, rebuild, reconsider |
| sub- | under, below | submarine, subordinate, substandard |
| super- | above, beyond | supervise, supernatural, superfluous |
| inter- | between, among | international, intervene, interact |
| intra- | within | intranet, intravenous, intramural |
| trans- | across, beyond | transport, transfer, transcend, translate |
| circum- | around | circumvent, circumscribe, circumstance |
| ex- | out of, former | export, exclude, extract, ex-president |
| pro- | forward, in favor | promote, progress, propose, proactive |

**Common CSE trap:** "inter-" vs. "intra-"
- inter- = between (international = between nations)
- intra- = within (intravenous = within veins)

#### Quantity and Size Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| mono-/uni- | one | monopoly, uniform, monologue, unicycle |
| bi-/di- | two | bicycle, bilingual, dioxide, dilemma |
| tri- | three | triangle, trilingual, trilogy |
| multi-/poly- | many | multiply, polygon, multilateral, polyglot |
| semi-/hemi- | half | semicircle, hemisphere, semifinal |
| over- | excessive | overestimate, overstate, overwork |
| under- | insufficient | underestimate, underpay, underperform |
| hyper- | excessive | hyperactive, hypersensitive, hyperbole |
| hypo- | under, below normal | hypothermia, hypothesis, hypoallergenic |

#### Time Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| pre- | before | prehistoric, prearrange, premature |
| post- | after | postwar, postpone, posterior |
| fore- | before, in front | foresee, forecast, forewarn |

### 4.4 Suffixes

A **suffix** is a bound morpheme added to the end of a root word. Unlike prefixes, suffixes frequently change the grammatical category (part of speech) of the word. This is critical for CSE questions that ask you to identify the correct form of a word.

#### Noun-Forming Suffixes

These suffixes turn verbs, adjectives, or other roots into nouns.

| Suffix | Meaning | Base → Noun |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| -tion/-sion/-ation | act or state of | educate → education, decide → decision |
| -ment | result or act of | govern → government, develop → development |
| -ness | state or quality | happy → happiness, dark → darkness |
| -ity/-ty | state or quality | active → activity, certain → certainty |
| -er/-or | one who does | teach → teacher, act → actor |
| -ist | one who practices | science → scientist, art → artist |
| -ance/-ence | state or quality | perform → performance, depend → dependence |
| -dom | state or realm | free → freedom, king → kingdom |
| -ship | state or position | leader → leadership, member → membership |
| -ism | doctrine or belief | capital → capitalism, national → nationalism |
| -logy | study of | bio → biology, psycho → psychology |
| -ure | act or process | fail → failure, depart → departure |

#### Verb-Forming Suffixes

These suffixes turn nouns or adjectives into verbs.

| Suffix | Meaning | Base → Verb |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| -ize/-ise | to make or become | modern → modernize, legal → legalize |
| -ify/-fy | to make or cause | simple → simplify, class → classify |
| -ate | to make or act | active → activate, valid → validate |
| -en | to make or become | wide → widen, strength → strengthen |

#### Adjective-Forming Suffixes

These suffixes turn nouns or verbs into adjectives.

| Suffix | Meaning | Base → Adjective |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| -ful | full of | hope → hopeful, care → careful |
| -less | without | hope → hopeless, care → careless |
| -ous/-ious/-eous | full of, having | danger → dangerous, courage → courageous |
| -able/-ible | capable of being | manage → manageable, access → accessible |
| -ive/-ative/-itive | tending to | act → active, create → creative |
| -al/-ial | relating to | nation → national, industry → industrial |
| -ic/-ical | relating to | history → historic/historical, economy → economic |
| -ant/-ent | performing | import → important, depend → dependent |
| -ary/-ory | relating to | revolution → revolutionary, contradict → contradictory |

#### Adverb-Forming Suffixes

| Suffix | Meaning | Base → Adverb |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| -ly | in the manner of | quick → quickly, careful → carefully |
| -ward(s) | in the direction of | back → backward, home → homeward |
| -wise | in the manner of | clock → clockwise, other → otherwise |

#### How Suffixes Change Word Class — Complete Example

| Root: "create" (verb) | Suffix Added | New Word | New Class |
|----------------------|--------------|----------|-----------|
| create | + -tion | creation | noun |
| create | + -ive | creative | adjective |
| creative | + -ly | creatively | adverb |
| creative | + -ity | creativity | noun |
| create | + -or | creator | noun (person) |

#### CSE Application

**Question type:** "Which word correctly completes the sentence?"

"The _____ of the new policy requires careful planning."
- (a) implement (verb)
- (b) implementation (noun) ✅
- (c) implementing (gerund/participle)
- (d) implemented (past tense)

The blank follows "The" and precedes "of" — it must be a noun. The suffix "-ation" creates nouns from verbs. Answer: (b) implementation.

### 4.5 Word Families

A **word family** is a group of words that share the same root and are related in meaning but differ in grammatical form. Mastering word families allows you to:
- Recognize the correct form needed in a sentence
- Expand your vocabulary by learning multiple forms of one root
- Identify relationships between words on the CSE

#### Complete Word Family Examples

**Family 1: "employ"**

| Form | Word | Example Sentence |
|------|------|-----------------|
| Verb | employ | The agency will employ 50 new staff members. |
| Noun (person) | employee | Every employee must submit a daily time record. |
| Noun (person) | employer | The employer is responsible for providing benefits. |
| Noun (concept) | employment | Employment opportunities are available in the region. |
| Noun (concept) | unemployment | Unemployment rates decreased this quarter. |
| Adjective | employable | Graduates must develop employable skills. |

**Easy:** "The _____ of the project depends on proper planning."

- decision (noun) ✅ — follows "a" (article requires noun)

**Hard:** "The policy was _____ implemented across all regional offices."
- systematically (adverb) ✅ — modifies the verb "implemented"

### 4.6 Derivational Morphology

**Derivational morphology** is the process of creating new words by adding derivational affixes (prefixes or suffixes) to existing words. The result is a new word with a different meaning or a different grammatical category.

#### Key Characteristics of Derivational Morphology

1. **Creates new words** — "happy" → "unhappy" is a different word with a different meaning
2. **Can change grammatical category** — "teach" (verb) → "teacher" (noun)
3. **Can change meaning without changing category** — "happy" (adj) → "unhappy" (adj)
4. **Is not predictable** — not every adjective can take "-ness" (we say "happiness" but not "funness" in formal English)
5. **Occurs before inflectional morphology** — derivational suffixes attach closer to the root than inflectional ones: "national-iz-ation-s" (derivational before inflectional "-s")

#### Derivation by Category Change

| Input Category | Output Category | Affix | Example |
|---------------|----------------|-------|---------|
| Verb → Noun | | -tion/-ment/-er/-ance | educate → education |
| Adjective → Noun | | -ness/-ity/-ism | dark → darkness |
| Noun → Adjective | | -ful/-less/-ous/-al | hope → hopeful |
| Adjective → Verb | | -ize/-ify/-en | modern → modernize |
| Noun → Verb | | -ize/-ify/-ate | symbol → symbolize |
| Adjective → Adverb | | -ly | careful → carefully |
| Verb → Adjective | | -able/-ive/-ent | manage → manageable |

#### Derivation Without Category Change

| Input | Affix | Output | Change |
|-------|-------|--------|--------|
| happy (adj) | un- | unhappy (adj) | meaning reversed |
| write (verb) | re- | rewrite (verb) | meaning: again |
| standard (noun) | sub- | substandard (noun/adj) | meaning: below |
| national (adj) | inter- | international (adj) | meaning: between |

#### Multiple Derivation (Layered Affixation)

Complex words are built layer by layer. Each layer adds one affix:

**"Uncharacteristically"**
1. character (noun) — base
2. character + -istic → characteristic (adjective)
3. characteristic + -al → characteristical (adjective, archaic) → simplified to characteristic
4. characteristic + -ally → characteristically (adverb)
5. un- + characteristically → uncharacteristically (adverb)

**"Industrialization"**
1. industry (noun) — base
2. industry → industrial (adjective, via -al)
3. industrial → industrialize (verb, via -ize)
4. industrialize → industrialization (noun, via -ation)

#### CSE Application

Understanding derivational layers helps you:
- Identify the base word in a complex term
- Predict what part of speech a word is based on its final suffix
- Recognize that the outermost suffix determines grammatical category

### 4.7 Inflectional Morphology

**Inflectional morphology** modifies a word's form to express grammatical information (tense, number, comparison, possession) WITHOUT creating a new word or changing its category.

#### English Inflectional Suffixes (Complete List)

English has exactly eight inflectional suffixes:

| Suffix | Function | Example |
|--------|----------|---------|
| -s/-es | Plural nouns | book → books, box → boxes |
| -'s | Possessive | employee → employee's |
| -s | Third person singular present | walk → walks |
| -ed | Past tense | walk → walked |
| -ed/-en | Past participle | walk → walked, break → broken |
| -ing | Present participle/gerund | walk → walking |
| -er | Comparative adjective | tall → taller |
| -est | Superlative adjective | tall → tallest |

#### Derivational vs. Inflectional — Key Differences

| Feature | Derivational | Inflectional |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Creates new word? | Yes | No (same word, different form) |
| Changes category? | Often | Never |
| Position | Closer to root | Outermost (after derivational) |
| Predictable? | Not always | Highly regular |
| Meaning change? | Yes (new meaning) | Grammatical only |

**Example:** "teachers"
- teach (root, verb)
- teach + -er (derivational → noun "teacher")
- teacher + -s (inflectional → plural "teachers")

The derivational suffix "-er" comes before the inflectional "-s." This order is fixed in English.

#### Why This Matters for the CSE

Inflectional morphology questions on the CSE typically test:
- Subject-verb agreement (singular/plural)
- Correct tense usage
- Comparative/superlative forms
- Possessive vs. plural confusion (its vs. it's, employees vs. employee's)

**Common CSE trap:** "The employees' performance was evaluated."
- employees' = possessive plural (the performance belonging to the employees)
- employee's = possessive singular
- employees = simple plural (no possession)

### 4.8 Contextual Word Analysis

**Contextual word analysis** combines structural knowledge (roots, prefixes, suffixes) with sentence context to decode unfamiliar vocabulary. This is the most powerful strategy for the CSE because it works even when you have never seen the word before.

#### The Three-Step Decode Method

**Step 1: Break the word into parts**
Identify any recognizable root, prefix, or suffix.

**Step 2: Assemble a structural meaning**
Combine the meanings of the parts into a rough definition.

**Step 3: Verify against context**
Check whether your structural meaning makes sense in the sentence. If it does, you have your answer. If not, the word may have shifted meaning over time — rely more on context.

#### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "The new regulation is designed to **preclude** any conflict of interest."
- Step 1: pre- (before) + clude (close, from Latin "claudere")
- Step 2: to close before = to prevent, to make impossible in advance
- Step 3: Context confirms — the regulation prevents conflicts before they happen
- Meaning: prevent, make impossible

**Example 2:** "The department head was known for her **magnanimous** leadership style."
- Step 1: magn- (great, large) + anim- (spirit, mind) + -ous (full of)
- Step 2: full of great spirit = generous, noble-minded
- Step 3: Context — positive description of leadership style confirms generosity/nobility
- Meaning: generous, forgiving, noble

**Example 3:** "The auditor found **discrepancies** between the reported and actual figures."
- Step 1: dis- (apart) + crep- (rattle, differ) + -ancy (state of)
- Step 2: state of being apart/different = differences, inconsistencies
- Step 3: Context — differences between reported and actual numbers confirms
- Meaning: differences, inconsistencies

**Example 4:** "The memorandum was **promulgated** to all regional offices."
- Step 1: pro- (forward) + mulg- (make public, from Latin "promulgare") + -ated (past tense)
- Step 2: made public forward = officially announced, disseminated
- Step 3: Context — sent to all offices confirms wide distribution
- Meaning: officially announced, made widely known

#### When Structure and Context Conflict

Some words have drifted from their structural meaning over centuries:
- "awful" = structurally "full of awe" but now means "terrible"
- "nice" = from Latin "nescius" (ignorant) but now means "pleasant"
- "manufacture" = structurally "make by hand" but now means factory production

**Rule:** When structure and context conflict, trust context. Structure gives you a starting hypothesis; context gives you the actual meaning.

### 4.9 Word Formation Patterns

Beyond affixation, English creates new words through several other processes. The CSE occasionally tests awareness of these patterns.

#### Compound Words

A **compound word** is formed by combining two or more free morphemes (independent words) into a single unit.

| Type | Formation | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Closed compound | Written as one word | notebook, workplace, deadline, headquarters |
| Hyphenated compound | Connected by hyphen | well-known, self-employed, decision-making |
| Open compound | Written as two words | ice cream, post office, civil service |

**CSE relevance:** Government vocabulary includes many compounds:
- headquarters, workforce, oversight, stakeholder, framework

### 4.11 Elimination Strategies

When facing a word formation question on the CSE, you do not always need to know the exact answer. Strategic elimination based on structural analysis can narrow your choices from four to one or two.

#### Strategy 1: Eliminate by Prefix Contradiction

If you recognize the prefix of the target word, eliminate any choice whose meaning contradicts that prefix.

**Example:** "What does **unprecedented** mean?"
- "un-" = not, "pre-" = before, "ced" = go
- Structural meaning: not gone before = never happened before
- Eliminate: (a) "common" ← contradicts "un-" (not)
- Eliminate: (b) "expected" ← contradicts "un-" (not)
- Keep: (c) "unparalleled" ← matches "not before"
- Eliminate: (d) "traditional" ← contradicts "un-" (not)

#### Strategy 2: Eliminate by Suffix Mismatch

If the question asks for a specific part of speech, eliminate choices that belong to a different category.

**Example:** "Choose the correct NOUN form."
- Eliminate any word ending in -ful, -less, -ive (adjectives)
- Eliminate any word ending in -ly (adverb)
- Keep words ending in -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity (nouns)

#### Strategy 3: Eliminate by Root Recognition

If you recognize the root in the target word, eliminate choices whose roots point to unrelated meanings.

**Example:** "What does **benevolent** mean?"
- Root: "bene-" = good/well, "vol" = wish/will
- Structural meaning: wishing good = kind, charitable
- Eliminate: (a) "hostile" ← opposite of good-wishing
- Eliminate: (b) "indifferent" ← no connection to "good"
- Keep: (c) "charitable" ← matches good-wishing ✅
- Eliminate: (d) "strict" ← no connection to "good"

#### Strategy 4: Eliminate by Morphological Impossibility

Some combinations of affixes are impossible in English. If a choice contains an impossible structure, eliminate it.

**Rules:**
- Double negation prefixes are rare: "un-in-" combinations are suspicious
- "-ness" does not attach to verbs directly (no "runningness")
- "-ize" does not attach to adverbs (no "quicklyize")

#### Strategy 5: Use Word Family Knowledge

If you know one member of a word family, you can deduce others.

**Example:** You know "economy" (noun) = management of resources
- "economize" (verb) = to manage resources carefully = to save
- "economical" (adjective) = resource-efficient = thrifty
- "economist" (noun, person) = one who studies the economy

#### The Structural Elimination Flowchart

1. Can you identify the prefix? → Eliminate choices that contradict it
2. Can you identify the root? → Eliminate choices with unrelated meanings
3. Can you identify the suffix? → Eliminate choices with wrong part of speech
4. Still stuck? → Choose the most formal option (CSE bias toward formal register)

### 4.12 Common Word Formation Errors

Understanding common errors helps you avoid traps on the CSE and recognize incorrect answer choices.

#### Error 1: Confusing Similar Prefixes

| Confused Pair | Correct Meanings | Common Mistake |
|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| anti- / ante- | anti- = against; ante- = before | Thinking "anteroom" means "against room" |
| inter- / intra- | inter- = between; intra- = within | Thinking "intranet" means "between networks" |
| dis- / dys- | dis- = not/opposite; dys- = bad/difficult | Confusing "disfunction" (wrong) with "dysfunction" (correct) |
| pre- / pro- | pre- = before; pro- = forward/in favor | Mixing up temporal and directional meanings |
| hyper- / hypo- | hyper- = over/excessive; hypo- = under/below | Thinking "hypothermia" means overheating |
| emigrate / immigrate | e- = out; im- = into | Confusing direction of movement |

#### Error 2: Incorrect Suffix Usage

| Error | Correct Form | Rule |
|-------|-------------|------|
| "arguement" | argument | Drop the "e" before "-ment" for "argue" |
| "occurence" | occurrence | Double the consonant: occur → occurrence |
| "responsable" | responsible | The correct suffix is "-ible" not "-able" |
| "goverment" | government | Keep the "n" from "govern" |
| "maintainance" | maintenance | The suffix is "-ance" (maintain → maintenance) |

#### Error 3: Misidentifying Word Classes

| Word | Looks Like | Actually Is |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| "oversight" | verb (over + sight) | noun (a failure to notice OR supervision) |
| "outstanding" | adjective only | can be adjective (excellent) or adjective (unpaid: outstanding balance) |
| "subject" | noun only | noun (topic), verb (to subject someone to), adjective (subject to approval) |
| "record" | one meaning | noun (REcord) vs. verb (reCORD) — stress changes meaning |

#### Error 4: Misunderstanding Root Meanings

| Word | Wrong Analysis | Correct Analysis |
|------|---------------|-----------------|
| "inflammable" | in- (not) + flammable = not burnable | in- (into/intensifier) + flammable = VERY burnable |
| "invaluable" | in- (not) + valuable = worthless | in- (intensifier) + valuable = extremely valuable |
| "president" | pre- (before) + sident | from Latin "praesidere" = to sit before/preside |
| "manufacture" | manu- (hand) + facture (make) | originally "make by hand" but now means factory production |

#### Error 5: Ignoring Context When Structure Misleads

| Word | Structural Meaning | Actual Meaning | Why |
|------|-------------------|----------------|-----|
| awful | full of awe | terrible | meaning shifted over centuries |
| terrific | causing terror | wonderful | meaning reversed |
| nice | ignorant (Latin nescius) | pleasant | complete semantic shift |
| silly | blessed (Old English sælig) | foolish | meaning degraded |

**Lesson:** Structure gives you a hypothesis. Context gives you the answer. When they conflict, trust context.

### 4.13 Exam Strategies

#### Time Management for Word Formation Questions

- **Easy questions (10-15 seconds):** Simple prefix/suffix identification — answer immediately
- **Medium questions (20-30 seconds):** Root word analysis with context — break the word, verify against sentence
- **Hard questions (30-45 seconds):** Multi-layered morphology with unfamiliar roots — use elimination

#### Quick-Decode Protocol

1. **Scan the word** — do you recognize it immediately? If yes, answer and move on.
2. **Check the prefix** — does it negate, reverse, or modify direction? This alone eliminates 1-2 choices.
3. **Check the suffix** — what part of speech is the word? Eliminate choices with wrong category.
4. **Check the root** — even partial recognition helps. "Bene-" = good narrows everything.
5. **Verify against context** — does your decoded meaning fit the sentence?

#### Red Flags on the CSE

Watch for these trap patterns:
- **The "obvious" wrong answer** — a choice that uses the same prefix but wrong root (e.g., for "predict," offering "prevent" because both start with "pre-")
- **The phonetic trap** — a choice that sounds similar but has different morphology (e.g., "illicit" vs. "elicit")
- **The partial match** — a choice that shares the root but has a contradicting prefix (e.g., for "include," offering "exclude")
- **The category trap** — a choice that is the right meaning but wrong part of speech

### 4.14 Real CSE-Like Examples

#### Easy Examples

**1.** "What does the prefix 'un-' mean in the word 'unfair'?"
- Answer: not
- Explanation: "un-" is a negation prefix. "Unfair" = not fair.
- Structural analysis: un- (not) + fair (just, equitable)

**2.** "Which word means 'to write again'?"
- (a) prescribe (b) rewrite (c) describe (d) inscribe
- Answer: (b) rewrite
- Explanation: "re-" means again, "write" is the base. Rewrite = write again.

**3.** "The suffix '-er' in 'teacher' indicates:"
- (a) the act of (b) one who does (c) full of (d) without
- Answer: (b) one who does
- Explanation: "-er" is an agent suffix meaning "one who performs the action." Teacher = one who teaches.

#### Medium Examples

**4.** "Based on its word parts, 'malfunction' most likely means:"
- (a) work well (b) work badly (c) work again (d) work before
- Answer: (b) work badly
- Explanation: "mal-" = bad/badly, "function" = to work/operate. Malfunction = to work badly, to fail.

**5.** "The word 'interagency' describes something that occurs:"
- (a) within one agency (b) against agencies (c) between agencies (d) before an agency
- Answer: (c) between agencies
- Explanation: "inter-" = between/among. Interagency = between or involving multiple agencies.

**6.** "Which suffix would change 'authorize' (verb) into a noun?"
- (a) -ful (b) -ation (c) -ive (d) -ly
- Answer: (b) -ation
- Explanation: "-ation" converts verbs to nouns. Authorize → authorization (the act of authorizing).

#### Hard Examples

**7.** "Based on its morphological structure, 'circumscribe' most likely means:"
- (a) to write quickly (b) to draw a boundary around (c) to describe in detail (d) to write before
- Answer: (b) to draw a boundary around
- Explanation: "circum-" = around, "scrib" = write/draw. To circumscribe = to draw a line around = to limit or restrict.

**8.** "The word 'antediluvian' (ante- = before, diluv- = flood) means:"
- (a) against flooding (b) extremely old-fashioned (c) waterproof (d) forward-thinking
- Answer: (b) extremely old-fashioned
- Explanation: Literally "before the flood" (Biblical flood). Figuratively means extremely old, outdated, or antiquated.

**9.** "In the word 'irreconcilable,' the prefix 'ir-' functions as:"
- (a) into (b) again (c) not (d) between
- Answer: (c) not
- Explanation: "ir-" before words starting with "r" is a variant of "in-" meaning "not." Irreconcilable = not able to be reconciled.

### Mini Practice Set

**1.** What does the prefix "mis-" mean in "misappropriate"?
- (a) again (b) not (c) wrongly (d) before
- **Answer: (c) wrongly** — "mis-" means wrongly or badly. Misappropriate = to wrongly take/use funds.

**2.** The suffix "-ment" in "government" indicates the word is a:
- (a) verb (b) adjective (c) noun (d) adverb
- **Answer: (c) noun** — "-ment" creates nouns from verbs. Govern → government.

**3.** Which word contains the root meaning "to lead"?
- (a) conduct (b) construct (c) contract (d) contact
- **Answer: (a) conduct** — "duct/duc" = lead. Conduct = to lead together.

**4.** "Unprecedented" means:
- (a) expected (b) never done before (c) repeated (d) traditional
- **Answer: (b) never done before** — un- (not) + pre- (before) + ced (go) + -ent + -ed = not gone before.

**5.** The word "biodegradable" can be broken into:
- (a) bio + degrade + able (b) bi + o + degrade + able (c) bio + de + grad + able (d) bi + odeg + radable
- **Answer: (a) bio + degrade + able** — bio (life) + degrade (break down) + -able (capable of) = capable of being broken down by living organisms.

**6.** What does "substandard" mean?
- (a) above standard (b) new standard (c) below standard (d) without standard
- **Answer: (c) below standard** — "sub-" = under/below. Substandard = below the acceptable standard.

**7.** Which suffix changes "nation" (noun) into an adjective?
- (a) -ize (b) -al (c) -ment (d) -tion
- **Answer: (b) -al** — Nation + -al = national (adjective meaning "relating to a nation").

**8.** "Decentralize" means to:
- (a) make more central (b) remove from the center (c) go to the center (d) center again
- **Answer: (b) remove from the center** — "de-" = remove/reverse, "central" = center, "-ize" = to make. Decentralize = to distribute away from a central authority.

**9.** The root "cred" in "incredible" means:
- (a) run (b) believe (c) write (d) see
- **Answer: (b) believe** — "cred" = believe. In- (not) + cred (believe) + -ible (able) = not able to be believed.

**10.** "Noncompliance" is formed by:
- (a) non + comply + ance (b) non + com + pliance (c) no + n + compliance (d) non + compliance
- **Answer: (d) non + compliance** — "non-" (not) attaches to the existing noun "compliance" (state of complying).

**11.** What part of speech is "administrative"?
- (a) noun (b) verb (c) adjective (d) adverb
- **Answer: (c) adjective** — The suffix "-ive" creates adjectives. Administrative describes something relating to administration.

**12.** "Retroactive" means:
- (a) acting forward (b) acting in the present (c) taking effect from a past date (d) acting quickly
- **Answer: (c) taking effect from a past date** — "retro-" = backward, "-active" = acting. Acting backward in time.

**13.** Which word means "one who studies the mind"?
- (a) pathologist (b) psychologist (c) biologist (d) geologist
- **Answer: (b) psychologist** — "psych" = mind, "-ologist" = one who studies.

**14.** The prefix "anti-" in "anti-graft" means:
- (a) before (b) after (c) against (d) with
- **Answer: (c) against** — "anti-" = against. Anti-graft = against graft/corruption.

**15.** "Oversimplify" means to:
- (a) simplify correctly (b) simplify too much (c) not simplify (d) simplify again
- **Answer: (b) simplify too much** — "over-" = excessively. Oversimplify = to simplify to an excessive degree.

**16.** The word "telecommunication" contains the root "tele" meaning:
- (a) many (b) self (c) far/distant (d) small
- **Answer: (c) far/distant** — "tele" = far. Telecommunication = communication over a distance.

**17.** Which is the correct noun form of "efficient"?
- (a) efficienty (b) efficiency (c) efficientness (d) efficientment
- **Answer: (b) efficiency** — The adjective "efficient" takes "-cy" (variant of "-cy/-ency") to form the noun.

**18.** "Insubordinate" means:
- (a) very obedient (b) not obedient to authority (c) below a subordinate (d) working together
- **Answer: (b) not obedient to authority** — "in-" (not) + "subordinate" (under authority) = not willing to obey authority.

**19.** The suffix "-fy" in "ratify" means:
- (a) full of (b) without (c) to make (d) one who
- **Answer: (c) to make** — "-fy/-ify" = to make or cause. Ratify = to make official/valid.

**20.** "Multilateral" describes an agreement involving:
- (a) one side (b) two sides (c) many sides (d) no sides
- **Answer: (c) many sides** — "multi-" = many, "lateral" = side. Multilateral = involving many parties/sides.

### Quick Recap

#### Core Concepts

1. **Root words** carry the primary meaning — Latin roots dominate formal/legal English; Greek roots dominate scientific/academic English
2. **Prefixes** modify meaning (negation, direction, quantity, time) without changing grammatical category
3. **Suffixes** often change grammatical category (verb → noun, noun → adjective, adjective → adverb)
4. **Word families** are groups of related words sharing a root but differing in form and function
5. **Derivational morphology** creates new words; **inflectional morphology** modifies existing words for grammar

#### The Decode Method

1. Break the word into recognizable parts (prefix + root + suffix)
2. Assign meaning to each part
3. Combine meanings into a rough definition
4. Verify against sentence context
5. If structure and context conflict, trust context

#### Common Traps

- Confusing similar prefixes (anti-/ante-, inter-/intra-, hyper-/hypo-)
- Assuming "in-" always means "not" (it can mean "into" or intensify)
- Ignoring that suffixes determine part of speech
- Treating structural meaning as absolute when words have shifted meaning over time
